# How Designers Style Console Tables for That Effortless, Collected Look
By
[Ashley Chalmers](https://www.thespruce.com/ashley-chalmers-5104746)
[Ashley Chalmers](https://www.thespruce.com/ashley-chalmers-5104746)

Figuring out [where to place your television](https://www.thespruce.com/where-to-put-your-television-1976760) can be tricky, and this is especially true if you have limited wall space. While some prefer to place theirs on a mantle, others look toward full entertainment centers, and [a console table is a great alternative](https://www.thespruce.com/tv-console-decor-ideas-5324282), too. 
It can be fun and exciting to decorate a console table, but important do so without blocking the view of the TV. With this goal in mind, we checked in with two designers to find out their top tips for decorating a console table when placed beneath a TV. 
  * [**Brittny Button**](https://www.buttonatelier.com/about) is an interior designer and the founder of Button Atelier. 
  * **[Alex Alonso](https://www.mralextate.com/mr-alex) **is an interior designer and the founder of mr alex TATE.


**Want more design inspiration? Sign up for our free[daily newsletter](https://www.thespruce.com/the-spruce-daily-inline-8610447) for the latest decor ideas, designer tips, and more!**
01 of 06
##  Note the Space Available 
The Spruce / Jacob Fox
Before you start picking out your decor, designer Brittny Button says it’s important to consider how much space you have to work with between the television and the console table. 
[If your television is hanging](https://www.thespruce.com/tv-stand-or-wall-mount-7152746), you might have more room to play with, but if you’re working with a TV on a stand, you’ll need to stick to low-profile objects. 
“Sometimes a central piece positioned in the middle of the table works,” says Button. “Or simply placing three objects on one end in varying heights might be best.” 
02 of 06
##  Focus on the Function 
[Casa Watkins Living](https://casawatkinsliving.com/)
Often, people try to [hide their TV](https://www.thespruce.com/tips-design-pros-always-use-to-hide-a-tv-8760004) or design the area to be concealed. But as an alternative, designer Alex Alonso likes to focus on the function of this area of the room and let that lead the design plan. 
“I like displaying coffee table books that are meaningful to the homeowners and reflect their passions,” he says. “Places they've traveled, artists they like, items they love to collect.” 
Beyond this, he also adds a lidded box for any media-related accessories, including remotes and cables, and a space for other cozy necessities like blankets and cushions. 
Everyday items are another great addition to this area. Alonso says picture frames are another great option here. 
“As long as the frames all have a cohesive design thread it makes for a great design feature in the room,” he says. 
03 of 06
##  Focus on Balance 
[Maite Granda](https://maitegranda.com/)
The important thing, says Button, is to make sure your console table decor balances the space so that this natural focal point integrates well into the rest of the room. 
“The items need to have enough visual weight to balance the size or your television,” says Button, who suggests playing around with location, as well. “Try to keep the styling on either side of the TV itself, beneath it if the object is low profile enough or keep the styling to just one side to create asymmetry." 
Then, within your styled vignettes, focus again on balancing out the overall shape. 
“If there’s more than one object, create a pyramid-like shape with the items,” says Button. “Add a piece with more height to draw the eye upwards and pair it with two lower height profile pieces.” 
04 of 06
##  Keep a Low Profile 
[Forbes + Masters](https://www.forbesmasters.com/portfolio)
Even if you have a decent amount of space between the table and the TV, Button says it’s still best to work with landscape-oriented objects. These can be sculptures, shallow and oblong-shaped bowls, stacked books, or wooden trays. 
“Overall, think of styling horizontally rather than vertically to avoid obstruction,” she says. 
You can also look for items that are stylish and functional, adding to the decor in a particularly useful way. 
“I like to keep remotes hidden from view, so a tray helps with this and keeps you organized,” says Button. 
05 of 06
##  Don’t Ignore the Black Box 
[Forbes + Masters](https://www.forbesmasters.com/portfolio)
One reason designers hate working with a large television on the wall is because it creates a huge, black void that’s hard to ignore. But Button says she actually doesn’t think it should be ignored at all. Instead, she likes to treat the television as the central focus and style it as a display wall. 
“Try to include items that have some black colored detailing given that the TV is black when not in use,” she says. “This will make the design feel intentional.” 
06 of 06
##  Don’t Hold Back 
Photo: Sara Ligorria-Tramp / Design: [Emily Henderson Design](https://stylebyemilyhenderson.com/blog/arlyns-moto-living-room-velvet-sofa)
As long as you’re not blocking the view, Alonso says this is a space in the room where less can absolutely be more. 
“I’m a maximalist, so I'm totally okay with filling up the space under a TV,” he says. 
Along with colorful books and meaningful mementos, Alonso loves anything that can add visual interest and tell your guests something about your personality. 
“Have fun, make it personal, make it relevant,” he says.
