# What’s The Best Way to Hang a Picture? Our Expert Guide to Displaying Art in Your Home
Plus tips for choosing the right height, hanging on bricks and tile, and more
By [Katherine McLaughlin](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/contributor/katherine-mclaughlin)
May 4, 2026
![Image may contain Agnès Varda Agnès Varda Agnès Varda Art Collage Adult Person Bicycle Transportation and Vehicle](https://media.architecturaldigest.com/photos/69e7be7b4d893b5edfdb0620/16:9/w_2560%2Cc_limit/GettyImages-2265138698.jpg)

## Key Takeaways
  * Knowing your wall type and picture weight will help you determine the best way to hang a picture.
  * The golden rule for picture placement is 57 inches from floor to the frame’s center, but trust your eye and adjust for the specifics of your space.
  * Double-check your measurements before making any holes, and remember that a level is your best friend in avoiding crooked paintings.


* * *
Enough of staring at blank walls—it’s time to learn the best way to hang a picture. Maybe you like to scour flea markets for [portraits of strangers](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/portrait-paintings-of-strangers-trend) or even dabble in [DIY art projects](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/martha-stewart-best-tips-for-diy-art-5-projects) to save cash, but if you don’t know the best way to hang pictures, they’ll just sit listlessly on the floor. Whether you want a [gallery wall](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/gallery/gallery-wall-ideas), or you’re just hanging a solo piece of art for a [minimalist](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/minimalist-interior-design-101) impact, the instructions are basically the same.
Take hammer and nail to the wall with these tricks so you know [how to hang a picture](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/helpful-picture-hanging-tool) like a pro.
## Step 1: Make a game plan
Well-arranged framed pictures can take your home decor to the next level, which is where proper planning comes into play.
Where should I place photos on the wall?
If you’re planning to hang more than one photo—perhaps you’re making a [gallery wall](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/gallery/gallery-wall-ideas)—you can also use painter’s tape to map out your wall design before making any holes. Start by measuring the picture frame, then cut and hang painter’s tape to get an idea of what the wall will look like with the arrangement of your choosing. You can also grab a friend. “It’s always best to hang artwork with a helper,” says [Tara McCauley](https://www.taramccauley.com/about), founder of her eponymous interior design firm. “I like to step as far away from the wall as I can, and have someone else hold up the art where I think I want it to hang.” Alternatively, there are even some [apps](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/gallery-wall-planning-app) you can download to visualize what your wall will look like when you’ve finished.
Tyson Ness, founder of [Studio Ness](https://www.studionessnyc.com/) follows a simple rule: “I use the wall sizing as my guide. Taller, narrower walls sing when they have taller, vertically oriented [art pieces](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/affordable-art-online) or groupings. Wider walls work well with horizontal layouts.”
![Image may contain Indoors Interior Design Wood Chart Plot Adult Person Measurements Art Painting and Hardwood](https://media.architecturaldigest.com/photos/69e7beb67cbc756a1d025352/master/w_1600%2Cc_limit/GettyImages-450574284.jpg)
Hanging a photo correctly requires measuring and often a level.
Boston Globe/Getty Images
Additionally, if you’re attempting the slightly more complex task of a gallery wall, McCauley suggests laying everything out on the floor to determine the most harmonious layout, before making any nail holes. “This not only allows you to figure out what goes where, but how much spacing to allow between pieces,” she says. “When arranging a gallery wall, you don’t need to have the exact same spacing between each piece; some pieces need more breathing room than others.”
Like many things in interior design, hanging art is subjective, so if you like it, well done.
How high should I hang a picture?
For a broad general rule, hang artwork [57 inches above the ground](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/how-to-hang-a-picture-frame-on-your-first-try)—the average eye level, and the height galleries and museums typically hang their pieces. “Most clients hang their pieces way too high—just lowering them a few inches makes them feel more considered,” says Ness. “If it’s going above furniture, I leave about 6-8” above a sideboard or console to the bottom of the frame, a little more above a sofa, so they don’t feel too intrusive.” Of course, go with your gut. If you’ve mapped out a plan using some of the tricks above, and like how it looks, then you’re good to go.
## Step 2: Gather your supplies
You know that you need a hammer, measuring tape, and pencil for hanging pictures on a wall, but other supplies will come in handy too. For plaster or drywall, the weight of the item will determine exactly what you’ll need. “I usually choose hardware that is rated higher than the weight of the piece so that I have the additional peace of mind,” adds Ness.
  * **For hanging light-weight pieces:** The best way to hang pictures that are light in weight is to invest in a pack of small nails.
  * **For medium-weight pieces:** If it weighs more than a few pounds, add a few [picture hangers](https://cna.st/affiliate-link/3z2cX3BbZjFAzTEuSzCNVZSQRts7ySoq5sipoWENNbY2TvP4NmimPq9QW2jf7s1KtZRXpDg1dTYpr3hZTqBZ2uq5KgbxRvM9H2Yp58GE1N7qFm3PosWwLnEcBViciNdRcTGstMhYkmCZYV1cKCjSEeYvcQVi4xKMUabt3w4TgCkGrA6kyjaRqpRtsRaJSepupg1fyEAxkRQuUQSauMw1NfHtb1PFJNQJn22ZkrwrWsqXcK7riQP349uws3gMa4gKvnKrmhBFopxv8MGS2pmk5dgZMcb7Vb8MBn2dcv1BLvjcaMeR3P7dXQ9Q39rokApviZ2jwHJyTZyVFRTrixvY4autEzdCDgpTbLtEZwCMXfMJSPqU1Tk4H1ou6NakrRHzSpvop8Yq1kQ) to your cart.
  * **For heavier pieces:** A heavy picture or heavy mirror will require a big nail, a stud-finder or [wall-plug anchors](https://cna.st/affiliate-link/qAQ8G3WQzHYvcqBFMKhryt9uSLZFNUr6pxcRudWeV9kUXx6Dajg7W9KmYxJ3wUUEdGw5RBecmedSNZWioJR6sifYYYvvgdmeygiqnXeqiezg5BCiDDcUGETMwiPqvZ9Lb3E6WtMZcB4XYmyJJCNCMBospcrd4ZxU83BqFCRjYHVdW867iv9UdwFhBLyBpA7EY3UVrQWtJx59zKTRqJRc7z4iUw22EcDsAct144w9q4R3p1AtrCQApguSfqoeP7BpZat9fq76uPrKeeRJcoNJifVfCakug6RVP71aTpo4Nx15iAZW3URGxNG1b7dpjVqd4pXQP197onPgVYSBz6PJhdP1AsooTi8q7aoZhLTSmmj6kjmV9XCvN9a4UAGDkFfpKTgWHsR4b8t7RogQtGfWBbVvZistcnVLxwh26oXx7gcDgBzEDWQKBaEkPCDNPj6EY6duXi4J8naUqjhjuqqRJCtHoSG5oiFks5B42oTGuXgujcuHzXS4kBYjygoFtH1ktgSFS5hpVxUi4k8f2ASai5QQacqcdPQTkB5XcY), screws that fit them, and a screwdriver.
  * **If hanging on tile or glass** : You’ll need good-quality and low-profile [adhesive hooks](https://cna.st/affiliate-link/m1C6KgLs7ft5zqFwHyhZvYgsvdzq4UfoV8aJjBduFBe5EbwHkQSmLBKNsbSjEi1SS4M5ZsDfAbebj4Cw9Ppp8YPtGPB7u7teqK6UFgJ4PsmxaHaVNsVVa2RQZ7CdahDeXmfN6trbX6myVBGZzG9deUo9TAeWadHdQhjzwLxCAizEA8PeGW118FHeJi9x2LqfxeXvkeczFoRCpZpEwrFfBS6Gu3akmWUwLN2NqBw4j3kjUh8ChvXf4PCQhrAKZkDoUcGHbWQ5Z9p7doagMHjuZqQrSRqYBi4Ndn7xQdu7vn6bkcdoPHXKGMDnt738iBxReVDCEmW5mPkZj6bXqV2cDPmeYkhCdHDnBi1mdiAo1cb4B5FAr7Y9rJaQh16wJapfbSCM1meZNHFUrkJehrRQjLGXKNY3GaiZSQ1mASgWrH7koveqaDU14f2yYAwGvaFpS2T7ocVu8B9DWgQzhsSM36ZfRBVUC4xURJdKGEW9tPbk8iRevgzqnZtUsqb8Xg6sWrSySWztKsaYmgUGv4qBvk4) rather than nails and screws, and if you’re hanging on brick, use [brick clamps](https://cna.st/affiliate-link/m1C6KgLs7ft5zqFwHyhZvYgsvdzq4UfoV8aJjBduFBe5EbwHkQSmLBKNsbSjEi1SS4M5ZsDfAbebj4CvMuW4brV8w9BvCm6qhWtr4Yo9XNvsvBLXY1PMDYvQDhHHNk56B484yzZ1vTtbDufVw67bqiNDbsuC2B69PMdxURX76MyXVaK4rpZkuh1ia4ek6WBXKCPBmer8YEKkgeYSFzU9xCY1KEwERiDhHw8ihxqPHqKuPGR8i37B6x38omFiBvs4bvYX6h2x33G2bwAyChtxNdMRa9wNum2WiWSquvWWLrUNLepnyx2hiSFhLfUSce9rmjwgsJefknxJFskfKi1L8eXNQF1oCWAE5auwVDWZ1dQw386iFzRK1URRxWJL99HWNpYqhsRvnPaGhqN485fGw7mWwwZ7Xy7bygX1MzMN4eb12z4yyEB1Qm3zSpU6TBMSJpeNTQA2nA1tzcwzG13qGH1EhfgqRy6myK3xu5fj998J7h1WTZRfpEd7ZHtm8ZsG4fKuzt99SYK9nhUKtiMZs4G).


Check the back of the picture before getting started to see what type of hanging hardware you’re working with. When you flip to the back of the frame, you’ll usually find hanging wire, D-rings, or a sawtooth hanger. Although this shouldn’t impact you _too_ much, make sure you don’t buy nails that end up being too big for your D-rings, for example.

## Step 3: Hang the thing
Once you’ve purchased your supplies and know where you want to hang your pictures, it’s time to actually do it. The most important thing is to know where to place your nail or other hanging device.
  * Mark on the wall where you want the center of your frame to be once hung. This is called the focal point. Map out the frame using painter’s tape on the wall, then use a measuring tape to find the exact center of the artwork.
  * Next, flip the piece over and measure the distance between the center of the frame and the hanging device (either where the picture wire hits when bent to bear weight, or where the [sawtooth hanger](https://cna.st/affiliate-link/Be77erS21HPv838fFo5PKKbHWCrKUNhuAa7Y7ZDQyLchoW7PJuDmkRSJrnr4Tuu7wnR87EUx6wQR8VRnaNDcv9sBNsqrF8p3E6ccK6dszktY9xKTkXGGwQBWQ6bHvUYU5pTM4Kc4pyC2iCAbb6AxePKV3aSpSQarpJsh8NaUjBkm6cCXR1Wcb7ok78Vc5FErLwu6btmsELgLM2Y4bskcVrgdhrdudK2eB74u2djhhTvySvWfQsecM4WPiNqx4HvhzGity7AD4tbbrT2SLMKvuGckjVws2wsCCazGmBrpgTSt2TijzEv2M2ZJWhbMM2bVTcYNnB5kyDQgH9KAuhEJDh7ynU9mgFfpVPTnWJbiQ8AMeb5HcqYRXoc6wysLf2kkkfJEpemfoz47UfjbFNMcFDdeRduERYX4dyxKsr) is). Write down that distance.
  * Turning back to the wall, mark that same distance from the focal point mark—that’s where the nail (or picture hanger, wall anchor, brick clamp) goes.


If you’re hanging a super-heavy piece, first use a stud-finder to determine if there is a stud where you plan to place the nail. If there is, hammer a big nail in and be done. If the stud is in a weird location, use the anchor-and-screw method instead: Drill a pilot hole, tap the plastic anchor into it, then screw a screw into that, leaving it to protrude just enough that you can loop the wire or sawtooth right over it the same way you would with a nail.

## How do you hang pictures on a concrete wall/brick wall?
For brick or concrete walls, use brick clamps or masonry anchors rather than standard nails. Masonry nails and a hammer drill work well for concrete, while brick clamps hook directly onto the mortar joints without any drill bits necessary.
That said: “Remember this material is not as forgiving of patching if you change your mind,” says Ness. “If possible, I prefer to suspend the artwork from the ceiling on thin aircraft cable to avoid damaging the surface all together.”
## How can I hang a picture without nails?
Adhesive strips or hooks (like Command strips) are the easiest nail-free solution. Just make sure to choose a strip rated for your picture’s weight, press firmly for 30 seconds, and allow the adhesive to set for an hour before hanging. This method is great on tile, glass, or rental walls where you want to avoid making holes.
“My office also has a picture rail that we utilize for our rotating art display,” adds Ness. Another good option for filling that empty space while avoiding nail holes!
## How to hang a picture with two hooks?
“This just requires patience and math,” says Ness. “Your secret weapon will be a laser line level that you can adjust to be sure that you are putting both of your hooks at the same level. From there just do your measurements on the back between the hooks and from the top of the frame to the hook to help determine your final spot.”
If you don’t have a laser accessible, you can measure the distance between the D-rings or hooks on the back of the frame, then transfer the exact measurement to your wall using a level to ensure both nails are perfectly even before hammering them in. A level is non-negotiable here, as even a slight difference in height between the two nails will leave your wall art visibly crooked.
