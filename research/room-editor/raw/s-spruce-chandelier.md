# How to Hang a Dining Room Chandelier at the Perfect Height
Experts say 30-36 inches above the table, but here’s what else to consider
By
[Lauren Flanagan](https://www.thespruce.com/lauren-flanagan-2213318)

How to Hang a Dining Room Chandelier

Hanging a dining room chandelier correctly means finding the right balance between form and function. Standard practice suggests placing it 30 to 36 inches above the table for the best lighting and visual appeal. If your ceiling is higher than 8 feet, adjust by raising the chandelier an extra 3 inches for each additional foot. Remember to consider room and table size, along with your personal style, when finalizing its position. 
The Spruce / Candace Madonna
##  Standard Height Recommendation 
The generally accepted rule for hanging dining room chandeliers is that the bottom of the fixture should hang between 30 and 36 inches above the top of the dining table. This range assumes you have an 8-foot ceiling. If the ceiling is higher, the recommended standard is to raise the chandelier 3 inches for each additional foot of ceiling height. For example, if your ceiling is 10 feet high, you might hang your chandelier 6 inches higher than in a room with an 8-foot ceiling. 
The standard recommendation is, of course, an average range; but before you settle on a height, consider the room size, the table size, and the light projected by the fixture. 
Keep your brand new chandelier looking it’s very best by [dusting it regularly](https://www.thespruce.com/the-right-height-for-a-foyer-chandelier-1976289).
The Spruce / Emily Mendoza
If the chandelier requires any wiring that’s beyond your expertise or weighs over 100 pounds, call an expert to help you install it.
##  Consider the Design of the Chandelier 
Some designers suggest that a chandelier’s height need not be determined by ceiling height or room size. The primary purpose of the fixture is to provide illumination over a table. However, chandeliers also serve as [design elements](https://www.thespruce.com/buy-lighting-fixtures-4176113) and the goal is to position them to enhance the room’s aesthetics. 
Think about how the chandelier looks when you are at the table, enter the room, or sit away from the table. If the dining room is part of an [open floor plan](https://www.thespruce.com/what-is-an-open-floor-plan-1821962), scrutinize the chandelier from every angle. 
Sometimes the chandelier’s size and style affect how high you should hang it. If it’s an elaborate chandelier that takes up a lot of visual space, consider hanging it a couple of inches higher than the standard height. 
The Spruce / Candace Madonna
##  Adjusting for Table Size 
The lighting industry recommends that a dining room chandelier’s diameter measure 1/2 to 2/3 of the dining table’s width (side to side). Another way to size a fixture is to choose a diameter 1 foot smaller than the table’s width. For example, if the table is 42 inches wide, the chandelier should be no more than 30 inches across. Staying within this range helps keep the table and chandelier in proper proportion. 
Consider headroom clearance when positioning a chandelier over the table. Ensure anyone getting up from the table won’t accidently hit their head on the chandelier. Keeping the chandelier properly proportioned will help prevent this problem.
##  Getting the Light Right 
How light is cast onto a dining room table is influenced by many things, including the fixture’s design, the light bulbs you use, and the fixture’s height. There’s no standard industry or decorating rule because each fixture is different. Getting the light right is about your preferences and what kind of mood you want to achieve with the chandelier. 
Safely and temporarily hang a chandelier above the dining table on a removable swag hook to see how it looks in all lighting conditions before permanently hanging the fixture.
The Spruce / Candace Madonna
