## Tips for Getting the Best Valuation
  * Working condition is expected - rewiring is inexpensive ($20-$40) and can make a non-working lamp sellable
  * Original shades in good condition add significant value, especially for designer pieces
  * Look for maker's labels on the base, inside the socket area, or on the felt bottom
  * Mid-century modern and Art Deco styles are in highest demand currently
  * Photograph the lamp lit (if working), the base details, and any labels for best AI identification


## Frequently Asked Questions
### How do I identify a valuable vintage lamp?
Check for maker's labels or stamps (often on the base or under the felt pad). Quality indicators include heavy construction, real materials (brass, ceramic, glass vs plastic), and distinctive design. Mid-century modern, Art Deco, and designer lamps from known makers like Laurel, Lightolier, or Murano glass command the highest prices.
### Are old lamps safe to use?
Old wiring can be a safety hazard. If the cord is frayed, cracked, or has a two-prong plug, consider rewiring before selling or using. Rewiring is straightforward and costs $20-$40 at a lamp repair shop. A lamp with fresh wiring is also easier to sell since buyers can use it immediately.
### What vintage lamps sell for the most?
Authentic Tiffany Studios lamps ($5,000-$500,000+) are in a class of their own. More commonly found valuable lamps include Murano glass ($100-$1,000), mid-century designer lamps ($100-$500), Art Deco figural lamps ($50-$400), and large-scale floor lamps ($100-$500). Unusual or eye-catching designs sell best.
