# Lane 13: Browser extensions and desktop utilities that save a reseller time

Date 2026-11-07. Author: research subagent. Question: Which Chrome or Edge extensions and small Windows utilities save a one-person vintage reseller time (comps on the fly, listing helpers, image search, clipboard and text expansion, screenshots and OCR, image resizing, passwords, and moving iPhone photos to a Windows PC)? Legend: V = page read today; U = unverified (snippet, roundup, memory, or the page would not load).

Method: 63 pages fetched today through the session's headless-Chromium fetcher. Every Chrome Web Store listing below was read live today (user counts, version and "Updated" dates are from those pages). Prices come from vendor pricing pages read today. WebSearch was only used to find URLs. The search budget ran out near the end, so a few follow-up URLs were guessed from known vendor help-center patterns; the ones that failed are listed in section 4.

One general fact first: Microsoft's Edge help page says "Add an extension to Microsoft Edge from the Chrome Web Store" and "Allow extensions from other stores" (V). Every Chrome extension below therefore also runs in Edge on Ricky's Windows PC.

## 0. Bottom line

- Comps: skip the eBay "sold comps" extensions. eBay's own Product research (Terapeak) in Seller Hub is free for all sellers and gives "the last 3 years of eBay sales data" (V). The extensions are either tiny (Comp Splitter: 15 users), built for dropshippers and card collectors (ZIK, Comp Buddy), or read listing pages in ways that brush against eBay's user agreement, which bans "any robot, spider, scraper, data mining tools" without eBay's permission (V).
- Etsy research: the eRank extension is the one to install. It is free with a free eRank account (5 keyword lookups a day, 1 shop, 50 listings), updated 2026-10-23, and shows keyword stats and sales estimates on Etsy pages. EverBee's free Hobby tier is thinner (10 keywords, no sales estimates) and its Growth plan is $24.99 a month. Keywords Everywhere is general SEO and needs paid credits for search volume; skip.
- Image identification: Google Lens is already in Chrome (right-click, "Search this tab with Google Lens"), free, and is the right tool for identifying a maker, a mark or a pattern. TinEye finds copies of the same image, not similar objects, and its FAQ says "TinEye is free for non-commercial use" (V). Use Lens.
- Writing: LanguageTool's extension needs no account and works in Chrome and Edge for free. Grammarly Free also works. Neither needs paying for listing copy.
- Text expansion: Espanso is free, open source (GPL-3), Windows-native, and fits a one-person shop's boilerplate (condition notes, shipping lines, measurements template). TextExpander's free tier is 5 active snippets; its Pro tier is $5 a user a month billed annually at $60 and only earns its place if Ricky wants snippets on the iPhone too.
- Windows built-ins and Microsoft's free PowerToys cover screenshots (Snipping Tool, Win+Shift+S, with local OCR "Text actions"), OCR anywhere (PowerToys Text Extractor, Win+Shift+T), bulk image resizing (PowerToys Image Resizer, right-click in File Explorer), bulk rename (PowerRename), accents for maker names (Quick Accent), and clipboard history (Win+V). ShareX adds scrolling capture, GIF recording and OCR for free. Ditto is the free clipboard manager if Win+V's 4 MB per item and reset-on-restart annoy.
- Passwords: Bitwarden Free ("Unlimited devices, unlimited passwords", browser, mobile and desktop apps) is enough. Premium is $1.65 a month billed annually at $19.80. 1Password has no free tier ($2.99 a month promo, $3.99 regular, billed yearly).
- iPhone photos to the PC: iCloud for Windows is the one path Apple documents for full-resolution originals ("iCloud Photos always uploads and stores your original, full-resolution photos", V). It needs iCloud storage (free 5 GB; 50 GB $0.99, 200 GB $2.99 a month). For ad hoc batches over home Wi-Fi, LocalSend (free, open source, Windows and iOS apps) is the quick route. Phone Link's photo feature is documented for Android; its iPhone photo support could not be verified today (U). Snapdrop is no longer a peer-to-peer tool: snapdrop.net now uploads files to LimeWire's servers. PairDrop (its fork) is live and still browser-to-browser.

## 2. Notes per tool

### Comps on the fly (eBay)

- eBay user agreement (V, read today): users may not "use any robot, spider, scraper, data mining tools, data gathering and extraction tools, or other automated means ... to access our Services for any purpose, except with the prior express permission of eBay". Any extension that pulls sold or purchase-history data off eBay pages for you sits in that grey zone. eBay's own Product research avoids it entirely.
- eBay Product research (Terapeak): "Sellers with access to Seller Hub can use Product research." Shows "Average sales price", "Sold price range", "Sell through rate (for searches of items sold 90 days ago or less)", over "the last 3 years". Sourcing insights needs a Basic Store or above. Hollie's Seller Hub comes with her eBay seller account.
- ZIK Analytics eBay Sold History Button: Chrome listing links to zikanalytics.com; developer "N.G.C.A Technology Enterprises LTD", Paphos, Cyprus; "Featured" badge; keyboard shortcut Alt+Z. ZIK's own extension page: "The free extension shows you what's there. The paid version shows you what it means, with sales velocity, price distribution, seller performance, and variation data unlocked." Pricing page: PRO "$19.90 1st month" then "$39.90" a month; PRO+ "$29.90" then "$59.90"; "Try everything for $1". The whole product is tuned for dropshipping ("With love, designed for dropshippers and eBay sellers"). Over budget and off-target for one-of-one decor.
- Comp Splitter: individual developer (Redding, CA), 15 users, 22.65 KiB, "Non-trader". Honest about scope: "It does not scrape listing data or replace your judgment." It is a convenience panel for opening sold and active searches side by side. Harmless but near zero adoption.
- eBay Sold and Completed (US & UK): "ts.GITWARE", London KY, "handles Website content". 458 users. Marketing copy, little detail on how data is pulled.
- Comp Buddy: "the pricing intelligence tool for collectors who shop on live auction sites"; Pro $15/month adds Whatnot live scanner, snip tool, Facebook Marketplace scanner. Not Hollie's world.

### Etsy research extensions

- eRank: the extension "is free to install and works with a free eRank account". "The extension only reads the public page you're viewing, and only on supported sites ... it never posts, buys, or changes anything on your behalf." Free plan: 5 keyword lookups a day, 1 shop with 50 listings, 5 listing audits a day, 1 AI Listing Helper use a day. Basic $5.99/mo lifts to 100 lookups a day and 200 connected listings. eRank's footer: "This app uses the Etsy API but is not endorsed or certified by Etsy." Gotcha: sales and revenue numbers are estimates.
- EverBee: 200,000 users, updated three days ago. Hobby is free forever but keyword research is capped at 10 keywords and product analytics show only views, reviews and favorites; "sales, revenue, conversion rate" start at Growth ($24.99 a month, or $299 a year). Business is $69 a month billed annually at $828. EverBee is built around finding products to make or source in volume ("Your next bestseller is already selling on Etsy"), which is the opposite of a one-of-one vintage model.
- Keywords Everywhere: the free version has traffic metrics, SERP widgets and Pinterest/Instagram metrics "(no volume data)". Search volume needs credits: "1 Credit = 1 Keyword", "Credits expire after one year". Cheapest plan is Bronze, $7 a month billed annually ($84). Google search pages burn many credits per query ("1 + 8 + 20 + 25 = 54 credits" in their own example). Skip unless Hollie starts doing Google SEO for her own site.

### Image search

- Google Lens in Chrome: "At the top right, select More > Search this tab with Google Lens. You can also right-click on a page and select Search this tab with Google Lens." Privacy note on the page: "a screenshot of the page and page data will be sent to Google. Google doesn't save any data beyond temporary storage for processing the specific query." For a photo on Hollie's own PC, drag it into images.google.com or the Lens page (not re-verified today).
- TinEye: "TinEye finds exact and altered copies of the images that you search for ... TinEye does not commonly return similar matches, and it cannot recognize the contents of any image." So it cannot tell you a lamp's maker; it can tell you if your listing photo has been copied. Licence: "TinEye is free for non-commercial use." The extension itself was last updated November 22, 2024.
- Save to Pinterest: 6 million users but a 2.7 rating. Data note on the listing: "When you visit a site that shares information with Pinterest, we can use information about that visit to personalize your experience ... measure ad effectiveness".

### Crosslister extensions (what they do only; pricing belongs to the crosslisting lane)

- Crosslist (Crosslist BV, Belgium, EU trader, D-U-N-S listed): v3.12.7 published November 6, 2026, so actively maintained. Price re-verified today: $29.99, Silver $34.99, Gold $39.99 per month on the monthly toggle; Gold is unchanged from the 2026-10-26 figure. "Add 1000 new listings to your inventory every month" on Gold. "Offers in-app purchases" on the listing. Handles "Personally identifiable information".
- Vendoo Crosslist Extension v3: 70,000 users but last updated September 26, 2025, more than a year ago, and rated 3.5. The listing says "This extension allows Vendoo to access and communicate with your marketplaces."
- List Perfectly Multi-Channel: updated October 30, 2026; "Requires a List Perfectly account"; handles "Web history" and "Website content".
- Watch for the name collision: "Crosslist Tool - Crosslisting Tool" is from Listifyx, not Crosslist BV, has 281 users, was last updated July 2024, and declares it handles "Financial and payment information" and "Authentication information". Do not install it by mistake.

### Writing

- Grammarly: now "2026 © Superhuman Platform". Free plan: "Write without mistakes", "See your writing tone", "Generate text with 100 AI prompts". Pro shows "$12 USD" with a "$0 payment today" trial; the billing period was not legible in the page text, so treat $12 as the annual-billing monthly rate until confirmed (U on the period). The extension lists "Superhuman Go" as an opt-in beta assistant.
- LanguageTool: extension needs no account and "will check your text by sending it to https://languagetool.org over a securely encrypted connection". Premium is $24.90 monthly or $69.90 a year ($5.83 a month), and raises the limit to "Up to 150,000 characters per text field"; the free per-check character limit was not stated on the page read (U). 12.6K ratings at 3.3; the extension is 15 MiB.

### Text expansion and clipboard

- Espanso: "Espanso supports Windows, Linux and macOS." "File-based configuration ... Though a GUI is planned in the future." Snippets can include dates, forms and shell output. Alt+Space opens a search bar for snippets. Cost nothing; configuration is a YAML file, which is the one learning curve.
- TextExpander: Free is "Active Snippets 5", "Snippet Swap 1 every 2 weeks", "Team members (users) 5". Pro is $5 a user a month billed annually at $60. Apps for Mac, Windows, Chrome, iPhone and iPad; Android is beta. The 30-day preview of advanced features starts on sign-up.
- Windows clipboard history: "select Windows logo key + V, and then select Turn on." "Your size limitations are 4 MB per item." "Clipboard history gets cleared each time you restart your PC, except for the items you've pinned." Sync across devices is tied to the Microsoft account.
- Ditto: "Ditto is an extension to the standard windows clipboard. It saves each item placed on the clipboard"; install with "winget install -e --id Ditto.Ditto" or the Windows Store; default hotkey Ctrl+`. GPL-3.0, 7.4k GitHub stars. The project's sourceforge.io site was Cloudflare-blocked today; GitHub is the readable source.

### Screenshots, OCR, resizing, renaming

- Snipping Tool: "Press Windows logo key + Shift + S to open the snipping tool overlay". "Once you've captured a snip, select the Text actions button to activate the Optical Character Recognition (OCR) feature ... All text recognition processes are performed locally on your device." Video snips with Win+Shift+R. Some features ("Perfect screenshot", "Color picker") are "only available on a Windows 11 AI + PC".
- PowerToys Text Extractor: "copy text from anywhere on your Windows screen, including inside images or videos"; default Win+Shift+T; "It's recommended to use the Snipping Tool instead of the Text Extractor for capturing screenshots." Needs the Windows OCR language pack for en-US (usually installed).
- PowerToys Image Resizer: "right-click on one or more selected image files in File Explorer and select Resize with Image Resizer". Presets with "Fill, Fit, or Stretch", sizes in "centimeters, inches, percent, or pixels". Good for making the 480/960 px web derivatives in bulk.
- PowerRename: "Perform a search and replace on a targeted section of file names", regex, "Check the expected rename results in a preview window", undo with Ctrl+Z in Explorer. Rename a shoot's IMG_xxxx files to the inventory ID in one pass.
- Quick Accent: hold a letter, press Space, pick the accent. Useful for maker names (Lladró, Murano is fine, Fürstenberg, Rörstrand, décor).
- PowerToys requirements: "Windows 11 or Windows 10 version 2004 (20H1 / build 19041) or newer", "64-bit processor: x64 or ARM64". Install from Microsoft Store or GitHub.
- ShareX: "free and open source screenshot tool for Windows", "Over 18 years of active development", "Scrolling capture", "Screen recording (GIF)", "Recognize text (OCR)", upload destinations and a custom-uploader system. More setup than Snipping Tool; worth it if Hollie records short GIF demos or needs scrolling captures of listing pages.

### Passwords

Premium $1.65 a month billed annually at $19.80 adds the integrated authenticator (TOTP), file attachments, emergency access and reports. Open source.
- 1Password: Individual "$2.99" promotional, "$3.99" regular, "Per month. Billed yearly."; "Try FREE for 14 days"; promo "not available through Google Play, the Apple App Store". No free plan.

### Tabs

- Session Buddy: "No accounts, no tracking, no data sharing. Everything stays local by default." "Does it sync across devices? No." Free, 1 million users, updated April 2026. Handy for parking a sourcing-research session.

### iPhone photos to the Windows PC (the pain point)

- Apple, Transfer photos (V): "iCloud Photos always uploads and stores your original, full-resolution photos. You can keep full-resolution originals on each of your devices, or save space with device-optimized versions instead." For USB import: "If you have iCloud Photos turned on, you must download the original, full resolution versions of your photos to your iPhone or iPad before you import to your PC." Compatibility: Settings > Camera > Formats > "Most Compatible" so the camera captures "JPEG and H.264 formats".
- iCloud for Windows (V): install from the Microsoft Store; Apple says "update your Windows PC to Windows 11 or later and Outlook 2021 or later" for the latest version. Photos go to "C:\Users\[user name]\Pictures\iCloud Photos\Photos". "Your photos and videos appear as thumbnails until you download them." Double-click downloads a file; the guide has a "Keep photos and videos downloaded" page (pinning) that was not read today. Option "Download High-Efficiency format photos and videos" exists in 14.2 or later; leave it off to get JPEG-converted copies, or on to get HEIC originals. "If you edit a photo or video on your Windows computer, those edits don't appear on your Apple devices." Storage: iCloud is "5 GB of storage for free"; iCloud+ "50 GB" "$0.99", "200 GB" "$2.99", "2 TB" "$9.99" a month, "billed monthly". 200 GB at $2.99 a month covers a shop's photo archive for a long time.
- Phone Link: Microsoft's page says "Phone Link lets you connect your Android or iPhone to your Windows PC" and lists "view recent photos from your mobile device" among features, but every detailed photo paragraph on the page refers to Android ("view recent photos from your Android device"). The page's iOS tab did not render as text and the "photos" sub-page returned not found. Treat iPhone photo transfer through Phone Link as unverified (U) and do not plan on it.
- LocalSend: "available for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS"; "Works completely offline. Your data never leaves your local network."; "All transfers are encrypted using HTTPS." iOS App Store: "Free · In-App Purchases" (donation tips), 756 ratings, "Requires iOS 13.0 or later", "No Size Limits. None." A reviewer on the App Store page: "I was finally able to send a ton of photos for from my iPad to my desktop". It sends the file as a file, so what the iPhone shares is what lands on the PC; whether the share sheet hands over the original HEIC or a converted JPEG depends on iOS settings and was not verified today.
- PairDrop: live at v1.11.2; "Pair devices or enter a public room to be discoverable on other networks"; six-digit pairing key or QR. Browser only, nothing to install, but the tab must stay open on both ends.
- Snapdrop: snapdrop.net is now a LimeWire page. "Files are stored by LimeWire and the link opens on limewire.com". Limits without an account: 4 GB per transfer, 20 files and 4 GB a day, links live 3 days. It is a cloud upload with ads for crypto signals on the page, not the old local tool. Skip.
- OneDrive camera backup (iOS): "Camera backup uploads photos and videos to OneDrive; it doesn't sync them both ways." "Keep the OneDrive app running in the background. Closing the app can prevent automatic uploads." The iOS tab's text did not render, so the known limitation that OneDrive cannot upload iCloud-optimized photos came from a search snippet (U). Storage: Microsoft 365 Basic "100 GB of cloud storage" at "$19.99 /year" or "$1.99 per month". OneDrive is already in Windows, so files land in a synced folder with no extra client, which is the main appeal.
- Dropbox: iOS camera uploads plus an iOS-only "overnight uploads" mode that keeps the app open and dims the screen. The desktop app can also "Import photos and videos to Dropbox" when the iPhone is plugged in by USB (Windows AutoPlay). But Basic is "2 GB to store and share your files" and Plus is $9.99 a month for 2 TB, so it is the most expensive useful tier here.
- Google Photos: iPhone app backs up in "Original quality" or "Storage saver"; "Each Google Account comes with up to 15 GB of storage". On the PC, photos.google.com in a browser is the way to download; browser folder backup needs the tab open; "Google Drive for desktop ends support for Google Photos backup on August 10, 2026". Google One paid prices did not render in the fetched page (U).

## 3. Recommendation for Hollie

Install or turn on today, all free:

1. Windows PowerToys (Microsoft Store). Enable Image Resizer, Text Extractor, PowerRename and Quick Accent. This replaces three or four paid utilities.
2. Snipping Tool (already there). Learn Win+Shift+S and the Text actions OCR button. Turn on clipboard history with Win+V.
3. Bitwarden Free, browser extension plus the iPhone app. Every marketplace, Supabase and Anthropic key goes in it from day one.
4. LanguageTool extension in Chrome or Edge. No account. Grammarly Free is the alternative if she prefers its tone suggestions.
5. eRank extension plus a free eRank account. Open an Etsy search for "pink depression glass" and read the keyword stats before writing tags.
6. Google Lens (built in). Right-click any photo of a mark or signature to identify it. Do not bother with TinEye for identification.
7. iCloud for Windows from the Microsoft Store, with iCloud Photos on. Pay $2.99 a month for 200 GB of iCloud+ only when the free 5 GB fills (it will fast); this is the one recurring charge the lane recommends, because it is the only documented full-resolution, hands-off path from iPhone to PC. Set the iPhone camera to Most Compatible so the PC gets JPEGs. Keep a USB cable as the fallback, and remember to download originals to the phone before a cable import if iCloud optimization is on.
8. LocalSend on the PC and the iPhone for same-day batches when she does not want to wait for iCloud to sync.
9. Espanso for listing boilerplate once she has ten listings' worth of repeated phrases (condition grading lines, shipping and local pickup lines, the disco-ball gift note). Start with five snippets in the YAML file.

Pay only once it earns it:

- Bitwarden Premium ($19.80 a year) if she wants the TOTP codes inside the vault instead of a separate authenticator.
- eRank Basic ($5.99 a month) if 5 keyword lookups a day is not enough during a listing push.
- TextExpander Pro ($60 a year) only if she lives in snippets on the iPhone as well as the PC; otherwise Espanso.
- Microsoft 365 Basic ($19.99 a year) is a reasonable alternative to iCloud+ if she would rather have OneDrive's folder in File Explorer, but the iOS app has to stay running to upload.

Skip and why:

- eBay sold-comps extensions (ZIK, Comp Splitter, eBay Sold and Completed, Comp Buddy). eBay's Product research is free, official and does not risk the user agreement's scraper clause. ZIK is $39.90 a month after the first month and built for dropshippers.

- TinEye for identification. Wrong tool, and its FAQ limits free use to non-commercial.
- Snapdrop. It is a LimeWire upload page now.
- Dropbox for photos. 2 GB free is a single shoot.
- Phone Link for photos. iPhone photo support is unverified and the documentation is Android-centric.
- The Listifyx "Crosslist Tool" lookalike. Stale, tiny, and handles payment and auth data.
