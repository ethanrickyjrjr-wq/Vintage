# Verify lane 03: product photo apps (price and claim check)

Date 2026-11-07. Author: research subagent (adversarial checker). Question: Do the prices, free tiers, platform claims and availability recorded by the first lane-03 agent (shoot, cut out, retouch, upscale, batch) match the vendors' own pages today? Legend: V = page read today; U = unverified (page would not load, or the claim is not on any page read today).

Method: 26 URLs fetched today through the session's headless-Chromium fetcher (fetch.sh, curl fallback); 24 loaded, 2 failed (both eBay). Photoroom's dollar figures are not in the rendered text, so its raw HTML was saved today (fetch_html.cjs) and the JSON-LD offers were read from it. Every quote below is copied from the page text as rendered today. No new tools were added. WebSearch was not needed: every URL came from the first agent's list or from a link inside a page already read.

Verdicts: confirmed = the exact figure or claim is on the page read today; refuted = the page says something different (correction given); unreadable = the page or the relevant part of it could not be read after one alternative attempt.

## 2. What the first agent got wrong or left out

1. Canva: the claim that "remove background" is listed under Pro on the pricing page is not in that page's text today; the page carries no background-remover line at all. Canva's own feature page says "Remove your photo's background for free". What a free account gets (resolution, quota) is still unverified because the FAQ answer did not render. Test with a free account before paying US$144 a year.
2. PowerToys Image Resizer: the Learn page never says "free" (free follows from the MIT licence on GitHub), and the "2000px for Etsy, 1600px for eBay" presets are not shipped; Ricky adds them under "Add new size".
3. Pixelcut: omitted that Pro includes "600 AI Credits Monthly" and a "3 Person Team". The $10 figure is the struck-through monthly rate shown on the Yearly toggle, consistent with "Save 20% with a yearly plan", but the words "billed monthly" do not appear in the text. One App Store review reports a $97 annual charge after a 3-day trial; cancel the trial in iPhone Settings, not inside the app.
4. Upscayl Cloud: "$24.99" is specifically the 300 credits per month plan on Monthly billing, and the free trial needs a login and a "trial application". Irrelevant for Hollie since the desktop app is free.
5. Removal.ai: "Lifetime" credits are not lifetime; "Credits available for use within 3 years". Slazzer's pay-as-you-go credits expire after two years. Both still cheap for occasional hero shots.
6. Erase.bg: the vendor's own FAQ says "starting at $5 for 10 credits" while the price card says "$6.00 billed once". The first agent used the card; the card is the checkout figure. Also sign-up is mandatory.
7. Squoosh and Windows Photos: neither page says "free". Squoosh is Apache-2.0 on GitHub; Windows Photos is a built-in Windows 11 app with no price anywhere on the page. The first agent's "free" is correct in substance but not a verbatim vendor claim.
8. eBay: the first agent marked the adding-pictures help page V; today it is captcha-walled and the one alternative URL is a 404, so the eBay claims could not be re-verified from this container.
9. Nothing in the PICKS was refuted on price. All eight PICKS prices (seven free tools plus Pixelcut Pro at $10 monthly / $8 yearly) hold on the vendor pages read today. Photoroom's prices also hold, read from the page's structured data because the rendered text still shows no dollar figures.
