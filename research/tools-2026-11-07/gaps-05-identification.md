# Gaps review, lane 05: identification and authentication tools

Date: 2026-11-07. Author: research subagent (completeness critic). Question: which identification and authentication tools did the lane 05 report miss, and is anything on its list defunct, acquired or renamed? Legend: V = vendor page read today (2026-11-07); U = unverified (snippet, roundup, store page only, or the page would not load).

Method note: the WebSearch budget for this run was exhausted before this agent started, so leads came from store category pages read directly (Chrome Web Store search, Google Play search, Shopify App Store search, GitHub topic page) plus a vendor roundup used only as a lead list. Every price below comes from the vendor's own page read today unless marked U.

## 0. Bottom line

- The lane missed the whole category of pay-per-item human appraisal and identification. Appraisily identifies for free and sells a working value for $9; Mearto charges $25 an item; ValueMyStuff $28 a credit. For a lamp, chandelier or painting with no mark, this is the cheapest expert opinion available.
- For original art and signed prints, two signature databases are missing: FindArtInfo (free tier, $4.95 day pass, $17.95 a month, 450,000+ signatures) and Art Signature Dictionary ($12 a month after 7 free days).
- Kovels is not free-only. The paid product is now "Kovels All Access Online" at $59.99 per year, and the newsletter and Antique Trader magazine merged into "Kovels Antique Trader" (print $29.95 a year). The first agent's "join and pricing pages 404" was a dead URL, not a missing product.
- A new Chrome extension exists for exactly Hollie's workflow: Antiqly for Chrome (Obzena LLC, version 1.0.0 dated October 19, 2026) identifies an item from a right-clicked eBay or Etsy photo. $7.99 a week or $39.99 a year, no trial, 4 users, built on OpenAI. Treat as a toy until it has reviews.
- The Android store has at least 27 AI "antique identifier" apps. The two with real review counts, Zophi (12.9K reviews) and Curio (3.27K reviews), carry the same complaints the lane found on iOS: inaccurate results and subscriptions that are hard to cancel. Curio and Vellum give free scans; Antiqly does not.
- Free human forums remain the best no-cost identifier for odd pieces: Antiquers.com (Antiques Board) had posts today, and The Marks Project (7,300 marks, American studio ceramics 1945 onward) is free.
- Microsoft Copilot's free tier includes camera and screen vision on Ricky's Windows PC; Ollama plus Qwen3-VL gives a free local vision model. Both are hypothesis generators, same caveat as ChatGPT.
- Nothing on the lane's list is confirmed defunct. Authenticate First (a seed the lane dropped) is live but its footer says 2018 and its services, pricing and brands pages return 404; price unverifiable.

## 2. Notes per tool

**Appraisily.** The free step is identification from photos. The $9 step adds "a working value, inspected sales and an unsigned PDF". Signed appraisals are $59 (standard), $99 (insurance), from $399 (donation). The page promises a refund "If we cannot deliver a reasonably supported appraisal to our published standard". Nothing on the page names individual appraisers; ask who signs before paying $59.

**Mearto.** $25 per appraisal, packs down to $17 each at ten. Its authentication FAQ says the appraisal is "on condition of authenticity" and that authentication is a separate product for art "with a minimum estimated value of $1,000 or more", handled by a subsidiary called Signature. Expect a prompt to sell at auction. Good for a chandelier or painting where Google Lens gives nothing.

**ValueMyStuff.** Credits, $28 single, cheaper in packs. Standard is 48 hours for one credit, Express 24 hours for two. "Credits don't have any expiration date". Report is a PDF with attribution, auction value and insurance value. Operated by VMS Grosvenor Ltd (UK); US pricing page used.

**FindArtInfo.** Free account shows artwork images, auction house names and estimates. Day pass $4.95 unlocks the full database and signatures for 24 hours, "One-time · No auto-renewal". Monthly $17.95, six months $79.95, year $129.95. Pro plans ($49 to $139 a month) are for dealers who list on its marketplace; not needed. 13.5M auction records, 901K artists, "450,000+ artist signatures, monograms and catalogue inscriptions".

**Art Signature Dictionary.** $12 a month after a 7-day free trial; yearly saves 15%. Search works without login for names; signature images need the subscription. Narrower than FindArtInfo but built for reading an unreadable signature ("smart search" with wildcards).

**Kovels.** The paid tier is "Kovels All Access Online" at $59.99 per year (price guide "with over 1 million items", 25,000+ articles, marks dictionary, newsletter archive). "All Access + Print" is $79.99 per year. The magazine alone (Kovels Antique Trader) is $29.95 print, $24.95 digital, $34.95 both, per year. The FAQ mentions a "free membership trial" and antiquetrader.com advertises "Start your FREE 7-Day Trial Now!" for the price guide. The lane's /join URL is dead; the live page is /membership-options, which redirects to subscribe.kovels.com.

**Antiquers.com.** Free forum ("Antiques Board"). Posts today included a swung vase ID, MCM smoky glass bowls, an unsigned oil seascape and a possible Murano vase. Same genre as Collectors Weekly Show & Tell, more active.

**The Marks Project.** Nonprofit, free, donation requested. "ceramic artists and sculptors working in the USA from 1945 onward". 7,300 marks. Useful when a ceramic piece has an incised studio mark, not a factory backstamp.

**Microsoft Copilot.** The individuals page lists free usage as the baseline and sells higher limits: Microsoft 365 Personal $9.99 a month, Premium $19.99 a month, both with "Try free for 1 month". Vision is "visual AI assistance from your screen or camera". Same reliability caveat as the other chat models: it guesses makers.

**Lot-Art.** Upcoming-lot search is free. Starter $7.90 monthly (or "6.5 USD / month (yearly plan)") adds alerts and wish list; Explorer $16.90 monthly adds the price database "of +75 Million lots" and aftersale lots; Collector $50.90 monthly adds analytics; Day Pass $25 one time. Image Search is "UNLIMITED" on every tier including Day Pass. Coverage skews European; the catalogue descriptions are the ID aid.

**Antiqly for Chrome.** Chrome Web Store: 4 users, 0 ratings, version 1.0.0 updated October 19, 2026. Right-click any image, get "a specific name and category, the likely era and place of origin, and the materials" plus marks interpretation and a USD range. The listing says "It is not an appraisal and it does not confirm authenticity". Data: image, alt text and page title go "to our server and our AI provider (OpenAI)". Vendor page: "There is no trial and no no-cost tier." Removing the extension does not cancel the subscription. Developer Obzena LLC also publishes antiqueidentifier.org, which has a free on-page photo appraiser.

**Antiqly app.** iOS 98 ratings. A one-star review says the first photo is refused "without paying for a one time $3.99 or weekly $7.99 subscription"; the developer replies that the app is not advertised as free. Android listing has 6 reviews.

**Curio, Zophi, Vellum, The Old (Android).** All free to download with in-app purchases; none prints its price in the listing text. Curio: 3.27K reviews, free first scans; one review says Google Lens gives "the same information for free", another reports a large upfront charge after a small weekly price was shown. Zophi: 12.9K reviews; reviews say "Woefully inaccurate" and values "quite inflated", with cancellation trouble; developer replies point to Google Play subscriptions. Vellum: 48 reviews, free tries before subscribing. The Old: 2.83K reviews; complaints of "no way to cancel" and that it "mostly recognize[s] high end antiques". The Google Play search returned 27 distinct antique-identifier packages; the Chrome Web Store search returned exactly one (Antiqly).

**Lenso.ai.** Reverse image search. Free search is limited and watermarked (paid plans list "Watermark-free"). Starter $19.99 a month plus tax for 50 unlocks. Developer tier $2,800 a month. Not an object identifier.

**Legit App.** Human authenticators plus AI, "FREE certificate of authenticity" included. Handbags, clothes, shoes, accessories from 10 USD (3-hour turnaround), 20 USD for 30 minutes; Hermès from 20 USD; tokens 1 USD each, 3 tokens 4.99 USD. App Store rating shown as 4.9. Relevant only if a designer label turns up in the 80s and 90s rack.

**Legit Check by Ch.** From $10 per check, two teams review, optional public certificate. The free library of 1,000+ guides is the useful part for a one-person shop.

**Ollama + Qwen3-VL.** Ollama installs on Windows with one PowerShell line; local use is $0. Qwen3-VL ships in 2B to 235B sizes; the 2B and 4B fit a laptop CPU, the 8B needs a GPU to be quick. Ollama Pro ($20 a month) is for its cloud models, not needed. Fit is for privacy and zero cost, not accuracy; small models will not out-identify Google Lens.

**Authenticate First.** Seed the lane dropped. Home page live, "create an account for free", options include "Item Identification" and "Valuation appraisal", but /services, /pricing and /brands all 404 and the footer reads "© 2018". Treat as possibly dormant; get a quote before relying on it.

**antique-marks.com.** Free, no paywall, but a content site whose latest articles are dated July 2022. Use the marks pages as a second reference only.

**eBay app.** The App Store text confirms AI-assisted listing from photos ("Create a listing in a snap with the help of AI"). The image-search help article returned eBay's 404 page; treat image search as U today. eBay's terms ban scraping, so use the app, not a bot.

**Heritage, HiFi Engine, Vinyl Engine, Barnebys.** All blocked (HTTP 403, Cloudflare) from this container twice each. They remain the best leads for Elvis memorabilia comps (Heritage) and the turntable's model and year (HiFi Engine, Vinyl Engine). Ricky should open them in his own browser.

**Shopify App Store (adjacent, not identification).** A search for "authentication" returned certificate generators for sellers: "VeriTag: Authenticity Lookup" (Free plan available), "Authentica: COA Certificates" (5.0, 2 reviews, Free plan available) and "VeriFined ‑ Brand Protection". These issue a QR certificate per order; they do not authenticate anything. "Real Authentication" (already on the list) is listed as Free in the Shopify store.

**GitHub.** The antiques topic has 16 repositories; the only identifier is "CuratorVault-Marketplace" (a student-style app with "Gemini AI visual appraisal"). No mature open-source antique identifier exists; the open-source route is Ollama plus a vision model.

## 2b. Defunct, acquired or renamed

- **Kovels (on the list): renamed and merged, not defunct.** The subscription page says "Two proud, pioneering brands — Kovels and Antique Trader — join forces to create a new collector experience: a vibrant, glossy, monthly magazine" called Kovels Antique Trader. Both kovels.com and antiquetrader.com carry "© 2026 Active Interest Media". The online membership is now sold as "Kovels All Access" ($59.99 per year). The /join page returns 404; /membership-options is live. Evidence: https://www.kovels.com/subscribe (redirects to my.antiquetrader.com), https://www.kovels.com/membership-options, https://www.antiquetrader.com/.
- **Authenticate First (seed, not on the list): live but stale.** Footer "© 2018 Authenticate First."; /services, /pricing, /brands return 404. Not confirmed defunct. Evidence: https://authenticatefirst.com/.
- **Design Addict (common MCM forum recommendation): repurposed.** The site is now "the #1 leading design marketplace for the most stunning things in Europe" (Brussels dealer), footer "Copyright © 2019 Design Addict"; a FORUM link remains in the nav. Low value as an ID community today. Evidence: https://designaddict.com/.
- **antique-marks.com: stale.** Newest articles dated July 2022. Evidence: https://antique-marks.com/.
- **Nothing on the lane's list was found defunct.** Marks4Antiques, 925-1000.com, Glass Encyclopedia, JSA and Beckett were not re-checked here (that is the verify agent's job).

## 3. Recommendation for Hollie

Free first, today:
1. Sign up at Antiquers.com and post the hardest three pieces (odd lamp, unmarked figurine, the chandelier). Zero cost, human answers.
2. Open a free FindArtInfo account for the original art and framed prints; when a signature matters, buy the $4.95 day pass and check every signed piece in one sitting.
3. Use Copilot's camera on the Windows PC as a second guess alongside Google Lens. Do not list a maker that only an AI named.
4. Try Appraisily's free identification on two pieces before paying anyone.

Pay only once it earns it:
- Mearto ($25) or ValueMyStuff ($28) for a single piece whose asking price depends on the maker (the chandelier, the Gehry-attributed chair, a painting). One sale covers it.
- Kovels All Access Online ($59.99 a year) once the shop lists weekly; it replaces several free marks sites with one search. Use the 7-day trial first.
- Art Signature Dictionary ($12 a month) only if FindArtInfo's signatures fail; cancel inside the 7 free days.

Skip:
- Antiqly for Chrome and the Android AI identifiers. Weekly billing, no or few free scans, accuracy and cancellation complaints, and Google Lens is free. Curio is the least bad if Ricky wants one on Android, because it gives free scans.
- Lenso.ai and other reverse-image subscriptions. They find copies of a photo, not makers.
- Legit App and Legit Check unless a designer label appears; then $10 is fine.
- Ollama locally, unless privacy matters more than accuracy; the Fedora box has no GPU.

For the signed Elvis photo: PSA/DNA (on the list) stays the pick; Heritage's archive for comps must be opened from Ricky's browser since it blocks this container.
