# Lane 12: Desktop video, caption, repurposing and design tools (Windows)

Date 2026-11-07. Author: research subagent (lane 12-video-desktop).
Question: Which desktop and web tools on Windows make editing, captioning, repurposing and designing short videos and graphics easier for a one-person vintage shop, beyond the phone apps already chosen (Instagram Edits, Canva Free, Buffer)?
Legend: V = vendor page read today (2026-11-07) with the fetch.sh headless browser; U = unverified (page would not load, or fact comes from a listing or snippet, not the vendor's own pricing page).

Method notes. WebSearch budget for this turn was already used up by other lanes, so every page below was fetched directly by URL from memory of vendor sites. 58 pages loaded; about 30 more URLs failed (section 4). Prices are quoted as the vendor writes them. Where a page has a monthly/yearly toggle and the text did not say which was showing, that is noted.

## 0. Bottom line

- A fully free Windows stack covers everything Hollie needs today: DaVinci Resolve (full edit, free, Windows), Clipchamp (quick cuts, free, 1080p, "Watermark-free video exports"), Subtitle Edit (free, offline captions via Whisper), OBS Studio (free recording and live selling), Canva Free video editor ("No watermarks"), Photopea or GIMP (images), Audacity (audio), CuePrompter (free browser teleprompter, commercial use allowed). All V.
- CapCut Desktop is free on Windows and has one-click "Remove BG" for video. CapCut Pro is "USD 19.99 /month". Its terms-of-service pages all failed today (404/500/502), so the earlier finding that library music is not cleared for commercial use could not be re-verified. Keep the rule: no CapCut library music in shop videos until the licence is read.
- The "AI clip and caption" category (Opus Clip, Submagic, Captions, Veed, Kapwing) is built for long talking-head videos cut into shorts. Hollie's clips are 15 to 60 second product walk-throughs. Free tiers watermark (Opus, Kapwing) or give no AI credits (Captions). Paid tiers run $12 to $29 a month. Skip for now; Veed Creator ($12 per user / month, billed yearly, "Unlimited Auto Subtitles") is the one to trial if captions become a daily chore.
- Music licences matter more than tool prices. ElevenLabs Free: "does not include a commercial license". Epidemic Sound Personal: "Covers online, non-commercial". YouTube Audio Library is cleared for YouTube and YouTube says it "can't give legal guidance ... off the platform". Pexels video is "free for personal and commercial purposes without attribution" (V). Pixabay and Uppbeat would not load (U).
- AI video generators do not fit a one-of-one resale shop: the buyer must see the real object. Sora was "discontinued on April 26, 2026" (V). Pika Free has "Not included: Commercial license". Runway Free is "125 one-time credits". Google Veo comes with Google AI Pro at "$19.99/mo". TikTok requires labels on realistic AI content; Meta adds "AI info" labels. Etsy's own policy page was walled (U).
- Adobe Premiere Rush should be treated as gone: adobe.com and helpx were 403 today, and both its App Store and Google Play listings return 404 (U, but consistent). Adobe Express pricing was also 403; the App Store listing confirms "Free · In-App Purchases" and that Premium works "across your desktop browser and mobile phone".
- Export targets (V): Reels accept "an aspect ratio between 1.91:1 and 9:16" with "minimum frame rate of 30 FPS" and "minimum resolution of 720 pixels"; Meta recommends 1440 x 2560 for 9:16 and leaving 14% top and 35% bottom clear. TikTok wants 9:16 at or above 540x960, .mp4 or .mov, 500 MB or less. One 1080x1920 master file serves both.
- Nothing in this lane needs money today. First paid candidates, in order, only once earned: DaVinci Resolve Studio one-time ("Was $295 Now $236", adds text-based editing and magic mask), Veed Creator ($12/mo yearly), ElevenLabs Starter ($6/mo) only if an AI voice is ever wanted, which this report advises against.

## 2. Notes per tool

Only facts from pages read today. Complaints are not listed unless they appeared on a page read; none did.

**DaVinci Resolve.** Free version has no watermark and no time limit stated. Limits: 8-bit formats, 60 fps, UHD cap. Studio ("Was $295 Now $236", a sale price on 2026-11-07) adds "the DaVinci AI Neural Engine ... text based editing, magic mask, film grain" and "10-bit video up to 120 frames per second". Studio is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. Page lists "Mac, Windows and Linux".

**Clipchamp.** Free account needs "a personal Microsoft account". Free includes "AI video editing tools", "AI audio editing tools", "Screen, camera, and voice recorder", "Watermark-free video exports", 1080p. Premium is not sold alone: it comes with Microsoft 365 Personal ($99.99/year or $9.99/month) and adds 4K, "Premium stock assets", and a "Single brand" kit. Microsoft 365 is only worth it if Hollie wants Word and 1 TB OneDrive anyway.

**CapCut Desktop.** Price page says "Displayed pricing is for reference only. Sign in to see final pricing". Pro at USD 19.99/month is over the lane's $50 ceiling only when stacked with others; alone it is under. The desktop page confirms Windows 7+ and "Remove Background / Transparent Background" tools; the remover page gives the path "Video" > "Remove BG" > "Auto removal". Licence gotcha: eight CapCut legal/resource URLs failed today (404, 500, 502), so the earlier finding that CapCut library music is non-commercial stands unverified. Do not use CapCut library music or sounds in shop videos until the terms are read. Watermark status of free exports was not stated on the pages read.

**Descript.** Windows 11 only on the desktop side ("macOS 14 (Sonoma) or Windows 11 and newer"). Free is 1 media hour a month and 720p. The pricing page says "AI credits track your usage of AI features like Underlord, Studio Sound, Green Screen, Eye Contact", so background removal uses credits. Hobbyist is $16 per month on annual billing ($24 monthly) for 10 hours and 1080p. Good fit only if Hollie records long talking pieces that need word-level cutting.

**Adobe Express.** adobe.com returned 403 on two pricing URLs. App Store listing: free app with in-app Premium; Premium "unlocks access to Premium features: Over 200M royalty-free Adobe Stock photos, videos, music tracks, design elements and fonts" and works "across your desktop browser and mobile phone". No price readable today.

**Premiere Rush.** Three Adobe URLs were 403. The App Store listing (id1188753863) and Google Play listing (com.adobe.premiererush.videoeditor) both returned 404 today. That is consistent with the app having been withdrawn, but no Adobe statement was read. Treat as unavailable.

**Opus Clip.** Free is a demo: watermark, "No editing", and "After 3 days, the clips will no longer be exportable". Starter $15 is monthly only. Pro has the annual discount ($174 a year). Imports "Local video (Up to 10GB per video)". Designed to find highlights in long videos; a 40-second lamp video has no highlights to find.

**Submagic.** Prices shown were the yearly toggle ("billed yearly"). Starter: 45 credits "= 15 videos", "2 min video duration", "No watermark", "Free b-rolls & audio". Monthly prices were not captured. Trial needs no card. Web only.

**Captions.** Pricing page states "Features and prices reflect iOS plans only." Free tier has "AI usage credits: None" but does list "Traditional video editing", "Teleprompter", "Trim". Web app exists ("Captions on Web Terms"). Max $24.99/mo and Frontier $69.99/mo buy AI actors and generated footage, which a resale shop should not use for product content.

**Veed.** Yearly prices only were readable: Creator $12, Pro $22, Studio $39, each "per user / month billed yearly". Creator includes "No watermark", "Unlimited Auto Subtitles", "Full audio & video catalog" and an "Online Teleprompter". Free tier exists ("Start for free") but its limits did not render. Best value in the paid caption group if ever needed.

**Kapwing.** Free: watermark on every export, 4-minute cap, 720p, 10 credits (about 10 minutes of auto-subtitles). Pro "$16" and Business "$50" were shown with a Monthly/Annual toggle; which was active is not confirmed. "We do not currently offer a free trial for Kapwing's paid plans." Pro removes watermark, 4K, 1,000 subtitle minutes.

**Riverside.** No free plan found; "Start 14-day free trial". Pro $29 monthly or $24 yearly includes "Teleprompter for host", "Unlimited text-based editing", "Magic clips". Download page lists a Mac app; otherwise browser. Podcast-first; wrong shape for Hollie.

**OBS Studio.** Release 33.0.1 (October 29). "Supports Windows 10 and Windows 11". GPL-2.0 on GitHub. Free for commercial use. Use for live selling (Instagram Live via a phone is separate) and for recording a clean camera feed on the PC.

**Loom.** Starter $0: 25 videos, 5-minute screen recordings. Business "$18 per user / month" adds "Unlimited videos", "Remove Loom branding". Desktop app records "HD (up to 4K)" with "Teleprompter style speaker notes"; Chrome extension records up to 720p. Only useful for how-to videos (for example a packing or room-styling walkthrough).

**Teleprompters.** CuePrompter: free, no sign-up, "Free for any use (both commercial and non-commercial)", runs in any browser, so it works on the Windows PC and the iPhone. Teleprompter.com: free tier records "with watermark"; no Windows app ("iOS, Android, MacOS and Web"). BIGVU free: "720p, watermarked". Loom and Riverside also include teleprompter notes. Captions Free lists a teleprompter.

**ElevenLabs.** Free: 10k credits a month, but "The free plan does not include a commercial license and cannot be used for any commercial purpose" and free content "always requires attribution when shared non-commercially". Starter is $1 the first month then $6 per month with "Commercial License". Judgment: do not use an AI voice. The brand is Hollie on camera, and TikTok and Meta label synthetic media.

**Music.** YouTube Audio Library: free, in YouTube Studio; some tracks need credit ("If you're using a track with a Creative Commons license, you must credit the artist"); YouTube says it cannot advise on use "off the platform", so treat it as YouTube-only unless the track's own licence says otherwise. Epidemic Sound: pricing cards did not render; the help centre snippet says the Personal plan "Covers online, non-commercial", so a shop would need the Commercial plan (price U). Artlist: only the AI plans page loaded ($11.99/month billed annually and up); stock pages blank. Uppbeat (429) and Pixabay (403) blocked. Mixkit: free licences per item type, but the licence text itself was in pop-ups the fetcher did not read. Practical rule: Instagram's and TikTok's in-app music libraries are the safe path for organic posts on those apps; for cross-posted files, use a track whose licence Hollie has read.

**B-roll.** Pexels: "free for personal and commercial purposes without attribution" (help centre). Pexels main licence page was 403 but the help article is the vendor's own.

**Figma.** Starter is free with "Unlimited drafts" and a daily AI allowance. Professional starts at $16/mo per full seat monthly. No reason to add it beside Canva.

**Photopea.** Free, browser-based, "There are no uploads. Photopea runs on your device". Opens PSD files. Premium removes ads; price not shown on readable pages (U). Good for touching up rembg cutouts and making framed panels.

**GIMP, Audacity, Shotcut, Kdenlive, Subtitle Edit.** All free and open source, all run on Windows (V). Subtitle Edit 5.4 adds "Speech to text via Whisper (several engines) and Crisp ASR" and needs "Windows 10 (22H2) or newer". It produces SRT files that DaVinci, Clipchamp and the social apps can burn in or attach. No credits, no watermark, offline.

**Canva.** Free plan "US$ 0", Pro "US$ 144 /year for one person" (monthly toggle not captured). Video editor page: "free online Video Editor. No watermarks. No credit card required." Stock footage "from collections like Artlist Video" is marked Pro.

**AI video generators and platform policy.** Sora: web and app discontinued April 26, 2026, API September 24, 2026. Pika Free: no watermark but "Not included: Commercial license"; Starter $10/mo adds it. Runway Free: 125 one-time credits; Standard $12/month yearly, "No watermarks". Google Veo access comes through Google AI Pro at $19.99/mo. TikTok: "we require creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people. Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted, or labeled". Meta: "AI info" labels appear "when people disclose that they're uploading AI-generated content" and for content detected as generated; edited-only content gets the label in the post menu. Etsy's Creativity Standards page returned 403; Etsy Help search listed "How Do Etsy's Listing Image Requirements Apply to My Listings?" but the article was not reached. For one-of-one vintage, generated footage of a product would misrepresent the item; use AI only for backgrounds or titles, and label if realistic.

**Filmora.** Cheap perpetual-style yearly plans ($49.99 a year Basic). The Free column shows "AI Credits None" and "Export Without Watermark" sits in the paid rows; free exports are therefore very likely watermarked, but the tick marks were not readable. DaVinci Resolve free is better value.

**Microsoft Designer.** Both URLs returned no text. Unknown today.

## 3. Recommendation for Hollie

Install or sign up today (all free, all verified today):
1. DaVinci Resolve (free) for anything longer than a Reel, color fixes under the burgundy lights, and clean titles.
2. Clipchamp (already in Windows 11, sign in with a personal Microsoft account) for 1080p cuts with no watermark when DaVinci is too much.
3. CapCut Desktop (free) for vertical templates and one-click video background removal. Do not use its library music or sounds in shop posts until the terms are read.
4. Subtitle Edit 5.4 (free) with a Whisper engine for offline captions; export SRT, then burn in with DaVinci or Clipchamp.
5. OBS Studio (free) for live selling from the PC and for recording a clean camera feed.
6. CuePrompter (free, browser) on the iPhone or a second window as the teleprompter.
7. Photopea (free, browser) and GIMP (free) for image fixes; Audacity (free) if a voice track needs cleanup.
8. Pexels for b-roll; Canva Free's video editor for covers and animated text.

Pay only once it earns it:
- DaVinci Resolve Studio, one-time ($236 today, a sale price) when text-based editing and magic mask would save hours a week.
- Veed Creator ($12 per user / month, billed yearly) if Hollie posts a captioned video every day and Subtitle Edit feels slow.
- Microsoft 365 Personal ($99.99/year) only if Word and OneDrive are wanted anyway; Clipchamp 4K is a side benefit.

Skip and why:
- Opus Clip, Submagic, Captions, Kapwing paid: made for long talking-head sources; free tiers watermark or expire.
- Riverside, Loom Business: podcast and meeting tools.
- ElevenLabs and any AI voice: free tier is non-commercial; the brand is Hollie's own voice.
- Runway, Pika, Veo, Sora: Sora is gone; the others would show a product that is not the product. Platform labels apply.
- Premiere Rush: listings gone from both app stores.
- Epidemic Sound Personal: non-commercial. Any music plan waits until the Commercial price is read.
