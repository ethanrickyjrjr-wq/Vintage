# 06-sourcing-online: Online sourcing and deal-alert tools

Question: which apps, sites and alert tools find underpriced vintage pieces to resell online, using each site's own allowed alert features, and what do they cost? Date 2026-11-07. V = vendor page read by the fetcher. U = unverified (page would not load, or the figure rests on a snippet or review).

## Bottom line

- Every source that matters has a free native alert. Speed is the gap. Only Proxibid (default once a week, daily option), Depop (weekly push) and Botifex (20 to 5 minute refresh by tier) state an interval. eBay states none; AutomatedSearches.com calls eBay's emails "once-a-day".
- Best gaps find: free eBay push apps the lane missed. Trove is "Free to use", "No fees. No subscriptions. No limitations.", alerts "usually within 60 seconds", up to 15 saved searches, eBay Partner Network permission stated. Dealerts and BayWatch are free with in-app purchases. They replace uBuyFirst (price unknown; verify refuted the "30-day" trial claim) and SeeSpotBid PRO (unpriced).
- Local estate sales: two free sign-ups today. EstateSales.net (free email notifications, lead day, sale types, radius, Treasure Tracker keywords, free iOS and Android app) and EstateSales.org (homepage modal form: 5 to 100 miles, weekdays, "Online Auction Items", categories, gsalr digest; official free iOS app Estate Sale Finder). Verify refuted the lane's .org URL: /subscribe is a 404.
- Auction aggregators are free to join. Verify corrected three details: Proxibid's email default is weekly, so pick daily; LiveAuctioneers alerts by both "search alert emails" and app push; CTBids Premier $99/year includes a "WorthPoint 90 Day All-Access Subscription", not a year. HiBid and Invaluable alert mechanics stayed unreadable.
- Facebook Marketplace and OfferUp have no API. Every alert tool reads Meta pages against Meta's terms, and the ban risk lands on the account Hollie sells from. Lowest-risk shapes if she accepts that: Sift (free filters and desktop alerts in her own Chrome; Pro $6.99 a month) and Dibs (free 3 searches every 15 minutes in her own session). Botifex, Swoopa, DealHunter, Marketplace Monitor and Pacto use proxies or dealer pricing. Skip them.
- Page monitors belong on estate-sale company calendars and auction-house pages, never eBay or Facebook. changedetection.io self-hosted on the Fedora box is free (Apache-2.0) and reaches Pushover through Apprise. No-install fallbacks: Follow That Page (free, 20 daily checks and 1 hourly), Wachete FREE (5 pages every 24 hours; STARTER $5.40 / month for hourly), Visualping Free (5 pages every 60 min).
- Defunct or changed: IFTTT's Craigslist service is a 404, so the RSS-to-Pushover glue idea is dead for Craigslist (Craigslist's own email alerts remain). YardSales.net failed DNS twice; treat as dead. Flipify is alive but still unpriced. Marketplace Monitor moved to marketplacemonitor.com.

## Picks

Use now (free):
- EstateSales.net account and app: Fort Myers and Naples, one day ahead, estate and online-auction sale types; add Treasure Tracker keywords (lamp, chandelier, decanter, depression glass, mannequin, turntable).
- EstateSales.org homepage sign-up (50 miles, weekdays, "Online Auction Items", decor categories, gsalr digest) and the Estate Sale Finder iOS app.
- Trove for eBay push alerts on maker names and categories; eBay saved searches as the slow backstop; AutomatedSearches.com as a second free channel; a weekly typo sweep with FatFingers or TypoHound.
- Proxibid saved searches set to daily. LiveAuctioneers followed searches with email and push. Free accounts on HiBid, Invaluable, EBTH, CTBids and ShopGoodwill; find each one's alert setting by hand.
- Craigslist search alerts on the fort myers and sarasota sites.
- changedetection.io on the Fedora box for estate-sale company calendars, MaxSold's Florida page and auction-house pages, notifying Pushover through Apprise.
- Gixen free for one-of-one auctions.
- Sift free in Chrome, only if Hollie accepts the Meta-terms risk.

Pay when it earns it:
- CTBids Premier "$99/year": when she wins CTBids lots monthly; the value is the Delivery Guarantee and the 90-day WorthPoint access, not alerts.
- Dealerts or BayWatch in-app purchases: when Trove's 15 searches run out; read prices in-app first.
- Wachete "STARTER $ 5.40 / month billed monthly": when daily checks miss sales and self-hosting is off.
- Visualping "Personal 1K $14/mo $120/yr ($10/mo)": when more than five pages need watching with no install.
- Sift Pro "$6.99 a month or $49 a year": when Marketplace finds come from desktop alerts and she wants them on the phone.
- Lot-Art "STARTER $7.90 MONTHLY": when art or design lots from auction houses become a sourcing line.
- Gixen Mirror "$11.99 a year": when she wins auctions regularly.

Skip:
- Botifex, Swoopa, DealHunter, Flipify, Marketplace Alerts, Marketplace Monitor, Pacto: scraping and proxies against Meta and eBay terms; a ban hits the selling account. Swoopa is $47/mo for Facebook only and vehicle-focused.
- uBuyFirst, SeeSpotBid PRO: prices unknown; Trove covers the need free.
- Zapier (100 tasks a month is thin) and IFTTT (Craigslist service dead, eBay service seller-side).
- Apify scrapers for ShopGoodwill, MaxSold, Craigslist: unofficial, same terms problem.
- Storage and government auctions, Catawiki, Whatnot, Huginn: wrong goods, wrong geography, or heavier than what is already planned.

## Contradictions with earlier decisions

None. Two notes. Pushover stays valid: changedetection.io reaches it through the Apprise library ("pover://"), not a first-party connector, same outcome. The IFTTT Craigslist 404 kills a lane idea, not a repo decision.
