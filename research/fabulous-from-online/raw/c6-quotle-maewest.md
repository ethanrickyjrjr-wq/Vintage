Context
## Why it sounds like her
Mae West built a career on inversion: taking the prim moral of a proverb and turning it into an invitation. “Too much of a good thing can be wonderful” is that engine in one line — it answers the old caution that you can overdo pleasure with a flat, delighted “yes, and?”
That is exactly why it resists verification. The line is so perfectly in character that quote collectors reached for a source after the fact, attaching her memoir or a film without ever showing the words on a page. The honest position: the sentiment is unmistakably Westian, but the citation is missing.
She didn’t coin the proverb — she coined the comeback to it.
On the proverb she flipped
The phrase “too much of a good thing” was already proverbial by the late 15th century. Its most famous early appearance is in Shakespeare’s _As You Like It_ (c. 1600), when Rosalind teases, “Why then, can one desire too much of a good thing?” For centuries it carried a note of warning. West’s contribution — whenever she first said it — was to strip the warning out entirely.

## Who was Mae West?
MW
[Mae West](https://quotle.info/authors/mae-west/)
1893–1980 · American actress, playwright and vaudeville-bred wit
**Mae West** was an American actress, screenwriter and playwright who turned double-entendre into an art form on the vaudeville stage, in her own scandal-courting Broadway plays, and in 1930s Paramount films such as _She Done Him Wrong_ and _I’m No Angel_. She wrote most of her own dialogue, and her comic method was to take a piece of received wisdom and flip it into something knowing and appetitive. So many quips are pinned on her that a West-sounding line and a documented one are not always the same thing.
