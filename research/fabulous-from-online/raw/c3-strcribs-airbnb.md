# How Much Does Airbnb Design Cost? Fees & Furnishing Guide
By [Mark Lumpkin](https://www.strcribs.com/authors/mark-lumpkin) · Published February 1, 2026 · Updated August 28, 2026
Property owners invest $4,000 to $50,000 in Airbnb design costs based on property size, location, and service level. Professional design services cost $75 to $200 per hour, while DIY approaches and cost-effective furnishing solutions can reduce labor costs but require significant time and market knowledge. Regardless of budget, maximizing Airbnb design for ROI through [strategic furnishing choices](https://www.strcribs.com/blog/property-preparation-short-term-rentals) enhances both guest satisfaction and rental income potential.
Well-designed Airbnb properties attract more bookings and higher rates. According to 2024 industry data, professionally designed properties see occupancy rate increases of up to 64% and annual revenue gains of $50,000 or more. Strategic design investments pay for themselves within 12-24 months through [improved booking performance and guest satisfaction](https://www.strcribs.com/blog/airbnb-layout-optimization) scores, making [data-driven design for ROI](https://www.strcribs.com/blog/airbnb-interior-design-ideas) a smart business strategy.
Companies like STR Cribs use vacation rental market data to inform design decisions that maximize guest satisfaction and host profitability. Their services range from concept development to full renovation, helping property owners make informed investment decisions about maximizing Airbnb design for ROI that deliver measurable results.
## Key Factors Determining Airbnb Design Cost
Your design investment is influenced by property characteristics and market conditions. Location is crucial, with urban markets and high-demand tourist destinations involving 30-50% higher material and labor costs compared to rural areas.
Property size and type set the budget. A studio apartment requires less investment than a four-bedroom vacation home, with costs scaling to square footage and room count. Design style and material quality choices can create price variations exceeding 300-400% between budget and luxury approaches, making professional design services a significant investment consideration.
The renovation dramatically affects costs. Cosmetic updates like paint and decor cost less than full-scale remodels involving structural changes, kitchen renovations, or bathroom upgrades. Professional designer involvement adds expertise and increases labor costs, while DIY approaches save money but require significant time and market knowledge.
## Airbnb Interior Design Cost Categories
Furniture is the largest expense for properties. It costs $3,000 for basic studio furnishing to $15,000+ for premium multi-bedroom setups. Cost variations are driven by quality and quantity. Mid-range pieces cost $8,000-12,000 for one-bedroom properties and deliver better durability than budget alternatives. Many hosts opt for professional Airbnb interior design packages to maximize their investment returns.
Decor elements create the memorable atmosphere guests remember and review. These investments span $800-5,000+ and include artwork, rugs, curtains, and lighting. Strategic investments in quality lighting and textiles generate disproportionate returns through improved guest experiences and social media sharing.

When planning your kitchen setup, expect $300-1,500+ for dishes, cookware, utensils, and small appliances:
  * Basic starter sets from major retailers
  * Mid-range options for frequent use
  * Premium cookware for luxury positioning


For comfortable guest stays, linen costs range from $400 to $2,000+:

Electronics and safety requirements add $600-3,400+ to budgets:
  * TVs, Wi-Fi equipment, and smart devices
  * Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
  * Fire extinguishers and security systems


## Designer Fees for Airbnb Properties
Interior designers offer various service levels from consultation-only to full-scale project management. Hourly rates range from $75 to $200 depending on experience and location, while flat project fees span $2,000 to $15,000+ based on scope and property size.
Percentage-based fees usually represent 10-20% of total project costs and work well for larger renovations. Each pricing model offers distinct advantages depending on your situation and budget. Experienced designers possess market knowledge, vendor relationships, and space planning expertise that can prevent costly mistakes.
Professional design services are most valuable for large-scale renovations, complex layout challenges, or when property owners lack time or design experience. STR Cribs has demonstrated these advantages through their data-driven design approach, helping clients achieve up to 64% increases in occupancy rates and 40% higher average daily rates.
Smaller properties with straightforward layouts may achieve satisfactory results through self-directed efforts, particularly when budget constraints are primary considerations. The decision depends on your experience level, available time, and risk tolerance for potential mistakes impacting guest satisfaction.
## DIY vs Professional Design Cost Analysis
Direct cost comparisons reveal significant differences between approaches. DIY labor represents a $20-50 per hour opportunity cost, while professional designer fees range from $5,000-15,000+ for complete projects. When evaluating these options, consider the value of your time and alternative income.
DIY approaches offer free design expertise but require substantial learning time and market research. Professional services include years of experience and market knowledge in their fees, preventing mistakes that could cost $1,000-5,000+ in corrections and replacements.
Resource access differs between approaches. DIY efforts limit you to retail options and standard pricing, while designers offer trade discounts and exclusive sources. Professional relationships can result in better pricing and unique pieces unavailable to individual consumers.
The optimal choice depends on your experience, time, budget, and risk tolerance. DIY approaches offer creative control and personal satisfaction but require substantial time. Professional services reduce stress, speed up timelines, and deliver more sophisticated outcomes.
## When Is a Professional Airbnb Designer Worth the Cost?
A professional designer is worth considering when the expected value of better decisions exceeds the fee. For a short-term rental, that value can come from launching sooner, avoiding incorrect purchases, increasing usable occupancy, producing stronger listing photography, and creating a stay that supports the intended nightly rate. The decision should be based on project complexity and financial goals rather than the assumption that every property needs full-service design.
### Professional Design Is Usually the Better Fit When
  * **The property needs renovation or major space planning.** Layout, electrical, lighting, materials, built-ins, and contractor decisions are costly to reverse.
  * **The market is crowded.** A generic setup can disappear among visually similar listings, while a clear guest profile and differentiated concept give the property a stronger position.
  * **You are launching or renovating at a distance.** Procurement, substitutions, freight, installation, and contractor coordination become a second job when the owner cannot be on site.
  * **The property serves large groups.** Seating capacity, sleeping arrangements, storage, dining, circulation, and amenity use must work together.
  * **You are scaling a portfolio.** A repeatable design and procurement process can reduce owner time and inconsistent purchasing across properties.
  * **Current performance does not support the target rate.** Weak photos, poor reviews, awkward spaces, or an unclear design concept may require a coordinated intervention rather than isolated décor purchases.


### DIY Can Be the Better Choice When
DIY may be rational for a small, straightforward property with a limited scope, a capable local owner, reliable vendors, and a market where a costly custom concept is unlikely to produce enough additional revenue. It also works when the project involves refreshes rather than construction: replacing worn items, improving styling, repainting, or correcting a short list of operational gaps.
Price the owner's time honestly. A DIY budget should include research, ordering, receiving, returns, damaged freight, assembly, installation, contractor communication, and the income lost if the property opens later than planned.
### What a Specialized STR Designer Should Deliver
A specialized short-term rental designer should connect the concept to the guest and business model. Depending on the package, deliverables may include market and competitor research, space plans, finish selections, furniture and amenity specifications, procurement, freight coordination, installation, styling, and listing-photo preparation. A proposal that provides only a mood board should not be compared directly with one that includes purchasing and installation.
The designer should also account for operations: durable materials, cleanability, replacement availability, owner storage, luggage, charging, lighting controls, smart locks, thermostats, Wi-Fi equipment, and the number of guests who need to sit, dine, sleep, and use amenities at the same time.
### Potential Drawbacks and How to Reduce Them
  * **Higher upfront spending:** separate design fees, furnishings, renovation, freight, storage, and installation so the total commitment is visible.
  * **Unclear scope:** require a written list of deliverables, revision limits, exclusions, responsibilities, and approval points.
  * **Design that misses the market:** ask how guest profile, local competitors, rate targets, and operating data informed the concept.
  * **Procurement delays:** establish substitution rules and identify who approves replacements when products become unavailable.
  * **Overdesign:** prioritize features guests can see, use, and value instead of fragile details that increase maintenance without supporting revenue.


### A Simple Cost-Benefit Decision
Estimate the total professional-design premium over a realistic DIY project, then compare it with conservative benefits: earlier launch income, avoided replacement purchases, owner hours saved, and a modest improvement in occupancy or average daily rate. Do not rely on a best-case revenue claim. If the conservative benefit does not recover the additional cost within an acceptable period, reduce the scope or choose DIY support such as a consultation or design-only package.
Before signing, review comparable completed properties, confirm who manages procurement and installation, and ask how success will be evaluated. The best decision may be full service, design-only guidance, or DIY—but the comparison must use the same scope and account for execution risk.
## Airbnb Design Cost by Property Size
Studio apartments require careful space optimization and multifunctional furniture. Total design costs range from $4,000-10,000, focusing on maximizing perceived space through strategic lighting, mirrors, and compact storage. Every purchase must serve multiple purposes while maintaining aesthetic appeal.
One-bedroom apartments allow for traditional furniture arrangements and separate living areas. Budget expectations of $8,000-18,000 enable distinct bedroom and living room designs for various guest needs and longer stays. This property type offers the best balance of manageable costs and revenue potential for new hosts.
Multi-bedroom houses typically demand higher investments, usually $15,000-50,000+ depending on size and market positioning. They offer opportunities for themed rooms, family-friendly amenities, and luxury features that justify premium rates. They require substantial upfront capital and ongoing maintenance budgets.
Unique properties like cabins, treehouses, or tiny homes require specialized design solutions and custom elements. Costs vary based on structural requirements, accessibility challenges, and the need for custom storage or furniture that fit unusual spaces.
## Airbnb Furnishing Budget by Room
Separate furnishing purchases from design fees and renovation work so quotes remain comparable. The following planning ranges reflect the retired furnishing guide and should be treated as starting estimates, not bids; local labor, delivery, occupancy, quality, and property condition can change the total substantially.
### Bedroom: $800–$3,500
Plan for the bed frame, mattress, two nightstands, lighting, dresser or closet organization, blackout window treatments, a mirror, and multiple linen sets. Protect sleep quality first: a durable mattress, washable textiles, and turnover-ready backups matter more than decorative extras.
### Living Room: $1,200–$5,000
Include seating sized for maximum occupancy, tables, lighting, window treatments, a rug, durable décor, and any television or media storage. Spend on upholstery that tolerates frequent cleaning and on lighting that works both for guests and listing photography.
### Kitchen and Dining: $600–$2,500
Budget for the dining table and chairs, cookware, place settings plus replacements, glassware, utensils, storage containers, linens, and small appliances. Quantities should follow maximum occupancy, while the retained [Airbnb kitchen essentials checklist](https://www.strcribs.com/blog/airbnb-kitchen-essentials) defines the item-level inventory.
### Bathroom: $200–$900
Include multiple towel sets, bath mats, shower hardware or curtains, storage, waste bins, and starter toiletries. Confirm that mirrors and permanent fixtures are already included before treating this as a furnishing-only allowance.
A small property may fit within these room ranges, but whole-property totals also need delivery, assembly, installation, taxes, initial consumables, removal of old items, and outdoor or specialty spaces. Keep a replacement reserve and obtain current local quotes before committing capital.
## How Interior Designers Charge
Professional fees are only one part of the complete project budget. Ask every provider to separate design labor, purchasing or product markups, project management, travel, installation, freight, contractor work, and reimbursable expenses in the proposal.
### Hourly Fees
Hourly pricing fits consultations, focused space-planning help, and open-ended advisory work. The retired fee guide cited a broad $50–$500 hourly market range, with many experienced providers between $75 and $200. Require time estimates, reporting intervals, and a written approval threshold so a flexible engagement does not become an uncontrolled budget.
### Flat Project Fees
A fixed fee works best when the rooms, deliverables, revision rounds, procurement duties, and installation responsibilities are clearly defined. Confirm what triggers a change order and whether the fee excludes purchasing, travel, freight, receiving, or styling day.
### Percentage-Based Fees
Some designers charge a percentage of furnishings, construction, or the total managed budget. Define the percentage base precisely, disclose any product markup, and require approval before the managed budget changes. This model can align management effort with project size, but it can also reward higher spending without firm controls.
### Virtual Design and Limited-Scope Services
E-design can reduce fees when the owner can measure, order, receive, assemble, and install independently. A useful package should still identify layouts, specifications, quantities, alternates, and implementation instructions. It is less suitable for construction-heavy work, difficult site conditions, or owners who cannot manage vendors.
## Hourly vs. Flat-Rate Interior Design Fees for STRs
The best fee model depends on how clearly the work can be defined. Neither hourly billing nor a flat package controls the full project cost by itself: the contract still needs a detailed scope, purchasing rules, approval thresholds, and a process for changes.
### When Hourly Billing Fits
Hourly billing is useful for a focused consultation, one-room review, uncertain renovation conditions, or an owner who only needs intermittent expertise. It lets the project adapt without pricing every unknown in advance. Ask whether different tasks carry different rates, whether calls, travel, sourcing, administration, and revisions are billable, and how often time reports will be provided.
The tradeoff is an open-ended total. Set an initial retainer, estimated hour range, reporting cadence, and written authorization threshold. A stop-work point prevents the designer from exceeding the approved amount while preserving flexibility.
### When a Flat or Package Fee Fits
A flat fee works well for a complete furnishing or design engagement with known rooms, deliverables, decision dates, and installation responsibilities. Milestone payments can tie invoices to concept approval, purchasing, and completion rather than elapsed time.
Predictability depends on scope quality. A fixed price may still exclude freight, receiving, product cost, contractor labor, travel, installation, storage, styling, or additional revisions. Define those exclusions, the number of concepts and revision rounds, and the rate or formula for change orders. A major mid-project pivot can cost more under a package, while a simpler-than-expected project normally does not reduce the agreed fee.
## What an STR Design Fee Should Cover
Compare proposals against the same required outputs rather than comparing headline fees alone. Depending on the engagement, the design scope may include:
  * **Market and guest analysis:** target-guest assumptions, competitor review, and a design direction appropriate to the property.
  * **Concept and visualization:** mood boards, color and material direction, layouts, renderings, or other approval documents.
  * **Space planning:** furniture plans, circulation, occupancy, luggage storage, work areas, and amenity placement.
  * **Specifications and procurement:** item schedules, quantities, alternates, purchasing responsibility, product markups, and damaged-item handling.
  * **Project coordination:** schedules, contractor or vendor communication, delivery tracking, receiving, and installation oversight.
  * **Final setup:** assembly, styling, inventory records, punch-list work, and listing-photography preparation.


Not every project needs every service. Removing an unwanted task is useful only when the owner or another vendor explicitly owns it; otherwise the apparent fee savings can become an uncovered operational gap.
## Seven Questions to Ask Before Signing
  1. What exactly is included and excluded from the design fee?
  2. How many concepts and revision rounds are included, and how are scope changes priced?
  3. Who measures the property, approves specifications, purchases products, receives deliveries, and resolves damage or returns?
  4. For hourly work, which activities are billable and what detail will appear on invoices?
  5. For flat-fee work, what assumptions, milestones, and events trigger a change order?
  6. Are product markups, commissions, freight, travel, storage, installation, contractor work, and reimbursable expenses disclosed separately?
  7. What is the estimated all-in project budget—not only the design fee—and what comparable STR work supports the recommendation?


Put the answers in the contract. A transparent proposal should make it possible to identify who owns every deliverable, what approvals are required, and what happens when timing, availability, or scope changes.
## Build a Complete Design Budget
Use one budget that distinguishes design fees, furniture and décor, materials and finishes, contractor labor, freight and delivery, installation, permits, disposal, taxes, staging, and contingency. For renovation work, hold a 10–20% contingency until unknown conditions are resolved. For furnishing-only projects, track delivery, assembly, damaged-item replacement, initial supplies, and storage or receiving costs rather than assuming the purchase price is the installed cost.
Compare multiple proposals on the same scope. Prioritize must-have guest functions, then phase optional décor or specialty amenities. Reuse sound items when they meet the design and durability standard, and reserve professional effort for layout, specification, procurement, or trade coordination where mistakes would be expensive.

Effective budget planning starts with realistic expectations based on your property type, local market conditions, and target guest demographics. Before setting design budgets, research comparable properties in your area to understand guest expectations and competitive positioning.

Cost management strategies include shopping across multiple retailers, considering secondhand refurbishment options, and phasing renovations to spread expenses. Maintaining detailed expense tracking helps with cost control and tax preparation, while planning for unexpected costs through 15-20% budget buffers prevents project delays.
Quality investments matter most for mattresses, linens, and appliances that impact guest comfort and operational efficiency. ## Regional Airbnb Design Cost Variations
Geographic location significantly impacts design costs through varying labor rates, material availability, and market expectations. Major metropolitan areas like New York, San Francisco, and Miami involve 30-50% higher costs for materials and services compared to smaller markets.
Tourist destinations often command premium pricing for design services due to high demand and specialized market knowledge. Specific design elements reflecting local culture and guest expectations required in mountain resort towns, beach communities, and wine country locations, increasing project costs.
Rural areas offer lower material and labor costs, but they may have limited selection and longer delivery times that can impact project timelines. Transportation costs for materials and furniture become significant, as remote locations involve substantial delivery fees that impact total project expenses.
Before finalizing budgets, research local costs by getting quotes from area designers, contractors, and suppliers. Consider joining local real estate investor groups or short-term rental host communities to share vendor recommendations and cost information.
## Hidden Costs in Airbnb Design Projects
Permits and inspections are required for electrical, plumbing, or structural modifications, costing $200-1,500 depending on scope and location. Professional installation fees for appliances, lighting fixtures, and wall-mounted items can add $500-3,000 to project costs but ensure proper setup and safety compliance.
Shipping and delivery costs for furniture and decor from multiple sources can add 10-15% to material costs. Assembly services cost $50-200 per piece but save time and ensure proper construction, especially for complex items or tight deadlines.
Utility setup and upgrades, including enhanced internet service for guests, may require $200-800 in connection fees and equipment. Initial cleaning and staging for photography typically costs $300-800 and is essential for attractive listing photos that drive bookings.
Ongoing maintenance reserves should include 5-10% of gross rental income for wear-and-tear replacement and repairs. Quality guests expect well-maintained properties, and proactive maintenance prevents negative reviews and costly emergency repairs that impact booking availability.
## Current Airbnb Design Trends and Budget Implementation
Modern farmhouse, Scandinavian minimalism, and biophilic design resonate with contemporary travelers seeking Instagram-worthy experiences. When implemented strategically within budget constraints and market positioning goals, these styles can enhance property appeal and booking rates.
Minimalist design trends offer budget advantages through reduced furnishing needs while creating clean, spacious atmospheres that photograph well. This style typically costs 20-30% less than traditional furnished approaches while generating higher guest satisfaction through uncluttered, calming environments.
Scandinavian-inspired design emphasizes natural materials, neutral colors, and functional furniture that balances style with durability. Initial costs may be moderate to high, but the timeless appeal and quality focus provide long-term value and reduced replacement needs compared to trendy approaches.
Incorporate trending elements through cost-effective approaches like accent walls, strategic lighting upgrades, or selected art pieces instead of complete style overhauls. These updates refresh property appeal without expensive replacement of functional items.
