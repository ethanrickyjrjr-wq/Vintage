Hitchcock is known for [his small cameos in his films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Alfred_Hitchcock "List of cameo appearances by Alfred Hitchcock").
A **cameo appearance** , also called a **cameo role** or just **cameo** ([/ˈkæmioʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")), is a brief [guest appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_appearance "Guest appearance") of a well-known person or character in a work of the [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts "Performing arts"). These roles are generally small, many of them non-speaking ones, and are commonly either appearances in a work in which they hold some special significance (such as actors from an original movie appearing in its remake) or renowned people making uncredited appearances. Short appearances by [celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity "Celebrity"), [film directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_director "Film director"), politicians, athletes or musicians are common. A crew member of the movie or show playing a minor role can be referred to as a cameo role as well, such as director [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock") who made [frequent cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Alfred_Hitchcock "List of cameo appearances by Alfred Hitchcock") appearances in his films.
## Concept
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cameo_appearance&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Concept")]
Originally, in the 1920s, a "cameo role" meant "a small character part that stands out from the other minor parts". The _[Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary "Oxford English Dictionary")_ connects this with the meaning "a short literary sketch or portrait", which is based on the literal meaning of "[cameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_\(carving\) "Cameo \(carving\)")", a miniature carving on a gemstone.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-1) More recently, in the late 20th century, a "cameo" has come to refer to any short appearance as a character.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-2)
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/10.14.11StanLeeByLuigiNovi9.jpg/250px-10.14.11StanLeeByLuigiNovi9.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:10.14.11StanLeeByLuigiNovi9.jpg)[Stan Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Lee "Stan Lee") was well known for his [cameo appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Stan_Lee "List of cameo appearances by Stan Lee") throughout most of the [Marvel films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_based_on_Marvel_Comics_publications "List of films based on Marvel Comics publications").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-techinsider-3)
Cameos are generally not credited because of their brevity, or a perceived mismatch between the celebrity's stature and the film or television series in which they are appearing. Many are [publicity stunts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicity_stunt "Publicity stunt"). Others are acknowledgements of an actor's contribution to an earlier work, as in the case of many [film adaptations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_adaptation "Film adaptation") of television series, or of [remakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remake "Remake") of earlier films. Others honour artists or celebrities known for work in a particular field, such as comic book writer [Stan Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Lee "Stan Lee"), who made [appearances in every Marvel Cinematic Universe movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Stan_Lee "List of cameo appearances by Stan Lee") up to _[Avengers: Endgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers:_Endgame "Avengers: Endgame")._[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-4)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-techinsider-3)
Cameos also occur in [novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novel "Novel") and other literary works. "Literary cameos" usually involve an established character from another work who makes a brief appearance to establish a [shared universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_universe "Shared universe") setting, to make a point, or to offer [homage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homage_\(arts\) "Homage \(arts\)"). [Balzac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzac "Balzac") often employed this practice, as in his _[Comédie humaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Com%C3%A9die_humaine "La Comédie humaine")_. Sometimes a cameo features a historical person who "drops in" on fictional characters in a [historical novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_novel "Historical novel"), as when [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin "Benjamin Franklin") shares a [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer "Beer") with Phillipe Charboneau in _[The Bastard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bastard_\(novel\) "The Bastard \(novel\)")_ by [John Jakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jakes "John Jakes").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
A cameo appearance can be made by the author of a work to put a sort of personal "signature" on a story. [Vladimir Nabokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Nabokov "Vladimir Nabokov") often put himself in his novels, for instance as the very minor character Vivian Darkbloom (an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram "Anagram") of his name) in _[Lolita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolita "Lolita")_.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-Straumann2008-5)

### Film directors
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cameo_appearance&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Film directors")]
[Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock "Alfred Hitchcock") is known for [his frequent cameos in his movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Alfred_Hitchcock "List of cameo appearances by Alfred Hitchcock"), as early as in his third film [_The Lodger_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lodger:_A_Story_of_the_London_Fog "The Lodger: A Story of the London Fog") (1927). In _[Lifeboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeboat_\(1944_film\) "Lifeboat \(1944 film\)")_ , as the action was restricted to the titular lifeboat, Hitchcock appeared in a newspaper ad.
[Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino "Quentin Tarantino") provides brief cameos or [small roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_part "Bit part") in all his movies.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-6)
Likewise, [Peter Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jackson "Peter Jackson") has made brief [cameos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cameo_appearances_by_Peter_Jackson "List of cameo appearances by Peter Jackson") in all of his movies, except for his first feature-length film _[Bad Taste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Taste "Bad Taste")_ in which he played a main character, as well as _[The Battle of the Five Armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Battle_of_the_Five_Armies "The Hobbit: The Battle of the Five Armies")_ , though a portrait of him appears in the film. For example, he played a peasant eating a carrot in _[The Fellowship of the Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring "The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring")_ and _[The Desolation of Smaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hobbit:_The_Desolation_of_Smaug "The Hobbit: The Desolation of Smaug")_ , a warrior of Rohan in _[The Two Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers "The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers")_ , and a Corsair of Umbar boatswain in _[The Return of the King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King "The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King")_. All four were non-speaking "blink and you miss him" appearances, although in the Extended Release version of _The Return of the King_ , his character was given more screen time and his reprise of the carrot eating peasant in _The Desolation of Smaug_ was featured in the foreground in reference to _The Fellowship of the Ring_.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-7) In addition, when he was directing _Heavenly Creatures_ (1994), he appeared as a person bumping who is kissed by one the main characters, and in the _Frighteners_ , Jackson appeared as a man with piercings.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-8)
Director [Tim Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burton "Tim Burton") briefly appears in his films. He made a short appearance as a street thug who confronts Pee-wee in the back alley in _[Pee-wee's Big Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pee-wee's_Big_Adventure "Pee-wee's Big Adventure")_ , and a visitor at the fair in Blackpool who gets a skeleton thrown at him in _[Miss Peregrine's Home for Peculiar Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Peregrine's_Home_for_Peculiar_Children_\(film\) "Miss Peregrine's Home for Peculiar Children \(film\)")_.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-9)
Director [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese "Martin Scorsese") appears in the background of his films as a bystander or an [unseen character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseen_character "Unseen character"). In _[Who's That Knocking at My Door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who's_That_Knocking_at_My_Door "Who's That Knocking at My Door")_ (1967), he played one of the gangsters; he was a lighting crewman in _[After Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_Hours_\(film\) "After Hours \(film\)")_ and a passenger in _[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver "Taxi Driver")_. He opened up his film _[The Color of Money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Color_of_Money "The Color of Money")_ with a monologue on the art of playing pool. In addition, he appeared with his wife and daughter as wealthy New Yorkers in _[Gangs of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangs_of_New_York "Gangs of New York")_ , and as a theatre-goer and can be heard as a movie [projectionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectionist "Projectionist") in _[The Aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Aviator_\(2004_film\) "The Aviator \(2004 film\)")_. He also appeared in his 2023 work _[Killers of the Flower Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killers_of_the_Flower_Moon "Killers of the Flower Moon")_ , in a minor role as a [radio drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_drama "Radio drama") narrator.
In a same way, [Roman Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Polanski "Roman Polanski") appeared as a hired hoodlum in his film _[Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown_\(1974_film\) "Chinatown \(1974 film\)")_ , slitting Jack Nicholson's nose with the blade of his clasp knife.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-10)
[F. Gary Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Gary_Gray "F. Gary Gray") has made many appearances in the films he has directed including _[Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_\(1995_film\) "Friday \(1995 film\)")_ , [_Set It Off_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_It_Off_\(film\) "Set It Off \(film\)"), _[Law Abiding Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Abiding_Citizen "Law Abiding Citizen")_ , and [_Straight Outta Compton_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Outta_Compton_\(film\) "Straight Outta Compton \(film\)").[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-11)
In addition to [George Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lucas "George Lucas")'s cameo in _[Revenge of the Sith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenge_of_the_Sith "Revenge of the Sith")_ , his children were cast in a number of cameo roles across the _[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars "Star Wars")_ prequels. Amanda and Katie Lucas both had cameo roles as three different characters each across _[The Phantom Menace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phantom_Menace "The Phantom Menace")_ , _[Attack of the Clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_of_the_Clones "Attack of the Clones")_ and _[Revenge of the Sith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenge_of_the_Sith "Revenge of the Sith")_ (in addition to Amanda's voicing a fourth character), and Jett Lucas has two cameo roles for different characters in _[Attack of the Clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_of_the_Clones "Attack of the Clones")_ and _[Revenge of the Sith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenge_of_the_Sith "Revenge of the Sith")_.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-12)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-13)

_[It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Mad,_Mad,_Mad,_Mad_World "It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World")_ (1963), an "epic comedy", features cameos from nearly every popular American comedian alive at the time, including [The Three Stooges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Stooges "The Three Stooges"), [Jerry Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Lewis "Jerry Lewis"), [Buster Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buster_Keaton "Buster Keaton") and a voice-only cameo by [Selma Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_Diamond "Selma Diamond").[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-14)
[Anthony Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Daniels "Anthony Daniels") made a cameo appearance in _[Attack of the Clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_of_the_Clones "Attack of the Clones")_ , despite already starring in the film. Daniels voiced the droid [C-3PO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-3PO "C-3PO"), but also made a brief appearance (revealing the actor's actual face and body) as a patron in the background of the Outlander Club.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-15)
"Murder on High C", a 1975 episode of the TV series _[Get Christie Love!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Christie_Love! "Get Christie Love!")_ , which starred former _[Laugh-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laugh-In "Laugh-In")_ cast member [Teresa Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_Graves "Teresa Graves"), featured a number of her former cast members, including the villain ([Arte Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte_Johnson "Arte Johnson")), [Johnny Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Brown_\(actor\) "Johnny Brown \(actor\)"), [Judy Carne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Carne "Judy Carne"), [Henry Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Gibson "Henry Gibson"), [Gary Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Owens "Gary Owens") and [Joanne Worley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanne_Worley "Joanne Worley").
_[Won Ton Ton, the Dog Who Saved Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Won_Ton_Ton,_the_Dog_Who_Saved_Hollywood "Won Ton Ton, the Dog Who Saved Hollywood")_ (1976) features cameos by dozens of actors from Hollywood's golden age.
_[The Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Player_\(1992_film\) "The Player \(1992 film\)")_ (1992) features cameos from 65 Hollywood actors.
_[Run for Your Wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_for_Your_Wife_\(2012_film\) "Run for Your Wife \(2012 film\)")_ (2012) is filled with cameos from 80 of Britain's film and TV stars from the 1960s, '70s and '80s.
[Aaron Sorkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Sorkin "Aaron Sorkin") also had cameos in some works he wrote: as a bar customer speaking about the law in his debut film screenplay _[A Few Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Few_Good_Men "A Few Good Men")_ (1992), as an advertising executive in _[The Social Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Social_Network "The Social Network")_ , and as a guest at the inauguration of President [Matt Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Santos "Matt Santos") in the final episode of _[The West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_West_Wing "The West Wing")_.
[Franco Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Nero "Franco Nero"), the actor who portrayed the Django character in the original [1966 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Django_\(1966_film\) "Django \(1966 film\)"), appears in a bar scene of the Tarantino film _[Django Unchained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Django_Unchained "Django Unchained")_. There, he asks Django ([Jamie Foxx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Foxx "Jamie Foxx")) to spell his name, which led to the famous promotional tagline for the film - "The 'D' is silent". Franco's character responds simply, "I know."
Many cameos featured in _[Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maverick_\(film\) "Maverick \(film\)")_ (1994), directed by [Richard Donner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Donner "Richard Donner"). Among them, [Danny Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Glover "Danny Glover") – [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson "Mel Gibson")'s co-star in the _[Lethal Weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Weapon_\(film_series\) "Lethal Weapon \(film series\)")_ franchise also directed by Donner – appears as the lead [bank robber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_robber "Bank robber"). He and Maverick (Gibson) share a scene where they look as if they knew each other, but then shake it off. As Glover makes his escape with the money, he mutters "I'm too old for this shit", his character's [catchphrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchphrase "Catchphrase") in the _Lethal Weapon_ films. In addition, a strain of the main [theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(music\) "Theme \(music\)") from _Lethal Weapon_ plays in the [score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score "Film score") when Glover is revealed. Actress [Margot Kidder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Kidder "Margot Kidder") made a cameo appearance in the same film as a robbed villager: she had previously starred as [Lois Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lois_Lane "Lois Lane") in Donner's _[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_\(1978_film\) "Superman \(1978 film\)")_ (1978).[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-RevEbert-16)
[Ben Stiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Stiller "Ben Stiller"), [Vince Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Vaughn "Vince Vaughn"), [Owen Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Wilson "Owen Wilson"), [Luke Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Wilson "Luke Wilson") and [Will Ferrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Ferrell "Will Ferrell") have made appearances in so many of the same films (whether as lead characters or cameos) that _[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")_ coined the term "[Frat Pack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frat_Pack "Frat Pack")" to name the group.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-17) Actor [Adam Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sandler "Adam Sandler") is also known for frequently casting fellow _[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live "Saturday Night Live")_ performers (including [Rob Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Schneider "Rob Schneider") and [David Spade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Spade "David Spade")) in various roles in his films (as well as making cameo appearances of his own in theirs, most of which he co-produces). [Sam Raimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Raimi "Sam Raimi") frequently uses his brother [Ted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Raimi "Ted Raimi") and [Bruce Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Campbell "Bruce Campbell") in his films.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-18)
The animated series _[The Adventures of Tintin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tintin_\(TV_series\) "The Adventures of Tintin \(TV series\)")_ featured its author [Hergé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herg%C3%A9 "Hergé") in all the episodes.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-19)
[Stephen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King "Stephen King") is famous for making short cameo appearances in almost every movie based on his novels.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-20)
_[An Adventure in Space and Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Adventure_in_Space_and_Time "An Adventure in Space and Time")_ , a drama about how _[Doctor Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Who "Doctor Who")_ began, features many [actors from the show's past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Adventure_in_Space_and_Time#Cast "An Adventure in Space and Time"), including two past companions in a party scene, another as a mother calling her children in for dinner and a fourth in a car park at the BBC as a guard.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-21)
In the movie adaptation of _[Les Miserables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_\(2012_film\) "Les Misérables \(2012 film\)")_ , [Colm Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colm_Wilkinson "Colm Wilkinson"), who originated the role of Jean Valjean in the West End and on Broadway, made a cameo as the [Bishop of Digne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Myriel "Bishop Myriel").[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-lati-22)
In the Soviet film _[Moscow Does Not Believe in Tears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Does_Not_Believe_in_Tears "Moscow Does Not Believe in Tears")_ , [Innokenty Smoktunovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innokenty_Smoktunovsky "Innokenty Smoktunovsky") appeared for a minute as himself.
In [_Percy Jackson and the Olympians_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Jackson_and_the_Olympians_\(TV_series\) "Percy Jackson and the Olympians \(TV series\)"), author [Rick Riordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Riordan "Rick Riordan") appeared as a teacher in the first episode.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-23)
### Other
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cameo_appearance&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Other")]
Films based on actual events occasionally include cameo [guest appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_appearance "Guest appearance") by the people portrayed in them. In _[The Pursuit of Happyness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pursuit_of_Happyness "The Pursuit of Happyness")_ , [Chris Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Gardner "Chris Gardner") made a cameo at the end. _[24 Hour Party People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hour_Party_People "24 Hour Party People")_ , a film about [Tony Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Wilson "Tony Wilson"), has a cameo by the real Tony Wilson and many other notable people. In the film _[Apollo 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_13_\(film\) "Apollo 13 \(film\)")_ , [James Lovell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lovell "Jim Lovell") (the real commander of that flight) and his wife Marilyn appeared next to the actors playing them ([Tom Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hanks "Tom Hanks") and [Kathleen Quinlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Quinlan "Kathleen Quinlan") respectively), and [Chuck Yeager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Yeager "Chuck Yeager"), whose story is told in the early part of the film, appears in a cameo in the airfield bar. [Domino Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_Harvey "Domino Harvey") made a short appearance in the credits of _[Domino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_\(2005_film\) "Domino \(2005 film\)")_ , while the real [Erin Brockovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Brockovich "Erin Brockovich") had a cameo as a waitress named Julia in the [eponymous movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_\(film\) "Erin Brockovich \(film\)") (where her role is played by the actress [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts "Julia Roberts")).[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] [Sophie Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Wilson "Sophie Wilson") had a cameo as a barmaid in _[Micro Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_Men "Micro Men")_ , which shows her work for [Acorn Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acorn_Computers "Acorn Computers"). In a [flashback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashback_\(narrative\) "Flashback \(narrative\)") sequence in _[Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_and_Loathing_in_Las_Vegas_\(film\) "Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas \(film\)")_ , Raoul Duke (played by [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp "Johnny Depp")) runs into the real-life [Hunter S. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_S._Thompson "Hunter S. Thompson"), upon whom the character of Duke is based, leading him to remark "There I was...mother of God, there I am! Holy fuck."
[Stephen Hawking in popular culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hawking_in_popular_culture "Stephen Hawking in popular culture") lists more than a dozen appearances of the scientist playing himself.
[Maria Von Trapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Von_Trapp "Maria Von Trapp") made an uncredited brief cameo appearance in the film version of her life, _[The Sound of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music_\(film\) "The Sound of Music \(film\)")_. She appeared in the background during the song "I Have Confidence" with her daughter [Rosmarie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosmarie_Trapp "Rosmarie Trapp") and stepson Werner Von Trapp. 
[Jacqueline Susann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Susann "Jacqueline Susann"), author of the best-selling novel _[Valley of the Dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Dolls_\(novel\) "Valley of the Dolls \(novel\)")_ , appears as a TV reporter in a brief scene in [the film based upon her novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Dolls_\(film\) "Valley of the Dolls \(film\)"). Similarly, [Peter Benchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Benchley "Peter Benchley") would appear as a TV reporter in the [Jaws movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaws_\(film\) "Jaws \(film\)"), based on his novel _[of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaws_\(novel\) "Jaws \(novel\)")_.
[Tom Morello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Morello "Tom Morello"), American guitarist and musician, made an appearance in the Marvel film _[Iron Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_\(2008_film\) "Iron Man \(2008 film\)")_ (2008), in which he also participated in the [soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_\(soundtrack\) "Iron Man \(soundtrack\)").
[Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk "Elon Musk") and [Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison "Larry Ellison"), both founders of large technology companies, were featured in cameos in _[Iron Man 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Man_2 "Iron Man 2")_ (2010).[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-24)
The king of Sweden, [Carl XVI Gustaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf "Carl XVI Gustaf"), was in the children's program _Mika_ (_Mika och renen Ossian på äventyr_[Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm "Stockholm") with his reindeer.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-25)
In _[The Wolf of Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wolf_of_Wall_Street_\(2013_film\) "The Wolf of Wall Street \(2013 film\)")_ (2013), the real [Jordan Belfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Belfort "Jordan Belfort") appeared as an [emcee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_ceremonies "Master of ceremonies") to introduce [Leonardo DiCaprio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio "Leonardo DiCaprio"), who played Belfort, in the final scene.
Boxer [Roberto Duran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Duran "Roberto Duran") and his wife Felicidad made a cameo appearance towards the end of the film _[Hands of Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hands_of_Stone "Hands of Stone")_ , about Duran's life.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-26)
In _[The Big Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Short_\(film\) "The Big Short \(film\)")_ , the real investor [Michael Burry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Burry "Michael Burry") appeared as an employee of his hedge fund "[Scion Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scion_Capital "Scion Capital")" while answering the phone saying "Doctor Burry's office".
An unusual example of a famous non-actor being given a small but speaking fictional role occurred in the _[Star Trek: The Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation "Star Trek: The Next Generation")_ episode "[Second Chances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chances_\(Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation\) "Second Chances \(Star Trek: The Next Generation\)")." Dr. [Mae Jemison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Jemison "Mae Jemison"), an [astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut "Astronaut"), the first Black woman in space, and a long-time fan of _Star Trek_ , was offered the opportunity to appear on the show. She was given the role of a Starfleet crewmember and a few lines, thus becoming the first real-life astronaut to appear on _Star Trek_.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-27) Somewhat likewise King [Abdullah II of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_II_of_Jordan "Abdullah II of Jordan") appeared briefly in a non-speaking role the _[Star Trek: Voyager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_Voyager "Star Trek: Voyager")_ episode "[Investigations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigations_\(Voyager_episode\) "Investigations \(Voyager episode\)")".[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance#cite_note-sts-28)
