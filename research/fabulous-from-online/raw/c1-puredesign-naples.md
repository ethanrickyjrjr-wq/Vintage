## How much does an interior designer in Naples charge?

## Which pricing model should you ask for?
![Which pricing model should you ask for?](https://puredesignofnaples.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Which-pricing-model-should-you-ask-for-1024x683.webp)
Push for the model that fits your scope. Then cap it in writing. Pure Design of Naples quotes [**interior design work in Naples**](https://puredesignofnaples.com/what-clients-are-really-looking-for-in-interior-design-in-naples-fl-right-now/) this way, so bring the question to your first meeting.
  * Single room refresh. Ask for a set fee. Hourly billing on a small job turns into meter-watching for both sides.
  * Whole-home furnishing. Ask for a fixed design fee plus a disclosed markup. Skip a straight percentage. It rewards spending more of your money.
  * Gut renovation. A percentage works here. The studio manages trades and buying, so the fee tracks the job. Cap it at a stated dollar ceiling.
  * Condo remodel. Ask for one fee, with Gulf Shore Boulevard board coordination written into scope rather than billed later as extras.
  * New construction. Per square foot or a phased fee, tied to builder milestones across North Naples and Marco Island.
  * Remote or seasonal project. One fee, with a set number of video reviews. Uncapped hourly gets expensive when you approve choices from Michigan.


Scope drives the number more than square footage does. A 2,000 square foot condo with custom millwork costs more to design than a 4,000 square foot home furnished from stock.
## What does an interior design consultation in Naples, FL cost?
An interior design consultation in Naples, FL runs $150 to $500 for a paid in-home session. Many studios, Pure Design of Naples included, open with a free discovery call instead.
The difference matters. A free call sorts out fit, budget range, and timing. Paid sessions are billable work. You should leave holding a scope sketch, a budget band, and a proposed billing model.
Ask one question before you book. Does the studio credit that fee toward your project? Plenty of Naples firms do, and almost nobody thinks to ask.
One clause matters more than the pricing model itself. Ask how many revision rounds your fee covers. ## What your design fee doesn’t cover
![What your design fee doesn't cover](https://puredesignofnaples.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/What-your-design-fee-doesnt-cover-1024x683.png)
Your design fee pays for the thinking, not the buying. Pure Design of Naples and most interior design studios in Naples quote these items separately:
  * Furniture, fixtures, and equipment, which designers call FF&E
  * Markup or trade discount margin on purchases
  * Freight, receiving, and warehouse storage
  * White-glove delivery and installation
  * Construction-phase site visits and builder coordination
  * Revisions beyond the agreed count
  * Sales tax on furnishings


Two of these surprise people most. Receiving warehouses in Naples bill monthly for storage, and that clock starts the day your sofa lands. Builders also invoice their own coordination hours on top of your designer’s.
Get that column in writing before work starts. Budget shocks come from this list, rarely from the design fee itself.
## Is hiring an interior designer in Naples worth it?
Hiring an interior designer in Naples pays off on renovations, new construction, and whole-home furnishing. It rarely pays off on one small room you plan to select yourself.
Designers hold trade accounts you can’t open alone. That pricing offsets part of the fee. They also catch specification mistakes early. A wrong stone slab or an unrated outdoor fabric costs far more than a few design hours.
The [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design/interior-designers.htm) puts the median interior designer wage at $63,490. Studio billing rates run well above that number. Studios carry overhead, sourcing time, and project management.
