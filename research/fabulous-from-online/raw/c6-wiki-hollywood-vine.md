**Hollywood and Vine** , the intersection of [Hollywood Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard "Hollywood Boulevard") and [Vine Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_Street "Vine Street") in [Hollywood, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_California "Hollywood, California"), became known in the 1920s for its concentration of radio and movie-related businesses. The [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") is centered on the intersection.
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hollywood_and_Vine&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Hollywood_and_Vine_Aerial_1920.jpg/250px-Hollywood_and_Vine_Aerial_1920.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Hollywood_and_Vine_Aerial_1920.jpg)Aerial view of Hollywood and Vine, 1920
The area was a [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon "Lemon") grove until 1903, when [Daeida Beveridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daeida_Wilcox_Beveridge "Daeida Wilcox Beveridge") allowed one corner of the dirt intersection on her property to be used for the [Hollywood Memorial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Memorial_Church "Hollywood Memorial Church").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower_1850-1) The streets were renamed in 1910, when the city of Hollywood was annexed into Los Angeles.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-Hundred_Years-2)
Beginning in the 1920s, during the [Golden Age of Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Hollywood "Golden Age of Hollywood"), the area began to see an influx of money and influence as movie and music businesses moved to the district, turning the local farms and orchards into movie [backlots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backlot "Backlot"). Hollywood and Vine was the second busiest intersection in Los Angeles, after [Wilshire Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilshire_Boulevard "Wilshire Boulevard") and [Western Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Avenue_\(Los_Angeles\) "Western Avenue \(Los Angeles\)"). In the 1930s, radio station [KFWB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFWB "KFWB") spoke of "broadcasting live from Hollywood and Vine," and newspaper columnists [Hedda Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedda_Hopper "Hedda Hopper") and [Jimmie Fidler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Fidler "Jimmie Fidler") regularly touted the intersection's mystique.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-Pool-3)
In 1960, the intersection became the crossing point of the newly installed t-shaped [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame").[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-4) Later, when [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong "Neil Armstrong"), [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin "Buzz Aldrin") and [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Collins_\(astronaut\) "Michael Collins \(astronaut\)") were added to the Walk, their shared markers were placed at all four corners of Hollywood and Vine.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-LATApollo-5)
By the 1960s, many studios and broadcasters had moved onto more upscale areas, and this area fell into disrepair. Many offices and stores were abandoned, while the streets were occupied by [squatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting "Squatting") and [panhandlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begging "Begging"). It took several decades for redevelopment to take hold, and tourists were often surprised by the area's contrast with other tourist destinations.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
The Hollywood/Vine subway station opened in 1999[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-lametro-6) and led to more sustained redevelopment in the area. On May 29, 2003, Hollywood and Vine was named "Bob Hope Square" to commemorate [Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope "Bob Hope")'s 100th birthday.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-bbcdeath-7) A [pedestrian scramble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_scramble "Pedestrian scramble") was added to the intersection in 2018.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-8)
A number of high-profile projects have attempted to restore the lost luster of the area, most notably the $600 million [W Hollywood Hotel and Residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_Hotels "W Hotels"),[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-latimes_whotel-9) which opened in 2010.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-laist_whotel-10) Other large projects include a $50-million conversion of the [Equitable Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equitable_Building_of_Hollywood "Equitable Building of Hollywood")[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower1925_3-11) and $70-million conversion of the [Dyas Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyas_Building "Dyas Building") into condominiums.[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-BBGLC-12)
## Buildings
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hollywood_and_Vine&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Buildings")]
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Taft_Building_Los_Angeles.jpg/250px-Taft_Building_Los_Angeles.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Taft_Building_Los_Angeles.jpg)[Taft Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft_Building_\(Los_Angeles\) "Taft Building \(Los Angeles\)")
Hollywood and Vine's first major building, located on the intersection's southeast corner, was [Hollywood Memorial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Memorial_Church "Hollywood Memorial Church"), constructed in 1903. It was later torn down to make way for the intersection's first [high rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_rise "High rise"), the 12-story [Taft Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft_Building_\(Los_Angeles\) "Taft Building \(Los Angeles\)"), built in 1923. The Taft Building was built in the [Renaissance Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance "Neo-Renaissance") style for A.Z. Taft Jr. by [Walker & Eisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_&_Eisen "Walker & Eisen").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower_1850-1)[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) In Hollywood's golden age, every studio as well as [Charlie Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin "Charlie Chaplin"), [Will Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Rogers "Will Rogers"), and the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences "Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences") had offices in the building.[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-ampas-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower1925_6-15) To the south of the Taft Building is the [Spanish Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_Revival "Spanish Colonial Revival") [Herman Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Building "Herman Building") and south of that was the complementary-designed [Hollywood Brown Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Brown_Derby "Hollywood Brown Derby"),[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) which housed [Satyr Book Shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyr_Book_Shop "Satyr Book Shop")[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-16) and the namesake restaurant and was demolished in 1994.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-latimes_brownderby-17) To the east of the Taft Building was the [Châteauesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teauesque "Châteauesque") [Gilbert Books Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Books "Gilbert Books"), former home of Gilbert Books,[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) which was demolished to make way for the [W Hollywood Hotel and Residences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_Hotels "W Hotels").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Capitol_Records_sunset.jpg/250px-Capitol_Records_sunset.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Capitol_Records_sunset.jpg)[Capitol Records Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records_Building "Capitol Records Building")
On the northeast corner, the [Equitable Building of Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equitable_Building_of_Hollywood "Equitable Building of Hollywood") was designed by [Aleck Curlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleck_Curlett "Aleck Curlett"), built in 1929, and expanded in 1931.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) Originally home to numerous talent and advertising agencies,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-recrep-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-heritage_equitable-19) it was converted to a condominium in 2007.[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower1925_3-11) East of the Equitable Building is the [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco "Art Deco") [Hollywood Pantages Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Pantages_Theatre "Hollywood Pantages Theatre"), designed by [B. Marcus Priteca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._Marcus_Priteca "B. Marcus Priteca") and built as a [movie palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_palace "Movie palace") in 1930 before being converted to a [live theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_theater "Live theater") in 1977;[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13)[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower_pantages-20) the building has also housed [Frolic Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frolic_Room "Frolic Room") since 1934.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-Smithsonian_Magazine-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-American_Trails_Mag-22) North of the Equitable Building is the [Welton Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welton_Becket "Welton Becket") designed, [Googie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Googie_architecture "Googie architecture")-styled [Capitol Records Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records_Building "Capitol Records Building").[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-23) Built in 1956, the Capitol Records Building is the world's first circular office building[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-24) and was home to the [first record label on the west coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records "Capitol Records").[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-kcet.org-25)
On the northwest corner, the [Laemmle Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laemmle_Building "Laemmle Building") was built in 1932 by [Richard Neutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Neutra "Richard Neutra")[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) for [Carl Laemmle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Laemmle "Carl Laemmle"), head of [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures"). The original design was not built because of the [1929 stock market crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Crash_of_1929 "Wall Street Crash of 1929"), and the building was significantly altered many times. The building was gutted by fire in April 2008 and razed six months later.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower1925_4-26) To the west is the former site of [Sardi's Diner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardi's#Other_locations "Sardi's") and current home of Cave Theater. Further west is [Vine Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_Theatre "Vine Theatre").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) To the north of the former Laemmle Building was the site of [Hollywood Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Post_Office "Hollywood Post Office"), which was vacated in 1988 and demolished soon after.[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-27) North of that is the Spanish Colonial Revival [Avalon Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon_Hollywood "Avalon Hollywood"), which was designed by [Gogerty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._L._Gogerty "H. L. Gogerty") and [Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jules_Weyl "Carl Jules Weyl") and opened in 1927.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13)[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-28) Avalon Hollywood has been known by many times over its history.[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-29)
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/20161005_Broadway_Hollywood_Building_from_Hollywood_and_Vine_%282%29.jpg/250px-20161005_Broadway_Hollywood_Building_from_Hollywood_and_Vine_%282%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:20161005_Broadway_Hollywood_Building_from_Hollywood_and_Vine_\(2\).jpg)[Dyas Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Hollywood_Building "Broadway Hollywood Building") and the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame")
On the southwest corner, the [Dyas Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Hollywood_Building "Broadway Hollywood Building") was built in 1927 by [Frederick Rice Dorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Rice_Dorn "Frederick Rice Dorn").[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) From 1931 to 1982, the building housed [The Broadway-Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Broadway_\(Department_Stores\) "The Broadway \(Department Stores\)") [department store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_store "Department store"), and in 2007 the building converted to multi-family residential, ground-floor commercial.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-30) The Dyas Building has two annexes, one to the south of the building and the other to the west, that were built in the late 1930s.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-waterandpower1925_2-31) South of the building is the [Hollywood Plaza Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Plaza_Hotel "Hollywood Plaza Hotel"), built by [Walker & Eisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_&_Eisen "Walker & Eisen") in 1924[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13) and at one point home to [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") star [Clara Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Bow "Clara Bow")'s "It Cafe".[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-changing_landscape-32)
Six of the aforementioned buildings are listed as [Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Historic-Cultural_Monuments "Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monuments"):[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-LAHCM-33)
  * [Hollywood Pantages Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Pantages_Theatre "Hollywood Pantages Theatre") (No. 193)
  * [Broadway Hollywood Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Hollywood_Building "Broadway Hollywood Building") (No. 664)
  * [Hollywood Plaza Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Plaza_Hotel "Hollywood Plaza Hotel") (No. 665)
  * [Taft Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taft_Building_\(Los_Angeles\) "Taft Building \(Los Angeles\)") (No. 666)
  * [Capitol Records Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Records_Building "Capitol Records Building") (No. 857)
  * [Equitable Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equitable_Building_of_Hollywood "Equitable Building of Hollywood") (No. 1088)


Furthermore, nine of the aforementioned buildings are listed as [contributing properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contributing_properties "Contributing properties") in the [Hollywood Boulevard Commercial and Entertainment District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard_Commercial_and_Entertainment_District "Hollywood Boulevard Commercial and Entertainment District"):[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-NRHP-13)
  * Hollywood Pantages Theatre
  * Broadway Hollywood Building
  * Hollywood Plaza Hotel
  * Taft Building
  * Equitable Building
  * [Hollywood Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Playhouse "Hollywood Playhouse")
  * [Herman Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Building "Herman Building")
  * [Gilbert Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Books "Gilbert Books")
  * [Hollywood Brown Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Brown_Derby "Hollywood Brown Derby")


In [urban folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_folklore "Urban folklore"), many of these buildings are considered to be part of "Haunted Hollywood", home to the ghosts of celebrities (and less stellar residents) of Hollywood's legendary past.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_and_Vine#cite_note-Pool-3)
## In popular culture
