## TL;DR

1. **The nearest comparable is local.** Vestige Vintage, 318 Tamiami Trail, Punta Gorda, FL (next county north of Lee, `inferred` from the address), sells a "Private Sourcing" product: "A non-refundable sourcing retainer of $50 reserves your request and is applied toward your final purchase." First reply in "three to five business days." "all purchases are considered final." That is the local price floor, and it is a vintage-fashion shop, same as the wardrobe side of Holliewood & Vine. `verified`.
2. **The dominant model is a small upfront fee credited toward the piece.** Vestige $50, Mercer Vintage $100, Potomac Collective $150, Zhiva $300, Sourced by Scottie $50/$100/$200 tiers. A percent on top is rarer: Mercer 6.5% plus the deposit, BrandWhy 12% with a $25 minimum and $175 cap, charged "ONLY if you choose to purchase". `verified`.
3. **The only menu that prices hourly and project work, from a shop that also sells its own stock, is Rescued and Reimagined Relics (Maryland).** Free find-request list, then $75 per hour (2-hour minimum, $150 start), then a $250 project retainer that includes 3 hours, then custom quote. Its fee is explicitly "not deposits toward merchandise." Closest structural match to a Hollie room-project ladder. `verified`.
4. **No shop in the sample labels its own stock versus sourced-elsewhere at the item level.** Closest signals: R&R "expand the search beyond the pieces currently available in the R&R Relics collection"; Potomac fills its wish list "as we rehome heirloom pieces" (`inferred` own-stock matching). The real disclosure rules come from outside vintage: the Art Dealers Association of America code, the British Institute of Interior Design, and two Virginia designer-markup cases. The Holliewood & Vine disclosure line below is **new drafting**, not copied from a shop. `verified` for the gap in this sample; I did not search every shop.
5. **Hidden markup is where this gets litigated.** In two Virginia federal cases (as reported by law-firm blogs, opinions not read) designers marked up furniture 35% to 100% with no contract language allowing it, and courts let breach-of-contract and consumer-protection claims proceed. Lesson that transfers: the web page, the contract and the invoice must say the same thing. Never write "at cost" unless supplier invoices will be shown. `verified` (blogs) / court opinions `unverified`.
6. **Nothing-found policy splits 4 to 1.** Mercer refunds the deposit. Vestige, Potomac, Zhiva and R&R keep the fee (Potomac softens it with credit toward a later purchase). Sources disagree on this and on whether the fee is credited to the piece. See the "X verified, Y need review" lines in section "Where sources disagree."
7. **Trade programs give sourcing help free, as a perk.** Chairish: "As a Trade Program member, we'd be happy to help you source!" 1stDibs: "personalized support from our trade service advisers on sourcing." Neither charges a search fee, both reward volume, so a one-person shop cannot copy the economics, only the intake list. `verified`.
8. **Gaps I could not fill:** no published rush surcharge anywhere in the sample; no published prop-sourcing fee (prop houses publish rental terms by inquiry only); no eBay or Etsy seller with a verified sourcing offer; no confirmation that Etsy permits a sourcing-fee listing (Etsy policy page walled). **Florida flag:** a Florida tax-law blog says design/procurement fees bundled with goods can be taxable. Needs a Florida CPA before any fee goes live. See flagged section at the end.

## Disclosure and conflict of interest: what the rulebooks say (no vintage shop in the sample says it)

I looked for a vintage dealer who says, in so many words, "this one is from our shelf, that one we found elsewhere, and here is how we are paid on each." I did not find one. What exists is in neighboring professions, and it lines up.

1. **Art Dealers Association of America (ADAA) code of ethics**, https://artdealers.org/about/code-of-ethics-and-professional-practices (raw `c2-adaa.md`). `verified`:
   - IV.B "A Member who owns or guarantees a work consigned to auction, or has any other financial interest in the sale of the work, is expected to disclose that fact to potential bidders on that work before he or she offers any advice about the work."
   - V.D "In any transaction in which another art dealer, advisor or other agent is acting for the counterparty to the transaction, a Member is expected to assure that it is clear who is being represented by the Member and who is being represented by the other art professional."
   - V.E "A Member does not seek or accept compensation from any party where such compensation would create a conflict with the Member's responsibility to its principal in the transaction, unless such compensation has been disclosed to and approved by the Member's principal."
   - Reading: disclose a financial interest before giving advice; be clear whose side you are on; any second paycheck gets disclosed and approved. Direct template for Hollie when offering own stock. Art-dealer code, not law.
2. **British Institute of Interior Design (BIID)**, https://biid.org.uk/conflicts-interest (`c2-biid.md`). `verified`: members "are required not to accept any job or take on a client where doing so would represent a conflict of interest"; a designer invoicing a supplier for a "handling fee" "that they have not informed their clients about" is listed as a conflict; recommends "a procedure ... to identify conflicts of interest, or perceived conflicts of interest, that includes steps on how these conflicts should be eliminated or declared." UK professional body, not US law.
3. **Design Ink Co, "Interior Design Markups: What You Must Disclose"**, https://www.designinkco.com/blog/interior-design-markup-disclosure (`c2-designink.md`). `verified` (blog, coach not lawyer, and it says to get a lawyer). It sets four models (pure cost-plus, cost-plus with disclosed markup, retail/resale, fee-only) and a defensible clause: "Client will be charged the net cost plus a procurement fee of X% on approved furnishings. Trade discounts, if any, are retained by the designer as part of studio compensation" is defensible. "Industry-standard markup" is not. Also: "No written approval, no order." and "Flag pricing changes before you invoice." And: "The legal exposure isn't tied to which model you choose. It's tied to whether your contract says one thing and your invoices show another."
4. **Berlik Law, "Interior Designers' Unauthorized Markups May Be Grounds for Legal Action"**, https://www.virginiabusinesslitigationlawyer.com/interior-designers-unauthorized-markups-may-be-grounds-for-legal-action/ (`c2-marcus.md`, July 8, 2022). `verified` that the page says: in *Johnson v. Robert Shields Interiors* "Shields was secretly marking up most of the furniture sourced for Johnson by anywhere from 35 to 100 percent" ($4,800 charged for a chaise that cost $2,481); in *Marcus v. Dennis* a table that "actually cost $2878.80" was charged at $4,750 and art bought for $14,000 at $18,000; "If an interior designer's contract is silent as to any ability to mark up a vendor's charges, it's implied that the designer cannot do this." Both were Virginia federal courts and Virginia Consumer Protection Act claims. The opinions themselves I did not read, so the holdings are `unverified`; a Florida court is not bound by them (`inferred`), and this is a law-firm blog.
5. **ID Law Shop, "Why Interior Designers Should Disclose Their Commissions"**, https://www.idlawshop.com/interior-design-law-blog/why-interior-designers-should-disclose-commissions-a-legal-perspective (`c2-idlaw.md`). `verified` for the quote: "the more comprehensive a designer's disclosures are regarding any markup, rebate, or commission arrangements with trade partners, the less likely claims of misrepresentation or deception will arise or gain credibility." Template seller; not legal advice.
6. **Interior Design Community, "Procurement Fees: Hourly vs Markup"**, https://interiordesigncommunity.com/procurement-fees-hourly-markup/ (`c2-procfees.md`, April 23, 2026). `verified`: three working models (one hourly rate; tiered hourly; hourly through proposal then markup on goods) and the instruction "Whatever you choose, make it legible." Also ready scripts, e.g. "We bill hourly through selections and proposal approval. After that, our markup on goods and services covers the ongoing ordering, tracking, and installation coordination work."

**Steal this:** the structure of the disclosure, not the words: (1) say you also sell, up front (ADAA IV.B, "before ... advice"); (2) label each option by whose shelf it came from (ADAA V.D); (3) show every charge as its own line and never earn a second payment you did not disclose (ADAA V.E, BIID); (4) the page, the agreement and the invoice must match (Design Ink, Berlik).

## Recommendation for Holliewood & Vine

**Constraint that decides everything:** Hollie is one person, also sells their own stock, has no public showroom, and the cleanest legal and trust position is that the web page, the agreement and the invoice say the same thing about money.

### Fee model: three lanes, one rule

| Lane | Price (proposed, Ricky sets the numbers) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Wish list** | Free. Email list. "No fee and no guarantee." | Potomac and R&R both do this. It feeds the newsletter and the shop's buying. |
| **Find it** (one piece or a few) | **$75 non-refundable search fee, credited in full toward any piece Hollie sources.** Sourced pieces also carry a **10% sourcing line**, shown separately. | $75 sits between the local floor (Vestige $50) and Mercer ($100). 10% sits inside the verified 6.5%-12% range. Credit-to-piece is stated by all five flat-fee or deposit shops that say either way (Vestige, Mercer, Potomac, Zhiva, Coffee and Clothing). |
| **Room / project** (corner, section, whole room, studio, house) | **$250 project retainer that includes the first 3 hours**, extra time only with written approval. Whole-house or multi-room: custom quote. Rate per extra hour: Hollie's call (R&R is $75). Retainer is **not** credited, because hourly time is the product. | Directly modeled on R&R's menu. Matches the virtual meeting, photos and email intake. |

**The one rule that keeps the shop's own stock clean:** A sourcing percent only makes sense on a piece Hollie finds elsewhere, because there is an outside purchase price to build on. A piece from the Holliewood & Vine collection is sold at its **listed shop price, with no sourcing line and no search fee credited against it twice**. Never describe anything as "at cost" or "cost-plus" unless Hollie will hand over the supplier invoice (the lesson of *Johnson* and *Marcus*, if the blog accounts are right).

**Upgrades.**
- *In person:* the only price data is BrandWhy at $175 virtual and $350 in person, exactly 2x. Reasonable opening: price in-person as a separate line (visit fee plus travel zone for Lee and Collier), not folded into the retainer. Hollie goes to the client, so state the visit fee and the mileage rule on the page. Numbers are Ricky's call. `inferred` from one data point.
- *Rush ("need it done now"):* **no source in the sample publishes a rush surcharge.** The only mechanism is Coffee and Clothing's deposit that "jumps to the front of the line." Recommended: a flat rush fee added to any lane, plus Zhiva's "Deadline (if any)" field on the form so a deadline is on record. Untested pricing; treat it as a first guess to adjust after the first three jobs.
- *Parties and events:* consider renting Hollie's own stock (silla and K&M both rent and publish no rates). That is a different product from sourcing; do not fold it into this section without a decision.

### Approval flow (one page, used for all lanes)

1. **Request.** Form fields from Chairish's intake list plus Hollie's needs: what, style, budget (per piece and project total), timeline and key dates, dimensions, colors/materials, ZIP code, photos or a screen-share video call of the space. (Chairish, Vestige, Mercer, BrandWhy all ask roughly this.)
2. **Accept or decline before payment.** "If accepted, you'll receive an email and payment link." (Potomac.) "Not every request is accepted." If budget or timeline do not line up, say so now. (Scottie.)
3. **Pay the search fee or retainer.** Sold as a normal product on Shopify. (Mercer, Vestige, Scottie do this.)
4. **Options in 7 to 10 business days.** 2 to 3 options per request. (Potomac 7-10; Mercer 7-14.) Each is labeled **"From the Holliewood & Vine collection"** or **"Sourced for you,"** with photos, measurements, condition and the all-in price, sourcing line included.
5. **Client approves in writing**, by reply email naming the piece and the price. "No written approval, no order." (Design Ink.)
6. **Client pays the merchandise before Hollie buys a sourced piece.** (R&R.) Own-stock pieces: pay and hold, 48-hour hold on a matched piece. (Potomac.)
7. **Inspect, then ship or deliver.** Sourced pieces come to Hollie first for a condition check and photos before they go out. (R&R.) Shipping and freight billed separately, quoted before payment. (R&R, 1stDibs.) Only say "at actual carrier cost" if receipts will be shown.
8. **Returns.** Not stated by most shops. Vestige: all final. Chairish trade: 14-day extended window. Scottie: 7 days store credit on listed items. Recommendation, `inferred` and a policy call for Ricky: sourced pieces are final once approved against the written condition photos, with a carve-out for pieces that arrive different from what was shown or damaged in transit. Own-stock pieces follow the shop's normal policy.

### When nothing is found

Use R&R's wording as the base, add Potomac's soft landing, and send a real report so the client feels the time was spent:

> "Hollie searched and came up empty on this one. The search fee covers the time, so it is not refunded, but the full amount is yours to spend on anything in the Holliewood & Vine collection for the next 90 days. Here is what Hollie looked at, plus two close cousins from the collection (labeled as such). The wish list stays open if you want Hollie to keep watching."

Why not Mercer-style refunds: Mercer is the only refund in the sample, it is a reseller with scale and authentication overhead, and a one-person shop spends the hours either way. Four of the five shops that state a no-find rule keep the fee. If Ricky prefers the trust signal of a refund, price the search fee low enough to refund without pain; this is a values call.

### Disclosure line for offering its own stock (new drafting; no shop in the sample does this)

For the "Fabulous From Online" page, directly above the form:

> **Yes, Hollie sells things too. We say so.** Some of what you are shown will come from the Holliewood & Vine collection. Some will be found elsewhere. Every option is labeled **From the collection** or **Sourced for you**, so you always know whose shelf it came from. Collection pieces are the listed shop price. Sourced pieces carry a **10% sourcing line**, printed on its own row, and you see the full price before anything is bought. No mystery math.

Matching invoice template: item, "From the collection" or "Sourced for you", item price, sourcing line (sourced only), shipping, tax, total. If an item's price changes after approval, tell the client before invoicing. (Design Ink.) The line builds on ADAA IV.B (disclose a financial interest before advising), V.D (be clear whose side you are on) and V.E (no second payment undisclosed).

Pronoun check: the copy above uses "Hollie" and no pronoun; keep it that way.
