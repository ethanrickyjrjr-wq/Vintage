# b3: Buy-outright and instant-offer channels (vintage / secondhand)

Researcher: b3 (Group B, consignment and buying). Date accessed for every page below: **2026-10-27**.
Raw crawls: `research/consignment/raw/b3-*.md` (one file per page; filenames cited inline).
Method: every figure below is quoted from a page fetched in this session. Anything I could not see on a live vendor page is marked `UNVERIFIED`. Third-party review sites (ResaleSonar, LuxuryCatalog, Reddit, blogs) showed up in search results and were deliberately not used as evidence.

## TL;DR

1. Only **three** buyers publish a percentage of resale value on a live page: Buffalo Exchange (25% cash / 50% credit), Crossroads (25/35/40% cash by price band, 45% trade) and 2nd Quest (consign at 70%). WGACA, Rebag and Fashionphile publish **no share**; their "+20%" / "+10%" figures are credit bonuses on top of an offer, not a share of resale. Treat any "we pay X% of resale" claim for them as `UNVERIFIED`.
2. Every serious buyer protects its own cash the same four ways: **pay after inspection**, **revise the offer after inspection**, **pay more if you take store credit instead of cash**, and **buy only what current inventory needs**. None of them pay on photos alone.
3. Headline finding: **Rebag markets Buyout as "get paid right away... the quickest... way to sell" while its help center says 2-3 weeks processing plus ~40 business days to payout** (changed for orders accepted on/after Oct 8, 2026). Even a large, software-driven buyer is stretching payout (its funding is not something I verified). Cautionary flow, not a model.
4. Best templates for a one-person vintage shop are **WGACA** (photos -> estimate -> prepaid label -> inspect -> counteroffer -> free return if you decline), **Crossroads Sell by Mail** (published payout bands, "donate or return" choice with a card hold) and **Vintage Vixen** (public want list, photo form, seller pays shipping, payment after inspection).
5. **Legal flag that could change the plan:** if the shop operates in Florida (UNVERIFIED, I do not know where it is licensed), Florida Chapter 538 treats anyone who "purchas[es], consign[s], or trad[es]" secondhand goods as a registered "secondhand dealer": DOR registration, seller ID + thumbprint + photos reported to the sheriff within 24 hours, and a 15- or 30-day hold before you can sell. Clothing and costume jewelry are carved out; lamps, art, antiques, decorative objects and real jewelry are not. See the legal section. Verify before taking in one dollar of cash buying.

## 1. Buffalo Exchange (in-person buyer, clothing + vintage)

- Homepage: https://buffaloexchange.com/
- Page read: https://buffaloexchange.com/how-to-sell/ (raw: `b3-buffalo-how-to-sell.md`)
- Still does this? Yes, live, with a current promo ("Now through October 31st").

**How a quote works:** In person only, no photos. FAQ: *"We have to see all items in person before we can say whether or not we’d be able to buy them."* Walk-in: *"We buy from open to close every day, no appointment required!"* with a digital waitlist: *"we’ll text you + call your name when it’s your turn to sell."*

**Payout share (stated):** *"On the day you sell, you’ll walk away with 25% of our selling price in cash or 50% in store credit for anything we’re able to buy."* Payout is same day: *"your buyer will tell you your cash and trade options... You can take any combination of cash and trade."* Trade card: *"it never expires!"*

**Refuses / selects:** *"Our buyers make selections based on the style and condition as well as our current inventory needs."* *"Every item we buy heads straight out onto the rack, so we look for clean clothing in good condition."* Vintage is on the buy list ("Designer, Vintage, Jewelry...").

**ID:** *"You’ll need a valid, government issued ID like a Driver’s License or Passport."*

**Steal this:** the cash-vs-credit split (credit pays 2x) and "selection by current inventory needs" as the honest reason for a pass. **Watch out:** this only works with a physical counter and volume. Not transferable to an online one-of-one shop.

## 2. Crossroads Trading (in store, drop-off, mail-in, consignment)

- Homepage: https://crossroadstrading.com/
- Pages read: https://crossroadstrading.com/buy-sell-trade-2-2/ , https://crossroadstrading.com/sell-by-mail/ , https://crossroadstrading.com/sell-by-mail-terms/ (raw: `b3-crossroads-buy-sell-trade.md`, `b3-crossroads-sbm.md`, `b3-crossroads-sbm-terms.md`)
- Still does this? Yes. Five channels: In Store, Drop-Off, Consignment, Mail, Concierge.

**Quote method:** In store: *"We evaluate and price clothing on the spot, so you can receive cash or trade right away."* Mail: you request a bag, fill it, ship it; no pre-quote. Concierge is *"by application only for sellers with qualifying premium and designer collections."*

**Payout share (stated, the most transparent of anyone I read):**
- In store page: *"We pay 25-40% based on what we price your items to sell for in the store"* cash; *"We pay 45% in trade."*
- Sell-by-mail terms, item 1, exact bands: *"For items priced up to $35.00, the cash payout is 25%. For items priced between $36-$84.99, the cash payout is 35%. For items priced over $85, the cash payout is 40%. We pay 45% in trade for all items, regardless of price."*
- Consignment (designer only): *"payouts up to 70% in cash."* (The payout image alt text says "For payout amounts, contact store." so the 70% is a ceiling, not a rate.)

**Speed and method:** In store, on the spot, balance goes to a digital account and *"you can take some or all of your account balance at any time."* Drop-off: *"return for a cash or trade payout after 48-72 hours (varies by store)."* Mail: *"Current processing time is 4 weeks"*; cash is *"mobile cash payment via Zelle"*; trade credit lands in a digital account (*"We no longer mail physical trade cards"*). Note the site pop-up promises *"quick processing"* while the same page says 4 weeks: marketing vs terms.

**Shipping:** Mail-in is a free prepaid UPS bag (*"pre-paid UPS bag"*). Terms: seller must use their bag; other packaging means seller *"will be held liable for shipping costs."*

**Revisions:** There is no counteroffer. Terms item 6: *"All items purchased will be priced immediately for sale without seller approval and will become the property of Crossroads Trading."* You find out what they took after the fact.

**If you decline / they pass:** Bag request form forces a choice up front: *"we'll either donate all unpurchased items to charity or return them to you for the cost of shipping ($32.99 per bag)."* They hold your card: *"We will place a temporary hold of $32.99 per requested bag on your card. If we return your bag, we'll charge the card at that time."* Terms item 7: *"No tax receipt will be provided"* on donation. Drop-off: *"In some stores, sellers must return to collect their unpurchased items within 3 days or choose to have them donated."*

**Refuses:** *"We can only accept clean, unstained, odor-free items in excellent condition."* *"If it’s missing a button, has a tiny tear, or the zipper’s broken, we can’t buy it."* Mail: *"Kids clothing or items from Old Navy, Forever 21, Target or Walmart."* Terms item 8: *"Crossroads only accepts authentic designer items."*

**Resellers:** Terms item 9: *"We reserve the right to limit payout to cash for professional sellers"* (people who buy and sell for profit). They block resellers from arbitraging credit.

**ID:** *"Must be 18 (or accompanied by an adult) & present a valid photo ID to sell."* The link behind that line points to a sentence in Crossroads' privacy policy saying they must keep a copy of the ID *"to comply with local and state regulations governing resale businesses"*; I did NOT fetch the privacy policy itself, so that wording is a pointer from the URL fragment, not a verified quote.

**Steal this:** published bands, the donate-or-return choice with a card hold (nobody has to chase you for return postage), and a pro-reseller clause. **Watch out:** 4 weeks of processing is how they protect their own queue; a one-person shop cannot match that and should not promise a faster payout than it can deliver.

## 3. What Goes Around Comes Around (WGACA), luxury accessories by email

- Homepage: https://www.whatgoesaroundnyc.com/
- Page read: https://www.whatgoesaroundnyc.com/en-us/sell-to-us.html (raw: `b3-wgaca-sell-to-us.md`)
- Still does this? Yes. Narrow brand list only (Chanel, Hermès, Gucci, Louis Vuitton, Dior, Fendi, Prada, YSL). It is a clean template even though the goods are not vintage-shop goods.

**Quote method:** Email only. *"To submit items for sale and receive a quote... please contact us at luxbuyers@wgacany.com, and you will receive a reply within 7-10 business days. Please note that our retail teams cannot offer this service, and it can only be done through e-mail."* Estimate comes after photos upload.

**Payout share:** `UNVERIFIED`. Pricing statement is qualitative: *"based on its current market value, which can vary by style, size, colorway, and, of course, condition. Other factors such as client demand, rarity, accessibility, and our retail price are also considered."* They add: *"we do not practice price-matching, we are open to hearing your price expectations and, in some cases, are open to negotiations."*

**Speed and method:** *"The selling process takes about 3 weeks total from acceptance to pay-out."* *"Store credit is processed the same day... Check and Direct Deposit payments... the payout is mailed or deposited within 10 business days."* Credit bonus: *"a store credit value that’s 20% greater than the cash estimate."* (A bonus over their cash offer, not a share of resale.) They buy outright only: *"we do not offer consignment."*

**Shipping:** *"you will be issued a prepaid FedEx shipping label via email... We do not provide shipping materials to sellers."*

**Revisions:** Yes. After QA/QC *"our team will then either confirm the estimated quote or propose a counteroffer in the case that the item(s) are in a different condition than the original photos indicated."*

**If you decline / they pass (three separate cases, all stated on the page):**
- They pass on condition or authenticity: *"update you via e-mail and ship the item(s) back to you immediately."*
- They cannot authenticate: *"you will need to provide us with a return label."*
- You reject the revised offer: *"we will ship the item(s) back to you immediately at no expense to you."*

**Refuses:** *"We do not accept footwear, items outside our list of accepted brands, or any items that have been damaged, altered, or customized."* No limit on count: *"There is no limit to how many items a seller can submit."*

**Steal this:** the five-stage structure and the explicit three-case decline policy. **Watch out:** WGACA absorbs return postage on declined revisions. A one-person shop absorbing return postage on glass lamps is how margin disappears. Decide that policy deliberately.

## 5. Rebag (instant quote app + buyout) - the cautionary one

- Homepage: https://www.rebag.com/
- Pages read: https://www.rebag.com/selling-with-rebag/ , https://www.rebag.com/buyout/ (both mostly JS-rendered, thin text; raw: `b3-rebag-selling.md`, `b3-rebag-buyout.md`) and the help center articles `b3-rebag-help-paid.md`, `b3-rebag-intercom-paid.md`, `b3-rebag-offer-changes.md`, `b3-rebag-offer-changes-old.md`, `b3-rebag-not-approved.md`, `b3-rebag-dont-like-offer.md`, `b3-rebag-offers-expire.md`, `b3-rebag-offer-values.md`, `b3-rebag-send-item.md`, `b3-rebag-clair.md`. Base: https://support.rebag.com/hc/en-us/categories/360002365211-Selling-to-Rebag
- Still does this? Yes. Three methods: *"Consign, Trade, and Buyout."*

**Quote method (the "instant quote"):** Clair: *"1) Find your bag - select your bag's brand and model... 2) Tell its history... 3) Reveal its value - Generate its current resale value in an instant. If we can’t provide you an instant offer, you can submit images of your item and our team will review and provide an offer it in 3-4 business days."* Offers expire: *"our offers expire after two weeks."* Offer drivers: *"brand, condition size, color, material, resale price, popularity, our own inventory and how well it sells on the resale market."* No share published (`UNVERIFIED`). Membership: *"Rebag⁺ members get up to 5% higher payouts on every sale."*

**The marketing vs the help center (headline finding):**
- Buyout page: *"When you prefer to get paid for your luxury item right away, turn to Buyout: the quickest, most straightforward way to sell."*
- Help center, "How and when do I get paid?": *"Processing is estimated to take approximately two to three weeks after we receive your item. Your payout is then added to your Rebag account within approximately 40 business days after Processing is complete."*
- Intercom copy of the same article: *"Buyout orders accepted before October 8, 2026 are not affected by this change."* So this is a policy change made about three weeks before today.
- By my arithmetic from their own two numbers: roughly 2-3 weeks + 8 weeks = **10 to 11 weeks from delivery to money in the account**, not "right away."
- Bank transfer after that: *"approximately five to seven business days"*, and *"Balances transferred within 15 calendar days of being added to your account are subject to a 3% Early Transfer Fee."* Leaving the balance in Rebag earns *"1% per month for up to 9 months"* bonus. Using it to shop earns *"a 10% Spending Bonus."* Every design choice nudges you to leave the money in the shop.
- Consignment for comparison: pending up to 22 business days then payout in ~15 business days.

**Shipping:** *"After accepting your offer, kindly ship your items within 14 days... prepaid FedEx shipping label that was emailed to you."* Drop-off only at staffed FedEx Ship Centers.

**Revisions:** Current article: *"After we receive and inspect your item, we may adjust your offer... you’ll have 7 business days to accept or decline the revised offer. If no action is taken within that time, the revised offer will be automatically accepted."* (Silence = yes. Worth noting.)

**If you decline / they pass:**
- Refused bag: *"returned to the address on file within 7 business days via FedEx... there is a flat $10 fee for shipping back a refused bag."*
- Declining before shipping: *"there is no obligation to accept our offer. You may decline your quote through your account."*
- **Discrepancy: old article verified, new article needs review.** The older article (same help center, ID 360037522752, "Does the offer ever change...") says: *"If you do not agree with the revised offer, we will send the handbag back to you at no cost."* The new article (49826222636045, "5 days ago") says you have 7 business days then it auto-accepts, and says nothing about return cost. Both were live today. Whether declining a revised offer is still free: `UNVERIFIED`.

**Steal this:** two things only. (1) Offers that **expire** in two weeks so you are not holding a price on a market that moves. (2) Telling sellers exactly which accessories are in the quote. **Watch out:** everything else here is the failure mode. The gap between the marketing line and the help-center line is the thing to avoid on a small shop's own page: promise only the timing you can hit.

## Quote-to-payout flows: the five best templates

### A. WGACA (cleanest staged flow, photo-based, low risk to seller)
1. Email photos + item details to the buying desk. Reply in 7-10 business days with an **estimate**.
2. Seller accepts estimate -> **prepaid FedEx label** by email. Seller packs with their own materials.
3. Package arrives -> "allow 1-3 days" for QA/QC (authenticity + condition).
4. They **confirm or counteroffer** if condition differs from the photos.
5. Seller agrees -> picks payout: credit (same day, +20% over cash), or check / direct deposit (within 10 business days). About 3 weeks total.
6. Seller declines the revised offer -> returned **free**. They pass -> returned. Can't authenticate -> seller supplies return label.

### B. Crossroads Sell by Mail (clearest published economics)
1. Request a bag. Choose cash (Zelle) or trade; choose Donate or Return up front; card held for $32.99 per bag only if you pick Return and it is used.
2. Seller fills the free prepaid UPS bag and drops at UPS.
3. 4 weeks processing. Items are priced for the shelf; **no seller approval**.
4. Payout = percent of their shelf price: 25% (<= $35), 35% ($36-84.99), 40% (> $85), or 45% as trade.
5. Unpurchased items donated (no tax receipt) or returned for $32.99 per bag (refund the difference if postage was cheaper).

### C. Fashionphile (fast payout once authenticated, credit bonus)

## The legal gate: Florida Chapter 538 (if the shop operates in Florida)

I do not know where Holliewood and Vine is licensed. Ricky's other projects are Southwest Florida, so I checked Florida. **If the shop is not in Florida this section does not apply; the equivalent law in the shop's state is `UNVERIFIED` and not researched.**

Source: Florida Statutes 2026, Chapter 538, https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/Chapter538/All (raw `b3-fl-ch538-2026.md`), and Florida DOR: https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/secondhand_dealers_recyclers.aspx (`b3-fl-dor-secondhand.md`).

- **Who is covered.** 538.03(1)(h): a "secondhand dealer" is anyone *"engaged in the business of purchasing, consigning, or trading secondhand goods."* 538.03(1)(a): *"'Acquire' means to obtain by purchase, consignment, or trade."* So **consignment is regulated the same as buying**. This is a cross-slice finding for the consignment researchers.
- **What is NOT covered.** 538.03(1)(i): *"The term does not include office furniture; pianos; books; clothing; organs; coins; motor vehicles; costume jewelry..."* So clothing and costume jewelry are carved out. Lamps, art, glassware, decorative objects, real jewelry, memorabilia are not on that list (whether Playboy magazines count as "books": `UNVERIFIED`).
- **DOR's plain-language version:** *"Most businesses that sell secondhand goods (for example: household furniture and jewelry) must register as secondhand dealers."* And for the mail-in model: *"Businesses that operate in Florida and contract with persons or entities to buy precious metals or jewelry through a website, the United States mail, or telemarketing must register as mail-in secondhand precious metals dealers."*
- **Registration.** 538.09(1): *"A secondhand dealer shall not engage in the business of purchasing, consigning, or trading secondhand goods from any location without registering with the Department of Revenue."* Fee: *"$6 per location... annual renewal fee of $6"*; fingerprints and photo ID required; applicant *"must be a natural person who has reached the age of 18."* DOR requires a sales-tax certificate first and Form DR-1S; DOR notes *"The registration fee is $6 for each location and for each license type per location."*
- **Record-keeping on every purchase (538.04).** A transaction form at the time of the transaction; *"within 24 hours after acquiring any secondhand goods, deliver to such official [sheriff / police] a record of the transaction"*; the record must include item description and **digital photographs of the goods**, the seller's full name, address, phone numbers, physical description, and **right thumbprint**; government photo ID verified; the seller signs *"a statement verifying that the seller is the rightful owner."* Keep forms at least 3 years.
- **Holding period (538.06).** *"A secondhand dealer may not sell, barter, exchange, alter, adulterate, use, or in any way dispose of any secondhand good... That is a precious metal, a gemstone, or jewelry; an antique furnishing, fixture, or decorative object; or an item of art... within 30 calendar days after the date on which the good is acquired"*; anything else, **15 calendar days**. "Antique" means *"at least 30 years old and has special value because of its age."* **This is a cash-flow cost nobody advertises: money out, goods frozen for 15-30 days, and the statute says you cannot "alter" them either (so no restoration during the hold; my reading, not legal advice).**
- **Exemptions that might matter (538.03(2)):** (g) *"Any person at antique, coin, or collectible shows or sales"*; (j) *"Any person accepting a secondhand good as a trade-in for a similar item of greater value"*; (l) a registered antique dealer buying from the owner at the owner's residence under a written agreement; (n) internet-only sales *"when that person or entity does not have, and is not required to have, a local occupational or business license."* Whether the shop qualifies for (n) or (l) is a legal-interpretation question I cannot settle: **ask the Florida DOR (850-717-6205 is the number DOR lists for law enforcement; use the DOR contact page for businesses) or a lawyer.**
- **Penalty.** 538.07: misdemeanor of the first degree with *"a fine not to exceed $10,000."* 538.09(4): civil fine up to $10,000 per violation.

Bottom line: buying, consigning and trading are all in. **A "We buy" page is not a casual marketing decision in Florida; it is a regulated activity.** Do the legal check first.

## Blunt read: cash-flow risk of buying outright for a one-person shop

(Analysis and opinion; numbers marked illustrative are not vendor facts. I did not fetch Etsy or eBay fee schedules this session, so I leave marketplace fees out rather than guess.)

1. **Buying outright converts a no-cash business into a cash-out business.** Right now Ricky's capital goes into stock he already has. A buy-out pays first and sells later. Every one of the big buyers builds its page to defer or shrink that cash-out: pay after inspection (WGACA, Fashionphile, Rebag), revise after inspection (WGACA, Rebag), pay more in credit than cash (Buffalo 2x, WGACA +20%, Fashionphile +10%, Rebag bonuses), and buy only what inventory needs (Buffalo, Vintage Vixen's Unwanted list). If those four are not in the plan, the plan is a loan to strangers.
2. **One-of-one means no turns.** Buffalo and Crossroads can absorb a miss because they sell hundreds of pieces a day and mark down. A qty-1 lamp is a single bet; there is no re-order and no averaging. *Illustrative:* pay $90 for a lamp you expect to move at $250; if it sits four months or turns out to be a reproduction, that $90 is dead capital with no sibling SKU to offset it.
3. **Even Rebag, a large software-driven operation, is stretching payout.** 2-3 weeks processing plus ~40 business days, a 3% early-transfer fee, and bonuses that reward leaving money in the shop. If a company at that scale needs ~10 weeks to cover its buyout float (my inference from the dates; Rebag does not say why), a one-person shop's float is not going to be better.
4. **Store credit is the cheap lever, but a one-of-one shop may have nothing to spend it on.** Credit costs no cash today and is redeemed against future stock, which is why everyone pays 1.2x-2x in credit. That only works if there is enough catalog depth that a seller wants to spend it. For a shop going live with a first inventory, credit is a promise you may not be able to keep.
5. **Inspection and returns are the hidden margin eaters.** Crossroads solves it with a card hold and a flat fee; Rebag charges $10 per refused bag; WGACA eats it. For heavy or fragile vintage (glass, lamps, framed art), return freight can exceed the item's margin. Make the decline policy a decision, not an accident.
6. **Compliance cost shows up before the first purchase** (in Florida, see above): registration, thumbprints, 24-hour reports, and a 15-30 day freeze. Multiply that by every item.
7. **Authenticity and provenance risk concentrates in your exact categories.** Signed items, memorabilia and ephemera are where fakes and stolen goods live. Crossroads' only-authentic-designer line and WGACA's "we cannot accept any item that we are not confident is authentic" are cheap for them because they have authenticators in-house. Ricky is the authenticator.
8. **Time is the real currency.** Quotes, inspection, packing, payouts and disputes are all one person's evenings. Vintage Vixen fixes payout day to Friday for exactly this reason. A "We buy" funnel with no cap becomes an unpaid second job.

**Opinion, ranked, for a one-person shop with qty-1 stock:**
- **Do it small and capped.** A "Show us what you have" inquiry form (photos, asking price, flaws, zip code) that routes to *buy a few specific pieces outright with a hard monthly budget*, *consign the rest*, or *pass*. Pay by Zelle / PayPal / check **after** inspection, never on photos alone.
- **Never promise a percentage.** Describe the method (Rebag / WGACA do), publish bands only if you are ready to honor them forever (Crossroads).
- **Seller pays inbound shipping on small items; local pickup with cash on the spot for big pieces** (cheapest model I found, and it is exactly what Antique Buyers Fort Myers and Furnish Me do), but only after the legal check.
- **Skip store-credit bonuses at launch.** Add them when the catalog is deep enough that credit is worth something.
- **Never auto-accept on silence.** Rebag's pattern is the opposite of trust-building.
- **A buy-outright "sell to us for cash" button is a gimmick if it is the main promise; it is a good second door if consignment is first.** The 2nd Quest "we buy the bulk outright and consign the standouts" framing is the right way to present it.
