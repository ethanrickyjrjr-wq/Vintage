##  Shipping After Kidizen: Labels, Costs, and Best Practices
Shipping can make or break your reselling profits. Since Kidizen is not a live 2026 selling channel, choose the shipping workflow based on the platform you move inventory to.
###  Platform Shipping Choices
Most kids’ clothing is lightweight, but bundles, boots, coats, toys, and baby gear can cross weight or dimensional thresholds quickly.
**Common 2026 shipping paths:**
  * **Poshmark:** Simple buyer-facing shipping, useful for bundles, but check current weight/label rules before bundling heavy coats or shoes.
  * **eBay:** More control over service, package dimensions, buyer-paid shipping, and calculated shipping.
  * **Mercari:** Easy listing flow, but verify current prepaid label rules, dimensional limits, and overage policies.
  * **Local kids resale stores / consignment:** No shipping, but lower pricing control and stricter acceptance standards.
  * **Direct/local sales:** No platform label, so meet safely and avoid payment methods that remove seller protection.


Use the [Shipping Box Size Calculator](https://www.underpriced.app/tools/shipping-box-size-calculator) and [DIM Weight Calculator](https://www.underpriced.app/tools/dimensional-weight-calculator) before listing bulky kids’ boots, winter coats, toy bundles, diaper bags, or baby gear.
###  Who Pays for Shipping?
On any live platform, you usually choose among these approaches:
  1. **Buyer Pays Shipping:** The safest beginner approach when weight or dimensions are uncertain.
  2. **Free Shipping (Seller Absorbed):** You build shipping costs into your price. This can increase conversions but requires careful pricing.
  3. **Bundle Shipping:** One buyer, one package, multiple items. This is usually the best fit for kids’ clothing lots.


**Pro Tip:** If you offer free shipping, make sure to factor the actual label cost into your pricing. A $20 item with free shipping can net less than a $15 item where the buyer pays shipping.
###  Shipping Best Practices
**1. Weigh Items Accurately** Invest in a small postal scale (available for under $15). Underestimating weight leads to shipping overages that come out of your pocket.
**2. Use Poly Mailers for Most Items** Kids’ clothing is typically lightweight and flexible. A poly mailer keeps items protected while minimizing weight and dimensional size.
**3. Ship Quickly** Parents shopping kids’ resale value fast shipping. Aim to ship within 1-2 business days on any live marketplace to maintain good ratings and encourage repeat buyers.
**4. Package Thoughtfully** A little tissue paper or a thank-you note goes a long way in kids’ resale. Parents appreciate sellers who take care with purchases for their children.
The same buyer psychology still applies outside Kidizen. Parents care that children’s clothing arrives clean, folded, accurately sized, and honestly described.

##  Winning Strategies for Kids-Clothing Resale in 2026
###  1. Master the Art of Bundling
Parents shopping for kids love bundles. A curated lot of items in their child’s size saves time and shipping costs.
**Effective Bundle Types:**
  * **Size bundles:** “12-month Summer Wardrobe” or “Size 4T Winter Lot”
  * **Brand bundles:** “Hanna Andersson Collection” or “Tea Collection Lot”
  * **Occasion bundles:** “Back-to-School Basics” or “Holiday Party Outfits”
  * **Mix-and-match:** Include a variety of tops, bottoms, and layers


**Bundle Pricing Strategy:** Offer a modest discount (10-15%) compared to individual pricing. This provides value to buyers while maintaining your margins and dramatically reducing per-item fee impact.
###  2. Photography That Sells
On a visual platform, photos are everything. Here’s how to make your listings stand out:
**Flat Lay Basics:**
  * Use natural lighting near a window
  * Choose a clean, uncluttered background (white posterboard works great)
  * Style items together to show outfit potential
  * Include a measuring tape or size reference


**Required Shots:**
  1. Full item front view
  2. Back view for patterns/details
  3. Close-up of brand tags
  4. Any flaws or wear (honesty builds trust)
  5. Detail shots of special features


**Bonus:** Include photos of the item on a hanger or flat with accessories/shoes for styling inspiration.
###  3. Write Descriptions That Convert
Your description should answer every question a parent might have:
**Essential Information:**
  * Brand name (spelled correctly for search)
  * Size (both tag size and measurements)
  * Material/fabric content
  * Condition (be honest about wear)
  * Smoke-free/pet-free home status


**Description Template:**

```
[BRAND NAME] [Item Type] - Size [TAG SIZE]

Measurements (laid flat):
• Chest: X inches
• Length: X inches
• [Relevant measurement]: X inches

Condition: [EUC/GUC/VGUC/NWT]
Material: X% cotton, X% polyester

[2-3 sentences about why this item is great, styling suggestions, or care notes]

From a smoke-free, pet-free home. Ships within 1-2 business days!

```

**Research Process:**

##  Avoiding Common Kids-Clothing Resale Mistakes
###  Mistake 1: Underpricing Quality Items
Many new sellers price too low out of eagerness to make quick sales. Research sold listings before pricing, and remember that parents will pay for quality when condition, brand, and sizing are clear.
###  Mistake 2: Over-Describing Flaws
Be honest about condition, but don’t talk yourself out of sales. “Small pen mark on inner hem, not visible when worn” is better than “THIS ITEM HAS A STAIN, PLEASE SEE PHOTOS.”
###  Mistake 3: Ignoring Seasonality
Kids’ clothing is highly seasonal. A winter coat listed in July will sit for months. Plan your inventory around:
  * Back-to-school (July-August)
  * Fall/Winter transition (September)
  * Holiday dressing (November-December)
  * Spring cleaning/selling (March-April)
  * Summer wardrobing (May-June)


###  Mistake 4: Poor Communication
Parents shopping for their kids want reliability. Always:
  * Respond to questions within 24 hours
  * Ship within your stated timeframe
  * Communicate any delays proactively
  * Be professional even with difficult buyers

## Frequently Asked Questions
### How does the Kidizen selling process work for beginners?
Kidizen selling starts with creating a free account, then photographing and listing each item with a title, size, brand, and price. Once a buyer purchases, Kidizen emails you a pre-paid USPS shipping label — you pack the item and drop it off. Payouts are released after the buyer confirms receipt, typically within a few days. The whole process is designed for parents clearing closets, not just full-time resellers, so the steps are straightforward even if you've never sold online before.
### What fees does Kidizen charge per sale in 2026?
Kidizen fees in 2026 total 19% of the sale price plus a $1.05 per-transaction fee. On a $15 item, that means $3.90 in fees and a $11.10 payout. On a $45 jacket, you keep $35.40. The flat $1.05 fee hits hardest on low-priced items, which is why many sellers bundle smaller pieces to spread the fixed cost. Knowing the full fee structure before pricing lets you set floors rather than discover lost margin after the sale.
### Is Kidizen or Poshmark better for selling kids clothes?
Kidizen is purpose-built for children's clothing, with a community of over 3 million parents actively searching for kids' items. Poshmark charges 20% on sales $15 and up versus Kidizen's 19% plus $1.05 flat, so the fee gap is thin. The real difference is audience intent — Kidizen buyers are searching for Gymboree or Tea Collection specifically, while Poshmark buries kids' items among adult fashion.
