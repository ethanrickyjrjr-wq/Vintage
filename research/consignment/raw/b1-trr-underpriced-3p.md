##  Step-by-Step: Consigning with The RealReal
###  Step 1: Evaluate Your Items
**Use this checklist before consigning:**  
| Question  | If Yes  | If No  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Is the brand in the current Designer Directory?  | Proceed  | Stop: it won’t be accepted  |  
| Is it in fair to pristine condition, with no flaws on The RealReal’s reject list?  | Proceed  | May be rejected or sent back  |  
| Will it sell for $300 or more?  | It earns 60% to 85%, by category  | Under $300 it earns 20% to 55%: compare selling it yourself  |  
| Do you have the original documentation?  | Include it  | Still may be accepted  |  
| Is authentication the main thing a buyer worries about?  | A good fit for The RealReal  | Selling it yourself may leave more  |  
**What The RealReal accepts:** brands in its [Designer Directory](https://www.therealreal.com/designers), which it updates as demand changes. Its pricing FAQ names Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Hermès, Gucci, Cartier and Rolex among the brands that keep the most resale value. The terms say accepted property needs to be in fair-to-pristine condition, listed in the current Designer Directory at evaluation, authentic, and as you described it. The RealReal can accept or reject items at its discretion and may take return shipping out of your next payment for items it can’t accept, so check the live directory before shipping.
###  Step 2: Request Consignment
**Online:**
  1. Create an account at [therealreal.com](https://therealreal.com) and start a consignment
  2. Request a free shipping label (fewer than 10 items) or a concierge pickup (10 or more, where available)
  3. List the brand, size and condition details it asks for
  4. Ship or drop off


**By text or phone:** text MYTRR to 50347 to reach your consignment concierge. For a remote appointment, The RealReal lists valuation@therealreal.com and 844-484-REAL.
**In person (stores and consignment offices):**
  * Book a valuation or drop-off appointment at a store or Luxury Consignment Office
  * On-site valuations cover luxury jewelry, watches and designer handbags at every location
  * You can drop off fashion, home decor and art there too


###  Step 3: Ship or Drop Off
**Free shipping:**
  1. Print the free UPS label and packing list from your account
  2. Put the packing list in the box
  3. Drop it at any UPS location, or take your items to a UPS Store, which packs and ships them for free
  4. Track the package from your account


**At-home pickup:**
  * Free concierge pickup if you have 10 or more items from the Designer Directory (the minimum doesn’t apply to collectibles)
  * Only where the service is available


**Store drop-off:**
  * Drop off at a store or consignment office by appointment
  * Ask questions in person
  * Useful for jewelry, watches and bags you’d rather not ship


###  Step 4: Authentication & Processing
**What happens after The RealReal receives it:**  
| Stage  | What The RealReal says  |  
| --- | --- |  
| Authentication  | Items are authenticated by its experts; it employs 150+ of them, including brand authenticators, gemologists and horologists  |  
| Pricing and photos  | Items are photographed, priced and added to your My Sales page  |

###  Step 5: Pricing & Sale
**How The RealReal sets prices:**
  1. It prices from its own sales data and outside market trends
  2. It weighs brand resale value, market trends, condition and how recent the item is
  3. It lists at the highest price it thinks the market will support
  4. It lowers the price in steps when views and “Obsessions” show weak demand


**What you control:**
  * Most items: nothing. The RealReal sets and discounts the price.
  * Price Review items: Hermès, Chanel and Louis Vuitton bags, alligator and crocodile bags, full fur (mink, chinchilla, sable), jewelry, watches, and art and home items, each priced $995 or more. You approve the first price and any discount; if you don’t answer a discount email within 7 days, The RealReal lowers the price anyway.
  * The RealReal aims to sell an item within 30 days of listing and may suggest a discount after that.


###  Step 6: Get Paid
**Payment timeline:**  
| Event  | When  |  
| --- | --- |

If a buyer returns an item inside the window, they get a refund and your item goes back on sale.

##  How The RealReal Works
You send luxury items, The RealReal authenticates, photographs, prices and sells them, and you get the chart’s share of the sale.
**What you get:** little work after shipping, and buyers trust its authentication.
**What it costs:** you give up the pricing and a big share of the sale. A $1,000 bag pays you $650; the same bag sold yourself on eBay at the same price leaves $849.60 after eBay’s handbag fee, before shipping.

##  Commission Structure 2026: Use the Live Chart, Not an Old Tier Table
To work out a payout:

```
your payout = net selling price x your category's rate (+ Rewards points on items of $200+)
net selling price = sold price - discounts/promotions, excluding tax and shipping

```

The terms define commission this way and point to the live chart for the rate. The chart on this page was copied on Oct 16, 2026; old copied tier tables (a single 40% to 70% or 15% to 45% scale) are wrong now, because each category has its own top share.
###  Commission check worksheet
Three common pieces, worked from the chart (no Rewards bonus) and from our fee engine for eBay, on the item price:  
| Item  | It sells for  | Your share  | The RealReal pays you  | eBay leaves you at the same price  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| Designer bag  | $1,000  | 65%  | $650  | $849.60  |  
| Designer bag  | $5,000  | 75%  | $3,750  | $4,549.60  |  
| Wallet (clothing & more)  | $86  | 20%  | $17.20  | $73.90  |  
###  What changes the answer
  * A higher price doesn’t mean a better payout if The RealReal discounts it hard: your share is of the discounted price.
  * A lower share can still be worth it if The RealReal’s authentication gets a sale you couldn’t get yourself.
  * A high self-sell payout can be worse in practice if you lack receipts, photos, buyer trust or fraud protection.
  * Rewards points and site credit are open to everyone. Promotions (like extra site credit for first-time consignors) come and go: count one only when it shows in your account.

###  Analysis: When TRR Made Sense
None of the six pays more at The RealReal (TRR for short). The Birkin comes closest; the Chanel shows what consigning a $5,000 bag costs.
**The Birkin:** The RealReal pays 80% on a handbag from $7,500, the same cut as Poshmark’s 20% fee. It would have to sell the bag for $14,787 to match eBay’s $11,829.60, inside the middle half of eBay’s own sales. On a piece where buyers worry about fakes and you don’t want to handle a five-figure sale, that’s the closest The RealReal comes to selling it yourself.
**The Chanel flap:** at $4,891 The RealReal pays 70%, $3,423.70, against $4,450.41 on eBay. It would need to sell it for $5,934 to match (at $5,000 the share rises to 75%, so the gap closes faster than 70% suggests). On a bag around $5,000, convenience costs you about $1,000.
###  Analysis: When Self-Selling Won
**The Neverfull and the Marmont:** at $840 and $600, The RealReal pays 65% and 60%. Selling either yourself on eBay leaves $509.60 to $713.60, and The RealReal would have to get $1,098 and $784 to match.
**The wallet and the trench:** under $300 the chart pays 20% to 55%. An $86 wallet pays you $17.20 at The RealReal and leaves $73.90 on eBay. Unless you’d rather not deal with it at all, these belong on eBay, Poshmark or Vestiaire.
##  The Faster Decision: TRR vs Self-Sell vs Other Luxury Channels
Each route wins in a different situation:  
| Situation  | Best channel  | Why  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| A bag or watch from $7,500 where buyers worry about fakes  | The RealReal, or eBay if you can handle it  | The RealReal pays 80% (bags) or 85% (watches), its smallest gap: eBay leaves about 91% on the Birkin and 93.5% on a $10,000 watch ($9,352.10 against $8,500)  |  
| A designer bag between $750 and $4,999  | Vestiaire or eBay  | The RealReal pays 65% to 70%; eBay leaves about 85% to 91% and Vestiaire 80%  |  
| Clothing or accessories under $300  | eBay, Poshmark or Vestiaire  | The RealReal pays 20% to 55% in this range  |  
| A niche piece with collector buyers but slow demand  | Direct sale or a specialist marketplace  | Better chance to control price and wait for the right buyer  |  
For any one piece, the cost of consigning is the gap between The RealReal’s payout and what selling it yourself leaves. Sorted by price:
  * **high-value and high-risk** (bags and watches from $7,500): The RealReal’s share is at its highest
  * **mid-value with steady demand** ($300 to $5,000): on clothing and bags, selling it yourself usually leaves 15 to 26 points more (the gap is smaller on watches and men’s sneakers, whose top shares are 85%)

##  8 Common Mistakes That Cost Consignors Money
###  Mistake #1: Consigning Low-Value Items
**The problem:** sending a bag that will sell for about $150 and expecting a meaningful return.
**The reality:** at $150, The RealReal’s chart pays 45%: $67.50. Under $100 it pays 20%.
**The fix:** check what the piece really sells for first. Below about $300, sell it yourself or bundle it.
###  Mistake #2: Not Negotiating Commission
**The problem:** assuming you can talk the rate up.
**The reality:** The RealReal publishes one chart per category and a Rewards bonus by sales volume, and its pages say nothing about negotiating individual rates. The levers it does publish are Rewards (+1, +2 or +5 points on items of $200+ once your Net Sales in the commission window pass $1,500, $5,000 or $10,000) and site credit (5% more).
**The fix:** if you have several good pieces, consign them inside one commission window so your Net Sales reach the next tier. Asking about a better rate costs nothing, but plan on the chart.
###  Mistake #3: Poor Item Presentation
**The problem:** sending wrinkled, dirty, or poorly packaged items.
**The reality:** condition is one of the four things The RealReal prices on, and it rejects items with flaws on its list (stains larger than a dime, missing brand tags, odor, broken zippers or buckles, excessive scuffing).
**The fix:** before shipping:
  * Clean items gently
  * Use dust bags and stuffing
  * Package carefully to prevent damage
  * Include all original accessories


###  Mistake #4: Missing Documentation
**The problem:** not including receipts, authenticity cards, or original packaging.
**The reality:** paperwork and packaging help a buyer trust the piece and can help authentication. The RealReal doesn’t publish how much they add to a price.
**The fix:** include everything you have:
  * Original receipt
  * Authenticity cards
  * Dust bags
  * Original boxes
  * Care booklets
  * Repair documentation


###  Mistake #5: Wrong Timing
**The problem:** consigning winter coats in May.
**The reality:** The RealReal prices to current demand and discounts when an item doesn’t move, so an out-of-season piece is more likely to be marked down.
**The fix:** send seasonal pieces ahead of their season:  
| Item Type  | Send it  |  
| --- | --- |  
| Winter coats  | Late summer to early fall  |  
| Summer items  | Late winter to spring  |  
| Holiday and party  | Early fall  |  
| Year-round (bags)  | Anytime  |  
###  Mistake #6: Expecting to Set Your Own Price
**The problem:** expecting a reserve or a minimum price.
**The reality:** The RealReal sets the price for most items and discounts as demand changes. Only Price Review items ($995 and up in select categories) let you approve the price and each discount, and if you don’t answer within 7 days the discount goes ahead.
**The fix:** if a minimum matters to you, sell it yourself, or ask for Price Review on an eligible piece before you send it.
###  Mistake #7: Not Monitoring Your Account
**The problem:** consigning and forgetting about it.
**The reality:** prices drop as The RealReal discounts. If you want an item back before 365 days, it costs a fee ($20 for most items, $100 for home, art or oversized pieces) plus shipping; after 365 days you can ask for it back at The RealReal’s cost.
**The fix:**
  * Check your My Sales page weekly
  * Review price changes
  * Decide on returns before discounts go further
  * Note what sells and what doesn’t


###  Mistake #8: Ignoring the Break-Even Analysis
**The problem:** not working out whether The RealReal or selling it yourself leaves more.
**The reality:** at the same price, selling clothing or a bag yourself on eBay usually leaves 15 to 26 points more between $300 and $5,000 (the case study above shows it piece by piece). Men’s sneakers come closest: from $1,000 The RealReal pays 85%, against about 92% on eBay.
**The fix:** use this formula:
  * The RealReal payout = sale price × your category’s share
  * Self-sell payout = sale price − the app’s fee (our [fee comparison](https://www.underpriced.app/tools/platform-fee-comparison) does it for each app)
  * If the self-sell payout minus the value of your time is higher, sell it yourself

##  FAQ
###  How much will The RealReal pay you?
20% to 85% of what the item sells for, by category and price. Clothing between $750 and $4,999 pays 65%, and so do bags from $750 to $1,499 ($650 on a $1,000 bag); bags from $1,500 pay 70%; under $100 pays 20%. Handbags reach 80% and watches 85% from $7,500. ###  How long does The RealReal take to pay?
It pays on the 15th of the month after the item sells, so anywhere from 15 days to about six weeks after the sale. Direct deposit can take up to 48 more hours; a mailed check takes 5 to 7 business days and costs $12.50. Listing takes about 10 to 15 days after The RealReal receives the item.
###  Does The RealReal buy items outright?
For select watches, jewelry and handbags, yes: its Get Paid Now program pays upfront, by direct deposit within 48 hours or 5% more in site credit right away. Everything else is consignment: you’re paid only after it sells.
###  Is it worth it to sell on The RealReal?
Only when not doing the work is worth the gap. Even at its best (80% to 85% on bags and watches from $7,500, 85% on men’s sneakers from $1,000) selling it yourself leaves more on every piece we priced; what you get for the difference is no photos, questions, shipping or worry about fakes. For clothing and bags between $300 and $5,000, selling it yourself on eBay usually leaves 15 to 26 points more at the same price, and Vestiaire 10 to 20. Under $300 it pays 20% to 55%, which rarely makes sense.
##  The verdict
The RealReal makes sense when convenience and authentication matter more than the commission. Its share rises with the price: 20% under $100, 65% to 70% on most clothing and bags from $750, and 80% to 85% on bags and watches from $7,500. Under $300, selling it yourself almost always leaves more.
