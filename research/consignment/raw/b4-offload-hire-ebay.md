# Can I hire someone to sell my stuff on eBay?
Short answer: yes, but the way you remember it working is gone. eBay retired its Trading Assistant program, the official directory of people you could hire, years ago. The need obviously didn't retire with it, because people still ask this question on eBay's own forums every week. Here's what actually exists in 2026.

Why do so many people want this? Because selling on eBay properly is a skill and a chore. Good photos, a title with the right keywords, honest condition notes, competitive pricing against sold listings, packing, shipping deadlines, returns, the buyer who asks four questions and vanishes. If you sell all the time, it's routine. If you have one box of stuff after a garage cleanout, it's a weekend you'll never get back.
## What happened to Trading Assistants?
eBay's Trading Assistant program was a directory of experienced sellers who'd list your items for a fee: drop your stuff off, they handle everything, you split the proceeds. eBay shut the directory down (along with most of the physical "drop-off store" franchises that grew around it; the 2008 recession finished off many of the rest). If you search eBay's help pages for it today you'll find nothing, which is why forum threads titled "Who will sell on eBay for me? It used to be called a trading assistant" keep appearing.
## Your actual options in 2026
### 1. eBay Consignment, if it's luxury
eBay's official replacement, but narrower than the old program: it covers handbags, jewelry, watches, and premium sneakers and apparel. You ship your item to a vetted professional seller who photographs, lists, and sells it, for a commission of roughly **20–40 percent** depending on sale price. For genuine luxury goods this is a fair trade; authentication and presentation really do move prices in those categories. For ordinary stuff, it's simply not offered.
### 2. A local consignment seller or tasker
Some experienced eBay sellers still take consignment clients. You find them through local listings, task platforms like TaskRabbit or Airtasker, or word of mouth. Expect them to keep **30–50 percent** after eBay's own fees (roughly 13–15 percent in most categories), and expect minimums: a pro won't touch a $20 item, because photographing and shipping it costs them more than their cut. Vet carefully: you're handing inventory and money handling to a stranger with no platform guaranteeing the arrangement.
### 3. An AI selling agent, for everything else
The reason human help has a price floor is that human time is expensive. That's the part that changed. AI can now do the labor that made listing tedious: identify the item from a photo, write the title and description, price it against sold comps, field buyer questions, and negotiate offers.
