# b2 - Furniture, decor, lighting, art: how consignment and "submit your stuff" actually works

Agent b2, accessed 2026-10-27. Raw crawls: `research/consignment/raw/b2-*.md`. Every figure below was read from a page fetched this session unless tagged `UNVERIFIED`. Shop: Holliewood and Vine (one person, one-of-one stock, lamps, glass, art, a little furniture).

## TL;DR

1. There are only four real models in this slice, and they differ on one thing: **where the item physically sits between "submitted" and "sold."** (A) owner keeps it, platform books freight after the sale (Chairish); (B) platform takes it and auctions it with no reserve (EBTH); (C) platform picks up and warehouses (Kaiyo, now dead; AptDeco and Sotheby's Home/Viyet, both walled); (D) local showroom, owner hauls it in (Turn Style, The Find, Posh Plum).
2. Chairish's headline "you keep 70% or more" is not what a new seller gets. A new account is "Consignor" at 40% commission (you net 60%) until you have 10 live listings. Its own commission examples also contain arithmetic errors (see discrepancies).
3. Local SWFL furniture consignment refuses exactly your core stock: Posh Plum says "we do not accept children's furniture, bedding, glassware, or light fixtures."
4. Kaiyo (free pickup, warehouse, check to the seller) collapsed in Aug 2024 and left sellers unpaid. A consignee that holds other people's goods and money is a liability, not a feature.
5. For a one-person shop: copy Model A's shape for large pieces (owner keeps it, buyer pays freight, local pickup is free) and Model D's contract clauses (90 days, 50/50, markdown ladder, abandonment date, risk stays with the consignor). Do not copy B or C. For lamps and glass, take possession and pack them yourself or do not take them.

## Discrepancies (rule: "X verified, Y need review")

- **Chairish tier bounds: verified, both worked $40,000 examples: need review.** Both pages agree the cumulative scale is 20% up to $2,500, 12% from $2,501 to $25,000, 3% above $25,000. Applying those bounds to $40,000: 20% x $2,500 = $500, 12% x $22,500 = $2,700, 3% x $15,000 = $450, total $3,650, seller nets $36,350. The support article charges 12% on "the next $25,000" (a band that is only $22,500 wide) and gets $3,875 / $36,125 / "approximately 90.31%". The Consign page uses the right bands ($500 + $2,700 + $450) but prints "You will earn 91% $35,350", which does not match its own lines ($36,350). The $2,000 and $15,000 examples on the support page check out.
- **Chairish payout headline vs default plan: need review.** The Consign page says "we send you 70% or more of the sale price." The same page and the support page say a new account is Consignor, 40% flat, i.e. 60%. 70% is Professional (30%), which needs 10+ active listings.
- **Chairish plans: need review.** Platinum (0% on auctions, tiered on vintage) appears only on the support article, not on the Consign page. Plus is shown as "22% Flat" on the plan cards, yet the tiered-commission example is labeled "Example of Plus or Elite Item Commission."
- **Kaiyo status: verified closed (third-party), kaiyoco.com not credible.** The Surface article (Sept 2024) reports the wind-down. `kaiyoco.com` presents Kaiyo as a live shop "based in Chino, California", but every button is "Buy On Amazon" with `tag=kaiyoco-20`, i.e. an Amazon-affiliate lookalike. Do not treat it as Kaiyo.
- **EBTH has two programs; only one has terms here.** The consignment-terms page governs the Drop-Off Day / Mail-In program. The in-home estate service (how-it-works page) has no commission terms fetched: `UNVERIFIED`.

## 1. Chairish (Model A: owner keeps it, Chairish books freight after the sale)

- Homepage: https://www.chairish.com/
- Pages read: https://www.chairish.com/pages/consign-with-us ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44165603442321-Chairish-Selling-Plans-Commission-Rate-Overview ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44246519895697-How-Shipping-Works ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44246824574865-Return-Policy ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44246709644689-Problem-with-a-Delivery ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44166117079313-Seller-Expectations ; https://support.chairish.com/hc/en-us/articles/44165873535889-New-Seller-Tips
- Accessed 2026-10-27.

**Commission (exact).**
- Consignor 40%, Professional 30%, Premium 25% vintage/used, Plus 22%, Elite 20% tiered, Platinum 20% tiered + 0% on auctions. Newly made/custom: 30% (40% on Consignor). Auctions 20%. Source: support article, "Consignor: 40% on all products sold on Chairish ... Professional: 30% ... Premium: 25% on vintage/used products ... Plus: 22% commission on vintage/used products ... Elite: 20% tiered commission."
- Paid plans (Consign page): Premium "$49 / Month or $539 / Year", Plus "$99 / Month or $1,089 / Year", Elite "By invitation only ... $149 / Month or $1,639 / Year."
- Default plan: "When you first open your account, your seller plan is assigned the 'Consignor' plan by default. When you reach 10 live listings, you'll automatically be upgraded to 'Professional'." Drop under 10 and you keep Professional for a 6-month grace period, then revert.
- Commission base: "Sale Amount ... excludes amounts assessed on the sale (like taxes and duties), shipping and delivery costs paid by the buyer, transaction fees such as credit card transaction costs."
- Listing is free: "Listing is fast and FREE!"

**What they accept / refuse.** "We approve items that appeal to Chairish shoppers." Seller Expectations: "Chairish reserves the right to inspect or reject any item as unsuitable for sale at any time"; items must be "clean and odor free." No explicit prohibited-item list was in the pages I read. Anti-circumvention: if you sell around Chairish to a buyer Chairish found, "the Commission will be due and payable to Chairish."

**THE SHIPPING QUESTION.** The owner keeps the item and never ships it to Chairish. Chairish is a marketplace, not a warehouse. After a sale:
- Small/décor: parcel. "Chairish provides the seller with a shipping label" (Chairish-Managed Parcel), or the seller buys the label (Seller-Managed). Consign FAQ: "For smaller items include packing costs in your pricing ... carefully package your items for shipping & use the Chairish-provided shipping label." Most items ship within 3 business days of a sale; domestic parcel 10 days or less.
- Large/furniture: "Larger items and furniture typically require special handling and must be shipped using specialized shipping partners." Chairish-Managed In-Home partners wrap and secure the item; "For larger items be available during business hours for furniture pickup." Most ship within ~15 business days; "Delivery typically occurs 3-6 weeks after pickup ... up to 8 weeks." "Delivery does not include installation or assembly."
- Local: Free Local Pickup (buyer collects from the seller) or Local Delivery (seller delivers).
- Routing between parcel and In-Home is described by size/handling ("smaller items and décor" vs "larger items and furniture"); no numeric threshold on the pages read.

**Who photographs / who prices.** Seller does both: "Add pics, a story and some details; then set your price." Chairish touches up: "touch up photos for maximum attraction"; "whited-out main images." Seller Expectations: "You are responsible for the quality of your item and its listing, including accurate product information, photos, and condition." Markdowns are one-way: "if you mark your items down, you cannot bring the price back up again."

**Shipping cost split.** "The Buyer always pays for shipping, unless the seller has decided to offer free shipping." Quoted at checkout from carrier rates (distance, size/weight, fragility, service level).

**Returns and who eats them.**
- Change of mind: 48 hours from delivery, then "all sales are final." "The buyer is responsible for the full cost of outbound shipping, return shipping, and packing charges. These will be deducted from your refund."
- Local Pickup and Seller-Managed Delivery: "All sales are final once you or your agent takes possession." The buyer must refuse the item at the door.
- Misrepresented: "If confirmed, we'll arrange a return at no cost to you and a full refund." The page does not say who funds it (likely the seller; `inferred`).
- Damage: Chairish In-Home is "insured for the full transaction amount." Parcel: "The seller is responsible for ensuring that each item is safely packed"; Chairish files a claim and "will only reimburse the seller if the claim is accepted." Seller-managed: "the seller is responsible for resolving any claims for loss or damage."

**Payout.** "Chairish withholds payment and only releases it to the seller once this 48-hour window has closed." Consign page: "Payment is issued via PayPal following delivery and the 48 hour return period." (ACH is listed under wholesale benefits only.) Delivery of large pieces takes 3-6+ weeks, so cash lands weeks after the sale.

**Unsold.** Nothing happens: the listing sits until you deactivate it ("deactivate it from your Chairish dashboard immediately"). New sellers: "it can take 3-6 months for new sellers to gain traction."

**Steal this / watch out.** Steal the structure: seller keeps stock, buyer pays freight, 48-hour window, payment held until the window closes, free local pickup. Watch out: in a parcel sale the packer carries the risk when the carrier denies a claim.

## 2. Everything But The House (EBTH) (Model B: platform takes it, no-reserve auction)

- Homepage: https://www.ebth.com/
- Pages read: https://www.ebth.com/consignment-terms (Drop-Off Day and Mail-In Program) ; https://www.ebth.com/how-it-works (in-home service). Accessed 2026-10-27.

**Two programs. Do not merge them.**

*Mail-In / Drop-Off (terms fetched).*

- Shipping: "You may send your Items to us or deliver your Items to one of our designated drop-off locations." Who pays inbound postage is not stated (`UNVERIFIED`); "Use of a mailing label provided by EBTH does not constitute any form or guarantee of insurance." For items over $750 each (or $1,500 per shipment) the terms say "you should have the Item or Items professionally packed."
- Photos / price: "We agree to photograph, describe and facilitate the sale ... online bidding." "The Items are being sold without reserve." You do not set the price.
- Commission table, quoted as written: "> $0 - <= $30 | 100% | 0%", "> $31 - <= $60 | $30", "> $61 - <= $250 | 50%", "> $251 - <= $500 | 40%", "> $500 - <= $1000 | 30%", "> $1000 | 20%." The ranges leave cent-level gaps ($30.01-$30.99 etc.) as printed. Example on page: "if an item sells for $50, EBTH will take a minimum commission of $30 ... you will receive $20."
- Cost split: seller share is "the total sale proceeds we receive ... subtracting our commissions and any applicable expenses we incur in connection with the sale, which shall include ... all taxes and shipping costs."
- Payout: "Within approximately thirty (30) calendar days of the sale closing ... by company check."
- Returns on rejected items: "Any such return will be at your sole expense, including our actual third party shipping costs plus a $30.00 handling fee."
- Risk: "You retain all risk of loss or damage"; "We are not responsible for insuring"; liability "limited to actual cash value ... up to $50 per Item."

*In-home estate service (how-it-works page; commission `UNVERIFIED`).* "Consultations are entirely free." "Our trained and knowledgeable staff sorts, catalogues, photographs and writes descriptions." "All items are up for auction for up to 7 days, and all bids start at $1." "We remove all auction items from the home and provide an itemized inventory list and settlement check within 30 days."

**Steal this / watch out.** Nothing to copy except the "we photograph and write it up" promise. A $1-start, no-reserve auction with a 100% commission under $30 and a $150 floor is a volume business with warehouse and staff. It is the opposite of one-of-one curated pricing.

## Blunt read for Holliewood and Vine

Ricky is one person with one-of-one lamps, glass, art, and the occasional chair. Ranked:

1. **Copy Model A's shape, not Chairish's scale.** Owner keeps the item, you list it with proper photos, the buyer pays freight, local pickup is free, money is held until a short return window passes. It needs no warehouse, no inventory cash, and no insurance on goods you never touch. It breaks for fragile pieces: on parcel sales, Chairish's own rule is that the packer is responsible and the seller is only reimbursed "if the claim is accepted." For furniture you need a freight partner who comes to the owner's door; Chairish has one, you would have to build or rent one. Use it for furniture and large art, and set a flat photo standard so you are not selling from someone's bad phone pictures.
2. **Steal Model D's contract, not its store.** Posh Plum and The Find give you a ready-made clause set: 90-day term, 50/50, a markdown ladder (10% at day 31, 20% at day 61), abandonment at day 95, "risk of loss stays with the consignor," "we do not call you when it sells," movers paid directly. But their economics assume foot traffic in a showroom. The Holliewood and Vine room is a style reference, not a public store, so you cannot copy the "sits on the floor" leg. Translate it as a listing term, not a floor term.
3. **For lamps and glass: take possession and pack them yourself, or do not take them.** The only SWFL shop whose FAQ I could read refuses "glassware, or light fixtures" outright. That is your whole shop. The page gives no reason; my read (`inferred`) is that breakage, wiring liability, and packing standards are the unsolved problem. If you take a consigned lamp, you want it in hand, tested, and photographed by you, which is Model D without the store. If the owner keeps it (Model A), the damage claim is only as good as their packing.
4. **Do not copy EBTH.** No-reserve $1-start auctions, 100% commission under $30, a $150 floor, and destroyed leftovers is a warehouse-and-volume machine. It also takes the pricing away from you, which kills the curated, theatrical feel.
5. **Do not copy Kaiyo, AptDeco, or Sotheby's Home.** Free pickup, a warehouse, and holding other people's money is capital-heavy and fragile; Kaiyo's collapse left sellers unpaid. If you ever take consignments, pay out fast and keep consignor money out of operating cash.
6. **Buying outright is the cleaner answer for cheap lamps and glass.** Every furniture and decor program I could verify is consignment or auction; none buys outright, so there is no template in this slice (the b1/b3 fashion files may have one). Outright buy is where your risk is clearest and your margin is yours; leave consignment for pieces where the owner wants to wait.
7. **Still open (not my slice):** the legal and tax side of holding or listing other people's goods in Florida and on Etsy (b5/b4 files exist in `research/consignment/raw/`; I did not read them). Do not set a consignment contract live until that is checked.
