## Typeface
The typefaces that are easiest for people who have low vision, language problems, or cognitive disabilities are sans serif or simple serif. Accessible faces have: proportions that contribute to legibility; a clear extension for lowercase b, d, g, h, j, k, l, p, q, t, and y; easily legible numbers (e.g. distinguishable 5, 6, and 8).

## Caps / italic
Do not set text in all caps. Type set in all caps is more difficult to read and should be limited to items such as titles and decorative heads.
Avoid use of script and italic type for essential information.

## Size
Minimum type size, at even the shortest distance, is an x-height of 4.5 mm (3/16 in.).

## Spacing / contrast / ground
Provide consistent letterspacing and word spacing. ... Do not center more than three lines of label text.
Research shows that dark on light works marginally better than light on dark for headlines. ... A 70% light reflectance value contrast is most effective.
Lighter type weight and greater letter spacing is required when type is reversed out of the background to ensure legibility.
Print only on a solid background.
