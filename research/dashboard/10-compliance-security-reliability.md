# Hollywood and Vine dashboard: compliance, security and reliability constraints

Date: 2026-10-26. Author: research agent (wave 2). Scope: constraints the design must satisfy, from the vendors' own legal texts, Supabase and OWASP docs, and the failures Ricky already hit. Not legal advice. Nothing was built or committed.

Labels used everywhere below:
- **VERIFIED**: the operative text was read in this session at the URL given (or at the Wayback copy named). Raw copies are in `research/dashboard/raw/` (files starting `compliance-` were fetched for this document; others came from wave 1 and are named where used).
- **INFERRED**: my reading or application of verified text. Reasonable, but a vendor could read it differently.
- **UNVERIFIED**: could not be confirmed from a primary source.

Section numbers for the eBay API License Agreement are the page's own nested list order (the HTML renders the sub-clauses as numbered lists without printing the numbers), so "8.1.3" means section 8, first sub-clause, third item. Wave 1 used the same numbering.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Ricky's own shop data is allowed everywhere; what is not allowed is deriving market data from the platforms.** eBay's license opens its second derivation ban with "Notwithstanding Your Users' access to and use of their own information"; Etsy's docs recommend Seller Apps for "reports" on "your shop only"; Shopify custom apps exist for "your Shopify store". The dashboard may analyse Ricky's orders. It may not build average-sold-price, deal-score, price-suggestion or ML features out of eBay or Etsy content.
2. **Etsy prohibited item #25 ("for purposes of analytics") does not, on the best reading, bar an own-shop dashboard** (section 1.2). Verdict INFERRED. The realistic written authorization is Etsy's approval of the Application Purpose, so state the purpose precisely at registration and keep the approval.
3. **Store no buyer personal data we do not need for the books.** eBay forces deletion on request and says retention is only for "specific and demonstrable legal requirements (e.g. tax...)"; Shopify wants 30-day deletion and retention periods; Etsy says "reasonably necessary". Keep order id, dates, item, price, fees, shipping, tax. Drop names, addresses, emails.
4. **eBay watch results are the most restricted data in the system**: delete when the listing ends, re-poll within 6 hours or show "as of", keep visually separate from other data, never feed ML or price models.
5. **The webhook receivers and the eBay account-deletion endpoint are compliance infrastructure, not features.** eBay cannot be opted out of (we store order data), and an unacknowledged endpoint for 24 hours starts a 30-day non-compliance clock. That makes always-on Supabase Pro a compliance need.
6. **Sourcing beyond eBay Browse is API-only or native-alerts-only.** Nearly every consumer resale site Ricky would want bans automated access in its terms. Scraping is "own risk" for at most a handful of sites with no located clause, and all of that is UNVERIFIED.

**Account deletion notifications (VERIFIED, verbatim, https://developer.ebay.com/develop/guides-v2/marketplace-user-account-deletion.md):**
- "All existing and new third-party developers integrated with eBay APIs via the eBay Developers Program are _required_ to: **1)** subscribe to eBay marketplace account deletion/closure notifications; or **2)** follow the process to opt out of subscribing to these notifications if they do not store any eBay data for various reasons."
- "New third-party developers coming to the platform must subscribe to or opt out of eBay marketplace account deletion/closure notifications before they make their first production API call."
- "Failure to comply with this requirement will result in termination of your access to the Developer Tools, and/or reduced access to all or some APIs."
- Opt-out is the "Not persisting eBay data" toggle; "failure to provide correct information may result in penalties or having their account disabled". We persist orders (buyer names, addresses), so we cannot truthfully opt out. This answers wave-1 open question 6: it is mandatory for Ricky's keyset.
- Acknowledge: "The callback URL should immediately acknowledge each eBay marketplace account deletion/closure notification with an HTTP status code indicating a successful response. 200 OK, 201 Created, 202 Accepted, and 204 No Content are all acceptable."
- Down rule: "After a 24-hour period of multiple, unacknowledged notifications from a callback URL, the callback URL is marked down, and eBay will send out an alert email to the developer about the callback URL being non-responsive. Upon receiving the email, the developer will have up to 30 days to resolve the problem with their callback URL acknowledging eBay marketplace account deletion/closure notifications. If the problem is not resolved within 30 days, the developer will be marked as non-compliant."
- Action: "they need to take the appropriate action to delete the user data, or in case developers plan to retain data, it is only retained to meet specific and demonstrable legal requirements (e.g. tax, collections, AML regulations). Deletion should be done in a manner such that even the highest system privilege cannot reverse the deletion." So hard delete or irreversible redaction, and a restore from backup must replay a deletion log.
- Volume: "you should be prepared to acknowledge up to 1500 notifications on any given day (event-driven and highly variable)". Payload fields: `notification.notificationId` (dedupe key), `eventDate`, `publishDate`, `publishAttemptCount`, `data.username`, `data.userId`, `data.eiasToken`.
- Challenge handshake: eBay sends `GET <callback>?challenge_code=...`; reply `200` JSON `{"challengeResponse": sha256(challengeCode + verificationToken + endpoint)}` in exactly that order with `Content-Type: application/json`; the endpoint must be https and "should not contain an internal IP address or 'localhost'"; the verification token is 32 to 80 characters. Use a JSON library; a BOM breaks it.
- Signature: decode `x-ebay-signature`, fetch the public key via `getPublicKey` (needs an app token), verify; "If signature verification fails, a HTTP status 412 - Precondition Failed is returned"; cache the key ("one-hour is recommended").
- **Doc conflict (rule 4):** the guide says to "immediately acknowledge" and "After the acknowledgement ... the developer should verify", while the SDK flow it describes returns 412 on a bad signature. The brief for this design says verify before parsing. Resolution (INFERRED): verify the raw bytes before any JSON parse when the public key is cached (the normal case, one key fetch per hour); on a cache miss, return 202 immediately, store the raw body in the inbox as `unverified`, fetch the key and verify asynchronously, and act only on rows that verify.

**Browse API access and the Buy-API gate (text VERIFIED, outcome UNVERIFIED):** the Buy APIs Requirements page (https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/static/buy-requirements.html, raw `ebay-buy-requirements.md`) says: "The use of eBay's Buy APIs in production is intended for eBay partners only. You must apply for production access through the eBay Partner Network." and "There is no guarantee that your application for production use of the APIs will be approved." The same page's browse/checkout UX rules (eBay logo, fixed price only, "Prohibited: Disabling eBay's sort functionality", shipping display) sit under "guest checkout" requirements; they do not apply to an alert list that never checks out, so they are not listed as constraints here. Wave 1 recorded that the Browse search spec carries no Limited Release note and the docs conflict; the smoke test it proposed (one production `item_summary/search` call) is still the first build action. The Growth Check list also says: "If you have a search application, it uses the Buy API or Finding API", which is eBay telling us that HTML scraping is not the sanctioned route.

**What "personal / non-commercial" tiers forbid:** eBay has no personal tier. Its default limits are "designed for individuals and smaller businesses" (https://developer.ebay.com/develop/api/buy/api_call_limits, raw `ebay-buy-call-limits.md`); raising them or using restricted APIs needs the Application Growth Check (https://developer.ebay.com/grow/application-growth-check, raw `compliance-ebay-growthcheck.md`: "Requesting an Application Growth Check is required if you want to: Increase the API call limits for your application; Use restricted APIs in production"). The tiers that do bite are elsewhere: Etsy's Seller App is "for your own shop use only", Vercel Hobby is "non-commercial personal use only" (section 1.5), and Shopify's Level 2 customer data on custom apps needs the Grow plan (section 1.3).

**Scraping eBay (VERIFIED):** the User Agreement forbids "use any robot, spider, scraper, data mining tools, data gathering and extraction tools, or other automated means (including, without limitation buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots, or any end-to-end flow that attempts to place orders without human review) to access our Services for any purpose, except with the prior express permission of eBay". The API license is that permission, for the API only. `https://www.ebay.com/robots.txt` (header `v30.2_COM_August_2026`, raw `compliance-ebay-robots.md`) disallows `/itm/*` item pages with many query shapes for `User-agent: *`. eBay's position is therefore: Browse API yes, HTML no.

**The two clauses, verbatim, with their lead-in (VERIFIED, Terms section 5 "Prohibited Behavior"):**
- Lead-in: "Your use of the Etsy API must be in good faith and in the spirit of Etsy. You agree that you will not:"
- #24: "Use or promote the use of automated systems or browser extensions to access, analyze, or scrape the Etsy Site, the Etsy API or any Etsy data, including but not limited to Etsy listings, shops, or user profiles, unless expressly authorized in writing by Etsy; or"
- #25: "Use the Etsy API to collect, scan, or otherwise request Etsy content for purposes of analytics, machine learning, training artificial intelligence models, licensing, or content removal, unless expressly authorized in writing by Etsy."

**Verdict: does #25 bar a shop owner analysing their OWN shop's receipts? Probably not. INFERRED, evidence below. It is not VERIFIED because no Etsy text says so in terms.**
1. *Read literally, #25 bars a sales dashboard, and #24 is broader still.* #24 forbids using "automated systems" to "access, analyze" "the Etsy API or any Etsy data". Taken literally that bars every API application, including Etsy's own recommended Seller App. Etsy cannot mean that, so both clauses are being read for their purpose: stopping scraping, harvesting, AI training and resale of Etsy content. Their neighbours say the same: #19 ("information about Etsy's internal systems"), #9 ("access or attempt to access any internal or legacy Etsy APIs or data feeds"), #18 ("Transfer or commercialize ... Etsy Member content or data to any third party"), #15 and #16 (spam and sending order information to buyers).
2. *Etsy's own docs endorse seller-owned reporting.* https://developers.etsy.com/documentation/ : "Seller Apps are for individual Etsy sellers who want to connect an app to their own shop data. This is the recommended starting point if you are a shop owner building custom tools, automations, reports, or workflows for your shop only." "Seller App access includes access to all public and OAuth-authenticated endpoints, scoped only to your registered shop." The help article: "Register for a seller app if you only need access to your own shop's data, including listing, orders, inventory, and more." The announcement: "a new tier that lets individual sellers build tools and services for their own shop ... read and write access to your own shop's data via OAuth".
3. *The Terms themselves contemplate it.* Section 2: "indicate whether you wish to use the Etsy API for personal (such as building tools for your own Etsy shop or for testing) or commercial use (such as building Applications for use by multiple Etsy shops)". Section 4: the developer is "a service provider to the applicable Etsy seller" for member data.
4. *There is a written-authorization route already built into access.* Section 3: "You must submit the purpose of your Application (the 'Application Purpose'), as well as any updates to the Application, for Etsy's prior approval, which may be granted or withheld at Etsy's sole discretion. You may receive access to the Etsy API only after Etsy's approval of your Application Purpose." and the Application must be "consistent with the Application Purpose submitted to and approved by Etsy". If the purpose says "sales and inventory reporting, and sold/low-stock alerts, for my own shop", Etsy's approval of that purpose is the closest thing to "expressly authorized in writing" that the process offers. Seller App approval is automated ("approved within minutes, with no manual review queue"), so it is a weaker authorization than a human review, which is why section 6 (open questions) still recommends one email to developer@etsy.com.
5. *Counter-evidence and residual risk.* No Etsy statement names "own-shop analytics" as allowed in so many words; Etsy decides alone ("Etsy's decision regarding an Application's quality or whether your Application ... violates the Terms ... is final and binding", section 6); the 2026-10-12 Terms are newer than the Seller App launch (Jul 2026) and both are Etsy's. Cross-shop or market analytics (competitor listings, trend scans, search-volume tools) is the case the clause is for, and the design must not do it.
6. A corollary: **Etsy as a sourcing source is out.** Seller App access is "scoped only to your registered shop" and "cannot be used to access other sellers' private shop data" (public listing search by a Seller App is a grey area at best), and scanning Etsy listings for price/keyword matches is the exact #24/#25 pattern. Wave 1's Phase-2 idea of Etsy `findAllListingsActive` as a source should be dropped (UNVERIFIED whether a Seller App can call public endpoints beyond its shop; the docs say "all public and OAuth-authenticated endpoints, scoped only to your registered shop").

**Other Etsy rules that shape the build (VERIFIED, Terms and docs):**
- Freshness and retention, section 5 "Display of Data": "You will not display listing content more than six (6) hours older than the corresponding information on the Etsy Site or the Etsy Apps. You will not display any other Etsy content more than twenty-four (24) hours older than the content displayed on the Etsy Site or the Etsy Apps. Once you've accessed, stored, or displayed Etsy content, you will not cache or store it longer than is reasonably necessary to provide service to your Application's users." Listing mirror refreshed within 6 h (sweep every 3 h); receipts fine for the books but buyer fields minimized.
- No buyer messaging: #15 "Use the Etsy API for purposes of transmitting spam or other unsolicited marketing communications, or to connect with any third-party advertising or marketing platform"; #16 "Use the Etsy API for purposes of sending to Etsy Members order, shipping and tracking information, whether via email, text or otherwise, unless expressly authorized in writing by Etsy". The dashboard alerts Ricky only, never buyers.
- Minimum data: #17 "Request more than the minimum amount of data needed from the Etsy API to provide Etsy sellers with the intended Application". Scopes `listings_r transactions_r shops_r`; add `listings_w` only for a feature that writes.
- Member data: #13 "Improperly handle any Etsy Member data, including processing it unless you have the necessary authorization and only to the extent compliant with your terms of service, your privacy policy and the Terms". Commercial-access apps must request `buyer_email` separately (docs, Commercial Access Review Criteria); a Seller App's `buyer_email` behavior is UNVERIFIED, so do not depend on it.
- One app, no extra keys: "Each API key may only be used for a single Application"; "Creating or attempting to create additional API keys to circumvent these limitations is strictly prohibited" (section 2). Etsy staff in https://github.com/etsy/open-api/discussions/1657 : "We do not allow users to have both Seller Apps **and** Personal/Commercial Apps." (raw `etsy-shopify-etsy-gh1657-commercial.md`). Register exactly one Seller App.
- Trademark line, prominent: "The term 'Etsy' is a trademark of Etsy, Inc. This Application uses Etsy's API, but is not endorsed or certified by Etsy." (section 1). Put it in the footer. Etsy's marks "must appear less prominently than your own branding".
- Also in the Terms: dormancy "If an Application has not made a successful call to the Etsy API for six (6) consecutive months, Etsy reserves the right to suspend"; "Service Support" (a monitored email for sellers using the app; applies to apps used by other sellers, harmless to publish); the Application Terms with a warranty disclaimer apply to third-party users (INFERRED not applicable to a one-person tool).
- Tokens: access token 1 hour; refresh token "has a longer functional lifetime (90 days)" (https://developers.etsy.com/documentation/essentials/authentication, raw `etsy-shopify-etsy-auth.md`). Whether the 90 days slide on each refresh is UNVERIFIED; the refresh response returns a new `refresh_token` that must be stored every time.
- Webhooks: Etsy signs with the Standard Webhooks scheme (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`; HMAC-SHA256 over `id.timestamp.body`); "if abs(now - webhook-timestamp) > 300: reject("Stale timestamp")"; retries "Immediately, 5 seconds, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours, 5 hours, 10 hours, 10 hours" with manual "Recover" in the portal (https://developers.etsy.com/documentation/essentials/webhooks/, raw `arch-etsy-webhooks.md`).
- Retention after token revocation: the Terms are silent on a revoked seller token for a Seller App; on "termination or suspension of your access" you "must immediately cease all use of the Etsy API and any Etsy data" (section 6). Build a `purge_etsy_data()` function too (INFERRED).

Level 2 adds encrypted backups, test/production separation, access logs and an incident policy; none of it is demanded of a custom app by the page (INFERRED), but the access-log item is cheap and worth copying (C33).

**Webhook compliance topics:** `customers/data_request`, `customers/redact`, `shop/redact` are "Mandatory compliance webhooks ... that Shopify requires for apps listed on the Shopify App Store", so not mandatory for a custom app (INFERRED from "listed on the Shopify App Store"). They are still the right shape for a deletion path: "Complete the action within 30 days"; `shop/redact` arrives "48 hours after a store owner uninstalls your app". Subscribe if free (a TOML line); handle by running the same buyer-field redaction as eBay's.

**Webhook delivery rules (VERIFIED):** HMAC in `X-Shopify-Hmac-SHA256` over the raw body with the app client secret; "Always verify HMAC before trusting payload contents"; "If you rotate your app's client secret, it can take up to an hour for the HMAC digest to be generated using the new secret"; dedupe on `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id`; "Shopify has a one-second connection timeout and a five-second timeout for the entire request"; "it retries 8 times over the next 4 hours. After 8 consecutive failures, the subscription is automatically deleted if it was configured using the Admin API" (https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/webhooks/subscribe/https). The troubleshooting page says "if failures persist, the subscription is removed" and "You don't need to re-subscribe if your app uses app-specific webhook subscriptions". Shopify's own advice: "A common practice is to also build a reconciliation job that periodically retrieves data you might have missed using Shopify APIs." `X-Shopify-Triggered-At` exists but the HMAC covers only the body, so the header is not authenticated (INFERRED) and a retry can legitimately arrive hours later: replay protection for Shopify must be the stored webhook-id set, not a timestamp window.

**Token:** client-credentials grant, "Tokens expire after 24 hours" (wave 1, https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/authentication-authorization/access-tokens/client-credentials-grant ), works only when "the app and the store belong to the same Shopify organization". Re-mint daily; no refresh token exists, so there is no 18-month cliff, but the client secret is the single point of failure (rotation procedure in section 3).

### 1.4 Sourcing-site scraping: the legal frame (later phase)

Five sentences, then the per-site class table. Not legal advice.
1. Under *Van Buren v. United States* (2021) the CFAA turns on a "gates-up-or-down" test: "one either can or cannot access a computer system, and one either can or cannot access certain areas within the system" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-783 , raw `compliance-vanburen-cornell.md`), and *hiQ v. LinkedIn* (9th Cir., April 2022 on remand) applied it to hold public-profile scraping outside the CFAA (secondary: Wikipedia and National Law Review summaries).
2. That did not save hiQ: on Nov 4, 2022 the district court found it breached LinkedIn's terms in a "mixed ruling", and the Dec 6, 2022 stipulated consent judgment (not precedential, per the NLR) entered $500,000 against hiQ, stipulated CFAA/Cal. Penal Code liability where hiQ used fake accounts, trespass to chattels, a permanent injunction against scraping "whether logged in to a LinkedIn account or not" and deletion of its code (https://www.natlawreview.com/article/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-landmark-scraping-case , raw `compliance-natlaw-hiq-settlement.md`).
3. *Meta v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal., Jan 23, 2024, Judge Chen) went the other way on contract, because Meta's terms governed a user's "use" of its products and "Bright Data did not 'use' Facebook and Instagram when it engaged in public logged-off scraping" (Farella Braun summary, https://www.fbm.com/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/ , raw `compliance-fbm-meta-brightdata.md`; secondary source), which helps only a scraper who holds no account and never accepted the terms.
4. Ricky holds accounts on and has accepted terms from eBay, Etsy, Shopify and the resale sites he would watch, so the realistic exposure is breach of contract, account termination and tort claims (trespass to chattels, as in hiQ), not criminal CFAA liability; the penalty that matters to him is losing his selling accounts, and one site (Craigslist) carries liquidated damages.

### 2.1 Webhook endpoints

What each vendor gives us to verify (VERIFIED, from sections 1.1 to 1.3):

| | eBay (account deletion; any other topics we are allowed) | Etsy | Shopify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authentication | `x-ebay-signature` (base64 JSON with key id), public key from `getPublicKey`, cache about 1 h; plus the one-time challenge handshake | HMAC-SHA256 over `webhook-id.webhook-timestamp.body`, secret = base64 part after `_` | HMAC-SHA256, base64, over the raw body, header `X-Shopify-Hmac-SHA256`, key = app client secret |
| Replay guard | Idempotency on `notificationId` (body is signed, so `eventDate` is trustworthy; retries are legitimately late) | `abs(now - webhook-timestamp) > 300` reject; the timestamp is the attempt time and changes per retry | The stored `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id` set only; `X-Shopify-Triggered-At` is not covered by the HMAC (INFERRED) and retries span 4 h |
| Event id | `notification.notificationId` | `webhook-id` ("unique ID of the webhook call (even on retries)") | `X-Shopify-Webhook-Id` (per delivery); `X-Shopify-Event-Id` groups deliveries of one action |
| Ack | `200/201/202/204`, "immediately" | any `2xx` | 200 range within 5 s ("one-second connection timeout") |

| Secret rotation | n/a (public key) | "Rotating Your Shared Secret" tutorial | HMAC may use the old secret for up to 1 h after rotation |

Receiver algorithm (one Edge Function per provider, `verify_jwt = false`, `auth: 'none'`):
1. Allow only the expected methods (POST; GET only on the eBay challenge path); anything else gets `405` (OWASP REST: "Reject all requests not matching the allowlist with HTTP response code 405 Method not allowed").
2. Cap the body before reading it (suggest 256 KB; UNVERIFIED that Supabase documents any request-body limit, the Edge limits page lists none): reject `413` on a larger `Content-Length`, and stop reading a chunked stream at the cap. OWASP API4 lists "Maximum upload file size" and "Execution timeouts" among the limits an API must define.
3. Read the raw bytes once. Verify the signature over the raw bytes before `JSON.parse` and before any database write, using WebCrypto (the Supabase example notes "Deno has no synchronous Node crypto") and a constant-time comparison ("When verifying symmetric signatures, use a constant time comparison function ... Failing to do so can expose consumers to timing-attacks and turn them into signing oracles", Standard Webhooks). Bad signature: `401` (Shopify requires `401` for invalid HMAC on compliance topics) or `412` (eBay's SDK flow); no DB write, no body logged.
4. Enforce the replay window where the vendor gives a signed timestamp (Etsy 300 s); otherwise rely on idempotency.
5. `insert into webhook_inbox (provider, event_id, received_at, body_raw, ...) ... on conflict (provider, event_id) do nothing`, commit, then return `200`. Idempotency key retention must exceed the longest retry window (Etsy about 28 h; Shopify 4 h; eBay until acked), so keep 14 days (INFERRED). Duplicates still get `200`.
6. Process from the table, never from memory. Supabase background tasks "will shut down when it reaches one of these limits" (wall clock, CPU, memory), so work promised after the response can vanish. A `pg_cron` job drains `webhook_inbox where processed_at is null` every minute and the reconciliation sweeps (section 3) cover anything lost.
7. Never put CORS headers on webhook routes; the senders are servers.
8. Rate limiting: Supabase gives no per-route limiter for Edge Functions. Reject unsigned traffic before any DB work, keep handlers cheap (CPU limit is 2 s per request), and treat a junk flood as a billing and quota risk. The Pro plan includes 2 million invocations and has a spend cap on by default; UNVERIFIED what the cap does to Edge invocations once reached. If it matters later, front the webhook host with a Cloudflare custom domain and a rate-limit rule (not researched).

### 2.2 Row Level Security, keys and roles for a single user

- Three roles matter: the browser uses the publishable key and the user's JWT (`authenticated`); only Edge Functions, `pg_cron` and the Fedora box use a secret key (`service_role`). VERIFIED: "Publishable key | No | anon", "Publishable key | Yes | authenticated", "Secret key | Not applicable | service_role"; "Policies never apply to a secret key, because service_role has the BYPASSRLS attribute."
- "A table in an exposed schema without RLS is readable and writable by any role with a grant on it. Enable RLS on every table in an exposed schema. On projects that still grant anon and authenticated by default, revoke those grants. Adding policies doesn't remove them." and "On existing projects, a new table in public starts with every privilege already granted to all three roles." So the migration template is: `alter table ... enable row level security; revoke all on ... from anon, authenticated; grant select, insert, update, delete (only what the UI needs) to authenticated;` then policies. Run Supabase's Security Advisor and `supabase test db` on policies.
- Single-user policies: gate every policy on one owner id, `using (auth.uid() = '<ricky-uuid>')` or an `owners` table, rather than "any authenticated user", and also disable sign-ups (section 2.4) so a second `authenticated` identity cannot appear. Never write `to anon using (true)`: "A policy that reads to anon using ( true ) grants every unauthenticated visitor read access".
- Tables the browser must never touch (`oauth_tokens`, `webhook_inbox`, `deletion_log`, `source_heartbeats`, Vault, any buyer-field table): no grants to `anon` or `authenticated` at all; reach them through `security definer` functions with a fixed `search_path` or through the secret key.
- Views: "Views bypass RLS by default because they are usually created with the postgres user ... In Postgres 15 and above, make a view obey the RLS policies of its underlying tables ... by setting security_invoker = true." Every reporting view gets `with (security_invoker = true)`.
- Secret key rules: "Never use a secret key in the browser or expose it to customers."; "A secret key bypasses RLS only when the request carries no user access token. If the request carries one, it runs under the RLS policies of that signed-in user". Use `sb_secret_...` keys, not the legacy JWT `service_role`: "Supabase is deprecating the anon and service_role keys by the end of 2026"; Supabase also rejects a secret key sent from a browser (`User-Agent` match, HTTP 401). Name a separate secret key per caller (`default` for Edge, `cron`, `fedora`) so one can be revoked alone; `auth: 'secret:<name>'` accepts a named key. The Fedora box should hold a key that is only allowed to call one insert RPC (wave 1 advice), not the project-wide secret key.
- Vite specifics: only the publishable key and project URL go in `VITE_*` variables; a secret key in any `VITE_*` var ships in the bundle. Add a CI grep for `sb_secret_`, `service_role`, `eyJ` in `dist/`.

### 2.3 Secrets and token storage

- Static third-party secrets (eBay cert id, Etsy keystring and shared secret, Shopify client id and secret, VAPID keys, Pushover token and user key, ntfy topic, Healthchecks ping URLs, Etsy and Shopify webhook signing secrets): Edge Function secrets (`supabase secrets set`; names must not start `SUPABASE_`; 100 secrets, 48 KiB each; local `supabase/functions/.env` in `.gitignore`). VERIFIED: "A .env file committed to Git exposes every secret in it to anyone who can read the repository."
- Rotating OAuth state (access token, refresh token, expiry): a locked-down `oauth_tokens` table or Vault. Vault: "stored using Authenticated Encryption on disk ... any backups or replication streams also preserve this encryption in a way that can't be decrypted or forged." Edge secrets cannot be rewritten by a running function every hour, which is why tokens belong in Postgres.
- Refresh with one writer: take `pg_advisory_xact_lock` (or `select ... for update`) around read, refresh and write, and persist the new refresh token in the same transaction as the new access token (wave 1, section 6). eBay documents that refresh tokens "can be unexpectedly revoked"; Etsy returns a new `refresh_token` on refresh; a lost write on either bricks the connection until Ricky re-consents.
- OWASP Secrets Management: apply least privilege to who can read a secret, prefer short-lived credentials, and automate rotation; eBay's own guidance (get-started guide) says to "Periodically reset your cert ids (client secrets)", "Never send cert IDs via email to anyone including eBay employees", and warns that "Active tokens can exist for a considerable post-reset period" (so a cert-id reset needs a DTS revoke request).
- `pg_cron` to Edge calls: the schedule-functions doc warns "Any role that can log in to your database can read the secret key while the request waits" in `net.http_request_queue` (wave 1); keep the cron key a dedicated named key, not the default.
- Healthchecks: "You should treat check UUIDs and project Ping keys as secrets. If you make them public, anybody can send telemetry signals to your checks and mess with your monitoring." (https://healthchecks.io/docs/). Store ping URLs as Edge secrets; set the check to "Only POST" if previews hit them.
- Pushover: the application token and the user key are server-side only. "As with application API tokens, user keys should be considered private and not disclosed to 3rd parties." Neither ever appears in the PWA bundle or in a push payload; the browser asks an Edge Function to "send test alert", never Pushover directly. Alert text contains item title, price and a link, no buyer name, address or email (the text transits Pushover and Apple/Google).

### 2.4 Auth: email OTP via custom SMTP

- Built-in email is not usable: 2 messages per hour, team-member addresses only, "best-effort", "non-production" (section 1.5). One failed login attempt plus one retry burns the hour. Configure custom SMTP (any provider; wave 1 priced Resend) before the first login test. Edge Functions cannot open outbound ports 25 or 587 ("Outgoing connections to ports 25 and 587 are not allowed"), but Supabase Auth's own SMTP client is configured in the dashboard and is unaffected; only functions that send mail themselves must use an HTTPS email API.
- Auth limits that still apply with custom SMTP (https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits): "Send OTPs or magic links ... Defaults to a 60 seconds window before a new request is allowed for the same user"; "Sign-ups and sign-ins ... 30 requests per 5 minutes" per IP; OTP "expire after 1 hour" by default (configurable; "strongly discouraged" beyond 86,400 s). Shorten the OTP lifetime to 10 minutes in Auth settings.
- Lock out strangers: call `signInWithOtp({ email, options: { shouldCreateUser: false } })` ("If the user hasn't signed up yet, they are automatically signed up by default. To prevent this, set the shouldCreateUser option to false") and switch off **Allow new users to sign up** ("If this config is disabled, only existing users can sign in") after creating Ricky's user. Then even a leaked publishable key plus a permissive policy has no second identity to exploit. Still UNVERIFIED from wave 1: whether the OTP flow works smoothly inside the installed iOS PWA (INFERRED: a typed code does not depend on a link opening in the right browser context, so prefer the code to the magic link).
- Add a second factor to the Supabase account itself: Going-into-prod lists "Ensure that you protect your Supabase Account with multi-factor authentication (MFA)" and "Consider enabling MFA enforcement on your organization". A passkey or TOTP on the app login is optional for one user (Supabase passkeys are in wave 1's `arch-sb-passkeys.md`), but the passkey relying-party id is bound to the domain, another reason to fix the domain first.
- Going-into-prod also lists: "Turn on SSL Enforcement", "Enable Network Restrictions for the database", the Security Advisor, and "Use a custom SMTP server for auth emails so that your users can see that the mails are coming from a trusted domain (preferably the same domain that your app is hosted on)". ### 2.5 CORS, PWA origin, push, CSP

- CORS applies only to browser-called functions (`/functions/v1/ack-alert`, `/functions/v1/test-push`). Return `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://<dashboard-domain>`, not `*` (Supabase's browser example uses `'Access-Control-Allow-Origin': '*'`; its `withSupabase` wrapper "handles CORS and preflight (OPTIONS) requests for you", so check what origin it emits and pin it). CORS is not authentication; each browser-called function still checks the user JWT (`verify_jwt` left at its default `true`: "Leave it on for user-facing functions").
- PWA origin binding (MDN, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/id , raw `compliance-mdn-manifest-id.md`): the manifest `id` "must be same-origin with the start_url"; "Cross-origin URL not allowed, falls back to start_url". A service worker is registered per origin ("The path to your service worker points to a service worker of a different origin to your app" is a listed failure) and "Service workers are restricted to running across HTTPS". A push subscription "is unique to a service worker" and its endpoint "is a unique capability URL: knowledge of the endpoint is all that is necessary to send a message to your application" (MDN Push API). Consequence: choose the production domain before installing the PWA on a phone or subscribing to push; changing it later orphans the installed app, the service worker, the push subscription and the Supabase Auth redirect allow-list in one go (INFERRED). Use the same domain for the Auth redirect URLs, the CSP, and (if added) the passkey relying party.
- Store push subscriptions in a table with no browser read grant (capability URLs are secrets).
- CSP (OWASP CSP Cheat Sheet): serve the SPA with a Content-Security-Policy whose `connect-src` is the Supabase project URL only, `script-src 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, plus `X-Content-Type-Options` and `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`. Buyer-controlled strings (item titles from listings, buyer notes) are untrusted input: render as text, never `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`. INFERRED hardening; no vendor requires it.
- Treat everything arriving from eBay, Etsy, Shopify and any scraped page as untrusted input (OWASP API10:2023: "Developers tend to trust data received from third-party APIs more than user input"; prevention: "Always validate and properly sanitize data received from integrated APIs before using it", "do not blindly follow redirects", use timeouts). Scraped or listing text that goes to an LLM must not be able to trigger actions: the matcher returns a structured score only and has no tools (INFERRED).

### 2.6 Logging and personal data

- OWASP Logging Cheat Sheet "Data to exclude" includes access tokens and sensitive personal data; do not log request bodies, tokens, signatures or buyer fields. Log event ids, provider, outcome and duration.
- Supabase Edge limits: "Maximum log message length: 10,000 characters" and "Log event threshold: 100 events per 10 seconds", so a burst of webhooks must not log per line. Retention: Free "Log retention (API & Database) 1 day", Pro "7-day log retention" (https://supabase.com/pricing). Keep your own `sync_runs` and `deletion_log` tables for anything you must prove later (eBay audit: 12.1; deletion log for account-deletion actions).
- Level 2 habit worth copying: an access log for reads of buyer fields (Shopify protected-data requirement 6). With one user and buyer fields mostly absent, a `buyer_pii_access` insert on each detail-view is enough.

### 3.1 Idempotent pollers with cursors

| Source | Push (primary) | Poll (backstop) | Cursor filter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Etsy orders/listings | webhooks `order.paid`, `order.canceled`, `order.shipped`, `listing.updated`, `inventory.out_of_stock` (wave 1) | receipts every 15 min; listings sweep (`state=active`, `sold_out`) every 3 h so the 6 h display rule always holds | receipts `min_last_modified` (in the OpenAPI spec) |

| eBay orders | none we can rely on (item-level push topics are ePN-gated, wave 1) | Fulfillment `getOrders` every 10 to 15 min | `lastmodifieddate:[ts..]` filter (Fulfillment API reference) |
| eBay watches | none | Browse `item_summary/search` per watch; `getItem` refresh of anything still displayed, at most 6 h old | `itemStartDate:[cursor..]`, `sort=newlyListed`, dedupe on `itemId` |

Rules for every poller (INFERRED engineering, driven by the vendor behaviors above):
1. One row per source in `sync_state (source, cursor, last_attempt_at, last_success_at, last_error)`. Advance the cursor only after the batch is committed; re-read an overlap window (cursor minus 10 minutes) each run; write with upserts on natural keys `(provider, external_order_id, line_item_id)` so a repeated read changes nothing.
2. One runner per source at a time (advisory lock). Supabase's own cron guidance (wave 1, 04 section 4.1) is at most 8 concurrent jobs of at most 10 minutes.
3. Edge runtime limits make long sweeps impossible in one call: CPU 2 s per request, wall clock 150 s Free or 400 s paid, so page the work and checkpoint after each page; the next tick resumes from the cursor.
4. Honor `429` and `Retry-After`; at most two retries per call (eBay Growth Check list); never loop on failure.
5. Money as integer minor units with currency; refunds and cancellations as new rows, not edits to the original sale; every row has `source_event_id` and `ingested_at`. This makes "restore from backup, then replay sweeps" safe.
6. Apply the eBay and Etsy staleness rules mechanically: a watch card whose `fetched_at` is older than 6 h is hidden (or shows "N h older than eBay"), a purge job deletes ended watch hits, and the UI always prints "as of".

### 3.2 Reconciliation sweeps

- Webhooks are the fast path; sweeps are the correctness path. Etsy retries for roughly 27.6 hours then gives up (sum of the published schedule) and Shopify stops after 4 hours, so any outage longer than that must be healed by a sweep with a wider window. After any known downtime, run `reconcile(source, since)` with `since = last_success_at - 1 day`.
- Daily full reconciliation per marketplace: compare counts and totals for the last 7 days between our ledger and the vendor, write the diff to `reconciliation_runs`, alert if it is not zero. This also satisfies eBay's 24 h freshness rule for order data.
- Daily check that Shopify still has our subscriptions: query `webhookSubscriptions`, recreate any missing topic. Shopify: "After 8 consecutive failures, the subscription is automatically deleted if it was configured using the Admin API"; app-specific (TOML-managed) subscriptions need no re-subscribe ("You don't need to re-subscribe if your app uses app-specific webhook subscriptions"), so use those and keep the daily check anyway because the Shopify troubleshooting page also says "if failures persist, the subscription is removed" without that qualifier. Warning emails go to the app's emergency developer email: set it to an address Ricky reads.
- Etsy: confirm the webhook endpoint is not "Disabled" in the portal weekly (a human checklist item; "Disabled endpoints do not receive events until re-enabled"; no auto-disable rule is documented, UNVERIFIED). Use the portal's "Recover" for a date range after any outage.
- eBay: a self-test that GETs our own deletion endpoint with a synthetic `challenge_code` daily and expects a correct `challengeResponse`; plus watch for eBay's "alert email" about a down callback (route the Alerts and Notifications email to Ricky and to the dead-man inbox).

### 3.3 Dead-man switch (Healthchecks.io)

- Every scheduled unit pings its own check: `.../start` at the beginning, success at the end, `.../fail` on error (VERIFIED: "You can append /start, /fail or /<exitcode> to the base ping URL"). With a start signal "Grace Time also sets the maximum allowed time gap between start and success signals".
- Checks (Hobbyist plan: "$0 / month, Monitor 20 jobs, 100 log entries per job"): `tick-etsy`, `tick-shopify`, `tick-ebay-orders`, `tick-ebay-watch`, `token-watchdog`, `deletion-endpoint-selftest`, `nightly-reconcile`, `weekly-db-dump`, `fedora-heartbeat`, one per Fedora job. About 12 of the 20.
- Period equals the job cadence, grace is 2 to 3 cadences (a 15-minute poll gets 45 minutes). The chain that runs the polls is `pg_cron` calling Edge Functions; if `pg_cron` or the function stops, no ping arrives and Healthchecks alerts. An in-database "last_success_at is old" query cannot catch that because it runs in the same chain.
- Alert path must not depend on the thing being watched: Healthchecks emails Ricky directly (default), with a second integration to ntfy or a webhook to a Telegram bot; whether those integrations are available on the free tier is UNVERIFIED (wave 1). Do not route dead-man alerts through Pushover alone, because the Edge Function that calls Pushover is the thing that failed.
- Send a daily "all green" digest at a fixed hour; a missing digest is itself a signal, and it exercises the Pushover path end to end (INFERRED).
- Healthchecks itself can fail or be wrong; accept that. It is a second system, not a guarantee.

### 3.5 Supabase plan, pausing, backups

- **Free is not acceptable for this system.** Free "projects are paused after 1 week of inactivity" (https://supabase.com/pricing); "Supabase pauses Free Plan projects that show low activity over a 7-day period" and the doc admits that "it's possible that usage is not enough to exclude it from automatic pausing" (wave 1 `arch-sb-pausing.md`). A paused project drops every webhook, poll and the eBay deletion endpoint. "Projects under a paid plan cannot be paused and are not subject to automatic pausing for inactivity." Free also has no backups and 1-day log retention.
- **Pro gives:** daily backups for 7 days ("Pro Plan projects can access the last 7 days of daily backups"); 7-day logs; no pausing. It does **not** give: Storage objects in backups ("Database backups do not include objects you store via the Storage API"), custom-role passwords ("daily backups do not store passwords for custom roles"), or sub-day recovery unless the PITR add-on is bought ("Projects that want to use PITR must also use at least a Small compute add-on"; "If you enable PITR, we will no longer take Daily Backups"). For a one-person shop daily backups are enough on RPO (at most one day of rows, and sweeps re-read from the vendors).
- Backups live with the same vendor. Add a weekly `supabase db dump` of the ledger and inventory (not Vault secrets) to an off-site location Ricky controls, encrypted, with its own Healthchecks ping. Free-tier advice in the same doc says "maintain off-site backups".
- **Restores must not resurrect deleted buyers.** Keep `deletion_log` (provider, hashed buyer id, deleted_at) outside the restorable set or re-apply it automatically after a restore, because eBay's rule is that "even the highest system privilege cannot reverse the deletion" (C14).
- Photos of inventory belong in Storage; they are not in the DB backup, so keep originals in Ricky's Drive (the Vintage project already does).

### 3.6 The Fedora box drops off the network

What happened (desk wiki, entry 2026-09-29): the box "was off the network from 2026-09-26 17:16 ET until Ricky fixed its Wi-Fi at 12:07 on 09-29: it was on the wrong network. It never rebooted ... so LUKS was not the cause, and its own timers kept running. Anything that needed the internet failed meanwhile", including jobs that died on `EAI_AGAIN`. About 67 hours; only the internet-dependent work failed, and nobody was told.

Design response (INFERRED):
1. Nothing compliance-critical runs on the box: no webhook receiver, no eBay deletion endpoint, no token refresh, no backups, no alert sending. It is an outbound-only, best-effort lane for scraping and heavy helpers.
2. The box proves it is alive in two independent ways: each job pings its own Healthchecks check (start, success, fail), and a 5-minute heartbeat calls an insert-only RPC into `source_heartbeats`. A missing Healthchecks ping emails Ricky within the grace period; the dashboard header shows "Fedora: last seen 3 d ago" in red from the heartbeat table.
3. Jobs that cannot resolve DNS exit non-zero immediately and ping `/fail`; they do not retry silently forever.
4. Jobs are cursor-based so a 3-day gap is closed by the next successful run. Anything time-boxed on the source (a listing that ended during the gap) is simply lost, so a Fedora-fed alert lane is stated as "may miss items during an outage", never as a guarantee.
5. The box never alerts Ricky itself; it hands a hit to an Edge Function, which dedupes and sends the push. That keeps one alert path to monitor and one place to enforce "as of" and ended-listing purges.
6. Wi-Fi power save was still on and the only saved profile was `Private42` (desk wiki); the cheapest real fix is a second saved network or Ethernet. Out of scope for the dashboard, in scope for the ops list.

### 3.7 Other failure cases worth designing for

- **Supabase outage or Edge failure**: vendors retry (Etsy up to about 27.6 h, Shopify 4 h, eBay until acknowledged), then sweeps heal; eBay's deletion callback is the one with a deadline: an outage longer than 24 hours triggers eBay's down-alert email and starts the 30-day clock, so the runbook is "restore the endpoint, or point the callback at a stand-in, inside that window". Add the Supabase status page to the runbook (not fetched).
- **Pushover or phone failure**: Pushover Emergency priority needs acknowledgement; ntfy is the documented fallback on non-2xx or an unacknowledged receipt (wave 1). iOS PWA push can revoke a subscription that fails to show a notification (wave 1, WebKit), so Pushover stays primary.
- **Clock skew**: Etsy's 300 s window and Healthchecks periods assume sane clocks. Validate webhook timestamps only inside Supabase functions, never on the Fedora box, whose clock health is unchecked (UNVERIFIED).
- **Bad deploy**: keep the last two Edge Function versions deployable, and deploy webhook receivers with `verify_jwt = false` set in `supabase/config.toml` (a redeploy that drops the flag turns every webhook into a 401 and starts vendor retry clocks).

## 6. Open questions (for Ricky or a vendor, in order of value)

1. **Etsy, written confirmation.** One email to developer@etsy.com: "My Seller App will show my own shop's receipts and listings in a private dashboard with sold alerts and sales reporting; does Terms section 5 #25 (analytics) apply? I read it as aimed at cross-shop collection." A yes closes R1.
2. **eBay Browse production access.** Smoke test result decides whether sourcing alerts are an API feature (C18).
3. **Shopify plan and app row.** Which plan is Ricky's store on (Starter, Basic, Grow)? Does a Dev Dashboard app in his own organization get Level 2 fields without review? (C16.)
4. **eBay DTS, four questions.** (a) Is a private owner-only dashboard "Public Display" for 8.1.2? (b) Is per-listing LLM inference allowed under 9.10? (c) Does 9.4 forbid a revenue-by-channel chart that includes eBay beside Etsy? (d) Does the 24-hour rule apply to stored historical order data, or only to displayed data?
5. **Etsy refresh token: sliding or absolute 90 days?** Watch the first refresh response; until known, C47 assumes absolute.
6. **Etsy Seller App and public endpoints.** Docs say "all public and OAuth-authenticated endpoints, scoped only to your registered shop"; whether a Seller App may call public listing search for other shops is unclear and we are not using it (C2).
7. **Platform numbers not published:** Supabase Edge maximum request body; what the Pro spend cap does to Edge invocations at the cap; Healthchecks free-tier integration types.
8. **Etsy `buyer_email` for Seller Apps.** Commercial-access apps request it separately; Seller App behavior is not documented. We do not store it anyway (C15).
9. **Does Shopify's Merchant Agreement requirement (6.1.2) bind a single-merchant app the merchant builds for himself?** Assumed no in substance; the one-pager costs nothing (C7).
10. **eBay Developer Program inactivity or audit practice for personal keysets** is not documented beyond 12.1 (monitor/audit); assume it can happen.
