# How much does it cost to hire an interior designer? (Hourly rates explained)

Thinking about hiring an interior designer but not sure what it costs? You are not alone. Pricing can feel confusing because designers charge in different ways. This guide breaks it all down, simply and clearly.
## What you will pay per hour
Most interior designers charge by the hour, especially for consultations and smaller projects. [According to a 2025 Studio Designer survey on design pricing](https://www.idco.studio/post/flat-fee-pricing-model), 85% of design and architecture studios use an hourly billing model.
Here is what hourly rates look like right now:
  * **Entry-level designers:** $50 to $75 per hour
  * **Mid-level designers:** $100 to $200 per hour
  * **Senior or high-end designers:** $300 to $500 per hour


[Sweeten's 2025 interior design cost guide](https://sweeten.com/blog/home-renovation-process/how-much-does-interior-designer-cost/) confirms that most designers fall in the $100 to $200 per hour range. Boutique firms or designers in major cities can charge up to $500 per hour.

## Online vs. in-person design (and what it costs)
Not every project needs someone to visit your home. Online interior design (also called e-design) has grown into a mainstream service category.
[Foyr's 2025 comparison of online vs. traditional design](https://foyr.com/learn/online-vs-traditional-interior-design/) shows clear cost differences:
  * **Online design:** $500 to $1,500 per room
  * **In-person design:** $5,000 to $30,000+ per project


Online design is handled digitally. You get mood boards, shopping lists, floor plans, and 3D renders. You purchase and install everything yourself. It takes 3 to 5 weeks instead of months.
In-person design includes site visits, project management, and hands-on coordination. It costs more, but you get full support from start to finish.
## Is hiring an interior designer worth it?
The interior design industry generated $29.2 billion in U.S. sales in 2024, up 8% from the prior year, [according to data published by the American Society of Interior Designers (ASID)](https://www.homes.com/news/interior-design-industry-sees-number-of-firms-and-sales-rise-but-period-of-correction-forecast/342761). That growth reflects real demand from homeowners and businesses who see the value.
A good designer saves you from costly mistakes. They help you avoid buying furniture that does not fit or finishes that clash. Over time, that saves money, not just aesthetics.
