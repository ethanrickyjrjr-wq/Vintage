# How to Send a Package: Domestic
Learn how to use USPS® services to send packages (like boxes, tubes, and some large envelopes) inside the U.S. and to U.S. territories and military bases:
  * Send packages directly from your home (or office), using [Click-N-Ship®](https://cns.usps.com/) to do everything you need: Pay for postage, print shipping labels, and even [Schedule a Pickup](https://tools.usps.com/schedule-pickup-steps.htm).
  * Or, get a few time-saving tips before you bring your package to a Post Office™ location.
  * If you just need to find the price of shipping a package, use the [Postage Calculator](https://postcalc.usps.com/).

**Things You'll Need**
  * The name and full address of the person or company you’re sending the package to.
  * [Packaging materials](https://store.usps.com/store/results/readypost-packaging/shipping-supplies/_/N-fnpz4fZ7d0v8v) (boxes, padding, and tape).
  * A way to pay for postage (if you’re sending from home, you can use stamps or create a prepaid shipping label using [Click-N-Ship](https://cns.usps.com/)).
  * Recommended: A kitchen or [postage scale](https://store.usps.com/store/product/shipping-supplies/usps-25lb-usb-postal-freight-scale-P_843031) (if you’re not using Flat Rate Boxes or Envelopes). Otherwise, you can weigh your package at the Post Office.

**You Should Know**
  * For some shipping methods, like Media Mail® service or sending live animals, you must bring your package to a Post Office™ counter.
  * You can get free Priority Mail Express and Priority Mail Boxes and Envelopes ([even shipped to you for free!](https://store.usps.com/store/results/free-shipping-supplies/shipping-supplies/priority-mail/priority-mail-express/_/N-alnx4jZ7d0v8vZ1dqlux5Zw7gohq)), but remember: if you use a Priority Mail box, you have to pay for Priority Mail service.
  * When [shipping to U.S. military bases abroad](https://www.usps.com/ship/apo-fpo-dpo.htm), you’ll pay domestic prices, though you’ll still need to fill out customs forms.

#### [Step 1: Ask, “Can I Send This?”](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm)
The maximum weight for a USPS package is 70 lbs. Also, there are some things that you can’t send at all (prohibited) and other things you can only send under certain conditions (restricted). Hazardous materials (HAZMAT) might be prohibited or restricted.
As a rule of thumb, if what you’re sending could harm people or cause damage (like lithium batteries), **very carefully** check the list of [Domestic Shipping Prohibitions, Restrictions, & HAZMAT](https://www.usps.com/ship/shipping-restrictions.htm).
![Ask, ‘Can I Send This?’ Prohibited items include ammunition and gasoline. Restricted items include batteries, hand sanitizer, and perfumes.](https://www.usps.com/assets/images/ship/packages/send-packages-drawer-graphics_step-1.jpg)
[ **Show More** Step 1: Ask, “Can I Send This?”](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm)
#### Prohibited Items
Some items are prohibited (completely forbidden): You can’t send them through USPS, so you’ll have to choose another carrier. Prohibited items include:
  * Ammunition, explosives, and fireworks
  * Items containing liquid mercury
  * Alcoholic beverages


#### Restricted Items
Other items are restricted: You have to follow the rules, stick to any limits, and properly package and label your items. Restricted items include:
  * Lithium batteries (like the ones that come installed inside small electronics, like laptop computers or cell phones) are usually OK to send if the items are **new** (or a manufacturer-certified repair); if the items are **used** , they can only be sent via ground transport (can’t go on planes).
  * Perishable items (like live animals, food, or plants) must be properly packaged and marked so they won't spoil or leak, and you'll need to pay an [extra handling fee](https://www.usps.com/ship/mail-shipping-services.htm#perishables).
  * Some items have special packaging requirements, or can only be sent via ground transport, like perfumes containing alcohol.


You have to follow all laws and USPS regulations. If you don’t follow the rules, you might face fines, or even criminal penalties.
If you have any questions about what you may ship through USPS, review the restricted and prohibited items list.

#### Special U.S. Addresses
**Puerto Rico**
Some Puerto Rico addresses include an urbanization or community code for a specific area or development. Addresses with an urbanization, abbreviated URB, should be written on 4 lines:
MS MARIA SUAREZ  
URB LAS GLADIOLAS

**U.S. Virgin Islands**
Virgin Islands addresses have the same format as standard addresses. The right abbreviation for this territory is "VI," not "US VI" or "USA VI":
MS JOAN SMITH  
RR 1 BOX 6601  
KINGSHILL VI 00850-9802

  * Do include unit and box numbers if they're assigned:  
  
SEAMAN JOSEPH SMITH

FPO AP 96691

#### How to Pay for Postage
You can pay for postage a few ways:
  * At the [Post Office Counter](https://tools.usps.com/locations/): A retail associate will weigh your package, help you choose a shipping method, and tell you how much postage will cost. After you pay, they’ll print the postage and put it on your package.
  * At a [Self-Service Kiosk](https://tools.usps.com/locations/home.htm?locationType=SSK): The kiosk machine will walk you through the steps of weighing your package, paying for postage (using a credit, debit, or EBT card), printing the postage and attaching it to your package, and placing it in the drop-off bin.
  * Using stamps: You can use any combination of stamps or printed postage that adds up to your total postage price. (You might need a lot of stamps, unless you’re using [higher-value stamps](https://store.usps.com/store/results?Ntt=High+Value+Stamps).) You can buy stamps at a [Post Office Counter](https://tools.usps.com/locations/) or [National Retailers](https://tools.usps.com/locations/home.htm?locationType=NR) like grocery and drug stores. You can also shop online for all stamps at [The Postal Store®](https://store.usps.com/store).  
**Note:** If you're mailing restricted hazardous materials, you can't use stamps—use one of the other methods to pay for postage. 
  * Use [Click-N-Ship](https://cns.usps.com/): You can pay online for Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express postage and you can buy Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, and USPS Ground Advantage postage at lower Commercial Rates; then, you can: 
    * Print your shipping label on your own printer. (You don’t need special label sticker paper; you can use regular printer paper—just tape the printed label to your package with clear packing tape.)
    * Or, see USPS's [options for printing shipping labels without a printer](https://www.usps.com/ship/label-broker.htm): With the USPS Label Broker® feature, you'll get a QR code that you can take with your ready-to-ship package to a [participating Post Office location](https://tools.usps.com/locations/home.htm?filters=lbroretail,lbrossk). Or, when you use [Click-N-Ship service](https://cns.usps.com/), for $1.65 per label, we’ll print the mailing label and deliver it to you.

#### Add-On Services

#### [Bonus: Package Postage Pro Tips](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm)
Don’t get tripped up on postage—follow our tips and avoid some of the most common mistakes people make when they send packages:
  * **Make sure the weight is accurate:** Use a kitchen scale or postal scale (not a bathroom scale), or have your package weighed at the Post Office. Include the weight of everything, including packing materials.
  * **Round up on weights:** Some USPS products round up to the ounce, while others round up to the pound. For example, if you’re sending a Priority Mail package that weighs 5 lbs 2 oz, you would round up to the pound and pay the 6 lb-rate.


[ **Show More** Bonus: Package Postage Pro Tips](https://www.usps.com/ship/packages.htm)
  * **Check the dimensions.** If you’re using your own packaging, dimensional (DIM) weight may apply, so always enter your package’s dimensions in the [USPS Price Calculator](https://postcalc.usps.com/) to ensure you’re paying enough for postage.
  * **Use the correct package label.** Make sure the label you’re using matches the packaging. (For example, if you use a Priority Mail Mailing Box or Envelope, you must pay the Priority Mail rate. Also, the only way to get Priority Mail Flat Rate pricing is to use a [USPS Priority Mail Flat Rate Mailing Box or Envelope](https://store.usps.com/store/results?Ntt=Flat+Rate).)
