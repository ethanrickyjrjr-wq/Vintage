## 1. eBay photo rules already verified (with quote and source file)

All from crawl4ai fetches dated 2026-10-30 (help pages) and 2026-10-27 (cutouts raw copies).

- Count and main photo. "You can add up to 24 pictures." and "The first picture you add, called the main photo, appears next to your item's title in search results." Source: research/ebay-clothing-photos-2026-10-30/photo-tips.md (https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/listings/adding-pictures-listings?id=4148). Seller Center: "Your first photo appears in search results. Make it your best shot, with your item centered and fully visible." (sellercenter-photo-tips.md).
- Size. "Images should have a minimum resolution of 500 x 500 pixels but we recommend images that are about 1600 x 1600." Seller Center says "at least 1600 x 1600". Policy page says "at least one photo that is 500 pixels on the longest side" (picture-policy.md). Different wording (longest side vs both sides), so make every side 1600 or more. Ratio: "photos in a 1:1 or 16:9 aspect ratio look best".
- File limits. "maximum 12MB each". Accepted: "JPEG, PNG, GIF, TIFF, BMP, WEBP, HEIC, or AVIF" (photo-tips.md). eBay keeps pictures 90 days.
- Background. "Display items in an uncluttered space against a neutral backdrop" and "White backgrounds are generally best, but for shiny or reflective items like a piece of jewelry, a darker background may work better" (photo-tips.md). Seller Center: "against a white/neutral background".
- Clothing-specific background rule (selling-clothes.md line 320): "Use a white or light-colored background for darker clothes, and a black or dark background for lighter clothes." This is eBay's own clothing page and it does not demand pure white.
- Light. Help page: "Reduce glare by avoiding using a flash, or overhead lighting". Seller Center: "Use natural lighting ... Avoid using flash or adding filters." (see contradiction in section 5).
- Angles for clothing. Selling-clothes.md: "Show every angle of your clothing ... a listing for a shirt should include a photo of the front, back, labels and any close-ups or unique features". Flaws: "show clear, closeup images of them" (photo-tips.md); "Highlight any imperfections ... a small hole ... or a stain" (selling-clothes.md). Size: "add a photo with a coin or ruler next to the item".
- Picture policy, not allowed (picture-policy.md): "Photos that don't accurately represent the item"; "Placeholder images used to convey messages"; "Stock photos for used, damaged, or defective items"; "Photos with added borders"; "Photos with added text, artwork or marketing material"; "Watermarks of any type, including those used for ownership attributions". Also "You can't use a catalog or stock photo if you're listing an item as used." Seller Center adds: "Don't add text, artwork, logos, or watermarks ... It can prevent your images from showing up in third-party search results."
- eBay's own background tool (photo-tips.md and sellercenter-photo-tips.md): web listing tool Background icon "automatically replace the background with a solid white color"; app: White, or AI-generated Studio, Surface, Outside; "Try to only use this option [AI scenes] for a few photos". Consequence recorded in 01-channel-image-rules.md: a white-background cutout of our own photo is something eBay itself makes, so it does not break the stock-photo or border rules.
- Single item vs bundle: "For single items, your main photos should not include any variations"; bundle main photo shows all items.
- Pre-owned condition (README.md): Pre-owned - Excellent, Good, Fair for vintage clothing categories; "Signs of wear and every imperfection must be shown and described."
- Live example (README.md, viewed in Chrome 2026-10-30, not a crawl): a Gunne Sax maxi dress, $450, main photo the whole dress on a mannequin beside a window in natural light, gallery of 6 or more. One listing only; chosen by price and watchers, so it proves a mannequin main photo sells, not that it is best practice.
- Title: 80 characters, brand type colour size material, no acronyms or ALL CAPS (README.md).

## 2. Tool decisions already made (with source)

Source for all: research/cutouts/02-cutout-tools-pipeline.md (fetched 2026-10-27) and docs/cutout-catalog-handoff.md (decisions by Ricky 2026-10-27), plus tools/cutout/cutout.py.

- Cutter: rembg 2.0.86 (MIT), pinned `-m birefnet-general -dc`, local, mask-based so original pixels are kept. Install `rembg[cpu]==2.0.86` (cutout.py header) or `rembg[cpu,cli]`. BiRefNet weights licence `mit` on the Hugging Face card (02, section 1d).
- Bria ban. "TRAP: bare `rembg i in out` runs bria-rmbg (RMBG-2.0) ... CC BY-NC 4.0, and commercial use needs a BRIA agreement." Rule: always pass `-m`; never bria-rmbg, never RMBG-1.4 (02 bottom line 2; handoff section 9; cutout.py comment "Always name the model: rembg's default (bria-rmbg) is licensed non-commercial only.").
- remove.bg: reject. 02 quote: "background removal is moving to Canva. The standalone website will no longer be available from 1 December 2026"; "Credits expire December 1, 2026"; "Go to Leonardo.ai for API Pricing". Pricing seen: $3 for 3 credits PAYG, $9/month for 40 credits.
- Photoroom: the only verified paid hosted fallback, "$0.02 per image" on https://www.photoroom.com/api/remove-background; docs say sandbox output "will produce a result with a watermark"; free accounts licensed "for your own personal, non commercial purposes"; pricing page showed no dollar amounts; API-specific terms not fetched. Photoroom used only if Ricky agrees (handoff section 5). Estimated about $2.40 for 40 items x 3 tries.
- Rejected: Google Product Studio (UI output is white, not transparent; "doesn't support images with hands, walled art, and light fixtures"; transparency only in v1alpha Merchant API); Gemini image editing (generative, SynthID watermark, no documented alpha); Apple Photos/Preview/Finder (one at a time, no batch, no mask control; Preview needs a Mac); Adobe Express (manual, good for rescuing one failed cutout by hand).
- Hard cases: BiRefNet_HR-matting (MIT, 2048 px) as the soft-alpha challenger; glass (chandelier, stemware, decanter) is NOT cut, shoot on a seamless backdrop. SAM 2 (Apache-2.0) is a manual promptable fallback. pymatting 1.1.16 needs a trimap, CPU only.
- Generative edits banned for catalog items (handoff section 1, decision 4; section 9): "it can quietly redraw the object". Mask-only. Proof check: at least 99% of pixels with alpha >= 250 within 3/255 of the source (02 section 6; implemented in cutout.py).
- Sharp 0.35.5 recommended for resize and WebP in the Node recipe, but cutout.py is Pillow only (the handoff accepts either, "one script, not two"). HEIC is not in Sharp's prebuilt formats; cutout.py reads HEIC through pillow-heif 1.8.1.
- Etsy rules (01, section 3): "Animated .gif files & transparent .png files are not supported. If a file contains transparency, the transparent parts of the image will appear black on Etsy." Formats ".jpg, .gif, .png, .svg, or .heic" (WebP not supported). "at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background". "You can't use editing to hide flaws". AI-edit disclosure is a suggestion. 2000 px recommended, first photo at least 635 px, up to 20 photos. File-size soft thresholds disagree (1 MB vs 300 KB).
- Google Merchant Center rules (01, section 1): main image the exact item; no text, border, logo, watermark, placeholder; product 75 to 90% of frame (recommendation); about 1500 px recommended; 500x500 minimum enforced from 2027-01-31 (until then 100 for non-clothing and 250 for clothing); AI-generated images must carry IPTC metadata ("TrainedAlgorithmicMedia"); white background is not stated as a rule but every example is white.
- Shopify (01, section 2): up to 5000x5000 or 25 MP, under 20 MB; "For square product images, a size of 2048 x 2048 px usually displays best"; keep a consistent aspect ratio; filenames must not end in pico, icon, thumb, testing, small, compact, medium, large, grande.
- Output sizes decided: channel flat JPEG 2048x2048, 1:1, sRGB, white #FFFFFF, quality about 85, at most 1 MB, subject 75 to 90% of frame (flat-white.jpg); Etsy variant with subject 60 to 70% (flat-etsy.jpg); site WebP 960x1200 and 480x600 at most 200 KB (our own storefront only); plus the untouched original always kept (handoff section 6). Alpha never goes to a channel.
- Machine: Windows PC RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB, i7-14700KF, 32 GB (observed locally 2026-10-27, not vendor data). Python 3.12.13 exists in crawl4ai-venv, which must not be reused. rembg needs Python >=3.11 <3.14; Fedora box (3.14) is out.
- Shoot guidance chosen (Shopify and Photoroom pages): window light only, flash off, tripod, same tripod spot all session, rotate the item not the camera, seamless paper curved up the wall, "Opt for pure white, as off-white or cream backgrounds are more challenging to edit to pure white", white foam board fills shadows, black foam board edges pale items, 90 degrees to the window, main 1x lens not 0.5x.

## 3. What tools/cutout/cutout.py does, and what it lacks for clothing

Does (116 lines, Pillow + rembg + pillow-heif, no CLI options beyond positional args `PHOTOS_FOLDER [OUTPUT_FOLDER]`):
- Reads every JPEG/PNG/WebP/HEIC/HEIF in one folder (not recursive), EXIF-orients, converts embedded ICC (Display P3) to sRGB, caps long edge at 4096.
- One rembg session, model fixed to `birefnet-general`, `decontaminate=True`; zeroes alpha under 8 and clears RGB where alpha is 0.
- Computes "pixels kept" and "solid" share; prints notes: PIXELS CHANGED (<99%), mostly see-through (<50% solid, glass), item small in frame (scale >1.25).
- Writes site/NAME.png (transparent, trimmed, max 2048) and listing/NAME.jpg (2048x2048 white, item fills 85%, quality 90). One alpha bounding box over everything kept.
- Header documents setup with uv and Python 3.12 and says glass is not cut.

Lacks for a clothing-on-a-form workflow (my inference from the code, REPO_VERIFIED as absence):
- No options or flags at all: model, fill, size, quality, background colour, folder recursion, GPU, and overwrite behaviour are hard-coded; it reprocesses every file each run and has no skip-existing or resume.
- No ghost-mannequin or form removal. The cutter keeps whatever is attached to the garment: the form, its neck block, stand pole, base, pins, and clips. The checklist already warns "The cutout keeps whatever is attached to the item" (tags, a mini disco ball).
- No item grouping: eBay wants up to 24 shots per item (front, back, labels, flaws), but the script treats each file as an independent item and names output by file stem only. No item-id naming convention check (checklist says A13-front.jpg), no ordering, no manifest (the handoff plans media/manifest.json and tools/cutout/items.json but neither exists).
- No shadow. A pure white flat with no contact shadow makes a garment on a stand look like it floats; CSS shadow is the rule for the site, and the handoff says "Bake a shadow only for marketplace images that must be flat JPEG", but cutout.py bakes none.
- Fill 85% square with centred bbox on every file, so a tall dress on a stand and a close-up of a label get the same treatment; close-up and detail shots should not be cut or re-scaled to a fixed fill. No per-photo mode (cut / flat / keep) as the handoff's items.json proposes.
- No consistent baseline or height across a set (a shared hem line or scale between items); no straighten, no white-balance, no exposure normalisation, no pure-white clamp (the near-white edge and floor can leave 250,250,250 rather than 255).
- White-on-white risk: pale garments on white. No check that the garment edge differs from the backdrop, and no dark-backdrop route even though eBay's clothing page says light clothes suit black or dark backgrounds.
- Single speck widens the bbox (code comment "ponytail ... Crop by hand if seen"); fine lace, fringe, sheer fabric and chains rely on `birefnet-general` only; no HR-matting arm, no `-a`/`-vm` retry, no side-by-side contact sheet for review.
- The "pixels kept" metric compares only opaque pixels, so it cannot detect an interior hole (a sheer sleeve or lace turned transparent and then flattened to white). The "mostly see-through" note exists, but it only fires under 50% solid.
- No metadata handling for AI edits (IPTC TrainedAlgorithmicMedia would be needed if any generative step is added; Google rule), no EXIF scrub, no file-size cap check (12 MB eBay, 1 MB Etsy soft target), no listing JPEG at 1600 variant, no Etsy 635 px check.
- The cutout.py model download is about 1 GB first run; rembg version pinned 2.0.86 in the header (see section 5). No GPU path is documented for the 4060 Ti; the header installs `rembg[cpu]` only.
- No test fixtures in tools/cutout; the only prior verification is the pilot plan in 02 section 7 (chandelier, pink glasses, boots, urn lamp), none for clothing on a form.
