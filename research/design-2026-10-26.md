# Hollywood and Vine: photo and category direction

Research requested October 26, 2026: five Luna agents, with crawl4ai fetching primary shop pages. Five separate agents completed the bounded research. Most evidence is page content and image metadata; YOWIE was also inspected visually. Markdown alone does not establish rendered spacing or proportions.

## Applied to the storefront

- Give the four peer departments equal 4:5 portrait frames, a shared top edge and matching caption placement. The photographs provide variation; the browsing grid provides order.
- Use four equal desktop columns and two mobile columns. Gutters are 32px on desktop, 26px on smaller desktops, and 20px on phones. Mobile rows have 32px separation, with space reserved for two-line category titles.
- Leave collage angles in the editorial room-story section. Arbitrary shapes in every category tile would compete with the already varied merchandise and make scanning harder.
- Replace homepage preview photos and video stills with the real full-resolution photographs. Serve 480, 960 and 2048px-wide versions responsively, plus 3840px for the hero; never enlarge beyond the original. Convert the originals' Display P3 colors to sRGB before removing embedded metadata. The hero source is 4284 × 5712px, compared with the previous 1050 × 1400px preview. Omit near-duplicate and unused large exports from deployment.
- Restore the featured glassware and trench crops from the original photographs. Keep the actual walkthrough video and the preparation labels for items represented only by video stills.

## Five research assignments

1. **Vintage interiors:** [Chairish](https://www.chairish.com/) provides a dedicated category collection distinct from editorial promotions and staff picks. Its named image-led categories support separating predictable browsing from expressive editorial content. The 1stDibs attempt returned a verification wall and was excluded.
2. **Vintage fashion:** [Georges Vintage](https://georgesvintage.com/) and [Pop Sick Vintage](https://popsickvintage.com/) organize personality-rich merchandise into clearly named collections. Use a strong section heading, category-specific photography and concise labels rather than extra decorative controls.
3. **Eclectic boutiques:** [YOWIE](https://www.shopyowie.com/) and [Coming Soon](https://comingsoonnewyork.com/) connect distinctive objects to a recognizable shop identity. YOWIE's observed collection treatment was the clearest visual reference for consistent image framing and repeated title placement.
4. **Physical-shop atmosphere:** [Sunbeam Vintage](https://sunbeamvintage.com/) pairs category images with short descriptions and direct shop actions, then groups additional collections separately. Its category image assets share square dimensions. [The Vintage Shop LA](https://thevintageshopla.com/) uses consistent portrait product assets, but is a contemporary wholesale brand and a weaker stylistic reference.
5. **Category navigation:** [Lost & Loved](https://lostandloved.co.uk/) and [Rething](https://www.rething.uk/) informed clear category naming and caption hierarchy. A whole clickable card with a visible label is our implementation recommendation, not a measured property of the crawled pages.

The individual crawl4ai captures and agent notes are saved beside this file with `luna1` through `luna5` in their names. Failed or empty captures are not design evidence.

## Ideas worth developing later

- **Room edits:** small cross-category selections such as brass and glass, using the real room photograph to connect a lamp, glassware and art. Curate from actual available inventory.
- **Why we picked it:** brief item stories about a texture, shape or period detail. State uncertainty where maker, date or authenticity is unverified.
- **New finds:** a clearly marked selection when real new stock arrives, supported by consistent product photography. Avoid invented release schedules or stock counters.

These are research recommendations, not claims of newly shipped storefront features.
