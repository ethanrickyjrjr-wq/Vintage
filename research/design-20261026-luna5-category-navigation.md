# Category navigation and image frames: boutique commerce references

**Research date:** 2026-10-26  
**Scope:** Homepage collection navigation, labels, tap-area evidence, and a design direction for Hollywood and Vine's four “Pick your obsession” category tiles.  
**Method:** Both primary pages were fetched with the existing `crawl4ai` command using `-o md -bc`; raw outputs are saved as [`Lost & Loved homepage crawl`](lostandloved-home-20261026-luna5.md) and [`Rething homepage crawl`](rething-home-20261026-luna5.md). These are commerce sites selling curated vintage/home objects, selected as references distinct from Chairish, 1stDibs, Georges Vintage, Pop Sick Vintage, ComingSoon, Yowie, and Sunbeam Vintage.

## Sources and observed page content

1. [Lost & Loved](https://lostandloved.co.uk/) — its homepage presents “Lost & Loved Vintage Homeware Store,” a one-sentence curation promise, and a direct “Shop Vintage Homeware” action before a “Shop By Collection” group. That group names and links four destinations: Vintage Homeware, Prints & Artwork, Vintage Clothing, and Past Treasures. The page then switches to “Featured collection” product links, prices, and stock states. The named collection links are direct and readily distinguishable in the extracted reading order.
2. [Rething](https://www.rething.uk/) — its homepage has a “Featured Collections” section with three image/label pairs: Colorful Glasses, Lighting, and Stainless Steel. Each label links to a specific collection (`/collections/fun-glasses`, `/collections/lighting`, `/collections/stainless-steel`). Below it, “New Arrivals” presents product images and longer descriptive names, separating category browsing from one-off product discovery.

## Three lessons for Hollywood and Vine

1. **Give the category group a clear name, then keep each caption short and literal.** Lost & Loved uses the heading “Shop By Collection” and labels that describe where a tap goes; Rething's “Featured Collections” does the same for thematic edits. For the four H&V tiles, keep “Pick your obsession” as the editorial heading and use concise category names (for example, Clothing, Lighting, Furniture, Art & Objects). A small supporting line can add personality if needed, but should not compete with the destination name. This follows the observed separation between section heading and linked destination labels.
2. **Make the picture and its caption behave as one obvious category target.** Both crawls show image/label content associated with collection destinations, but Markdown extraction does not establish the actual rendered hit area, nor whether the whole image-card is clickable. Treat full-card linking as a recommendation to verify in the implementation: the photo, label, and any small arrow should lead to the same category, with enough padding around the text to avoid a tiny caption-only target. This is an implementation recommendation, not a claim about either reference site's tap geometry.
3. **Use one consistent frame system for the four primary categories; let the photography provide the variation.** Rething supplies a useful model for image-led collection shortcuts, while Lost & Loved demonstrates that category names can stay scan-friendly even when the shop's products vary. H&V's four choices are peers, so a shared aspect ratio, crop rule, and caption baseline will help visitors compare them quickly. Let the eclectic shop personality come from the image choice and image content (burgundy, neon pink, gold shelving, chrome, orange rug, fashion, lighting, art, prints), rather than mixing unrelated frame shapes. If one category deserves editorial emphasis, make a deliberate asymmetric composition with a visibly larger lead tile and three aligned supporting tiles; avoid slight, arbitrary shape differences across all four.

## What this crawl can and cannot establish

The crawls establish section order, category wording, the destination URLs, and that Rething's featured-collection entries include image references. The extracted Markdown does **not** show rendered tile dimensions, image ratios, gutters, caption alignment, breakpoint behavior, or the clickable hit-area boundaries. No numerical spacing or visual-size claims are made here. Those details require inspecting screenshots or the live page in a browser. The equal-frame recommendation is a design judgment for four equally important navigation choices, informed by the references' separation of collection browsing from product listing—not a measured description of either site.
