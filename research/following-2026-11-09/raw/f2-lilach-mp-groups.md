**Facebook Marketplace groups work by letting you post listings into buy-and-sell communities where local, ready-to-buy members see them alongside your main Marketplace listing.** I join groups tied to my town, my niche and nearby areas, then post the same listing into each one for wider reach without extra effort. Group posts often get faster replies than Marketplace alone, since members check them daily looking to buy. Just check each group's rules first, some limit posts per day.
**The short version:** Facebook Marketplace groups sell more stuff when you join a small, deliberate mix of local and niche groups, post at the times those specific groups are busiest, and relist rather than edit. Most sellers join fifty groups and post once. That is why most sellers make almost nothing from them.
## Why groups outsell the main Marketplace feed[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-0-why-groups-outsell-the-main-marketplace-)
The main Marketplace tab shows buyers what Facebook's algorithm thinks they want. Groups show buyers what other members in that group have decided is worth their time, because someone has to approve you into the group, and the members already share a postcode, a hobby, or a reason to trust each other. A listing in a well-run local group with 40,000 members gets seen by people who are actively hunting for exactly your kind of thing that week, not scrolling past it in a general feed.
I found this out the unglamorous way. In early 2024 I was downsizing my home office after cutting my consultancy back to a smaller team, and I had a standing desk, an old MacBook, four filing cabinets, and a box of unopened stationery to shift. I listed everything on the standard Marketplace tab first. Ten days, three enquiries, zero sales. I then joined eleven groups, a mix of local "buy and sell" groups for my two nearest towns, one home office declutter group, and one Apple resale group with strict rules about proof of condition. Same items, same photos, better titles. Fourteen items sold in nine days for a total of 1,140 pounds. The difference was not the product. It was where I put it.
## Step one: pick the right groups, not the most groups[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-1-step-one-pick-the-right-groups-not-the-m)
Joining forty groups feels productive and does almost nothing. Facebook throttles how many groups you can post the same listing to in a short window, and it can flag your account for spam if you blast identical text everywhere at once. Aim for eight to twelve groups that fit one of three categories:
  * Local groups for your town and the two or three towns nearest you, since most Marketplace buyers will not travel more than 20 minutes for a collection item under 50 pounds
  * Category groups that match what you sell (baby items, tools, vintage clothing, office furniture) because these buyers are already primed to buy that thing
  * One or two "declutter" or "moving house" groups, which tend to have buyers looking for job lots rather than single items, useful if you have a lot to shift quickly


If you are struggling to find groups you have already joined months ago and forgotten about, this [walkthrough on where to find your Facebook groups in settings](https://www.lilachbullock.com/where-to-find-facebook-groups-in-settings/) shows you exactly where they hide on the app, desktop and browser versions, because Facebook has moved this menu at least three times in the last two years.

## Step two: write the listing once, adapt it for each group[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-2-step-two-write-the-listing-once-adapt-it)
Copying the exact same wording into ten groups looks lazy and buyers notice. It also gets flagged faster by Facebook's spam detection than varied text does. Change the first line for each group. For a local group, lead with the town name and collection point ("Collection from Reigate, near the station"). For a category group, lead with the specific detail that group cares about ("Genuine Herman Miller Aeron, size B, mesh in good condition, no rips"). This takes an extra two minutes per group and roughly doubles the response rate compared with pasting identical text everywhere, based on what I tracked across my own sales that month using a simple spreadsheet of views versus messages per group.
### The pricing move that gets replies
Price 10 to 15 percent above what you will accept, and say "open to reasonable offers" in the description. Buyers in Marketplace groups expect to negotiate, and a fixed "no offers" listing gets fewer messages even when the price is fair. I sold the standing desk for 65 pounds after listing it at 75, and the buyer messaged within four minutes because the price gave them room to feel like they'd won something.
## Step three: time your posts to the group, not the clock[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-3-step-three-time-your-posts-to-the-group-)
General advice says post in the evening. That is only half true. Local buy-and-sell groups peak on Sunday afternoons between 4pm and 8pm, when people are planning the week and thinking about what they need. Category groups tied to hobbies, like a vintage clothing or tool-collecting group, peak midweek in the evening, around 7pm to 9pm, because that is when hobbyists browse rather than shop for a household need. I ran the same listing on a Tuesday morning and a Sunday evening in the same local group and the Sunday post got three times the comments in the first hour.
Check each group's own "Insights" if you're an admin, or simply watch when other posts in that group get the most comments over a week, and match your posting to that window rather than a generic rule.

## Step four: relist instead of editing[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-4-step-four-relist-instead-of-editing)
This is the bit most guides skip because it sounds like a trick, but it's just how the system works. If an item has been sitting for a few days with no interest, editing the price or description does not push it back up the feed in most groups. [Deleting the listing](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-delete-a-facebook-marketplace-listing/) and posting it fresh, with a slightly different photo order and a new first line, does. Facebook's Marketplace ranking leans heavily on recency, so a five-day-old listing with a perfect description will often sit behind a two-hour-old listing with a worse one. I relist anything that hasn't sold within 72 hours rather than tinkering with the wording, and it consistently gets more views the second time round.
## The uncomfortable part nobody tells you[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-5-the-uncomfortable-part-nobody-tells-you)
Here is the bit that sits badly with the "community marketplace" framing most sellers use. A large share of the active buyers in busy local groups are resellers, not neighbours. They are running the same numbers you are, watching for underpriced job lots, DMing within seconds of a post going live, and flipping items on for a margin. If you are hoping for a warm, trusting local exchange every time, you will be disappointed by how many messages are "final price, collecting now" from someone who clearly buys and resells for a living. This isn't a problem to solve, it's just what the marketplace has become, and once you accept it you stop taking lowball offers personally and start treating them as one more channel where volume and speed matter more than sentiment. If you want genuine community buyers rather than dealers, category groups tied to a specific hobby tend to have far fewer resellers than general local "buy and sell" groups, because the resale margin on niche items is thinner and the knowledge needed to flip them is higher.

## Cross-posting without getting your account restricted[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-6-cross-posting-without-getting-your-accou)
Posting the same item into several groups at once is fine, but doing it too fast, or into groups that are formally linked, can trip Facebook's spam checks. If you run or help admin a set of related groups, there's a cleaner way to cross-post that Facebook itself supports, and this [guide on linking related Facebook groups together](https://www.lilachbullock.com/link-related-facebook-groups-together/) walks through the exact settings so a listing posted in one group can surface in the linked ones without you manually pasting it ten times.

## When Marketplace groups become a proper income stream[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-8-when-marketplace-groups-become-a-proper-)
Some sellers treat this as a one-off clear-out. Others build a genuine sideline buying job lots, cleaning them up, and reselling piece by piece across a handful of groups.   * Does your price leave room for a small negotiation rather than being your bottom line

## Frequently asked questions[#](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-marketplace-groups-sell-more/#h-10-frequently-asked-questions)
### How many Facebook groups should I post a Marketplace item in?
Eight to twelve groups is a sensible range: enough local and category coverage to reach real buyers without triggering Facebook's spam checks for posting identical listings too widely, too fast.
### Why is my item not selling even though it has lots of views?
Views without messages usually means the price feels fixed rather than negotiable, or the first photo isn't strong enough to earn a second look; try repricing with slight room for offers and relisting with a sharper first image rather than editing the existing post.
### Do Facebook Marketplace groups take a cut of my sale?
No. Groups are free to post in and Facebook does not take a percentage of local, cash or bank-transfer sales arranged through group listings; fees only apply if you use Facebook's own shipping and checkout feature for certain categories.
### Is it worth joining groups outside my local area?
Only for lightweight, postable items. For anything requiring collection, buyers rarely travel far, so your time is better spent in a tight ring of nearby local groups plus one or two category groups rather than joining groups across the country.
