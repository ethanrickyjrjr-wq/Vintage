##  Why Facebook Marketplace is a Goldmine for Resellers
Marketplace is where your neighbors sell the stuff they want gone, and a lot of it is priced to move rather than priced to its value.
###  The Numbers
  * **Users:** Mark Zuckerberg said on Facebook’s April 2021 earnings call that Marketplace had reached one billion users. Meta hasn’t published a newer count.
  * **Categories:** everything from furniture to electronics to vehicles
  * **Fees:** $0 for local pickup; shipped Marketplace orders cost 10% of the whole order (item, shipping and tax), with a $0.80 minimum, under Meta’s [Seller Protection Policy](https://www.facebook.com/legal/merchant_policies) (checked Oct 16, 2026)
  * **Prices:** set by each seller; Facebook publishes no sold prices, which is why this page uses eBay’s


###  Is Facebook Marketplace Still Worth It in 2026?
Yes, if you source in categories with a real spread, can comp a listing fast enough to beat slower buyers, and know where the item goes next (local resale, eBay, OfferUp or another marketplace). It’s at its best for bulky local items like furniture, gym equipment, tools and outdoor gear, for cleanouts where the seller wants speed more than price, and in metro areas dense enough that pickups don’t eat your day. It’s a weak bet if you only want small shippable items with national demand, live somewhere rural with few listings and long drives, or can’t comp a category in a minute or two. Then thrift stores, estate sales or eBay usually pay better for the same hours.
If Marketplace is missing from your account, check Meta’s app and help center. Access varies by account, country, age and policy eligibility, and only Meta can tell you why it’s missing.
###  2026 Official Marketplace Rules to Verify Before You Flip
Marketplace flipping splits into two different businesses: **local pickup sourcing/resale** and **shipped Marketplace selling**. Treat them differently.  
| Rule area  | Current official check  | Reseller impact  |  
| --- | --- | --- |  
| Local pickup fees  | Local pickup sales don’t carry a Facebook selling fee when you take payment outside Facebook checkout.  | This is why bulky local inventory can beat eBay after fees and shipping. Still track payment risk and keep records for taxes.  |  
| Shipped-order fee  | Meta’s Seller Protection Policy: a selling fee of 10% per transaction, $0.80 minimum, worked out on the whole transaction (the item, any shipping and taxes). Items shipped together are one transaction. Source: [Meta Seller Protection Policy](https://www.facebook.com/legal/merchant_policies).  | Don’t use the old 5% math. A $40 item with shipping and tax costs you more than $4, before packaging, postage, returns and payout timing.  |  
| Shipping responsibilities  | Sellers on shipped orders need to ship within 7 calendar days of confirming the order. Source: [Facebook shipping policies and best practices](https://www.facebook.com/help/464174927656471).  | If you can’t pack and ship fast, keep the item local or sell it on a platform where your shipping workflow is stronger.  |  
| Prohibited items  | Listings must follow Meta’s Commerce Policies and the Facebook Community Standards. Facebook’s own summary names counterfeits and knockoffs, healthcare products, recalled products, animals and services. Source: [Facebook Help, things that can’t be listed](https://www.facebook.com/help/130910837313345).  | Check the category before you source it. For baby gear, car seats and anything with a motor or battery, look the model up on the US Consumer Product Safety Commission’s recall list before you buy.  |  
| Safety and scams  | Facebook tells users to stop communicating and cancel the transaction when suspicious activity appears, and to inspect items before completing in-person purchases. Sources: [buy and sell responsibly](https://www.facebook.com/help/1156544111079919), [Marketplace scam guidance](https://www.facebook.com/help/2374002556073992).  | A deposit request, pressure to pay off Facebook, stock photos, a vague identity or a changed pickup spot: pass unless it’s cleared up.  |  
So local pickup is still where you pay no fee and no shipping, while shipped Marketplace orders need fee math closer to other marketplaces.

##  The Facebook Marketplace Advantage
###  1. Zero Selling Fees (Local Pickup)
Buying on Marketplace costs nothing extra, and reselling locally with pickup carries no selling fee either. Compare that with reselling on:
  * eBay: 13.6% + $0.40 per order on most categories (payment processing included)
  * Mercari: 10% selling fee on the item and the shipping the buyer pays, with no separate processing fee ([Mercari Help](https://www.mercari.com/us/help_center/article/169/))
  * Poshmark: 20% on items of $15 or more ($2.95 under $15)


For every Marketplace fee case, see our [Facebook Marketplace fees](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/facebook-marketplace-fees-2026) guide.
**Worked example:** Buy an item for $50 on Marketplace and sell it for $150 on eBay.
  * eBay fees (13.6% + $0.40): $20.80
  * Left after the fee: $129.20
  * After what you paid: $79.20, before shipping


eBay fees verified Oct 1, 2026 ([eBay Help](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)).
Resold locally on Marketplace there’s no fee at all, though a local buyer usually pays less than eBay’s price; for bulky items the saved shipping more than makes up for it, which is why they’re the classic Marketplace flip.
###  2. Motivated Sellers Who Don’t Know Values
Common seller motivations:
  * Moving and needing everything gone by a date
  * Downsizing or clearing out a relative’s home
  * Needing cash quickly
  * Decluttering and wanting it out of the garage


These sellers price for speed. A KitchenAid mixer that sold for a median $150 on eBay can turn up locally for much less because someone wants the counter space back.
###  3. Local Pickup = No Shipping Hassles
Bulky, heavy items that are a nightmare to ship (furniture, appliances, exercise equipment) are natural Marketplace flips.
**Example:** the original Peloton Bike sold for a median $280 on eBay over 13 weeks (35 sales), and many of those listings were local pickup only. Resold locally, you skip the freight and the fee. Standard bicycles follow the same local-flip logic: [bicycles are a steady bulky category on Marketplace](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/bicycles-flipping-guide), sourced from garage clear-outs and sold to buyers who want to ride them first, with demand peaking in spring and summer.
###  4. Same-Day Cash Transactions
A local sale can turn around in a day when the price is right, and a cash sale has no payout to wait for.
The faster the cash comes back, the sooner you can buy the next item.
###  5. Arbitrage Opportunities Between Platforms
What sells cheap on Marketplace can sell for more on:
  * eBay (national audience instead of local)
  * Poshmark (fashion focus)
  * OfferUp (a different set of local buyers)
  * Mercari (general items, simple fee)


**Example:** vintage Nike jackets and windbreakers sold for a median $35 on eBay (136 sales). At that price one jacket barely pays for the trip, so buy them inside cheap “used clothes” bundles and sell the good ones on eBay, where buyers search for the brand.
For items with strong neighborhood appeal (furniture, tools, exercise equipment), [Nextdoor is another underused local selling channel](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/how-to-sell-on-nextdoor-reselling-guide-2026), where buyers are your neighbors and pickups are short.

##  Setting Up Your Profile for Trust and Visibility
Your profile affects whether sellers respond to you and accept your offers.
###  Profile Optimization Checklist
**1. Profile Photo**
  * A clear photo of yourself (not a logo, meme, or blank)
  * Why: sellers answer real people faster than anonymous accounts


**2. Cover Photo (Optional but Helpful)**
  * Shows you’re an active Facebook user
  * Anything family-friendly works


**3. Bio (Optional)**
  * “Local buyer, cash in hand, quick pickups”
  * Tells sellers you’re serious


**4. Marketplace Activity**
  * List a few low-value items to build ratings
  * Reply to messages quickly
  * Complete transactions to earn good ratings


**5. Ratings**
  * Aim for consistently good ratings
  * Show up on time for pickups

Sellers can see:
  * Your ratings
  * How quickly you usually reply
  * How long you’ve been on Facebook


A buyer with good ratings and a real profile gets answers. A blank profile gets ignored.

##  Finding Underpriced Deals: Search Strategies
Marketplace search shows you a lot of what you didn’t ask for. Here’s how to work around it.
###  Strategy 1: Saved Searches and Alerts
**Manual method:**
  1. Search for specific items (“Herman Miller chair”, “PS5 console”, “Lululemon”)
  2. Sort by “Date listed: newest first”
  3. Save or bookmark the search
  4. Check it a few times a day


**Browser extension method:**
  * Use a page monitor such as Distill Web Monitor (Chrome/Firefox)
  * Have it check your saved searches every 30 to 60 minutes
  * Get a notification when new listings appear


**Why it matters:** good deals go to whoever messages first, so the new listings are the ones worth watching.
###  Strategy 2: Misspellings and Vague Titles
Most buyers search correctly (“Herman Miller Aeron”). Search the way careless sellers type:
**Examples:**
  * “Herman Millar” (common misspelling)
  * “Areon chair” (typo)
  * “Nice office chair” (vague listing = seller may not know the brand)


**Why this works:** fewer buyers find these listings, so there’s less competition and more room to negotiate.
###  Strategy 3: “Free” and “$1” Listings
Sellers sometimes list items as “free” or “$1” to get attention, with the real price in the description.
**Filter to:**
  * Sort by price: low to high
  * Check the cheapest listings in your categories
  * Read the description: some are mispriced, some are bait


###  Strategy 4: Category Browsing + Newest First
Instead of searching, browse categories:
  * Furniture → Office
  * Electronics → Video Games
  * Home Goods → Appliances


Sort by newest first to see what was posted in the last hour or two.
**Why it works:** you see items before most buyers find them in search.
###  Strategy 5: Radius Expansion
**Widen the radius for** (our rule of thumb):
  * High-value items ($300+)
  * Rare or specialty items
  * Trips where the profit is worth the drive

##  The Flip Decision: Buy or Pass in 60 Seconds
You find a listing. Here’s a framework to decide in about a minute:
###  The 60-Second Framework
**Step 1: Visual Inspection (10 seconds)**
Look at photos:
  * Is it complete? (All parts, accessories)
  * What’s the condition? (Scratches, wear, damage visible?)
  * Does it work? (Check description for “as-is” or “for parts”)


**Red flags:**
  * “As-is” or “sold as-is” (often broken)
  * Blurry photos (hiding damage)
  * Only 1 photo (low-effort listing = might be scam)


**Step 2: Price Check (30 seconds)**
Open the eBay app, search sold listings for the exact item, and note the middle price of the last 10 to 20 sales (the median: half sold for more, half for less).
**Quick math (this page’s rule of thumb):**
  * Median sold price × 0.50 = most to pay (for local resale)
  * Median sold price × 0.40 = most to pay (for eBay resale)


**Example:** A Vitamix blender is listed at $80. Vitamix blenders sold for a median $150 on eBay.
  * Most to pay for an eBay flip: $150 × 0.40 = $60
  * Most to pay for a local flip: $150 × 0.50 = $75
  * Asking $80 is over both: offer $60 to $75 or pass


**Step 3: Profit Potential (10 seconds)**
Work out net profit:
  * Reselling on eBay: sold price − fees (13.6% + $0.40) − shipping − what you paid
  * Reselling locally: sold price − what you paid (no fees, but count your gas and time)


**Your floor (our rule of thumb):** at least $20 profit after fees and shipping on anything you drive for, and more for bulky items that take a truck or an afternoon. The 40% line usually clears it on items that resell for $100 or more; under $50, it rarely does unless the item is nearly free.
**Step 4: Message or Pass (10 seconds)**
  * Profit meets minimum → Message seller immediately
  * Profit is marginal → Pass (not worth time)
  * Need more info → Ask specific question


**Total time:** about a minute
###  Adjust the Comp, Then Set Your Walk-Away Price
Ignore the seller’s “paid $400 new” line. Retail price doesn’t matter for a used item. What matters is what the same model sold for used, and whether it’s on sale new right now or has been replaced by a newer model. Then trim the sold comp before you run the max-buy math: knock it down for worse condition, missing cords, lids or accessories, cleaning or repair risk, and the drive. If shipping would eat the margin, comp it as a local-only sale. Pick your top number before you leave home, and if the seller won’t meet it, walk away.
###  A Worked Example: A Steelcase Leap Chair
**Listing:** “Steelcase Leap office chair - $100”
  1. **Visual check:** photos show the Steelcase label, all adjustments and arms, light wear (10 sec)
  2. **Price check:** whole Steelcase Leap chairs sold for a median $299 on eBay (24 sales in 13 weeks) (20 sec)
  3. **Quick math:** $299 × 0.40 = $119 most to pay for an eBay resale; $299 × 0.50 = $149 for a local one. Asking $100 is under both (10 sec)
  4. **Profit check:** the best case is an eBay local-pickup sale at the median, where eBay’s fee leaves $257.94: $157.94 over the $100 you paid. A local Marketplace buyer will likely pay less than $299, but even if it only sold for half the eBay median ($149), you’d make $49 over the $100 you paid, well above the $20 floor (10 sec)
  5. **Decision:** worth the drive. Message the seller. (10 sec)


The numbers that decide it are the comp and your top price. Only 24 Leaps sold on eBay in 13 weeks, so plan to sell it locally and treat the eBay median as a ceiling.

##  What “Virtual Flipping” Means
People use the phrase for two different things.

##  Negotiation Scripts That Work
You found an item worth buying, but the price is a bit high. Here’s how to negotiate.
###  Script 1: The Quick Cash Offer
**Use when:** the price is over your top number
**Message:** “Hi, Is this still available? I can pick up today with cash. Would you take [offer]?”
**Why it works:**
  * “Cash” = a real buyer, not a tire-kicker
  * “Today” = urgency, saves the seller time
  * A direct offer cuts the back-and-forth


**Example:** A KitchenAid mixer is listed above your top number. KitchenAid stand mixers sold for a median $150 on eBay, so your local top is $75: offer that, cash today.
###  Script 2: The Bundle Discount
**Use when:** the seller has several items
**Message:** “Hi, I’m interested in [Item A] and [Item B]. Would you do [total] for both if I pick up today?”
**Why it works:** One trip instead of two. Sellers like moving several items in one go.
**Example:** A desk and a chair listed separately: offer a round number for both, about a fifth under the two prices added together.
###  Script 3: The Condition Acknowledgment
**Use when:** the item has visible wear or damage
**Message:** “Hi, I see [item] has [specific issue]. I’m still interested, but would you take [lower offer] considering the [issue]?”
**Why it works:** It shows you’re not lowballing blindly: you have a reason.
**Example:** An office chair with a torn armrest: price the repair, take it off your top number, and offer that.
###  Script 4: The Comparison
**Use when:** similar items are listed for less
**Message:** “Hi, I’m interested, but I saw [similar item] listed for [lower price]. Could you match that?”
**Why it works:** It’s proof the price is high, and sellers don’t want to lose the sale.
**Example:** Two PS5s listed nearby at different prices: ask the higher one to match the lower one.
###  Script 5: The Patient Buyer
**Use when:** the item has been listed for a week or more
**Message:** “Hi, I noticed this has been listed for a while. Still available? I can do [offer] cash, pickup this weekend.”
**Why it works:** It hints the price isn’t getting bites. Time to lower it.
**Example:** A couch that has sat for ten days: offer about a third under the asking price and see what comes back.
###  Negotiation Rules
**Do:**
  * Be polite and respectful
  * Offer cash (builds trust)
  * Pick up quickly (shows seriousness)
  * Have exact change ready


**Don’t:**
  * Make insulting offers
  * Renegotiate at pickup (pay the agreed price)
  * Waste the seller’s time with endless questions
  * Show up late or ghost

##  Safety and Scam Prevention
For more on scams, read our guide on [Facebook Marketplace scams to avoid in 2026](https://www.underpriced.app/blog/facebook-marketplace-scams-to-avoid-2026).
###  Common Scams (and How to Avoid)
**Scam 1: Counterfeit Designer Items**
**How it works:** fake Gucci bags, replica sneakers, bootleg electronics listed as authentic
**Red flags:**
  * Price too good to be true
  * Seller has many designer items
  * Photos are stock images (reverse image search)
  * “Authentic replica” in the description (that means fake)


**Solution:** only buy designer if you can authenticate it. When in doubt, pass. Counterfeits aren’t allowed on Marketplace at all.
**Scam 2: Bait-and-Switch**
**How it works:** photos show a perfect item, but at pickup it’s damaged or a different item
**Red flags:**
  * Seller won’t send more photos
  * Vague answers to condition questions
  * Stock photos


**Solution:** ask for a photo with a note showing your name and the date. Check it’s their actual item.
**Scam 3: Payment Scams (Fake Checks, Zelle Scams)**
**How it works:** a “buyer” sends a fake check or a fake payment confirmation and asks you to ship before the money clears
**Red flags:**
  * They want shipping on a local-pickup listing
  * Overpayment and a request to refund the difference
  * Pressure to ship right away


**Solution:** cash or a payment you can see in your own account for local sales. Never ship before payment clears.
**Scam 4: The Phantom Listing**
**How it works:** the listing looks great, but the seller vanishes after you message, or asks for a deposit
**Red flags:**
  * Asks for a deposit by Venmo or Zelle before you see the item
  * Won’t meet in public
  * Pressures you to commit without seeing the item


**Solution:** never send money before seeing the item in person. Public meetups only.
###  Safety Best Practices
**1. Meet in Public Places**
Safe locations:
  * Police station parking lots (many have “exchange zones”)
  * Bank parking lots (cameras everywhere)
  * Busy shopping center parking lots
  * Coffee shop parking lots


**Avoid:**
  * Their house when you’re alone
  * Your house (a stranger learns where you live)
  * Deserted areas


**2. Daytime Meetings**
  * Meet in daylight
  * Avoid late-night pickups


**3. Bring Someone With You**
  * A friend or family member
  * Especially for high-value items
  * Especially if the seller wants to meet at their home


**4. Tell Someone Where You’re Going**
  * Share your location with a friend or partner
  * Share the listing
  * Set a check-in time


**5. Cash Handling**
  * Bring exact change (don’t flash large bills)
  * Count money in your car before getting out
  * Keep cash in a front pocket


**6. Inspect Before Paying**
  * Test electronics (bring a charger if needed)
  * Check for damage
  * Check it’s complete (all parts and accessories)
  * Walk away if it doesn’t match the photos or description


###  Red Flags to Avoid
**Profile red flags:**
  * A brand-new profile
  * No profile photo
  * No ratings
  * Only a few listings, all high-value items


**Listing red flags:**
  * Stock photos only
  * A price far below what it sells for
  * A vague description (“nice tv”, “works good”)
  * “Sold as-is, no returns” (often broken)


**Communication red flags:**
  * Won’t answer specific questions
  * Pushes for an immediate decision
  * Asks for a deposit before viewing
  * The story changes (first it works, then a “minor issue”)


**When in doubt, pass.** A deal you skip costs nothing; a bad one costs the money and the afternoon.

##  Common Mistakes That Cost Money
###  Mistake 1: Buying Without Checking Comps
**The error:** “This looks valuable, I’ll buy it”
**The cost:** you overpay for items with no resale demand
**Fix:** always check eBay sold listings before buying. For furniture and other pieces that rarely ship, check what similar local listings are asking and how fast they go. No comps at all means no proven demand: pass.

**The error:** meeting at a seller’s house late at night
**The cost:** safety risk
**Fix:** public places, daylight, and bring someone for high-value items.
###  Mistake 3: Not Inspecting Before Paying
**The error:** handing over cash, then finding the item is broken
**The cost:** you’re stuck with it
**Fix:** test electronics, check for damage, and check it’s complete before paying.
###  Mistake 4: Ignoring Shipping Costs
**The error:** buying a bulky item and then finding out what shipping costs
**The cost:** the shipping eats the margin
**Fix:** price shipping before buying. Heavy, bulky items need a local buyer or a big margin.
###  Mistake 5: Forgetting About Your Time
**The error:** driving a long way for a small profit
**The cost:** a $30 profit for three hours of driving and listing is $10 an hour
**Fix:** count your hours. Short trips, batched pickups and higher-value items fix this.
###  Mistake 6: Falling for Counterfeits
**The error:** buying an “authentic” designer bag that turns out to be fake
**The cost:** the whole purchase price, since you can’t resell it
**Fix:** if you can’t authenticate designer items, don’t buy them. Stick to categories you know.
###  Mistake 7: Not Negotiating
**The error:** paying the asking price when the seller would have taken less
**The cost:** less profit on every flip
**Fix:** always make an offer. The worst outcome is a no.
