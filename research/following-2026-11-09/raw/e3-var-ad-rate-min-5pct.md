# eBay Promoted Listings General Minimum Ad Rate Jumps To 5% For Some Sellers

eBay appears to be testing increasing the minimum Promoted Listings General fixed ad rate to 5% for some sellers ahead of Q4 peak holiday shopping season.
Reports are coming in across social media, YouTube and eBay community forums indicating some sellers are no longer able to set their minimum ad rate at 2%, but with no official announcement from eBay, it's unclear if this is a glitch, a test or an early roll out of a soon to be updated policy.

The campaign is set to 3%, and many of my existing listings are still ru… ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/icon/favicon_iWhYEYg-f46e3907-e777-4de7-86d3-22b76ed4f000.png)eBay Community eBay Advertisingrayneedsthespace ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/thumbnail/avatar_ci86VQ0-a01eccad-a003-4ba2-a53b-0e7a1297f1b0.png)](https://community.ebay.com/forum/ebay-advertising-57924/topic/has-ebay-raised-the-minimum-promoted-listings-general-ad-rate-to-5-484035/?focus_post=1872911)
> I have an active Promoted Listings General campaign using a fixed ad rate, not dynamic. The campaign is set to 3%, and many of my existing listings are still running at 3%.  
>   
> 
> The problem is that when I try to add certain listings to the campaign or lower a listing’s ad rate to 3%, eBay will not allow it. If I enter 3%, I get the error:“Set an ad rate between 5% and 100%.”  
>   
> 
> I called eBay about this. The representative told me to promote the listing at 5% first, then go into Campaigns afterward and change it back down to 3%. I tried exactly that, but it still does not work. When I go into the campaign and try to change the listing from 5% to 3%, eBay still says the minimum is 5%.  
>   
> 
> So my campaign itself is still a 3% fixed-rate campaign, and many older listings remain at 3%, but I cannot set some newer or edited listings below 5%.
One post in a [reseller group on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/groups/resellersconnected/posts/1569124944538680/) shared a screenshot showing exactly the same issue - an error message saying the minimum rate is 5%.
> Did eBay change the minimum ad rate? I went to list something and it wouldn't let me list it at 3%. I thought maybe it was just that particular item or its category but I tried to revise several of my listed items and I'm getting the same error. Like WTF eBay? You already take enough!  
>   
> 
> ![831142402_10238812979681889_5135477734789327512_n.jpg](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2026/10/831142402_10238812979681889_5135477734789327512_n.jpg)
Reseller and Flipwise founder Justin Glow also posted a video on YouTube saying that the eBay API is returning a "bid percentage is not valid" error for some of his clients, indicating the minimum value is now 5%.
![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2026/10/justinadrates.jpg)
But Glow says not all Flipewise users are impacted and even for those who are, it does not appear to be affecting all of their listings.
Justin shared the following information in an email to eBay, though to be clear these are only his observations based on the data set he is looking at, not a definitive description or diagnosis of the issue.
>   * About 280 listings across 30 Flipwise sellers have been affected.
>   * Only sellers using an ad rate below 5% are impacted, and most of their promotions still go through.
>   * Since the errors began, roughly 12,000 promotions under 5% have succeeded, about the same pace as before. We confirmed on eBay that these ads are live at the lower rate (2% in the case we checked).
>   * Only about 2% of sub-5% listings are being rejected.
>   * The same seller can have one listing accepted and another rejected at the same rate, and some rejected listings were accepted on a later retry.
> 

Some sellers are speculating eBay may be testing raising the rate based on things like whether the seller has a Store subscription, business vs individual account, item category or price, but unfortunately there isn't enough data yet to make an accurate assessment of what criteria eBay may be using and eBay has not made any public statements about this issue.
The [official eBay help page](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/ebay-advertising/promoted-listings/general-campaign-strategy?id=4164) still says the minimum is 2% for General Promoted Listings ads as of October 2, 2026.
![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2026/10/ebayadfees10226.jpg)
However, this wouldn't be the first time that eBay has made a change to Promoted Listings ad rates without notice and didn't update the policy page. The same thing happened in 2024 when eBay raised the minimum ad rate for Promoted Listings General ads using the dynamic pricing strategy from 2% to 5%.
[ eBay Boosts Promoted Listings Dynamic Ad Rate Minimum To 5% With No Notice As Q4 Revenue Pressure Mounts eBay is ratcheting up Promoted Listings dynamic ad rate minimum requirements during prime holiday shopping season as Q4 revenue pressure mounts. ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/icon/Value-Added-Resourcefav-f6490953-d1f0-4996-8946-3249a4a8ad47.png)Value Added ResourceLiz Morton ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/thumbnail/ebayadfees11624-e3b6debb-3c7b-4795-aa35-7012de9abc43.jpg)](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-boosts-promoted-listings-dynamic-ad-rate-minimum/)
At that time, eBay also made [changes to its Marketing Terms](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-marketing-terms-update-december-2024/) which gave the company much more wiggle room to make future rate changes with less transparency required.
Promoted listings continues to be a significant source of revenue for eBay. In Q2 2026, nearly 1.3 billion of the 2.6 billion total listings on platform used at least one type of advertising, bringing in $570 Million in first party ad revenue. eBay CFO Peggy Alford said the company considers their target of ad revenue reaching 3% of Gross Merchandise Volume only a midterm milestone, not a ceiling, assuring investors that the ad fee machine will continue rolling full steam ahead throughout 2026 and beyond.
![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2026/08/2026-08-05_16-51-03-1.jpg)
What those numbers don't show is how much of the revenue growth came from greater seller demand versus eBay’s expanded attribution rules.
eBay began rolling out a new ad attribution model in smaller markets last year which expanded into the US and Canada in January, charging a Promoted Listings General fee when any buyer clicked the ad within the previous 30 days, even if a different buyer ultimately completed the purchase.
The new attribution model makes more sales eligible for ad fees, but sellers say they [haven't seen a commensurate increase in the number of sales or return on investment](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/ebay-promoted-listings-ad-attribution-update-fallout/) for their ad spend.

Here's the important piece from the article:  
  
"These negotiated USPS rates can change independently of published retail and commercial pricing, allowing eBay and other shipping platforms to offer different discounts and adjust prices outside the regular postal rate schedule."  
  
USPS still has to go through the Postal Regulatory Commission (PRC) process for published retail and commercial pricing for both Market Dominant products (like Media Mail and First-Class Mail) and Competitive products (like Ground Advantage, Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express).  
  
The PRC has generally limited Market Dominant rate increases to once per fiscal year through 2030. Competitive rate changes can happen more frequently, but still require PRC review and public disclosure.  
  
Marketplaces and other third-party label providers usually have Negotiated Service Agreements (NSAs) or Non-Published Rate (NPR) contracts with USPS that establish different rates from those published retail and commercial prices.  
  
Those contracts can allow rates to change outside the regular published rate schedule. They are still subject to regulatory requirements, but some operate under previously approved pricing structures without requiring separate PRC approval for every adjustment.  
  
Because the specific contract terms and negotiated prices are generally confidential, those changes don't usually come with a public USPS rate announcement.
