### My Take
Those who would ask "what's in it for eBay?" need look no further than a typical listing page - with their ad blocker turned off. 😉
[ Is eBay Becoming A Pay To Play Platform? Media coverage of Amazon’s “pay to play” sponsored search placement begs the question- is eBay any better? ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/size/w256h256/2022/03/Value-Added-Resource-copy--3-.png)Value Added ResourceLiz Morton ~ Founder ![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2021/09/toothpastesearch-2.jpg)](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/is-ebay-becoming-a-pay-to-play-platform/)
eBay typically stuffs anywhere from 25-30 competitor's ads immediately viewable on the listing page a seller pays for (either through insertion fees or store subscription fees). If you count the scrolling ad carousel bars, there may be as many as 52 ad slots on this page!
![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2021/10/toothpastelisting.jpg)
When you post a link on social media to drive traffic to your listing, you're also potentially driving traffic to all of those paid promoted listings eBay shows on the page.
Of course, if the buyer were to click through to one of those competitor listings to purchase instead, eBay would be collecting the full FVF plus additional ad fees on the sale. Sounds like a great deal for eBay and more than worth the tradeoff that if the buyer chooses your item, eBay will have to eat the reduction in fees.
As several sellers in that thread stated, why would you want to take the sale back to eBay instead of completing it either on the social media site or some other direct transaction between you and the buyer, like sending them a PayPal invoice?
Facebook has once again extended their waiving of selling fees through Facebook Marketplace - this time out to June 2022. If you and the buyer are already on Facebook, why in the world would you not take advantage of that and give eBay 2.5% + $0.30 instead?!
![](https://storage.ghost.io/c/ed/30/ed30d72b-9e06-4849-b9ff-511f2907ce30/content/images/2021/10/FBfees.jpg)
eBay has been pretty tight lipped on this subject with no further official details released since August. I had hoped we would see it roll out in time for Q4 holiday sales, but at this point I'm not holding my breath it will happen before EOY.
For sellers who _only_ sell on eBay and simply have no desire to deal with sales anywhere else, there may be some benefit to this program. Personally, it would be a hard pass for me as long as eBay continues stuffing all those ads onto seller's listing pages.

Here's the important piece from the article:  
  
"These negotiated USPS rates can change independently of published retail and commercial pricing, allowing eBay and other shipping platforms to offer different discounts and adjust prices outside the regular postal rate schedule."  
  
USPS still has to go through the Postal Regulatory Commission (PRC) process for published retail and commercial pricing for both Market Dominant products (like Media Mail and First-Class Mail) and Competitive products (like Ground Advantage, Priority Mail and Priority Mail Express).  
  
The PRC has generally limited Market Dominant rate increases to once per fiscal year through 2030. Competitive rate changes can happen more frequently, but still require PRC review and public disclosure.  
  
Marketplaces and other third-party label providers usually have Negotiated Service Agreements (NSAs) or Non-Published Rate (NPR) contracts with USPS that establish different rates from those published retail and commercial prices.  
  
Those contracts can allow rates to change outside the regular published rate schedule. They are still subject to regulatory requirements, but some operate under previously approved pricing structures without requiring separate PRC approval for every adjustment.  
  
Because the specific contract terms and negotiated prices are generally confidential, those changes don't usually come with a public USPS rate announcement.
