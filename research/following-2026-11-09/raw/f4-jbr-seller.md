# [Facebook Seller App and Free Verification: What Marketplace Sellers Need to Know](https://justbeingresourceful.com/2026/08/06/facebook-seller-app-and-free-verification-what-marketplace-sellers-need-to-know/)
![Round medieval shield with intricate metalwork and a glowing green checkmark in the center](https://justbeingresourceful.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/just-being-resourceful-6a6a13241b43e.png?w=1024)
#### **Facebook Marketplace just turned ten, and Meta marked the occasion by pulling selling out of the main app entirely. There is now a standalone app called Seller, plus a free selfie-based verification badge that shows up next to your name on Marketplace, Dating and Groups. If you sell more than a couch every three years, this changes your workflow. Here is what shipped, how to set it up, and which platforms are worth running alongside it.**
Meta announced the changes on 24 July 2026 through a post from Tom Alison, who runs Facebook. Three things landed at once: the Seller app, a badge called Facebook Verified, and an upcoming test of a full-screen video experience that greets you the second you open the app.
The video test is the one that will get argued about on social media. The Seller app and the verification badge are the two that actually affect anyone earning money on the platform.
* * *
## What Meta actually shipped
Marketplace is not a side feature any more. Meta says [430 million items and 44 million vehicles](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/connecting-real-people-on-facebook/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) get listed globally every month, and that one in three young adults on Facebook in the US opens Marketplace daily. That is a lot of commerce running through a tab inside a social app.
**Seller** is the answer to that. It is a dedicated app for people who list constantly, with AI listing creation, a unified inbox, inventory management and performance data in one place. It syncs both ways with Marketplace, so existing listings, message threads and selling history carry over, and anything created in Seller shows up on Marketplace where buyers already are.
**Facebook Verified** is a free badge. You record a short video selfie, Meta checks it against the photos already on your profile, and if it matches and your account is in good standing, a check mark inside a white circle appears on your profile. It is deliberately different from the blue badge you get by paying for [Meta Verified](https://www.meta.com/meta-verified/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified).
Meta says the badge shows up on Marketplace, Dating, Groups and profiles first, with Feed posts coming later. Verify once, and it travels with you.
* * *
## What is inside the Seller app
The feature list from Meta’s [announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-seller-app-facebook-marketplace/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) is short and mostly familiar to anyone who has used eBay or Etsy seller tools:
  * **Seller home.** A dashboard showing what needs attention: items to ship, buyers waiting on a reply, listings that should be repriced.
  * **AI listing creation.** Upload photos and Meta AI fills in title, description, category and a suggested price. Bulk listing lets you push several items at once.
  * **Listing management.** View, edit, relist and manage inventory from one screen instead of scrolling your profile.
  * **Unified inbox.** Buyer messages threaded by item, so every conversation about a specific listing sits together.
  * **Performance insights.** Views, clicks, message threads and sold listings.


None of this is novel. eBay and Etsy have offered versions of it for over a decade. What is different is the audience it reaches. Marketplace sellers have never had proper analytics, and a lot of them are running real businesses on a tool built for garage sales.
* * *
## How to get set up on Seller: step by step
  1. **Check that you qualify.** Seller is live on the App Store for people aged 18 and over in the United States. Meta says a web experience and Android testing are underway, so international sellers are waiting for now.
  2. **Download the app.** Search for Seller on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seller-a-facebook-app/id6766382815?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) or go directly to the listing.
  3. **Log in with your existing Facebook account.** Do not create a new one. Your listing history, ratings and buyer conversations are attached to the account you already sell from, and starting fresh throws away every review you have earned.
  4. **Confirm your listings synced.** Open the listings tab and count. If anything is missing, check that the listing is still active on Marketplace rather than expired.
  5. **Test AI listing creation on one item first.** Photograph something you know the value of, let the AI write the description and suggest a price, then compare that price against what similar items actually sold for. AI pricing models tend to anchor on listed prices, not sold prices, and those are two very different numbers.
  6. **Edit the AI description before you publish.** Add the specifics the model cannot see: a scratch on the left corner, the year you bought it, whether the original box is included. Buyers who message you about a flaw you disclosed rarely ask for a discount. Buyers who discover it at pickup always do.
  7. **Set a check-in rhythm.** Look at the performance tab weekly, not daily. Views tell you whether your title and photos are working. Message threads with no sale tell you your price is wrong.


* * *
## How to get the Facebook Verified badge
  1. **Confirm eligibility.** You need to be 18 or over and in good standing with Meta’s [Community Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/fraud-and-scams/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified), specifically the rules on fraud, scams and inauthentic behaviour. Pages and ProMode accounts are not eligible.
  2. **Make sure your profile has real photos of you.** The check works by comparing your video selfie against photos already on your profile. If your profile picture is a logo, a pet or a landscape, the match will fail. Add at least one clear face photo first.
  3. **Record the video selfie.** Good light, no sunglasses, no hat. Meta says the process usually takes a few minutes.
  4. **Wait for the result.** Verification is rolling out in phases, starting in select markets with global expansion planned. If you do not see the option yet, it has not reached your market.
  5. **Mention it in your listings once it lands.** A line like “verified seller, happy to meet at a public location” does more for buyer confidence than the badge alone.


* * *
## What the badge means, and what it does not
This part matters, and Meta is unusually direct about it in the [verification announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/introducing-facebook-verified/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified). The badge means a real person completed a selfie check and the account meets trust and safety standards. It does not mean Meta endorses that person or guarantees they are trustworthy.
Which is exactly right, and worth repeating to anyone who reads a check mark as a safety guarantee. A verified account is still an account that can ghost you, misdescribe an item or take a deposit and vanish. Verification raises the cost of running a throwaway scam profile. It does not eliminate scams.
The practical read: treat the badge as one signal among several. Public meeting spots, cash on collection, inspecting before paying and refusing to move the conversation off-platform still do more work than any badge.
* * *
## The fee question nobody mentions in the launch coverage
Local pickup on Marketplace remains free. You meet the buyer, take cash or a transfer, and Meta takes nothing. That is the single biggest reason bulky items sell there rather than anywhere else.
Shipped orders are a different story. Under Meta’s [merchant policies](https://www.facebook.com/legal/merchant_policies?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified), individual sellers pay 10% per transaction with a minimum of $0.80, calculated on the full amount including item price, shipping and applicable taxes. That rate went up from 5% in 2024, and a surprising amount of published guidance still quotes the old number.
So the maths for a US seller looks like this: a $100 item collected locally nets $100. The same item shipped through Marketplace checkout nets about $90 before your packaging and time. Worth knowing before the shiny new dashboard convinces you to switch everything to shipping.
* * *
## The alternatives worth knowing about
Meta is not building this in a vacuum. Resale and peer-to-peer selling have become genuinely competitive, and fee structures now vary wildly. Here is the global picture as of mid-2026.  
| Platform  | Where it works  | Seller cost  | Best for  |  
| --- | --- | --- | --- |  
| **Facebook Marketplace**  | 100+ countries  | Free local, 10% shipped (US)  | Furniture, appliances, vehicles, anything bulky  |  
| **Vinted**  | 20+ countries, Europe plus US  | 0% for sellers, buyer pays protection fee  | Everyday fashion, low-value items where fees kill margin  |  
| **eBay**  | Global  | Roughly 13% final value fee plus per-order charge  | Electronics, collectibles, parts, anything needing search demand  |  
| **Depop**  | US, UK, Europe, Australia  | No commission in US and UK, payment processing applies  | Vintage, streetwear, Y2K, Gen Z buyers  |  
| **Poshmark**  | US, Canada, Australia, India  | 20% commission on most sales  | Branded womenswear where the buyer pool justifies the cut  |  
| **Mercari**  | US, Japan  | 10% seller fee  | General household goods, low-friction listing  |  
| **Etsy**  | Global  | Listing fee plus transaction and processing fees  | Handmade, craft supplies, digital downloads  |  
| **Whatnot**  | US, UK, Europe  | Commission on live sales  | Collectibles, cards, sneakers, anything that sells on personality  |  
| **OfferUp**  | US  | Free local, fee on shipped  | Local pickup, direct Marketplace competitor  |  
| **Regional classifieds**  | Gumtree, Kleinanzeigen, Leboncoin, Carousell, OLX  | Mostly free to list  | Local selling outside US coverage  |  
Two of those deserve a closer look.
**[Vinted](https://www.vinted.com/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified)** is the structural outlier. Sellers pay nothing. Not a discounted rate, not an introductory offer. The buyer covers a protection fee at checkout instead, roughly 5% plus a small flat charge. The Lithuanian company crossed 100 million users, dominates several European markets, and opened properly to US sellers in early 2026. The US buyer pool is still thin compared to Europe, but listing there costs you nothing, which makes the decision easy.
**[Whatnot](https://www.whatnot.com/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified)** is the one people underestimate. It reported more than $8 billion in live gross merchandise value in 2025, roughly double the year before. Live selling is not for everyone. It rewards people who can talk to a camera for two hours and enjoy it. But for cards, sneakers, jewellery and vintage fashion, the sell-through rate embarrasses static listings.
Worth noting for context: eBay agreed in February 2026 to [acquire Depop from Etsy](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/eBay-to-Acquire-Depop-from-Etsy/default.aspx?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) for around $1.2 billion. Consolidation is happening quickly, and fee structures at recently acquired platforms have a habit of changing.
* * *
## If you sell on more than one platform
Most people who sell seriously list the same inventory in three or four places. That creates one real risk: selling the same one-off item twice because you forgot to pull it down elsewhere. Overselling wrecks seller ratings faster than almost anything.
Cross-listing tools exist for this. [Vendoo](https://www.vendoo.co/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) and [List Perfectly](https://listperfectly.com/?utm_source=justbeingresourceful&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=facebook-seller-app-marketplace-verified) are the two most established. Both let you build a listing once, push it to multiple marketplaces, and delist everywhere when something sells. They cost money, so run the numbers: if you list fewer than 20 items a month, a spreadsheet works fine.
The Seller app does not cross-list. It manages Facebook. Keep that in mind before assuming it replaces your whole stack.
* * *
## A practical way to decide where to list
  1. **Bulky and local?** Facebook Marketplace or the strongest regional classifieds site where you live. Zero fees, buyer collects, done.
  2. **Fashion under $30?** Vinted first. A 20% commission on a $20 top is the difference between worth doing and not worth doing.
  3. **Branded fashion over $50?** Test Poshmark and eBay side by side and compare actual sell-through, not fee rates. A fast sale at 20% beats a slow one at 0%.
  4. **Electronics, parts, collectibles?** eBay. Search demand is the whole reason it still wins here.
  5. **Something with a story or a following?** Whatnot or live selling on eBay.
  6. **More than 20 listings a month across platforms?** Pay for a cross-listing tool before you oversell something.


* * *
## What to watch next
Three things are worth tracking.
**Availability.** Seller is US and iOS only right now. Android and web are in testing. Facebook Verified is rolling out market by market. Neither is global yet, and Meta has not committed to dates.
**Fee drift.** Meta already doubled its shipped-order fee once. A dedicated seller app with inventory tools and analytics is exactly the kind of product companies later attach a subscription to. Nothing has been announced, but plan for the possibility.
**Facial recognition.** Meta shut down its face recognition system in 2021 and deleted the templates. Selfie verification is a narrower use case, matching your face to your own profile photos rather than scanning everyone in a photo, but it is still biometric processing. Read the terms before you upload, especially if you are in a jurisdiction with strict biometric privacy law.
* * *
## The honest summary
Seller is a good app for a problem that has been ignored for a decade. If you list 30 items a week on Marketplace, the unified inbox alone justifies the download. The AI listing creation will save you time and occasionally embarrass you, which is the current state of every AI listing tool.
Facebook Verified is a smaller change with a bigger long-term implication. As AI-generated profiles get harder to spot, proof of humanity becomes the thing platforms compete on. Meta making it free while charging for the blue badge tells you which one it thinks matters more.
Neither of these should make you consolidate onto one platform. The sellers who do best still spread inventory across three or four, price for each audience, and treat every marketplace as a channel rather than a home.
