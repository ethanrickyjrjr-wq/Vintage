##### Promoted Listings General Attribution Changes Coming To US & Canada January 13, 2026
It's official, the Promoted Listings General ad attribution changes rolled out to UK, EU, and Australia earlier this year are coming to US and Canada January 13, 2026.
<https://pages.ebay.com/sell/pl/attribution.html>
![PLads102825.jpg](https://res.cloudinary.com/startup-grind/image/upload/c_fill,dpr_2,f_auto,g_center,q_auto:good/v1/gcs/platform-data-ebay/discussions/726x425_80h6dA5)
_**Starting January 13, 2026, for items listed on ebay.com, ebay.ca, and cafr.ebay.ca (and currently for items listed on ebay.co.uk, ebay.de, ebay.com.au, ebay.fr,** **ebay.it, and ebay.es):** eBay will report an Attributed sale from a general campaign when a buyer purchases the same item that was featured in the ad that was clicked on by any buyer in the most recent 30 days._
_The ad fee is charged when a buyer purchases the promoted item from a general ad that any buyer clicked on in the most recent 30 days. The item must be promoted at the time of click and the time of sale. The ad fee is based on the ad rate in effect at the time of the sale._

@rsoxnation2004
First, keep in mind that when you have an item in a General Ad Promoted Listing Campaign, ebay can _choose_ to display it as "sponsored" or as "organic." A "sponsored" listing will (generally) be identified by the word "sponsored". An organic listing in a Campaign will look exactly like a true organic listing (which is a listing that is NOT in a promoted listing campaign.)
If buyer A clicks on a sponsored listing, and buys the item within 30 days, as long as it is in an Ad campaign when he buys it, even if it is shown at the time of purchase as organic, the ad fee will be charged to the seller. This is true today, and it will be true when the change happens.
If buyer A clicks on a sponsored listing, and buys the same item within 30 days, but the item is NOT in an ad campaign when he buys it, the ad fee in effect at the time of the first click will be charged to the seller. This is true today, _but will no longer be true_ when the new rule goes into effect. Under the new rule, a true organic listing---one that is NOT in an ad campaign at the time of purchase---will _never_ be charged an ad fee.
If buyer A clicks on a sponsored listing, and buys a DIFFERENT item FROM THE SAME SELLER within 30 days, as long as that item is in an ad campaign when he purchases it , even though it is shown to him as organic, an ad fee will apply. This is true even though it is a completely different item. This is known as the Halo rule. Under the new rule, a click on a different item from the one ultimately purchased will NOT trigger an ad fee. 
Under the new rule, as stated above, if Buyer A clicks on a sponsored listing, and buys the item within 30 days, even though it is displayed as organic when purchased, as long as it is in an ad campaign, the fee will be charged. In that case, there is no change.
So, here's the big difference: Under the new rule, if Buyer A clicks on a sponsored listing, and ---here's the difference---Buyer **B** buys the item within 30 days, even though it is displayed as organic when purchased, as long as it is IN an ad campaign, the fee will be charged. This is true even though there is NO connection between Buyer A and Buyer B, and no connection between Buyer A's click and Buyer B's purchase. Basically, as long as SOMEONE---ANYONE--- clicked on the sponsored item within the last 30 days, and it is purchased while it is in a campaign, a fee will be charged.
