What’s the best time to post on Instagram if you want your content to (actually) get seen?
**To find out, the Hootsuite team partnered with Critical Truth to analyze over 1 million social posts to uncover the best times to post on Instagram by day, industry, and time zone.**
Whether you want to grow faster, maximize your reach, or optimize your broader social media strategy, these are the best times to post on Instagram in 2026 — plus insights to help you find your optimal posting times.
### Key takeaways
  1. Getting your timing right on Instagram can make a major difference in engagement, especially if your followers are active in different time zones.
  2. The best time to post on Instagram by day is3–9 PM on Mondays, 5–8 AM and 3–7 PM on Tuesdays, 5 PM on Wednesdays, 4–5 PM on Thursdays, 4 PM on Fridays, 11 AM and 5 PM on Saturdays, and 12–3 PM on Sundays.
  3. Morning posts perform best for industries like consumer goods, retail, marketing, and media.
  4. Afternoon postswork better for sectors like construction, finance, hospitality, and government.
  5. Your best time to post depends on your industry, audience habits, and time zone.


## **What is the best time to post on Instagram by day of the week?**
**According to[Hootsuite’s 2025 social media analysis](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-social-media/), Instagram engagement peaks during early mornings and early evenings — especially early in the week.**
Here’s how engagement looks day by day, based on data from over one million posts.
**Note:** Unless stated otherwise, the time of day was localized across 118 countries where sample data came from, i.e. the graphs below are accurate across time zones.
###  **Best time to post on Monday**
The best time to post on Instagram on Monday is **between 3 PM and 9 PM**.
Engagement ramps up later in the day as people shift out of work mode and catch up on what they missed over the weekend. **Avoid posting after 10 PM, when activity drops off.**
###  **Best time to post on Tuesday**
The best times to post on Instagram on Tuesday are **from 5 AM to 8 AM** and **from 3 PM to 7 PM**.
A morning post can reach early risers checking their feeds before work, while an afternoon slot captures users winding down after hours.
###  **Best time to post on Wednesday**
The best time to post on Instagram on Wednesday is **5 PM**.
Evenings see higher activity as people look for inspiration, motivation, or a mental break.
###  **Best time to post on Thursday**
The best time to post on Instagram on Thursday is **between 4 PM and 5 PM**.
Engagement builds late in the afternoon as people start wrapping up their workweek and browse for evening plans or weekend ideas.
###  **Best time to post on Friday**
The best time to post on Instagram on Friday is **around 4 PM**.
An evening post can catch people as they finish work, scroll during their commute, and shift into weekend mode.
###  **Best time to post on Saturday**
The best times to post on Instagram on Saturday are **at 11 AM** and **around 5 PM**.
These windows align with leisurely mornings and early-evening downtime. **Avoid posting early in the morning (before 8 AM)** when users are least active.
###  **Best time to post on Sunday**
The best times to post on Instagram on Sunday are **between 12 PM and 3 PM**. Midday posts perform well as people relax at home or recap their weekends.
**Avoid early mornings (before 8 AM)** and **evenings after 9 PM** , when users are logging off and getting ready for the week ahead.
If you’re just starting off on Instagram and don’t have a lot of past data or audience insights to work with, try posting around these peak times.

## **What factors influence the best time to post on Instagram?**
The best time to post on Instagram is shaped by your audience, industry, and even how Instagram’s algorithm surfaces your content. Here’s how those factors play out:
###  **Your audience’s routines and habits**
Every audience scrolls differently. The trick is figuring out when your followers are most likely to scroll their screen.
For example, an online sneaker shop might catch late-night scrollers unwinding after work, while a wellness influencer could see early-morning spikes as followers check Instagram Stories before heading to the gym.
You really can’t know when your audience is most active on Instagram without looking into your data with [analytics tools](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-analytics-tools/). Only then will you see what _your specific audience_ tends to do (and not do) online.
**Pro tip![💡](https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/svg/1f4a1.svg)** : Social listening is one of the [best ways to understand your audience](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-listening-business/).
The Instagram Insights tool can give you some data about your audience’s most active times.
![instagram insights dashboard showing audience most active at 12pm on a monday](https://blog.hootsuite.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/instagram-insights-dashboard-showing-audience-most.jpeg)
Or, take advantage of personalized posting recommendations on Instagram, right [in Perch by Hootsuite](https://www.hootsuite.com/perch).
![Best time to publish heatmap in Perch by Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/image-30-620x310.png)
As you read through your data, keep an eye on the following:
  * **Audience demographics:** Younger audiences, like teenagers and young adults, tend to be online late at night and on weekends. Older users might check Instagram early in the morning or during lunch breaks.
  * **Work schedules:** For working professionals, posting during commute times (early morning and late afternoon) or lunch breaks can get you the most engagement.
  * **Daily routines:** Think about what your audience does each day. Fitness fans might check Instagram around their workout times, while parents might be more active during school hours.


The more you know about your audience’s habits, the better equipped you’ll be to reach them when they are most likely to interact with you. And interactions = results (well, most of the time, anyway).
**Pro tip![💡](https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/svg/1f4a1.svg) : **[Use Perch](https://www.hootsuite.com/perch) to learn about your audience’s habits and tailor your posting schedule to fit their routines.
###  **Your industry**
Your industry influences when your audience is actually online — and, by extension, the best times to post on Instagram.
If you’re targeting healthcare professionals, for example, you might see an influx of activity late at night, or super early in the morning. Meanwhile, knowledge workers tend to scroll before and after work, with a quick midday check-in around lunch.
Knowing those rhythms can help you post when your content fits naturally into their day.
To go deeper, use [Perch’s Industry Benchmarking tool](https://help.hootsuite.com/hc/en-us/articles/10779068088603-Compare-your-performance-with-your-industry-average#customize-your-view-0-1) to compare your posting times and engagement against others in your sector. You can also see what your competitors’ audience is doing (remember, they might be your audience one day, too).
![Industry benchmarking dashboard in Perch](https://blog.hootsuite.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/image-31-620x300.png)
Use this data to paint a picture of your ideal audience, and begin to learn how to target and engage them with your posting times.

###  **Time zones**
Instagram moves fast, so timing is everything. Posting when your audience is asleep means your content could get buried before they log on.
If your followers span multiple regions, plan posts that straddle time zones. For example, posting at 6 a.m. PST (9 a.m. EST) captures both coasts at once, while 9 p.m. PST (9 a.m. in India) can reach international audiences. You get the drift.
Similarly, posting once in the morning for your North American audience, and again in the late evening for your overseas audience, can help open your content up to a more globalized audience.
As always, don’t go about this uninformed. Use your data as a guide to see when your audience is online and most engaged.
###  **Instagram algorithm changes**
Algorithm changes are the bane of every social media manager’s existence. One day you’re ranking high, and the next you might as well be invisible.
We get it. But keeping up with Instagram’s algorithm is key when figuring out the best times to post.
Here’s [what the Instagram algorithm is prioritizing ](https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-algorithm/)right now:
  * **Early interactions:** Instagram’s algorithm loves posts that get lots of likes, comments, shares, and saves right after being posted. So, posting when your audience is most active can help boost your content.
  * **Relevancy:** Instagram decides how relevant each piece of content is by analyzing its fit with trending topics and its timeliness. Posting at peak times ensures that your content is fresh and more likely to appear in their feeds.
  * **Content type:** The algorithm also takes into account the type of content users interact with the most. If your audience prefers Instagram Reels over carousels, posting these types at optimal times can help your content stand out.


Always stay [updated with Instagram’s changes](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained) and adjust your Instagram marketing strategy accordingly to keep your posts visible.
###  **Current events**
Even the best posting schedule can get thrown off by real life. Big events — whether they’re global, local, or industry-specific — can shift when your audience is online.
A holiday weekend might mean lower engagement during travel days. A major news story or local power outage could change when your followers are scrolling (or if they’re scrolling at all).
You may also find that a [social media crisis](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-crisis-management/), whether or not it’s related to your brand or industry, can affect how and when your posts are seen.
Holidays, school schedules, and political events can all influence the way people act online, so be on the lookout for any changes in engagement, and adjust your posting times accordingly.
