# w1f: other vintage sellers, how they make money, how they grow

Agent w1f, accessed 2026-10-29. Raw captures: `research/social-2026-10-29/raw/w1f-*.md`. Budget used: 20 searches (19 WebSearch + 1 SearXNG, which returned Memphis junk) and about 52 crawls (over the 40 target; many were walls). The first save attempt (Bash heredoc) failed on a shell parse error, not a refusal; this file was written with the Write tool instead.

**Tags.** `V` = read live this session (for press, the quote is verified, the underlying claim is seller-reported). `S` = search-tool summary, page not read. `A` = aggregator (Feedspot, which sells contact databases; list updated 2026-09-16). `I` = my inference. Instagram and TikTok were walls, so follower counts, cadence and formats are mostly **not read**; I say so instead of estimating.

## TL;DR

1. **No survey splits a vintage seller's revenue between Instagram, marketplaces and an own site.** One dealer statement exists: Dobbin St. Co-Op said Instagram was "over 50 percent" of revenue "since the pandemic began" (Curbed, Feb 1 2021; a shop with two stores). `V`, seller-reported, one shop, pandemic year.
2. **Almost every growth story is a 2018-2021 winner profile.** I re-checked 5 sites from Marie Claire's May 2021 list: 3 trading, 1 ambiguous (domain now an art-print store), 1 unreachable. The survivors changed shape (imports, a booth, a new city, 50% off banners). `V`.
3. **Nobody states time to the first 1K or to consistent sales.** Closest: 7,100 followers in about 12 months (Halifax, 2019-20), 5,000+ in about 11 months (doubles_tennis, 2020-21), about 35,000 in 4 years (a Toronto shop with a store), and 12 years to a 6,000-person email list (a Massachusetts estate-sale company). All press. `V`.
4. **Content-first flippers earn from content, not stock.** Kara Ward: "YouTube, sponsorship deals... eighty percent of my income... flipping furniture is twenty percent" (Dwell, Aug 27 2025). One anecdote; another flipper in the same piece warns most can't profit. `V`.
5. **Antique-mall booth:** median $275 a month for about 10x10, and 21 of 35 malls also keep 7% to 15% (Underpriced, Oct 14 2026; the publisher sells an app). A second channel, not a growth engine. `V`, vendor.
6. **Chairish commission** (verified): Consignor 40%, Professional 30%, Premium 25% on vintage, Plus 22%, Elite and Platinum tiered 20% up to $2,500. Chairish Trade offers designers "up to 50% off", which collides with the no-discount rule. `V`.
7. **Live selling is real but auction-shaped.** Whatnot says 53% of sellers get most annual sales from live (41% a year earlier; via secondary coverage; the 4,016-seller sample is from a search summary only). Vendor claim. The repo already said monthly live at most; claims and bids go to a lawyer.
8. **Workshops sell:** a Philadelphia vintage shop listed an $85 lamp-wiring workshop as sold out (Aug 10 2026). `V`.
9. **South Florida rental is crowded:** Mi Vintage ("a decade"), Chic Patina, KM Props, Vintage Unique Props. CLAUDE.md says "no rentals in v1"; whether that covers prop rental is unclear. `V` (Mi Vintage, Chic Patina), `S` (KM Props, Vintage Unique Props).
10. **Best fits, ranked:** hunt list plus weekly drop; the Vintage Market Days booth; Chairish only if offers and "was" prices can be avoided; everything else later or never. `I`.
