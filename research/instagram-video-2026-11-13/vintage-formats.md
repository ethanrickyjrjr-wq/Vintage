## Headline
No reseller-specific number proves Reels convert to sales. What exists is anecdote, vendor case studies and general marketing guides. The consistent advice: Reels reach new people, Stories and DMs/links close the sale, and the sale usually happens on a marketplace (Etsy/eBay), not in Instagram.

## Findings

### 1. Do Reels convert for small resellers? (weak evidence)
- Scavenger Life forum, thread "Has anyone tried selling on Instagram?" (posts dated 2019, so old, pre-Reels-era): one seller says "I do use Instagram to sell books. For me, I find it easiest to have an Etsy store I advise buyers to go to ... Sometimes, items sell within hours. Other times, it takes months." Another: a local antiques seller with "over 8,000 followers" sells by DM/comment "SOLD", "Everything she posts sells"; "most resellers I follow have a couple hundred to a couple thousand." Sample size: a handful of forum posts. https://www.scavengerlife.com/forums/topic/has-anyone-tried-selling-on-instagram/
- List Perfectly (reseller tool vendor): a buyer found a teapot in an Instagram feed post and bought by emailed invoice; "Instagram gives you the advantage of reaching so many buyers ... who will make a purchase because of the sentiment and meaning behind the item." One anecdote, vendor has a crosslist-to-Instagram feature to sell. https://listperfectly.com/?p=4582
- Paloma (vendor) case study, @tipsy.thrifting, 46.6k followers, Live/Story/feed sales: "140% Increase in average monthly sales" after adopting Paloma. Promotional, self-reported, one seller, sells via Live/Stories not Reels. https://www.getpaloma.com/case-studies/tipsythrifting/
- Glossy (trade press, 2026): a vintage seller on Whatnot "has built a following of more than 22,200 followers on Whatnot and 11,500 followers on Instagram, where she previews inventory and promotes upcoming shows." Sales happen on the live platform; Instagram is the preview/promo channel. https://www.glossy.co/fashion/why-vintage-sellers-are-taking-resale-live/
- Metricool (analytics vendor, 2026): "Reels ... pull 4x more interactions than single-image posts"; "average watch time is up 117% over 2025, to 8.5 seconds per Reel"; "DM shares now carry more weight than likes for reaching new audiences." General brand data, not resellers. https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/

### 2. Formats named by reseller sources
- The Vintage Seeker (reseller education site, list from a Later.com webinar, Dec 2022, members-only page but list was readable): "Come thrifting with me, Booth restock, Try-ons, This or that, Styling an outfit or a space, Process videos (washing, sewing, cleaning), How-tos (how to measure clothes lying flat), Thrift hauls, Price guessing game, Behind the scenes, Packing orders, Makeovers, Before and after, Still photos of products from your shop, timed to music, Sneak peeks, Stop motion, Get ready with me, Repurpose content you're using for listings — you've done the research on these items, so share a fact as a Reel!" https://www.thevintageseeker.ca/magazine/icp-member-reel-talk-how-resellers-can-navigate-instagram-reels-1290343007
- Same page, on effort: "Have a stable of 'low-lift' content ... Examples: using still images in your Reel with a thoughtful or engaging caption, or using a trending sound". On length: "Short videos perform best (i.e. under 10 seconds)." On loops: "make it sound like it's looping." On captions: "Use Instagram's closed-captions and text features on Reels ... The algorithm prefers in-app text." Hashtags "between 5-12". (Webinar-derived opinion, not platform data.)
- Socialmon (thrift-post idea site, tool vendor): "New arrival Reels work well when they feel like an unboxing rather than a stock update ... pull out the standout pieces, flip the tag. That combination of movement, discovery, and price reveal compresses the in-store treasure-hunt experience into 30 seconds." Also: "Reels that have a clear hook in the first two seconds ... a transformation, a surprising find, a styling moment, a 'guess the era' reveal." Also: "Weekly 'New Arrivals Monday' drop post with a fixed posting time"; "Countdown sticker in Stories before a major restock or themed drop"; "Split-screen before (hanger) and after (on body)"; "What the label tells you"; "The biggest mistake stores make here is posting rack shots ... What actually works is isolation: pulling one or a few items out." Its "40,000 views" example for a "1 piece, 3 ways" Reel is an unnamed store claim: unverified. https://www.socialmon.ai/blog/65-thrift-store-instagram-post-ideas-with-examples-that-sell
- The Vintage Seeker, drops: "Plan drops based on space and time ... Themed drops could be seasonal, by colour, by style, by theme, by mood." And "if you're advertising a new website drop on social media, it's more streamlined for the buyer to click your link in bio ... Buyers don't need to compete in the post comments or direct messages." https://www.thevintageseeker.ca/magazine/15-growth-hacks-for-running-your-vintage-business
- Glossy on why video helps vintage: "You can zoom in on details like stitching or tags and explain why a piece is rare." and "I'll promote a dollar-start auction for a week before running the item ... It turns into an event." (Live, but the macro-detail and pre-announcement tactics carry to Reels.)

### 4. Driving viewers to an eBay listing
- Link in bio: Vintage Seeker advises it for website drops (above). One link only; a per-drop landing page or eBay store link must be updated each drop (practical inference, not sourced).
- Story link sticker: Engadget (Oct 2021): replaced swipe-up which "was only available to verified accounts or those with at least 10,000 followers"; now "almost all users", but "New accounts and those that often share misinformation and hate speech will be blocked." Whether Hollie's account has it today: could not verify (needs to be checked in the app). https://www.engadget.com/instagram-stories-link-stickers-roll-out-150024795.html
- Comment-to-DM ("comment LINK"): Planoly (a scheduling-tool vendor, updated Oct 23, 2026) says Meta's Private Replies supports it: "An app can send one private reply to a person who commented on your post or reel, within 7 days of the comment. After that: 'Follow-up messages can only be sent if the recipient responds, and must be sent within 24 hours of the response.'" Rate limit quoted: "750 calls per hour per Instagram professional account". Avoid "Cold DMs", "Repetitive public replies", "Password-based bots". Needs a professional (Business/Creator) account and a Meta-API tool (ManyChat is cited as one). Secondary source quoting Meta docs; I did not fetch Meta's docs (not attempted past search). https://www.planoly.com/blog/is-comment-to-dm-allowed-on-instagram
- Reseller practice: Etsy/website as destination, DM/"SOLD" comments for direct sales (forum, 2019). Paloma (above) is a paid tool for Live/Story/DM claims.

## Ranked formats for Holliewood and Vine
Rationale: ranked by fit with sources (isolated single piece, price/tag reveal, hook in 2 s, under ~30 s, text on screen), shop look, and effort. All end on: "link in bio / comment LINK" plus drop-day name. Keep captions in-app text (Vintage Seeker).

1. **Price-tag reveal "just listed" (single item, 10-20 s).** (a) Phone: 0-2 s hold item/form close with big text "New today: [era] [item]"; 2-6 s slow orbit around the dress form or lamp; 6-10 s macro of label/tag; 10-14 s flaw honestly shown; 14-18 s flip the price tag; 18-20 s end card "Link in bio, ships in 2 days". (b) Stills only: cut the Photoroom white-background shots, 1.5 s each: hero, side, back, label/detail, flaw; zoom-in motion (Ken Burns) on each; price slams in at the end; trending-or-soft sound; captions as in-app text.
2. **Detail macro: "what the label tells you" (15-25 s).** (a) Phone: hook "This tag dates it to the 1970s"; close shot of label, then fabric, zipper, stitch; end on whole piece; one line of voice-over or text per beat. (b) Stills: use label/detail photos as the whole reel, each with an arrow/circle and a one-line fact; final full-item shot plus price. Doubles as listing research repurposed (Vintage Seeker).
3. **Weekly "drop day" teaser-to-reveal (15-30 s).** (a) Phone: Story countdown sticker the day before; Reel shows covered or silhouetted items on the rack, 5 quick reveals with price, text "Drop: Saturday 10am". (b) Stills: 4-6 product photos, first blurred or silhouetted, unblur in sequence with price text; end card with date and time. Same time each week, series name.
4. **Styled 3 ways / one piece, three looks (20-30 s).** (a) Phone: hook "One vintage [dress], three ways"; three 6 s styling cuts with jump transitions; end on sale card. (b) Stills: same item in three layouts of cut-out photos (item alone, with a lamp or art piece from the room, with neon backdrop graphic), swipe transitions. Shop's maximalist room is the advantage.
5. **Before/after of a find (15-25 s).** (a) Phone: unlovely thrift-bag or dusty state, then steamed/polished on the form; text "From estate sale box to listed". (b) Stills: needs a real before photo; if none exists, skip or use rough-photo to Photoroom-white split-screen (uses existing material).
6. **Guess the decade / price guessing game (10-20 s).** (a) Phone: item shown, text "Guess the decade", 3 s countdown, reveal with tag clue, then price. (b) Stills: hero photo with "Guess the decade" and countdown graphic, reveal slide with label photo; comments prompt drives engagement, and "comment LINK" for the listing.
Lowest priority (no evidence either way): sourcing-haul-to-listing; Live selling (evidence strong for Whatnot/Instagram Live but a different, heavier format).
