# a4 - Commerce mechanics and merchandising for a one-of-one vintage shop

Agent a4, accessed 2026-10-27. Raw crawls: `research/site-ideas/raw/a4-*.md`. Every figure below was read on a page fetched this session; anything else is labeled `UNVERIFIED`. Visual claims are `inferred` unless noted (crawl text only, nothing rendered).

## TL;DR

1. The mechanic that matters most for quantity-1 stock is a **fixed weekly drop**, because a one-of-one has no restock hook. The Rareté does exactly this ("New drops every week at 11 AM CST"). Shopify can schedule it natively; Etsy's listing help shows no schedule option.
2. **Keep sold pieces public.** Four real shops do it (Rareté, Archive, Bygone Relics, Ça Marche). It costs nothing, proves taste and price, and the Rareté turns the archive into a "tell us what you want" lead form. Etsy keeps sold listings visible unless you hide them.
3. **Per-piece story + honest condition notes + a short light-on video** beat every gadget. Ça Marche (one-off antiques on Shopify) puts a "Disclaimer: two small chips under rim" line on each piece. A 3-15 s silent video is the only 360-ish thing worth the effort; true 360/3D is hype for a lamp-and-glass shop.
4. **Disco ball gift = pack it in the box, do not build cart logic.** Shopify's Buy X Get Y "never" auto-adds the free item; Etsy gift wrap cannot be priced at $0. Per Ricky's own correction, do not promise it on every order.
5. **Live selling is real for decor** (Instagram Live claim-sales per Domino, Whatnot at 8% + processing) but it is a second job. Do it monthly, as an event, not weekly. Mystery bundles, VIP tiers, and appointment pickup do not fit this shop right now.

## Sites and platforms

### 1. The Rareté (drops + sold archive + sourcing requests)
- Homepage: https://therarete.com/ . Pages read: https://therarete.com/collections/sold-archive , https://therarete.com/pages/sourcing-requests . Accessed 2026-10-27. Shopify storefront (inferred from `cdn/shop` asset paths and Shopify customer-auth URLs). Archival designer vintage clothing, so price tier and category differ from ours; the mechanics transfer.
- **Drop cadence.** Site-wide announcement bar: "New drops every week at 11 AM CST" linking to a `first-look` collection.
- **Sold archive.** The page says: "We keep sold pieces visible here as a reference, not a waitlist." and "Each sold listing keeps the product-level notes that accompanied it when live". It lists 28 out-of-stock pieces, 0 in stock, filterable by brand. It also says "sold means sold, and purchase paths begin from current listings only."
- **Demand capture.** The archive points to a Sourcing Request form: "Sourcing Requests is how clients ask The Rareté to look for a specific archival or vintage designer piece that isn't currently listed". Honest limit stated on the page: "It's a search request, not a guarantee or a reservation."

- **Watch out:** the archive's SEO-style copy ("reference library", "researchers") is long-winded for our voice. Write ours in the shop's voice.

### 5. Instagram Live claim-sales (Domino guide, written by the owner of Ça Marche)
- Homepage: https://www.domino.com/ . Page read: https://www.domino.com/style-shopping/how-to-shop-instagram-live-sales-decor/ (2026 upload dates in image paths). Accessed 2026-10-27. This is an insider how-to, by the seller, not independent data.

- Other signals: sellers "announce ... ahead of time" and preview pieces in Reels/Stories; some run "pre-claims"; solo or groups of up to four sellers. Viewers on desktop have a "20- to 30-second lag".
- **Platform status:** Shopify's blog says "Meta discontinued the native Instagram Live Shopping product-tagging feature in 2023" and that in June 2026 Meta introduced "Live Video Ads on Instagram". Source: https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/instagram-live-shopping . **Meta's own help page was walled (4 bytes), so the 2023 discontinuation is verified via Shopify's blog, Meta's page needs review.**
- **Steal this:** the claim-by-comment format is zero-integration. Disclosures-before-price matches our condition-note habit. **Watch out:** payment by Venmo/PayPal DM is off-platform, so there is no checkout, tax handling, or buyer protection; if Ricky does this, send an invoice from his own checkout instead (opinion). ## Idea-by-idea notes (hype vs what moves sales)

1. **Weekly drop.** Moves sales. For one-of-ones the drop is the product launch. Pick one weekday + time and never miss it. Shopify: schedule 10 products Sunday night, they go live Monday 7 PM on the Online Store (keep status Active). Etsy: no scheduling found, so run the Etsy listings as the "after-drop" overflow or publish by hand at drop time. A countdown on the own site is cheap theater; use it only when the drop time is real. A fake evergreen timer is cheap and wrecks trust (opinion).
2. **Sold archive.** Moves sales indirectly and is almost free. Leave pages up with a "Sold" badge. Add one sentence of story ("went to a bar in Tampa", no buyer names). Show price only if comfortable; Archive and Bygone show it, and the Rareté's sold page shows at least one ("$8,200.00" appears in the crawl; whether every card carries a price I did not check). Add a "hunt list" form at the bottom.
3. **Story + condition note.** Highest return per hour on a piece nobody has reviewed. Ça Marche's disclosure-before-price habit is the pattern. Matches the Haiku-drafts-from-photo-plus-one-line workflow already planned in `docs/dashboard-handoff.md`.
4. **Video.** Light-on, 5-15 seconds, works on both Etsy (silent) and Shopify. 360 spin is hype: I found no verified vintage-lighting shop using a true spin viewer, and Shopify's 3D path means building a GLB per piece (up to 500 MB each). Exception: the own site already has WebGL; 2-3 hero pieces as interactive models could be a flex, not a conversion tool.
5. **Wishlist.** Etsy's favorited-item discount is the only automated one I could verify. On Shopify it needs an app. For a one-of-one a "someone has this saved" nudge is the real value; do not discount by default.
6. **Disco ball.** Do not create a $0 gift product. On Shopify the gift is never auto-added; on Etsy a gift-wrap line can't be $0. Just pack it and photograph the unboxing for social. Ricky's own correction: do not imply every order gets one. Say "sometimes a disco ball rides along."
7. **Live.** Domino's piece plus Ça Marche are the proof that live sells decor. It rewards a personality and regulars, which suits "a room you walk into". It also needs a steady comment-reading, packing, invoicing routine. Start with one scheduled Instagram Live a month, 8 pieces, announce a week ahead, post previews. Skip Whatnot until there is an audience to bring.
8. **Complete the room.** Free on the own site (a vignette page where every object is a link). The discounted bundle only makes sense on Etsy for slow-moving pairs.
9. **Mystery.** Only for flat, light items (matchbooks, scarves, ephemera) and always "no returns" with a checkbox like Forager's. Never for glass or lamps.
10. **VIP.** With no list, VIP is just a word. Easiest honest version: email the drop link one hour early.
11. **Email/SMS capture without corporate voice.** The Rareté's sourcing form is the model: ask "what are you hunting for" instead of "get 10% off". Shopify Messaging's free 10,000 emails a month is enough for years at our size. SMS cost and consent rules were not verified, so check before collecting phone numbers (`UNVERIFIED`).
