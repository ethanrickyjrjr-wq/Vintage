# 18 Best Retro Websites For Exceptional Design Inspiration 2026
  * October 15, 2026
  * [Aigars Silkalns](https://colorlib.com/wp/author/aigars-silkalns/)
  * [Website Design](https://colorlib.com/wp/category/website-design/)
  * [0 Comments](https://colorlib.com/wp/retro-websites/#respond)


Boost your creativity and improve your web design skills as you browse these retro websites collection. Whether you’re working with portfolios, photo studio websites, designers, eCommerce, or travel companies, retro designs can help you create stunning web designs.
Retro-style websites are gaining popularity among designers. By adding modern flair to the design, websites that embrace the retro or vintage style stimulate a good impression. Various websites that borrow the designs from the past look exemplary. Combining past and modern elements yields creative, beautiful, and memorable designs. Additionally, they even look modern and innovative with the functionality they implement. Of course, these retro themes are also timeless! If you plan to adopt it into your upcoming projects, you may want to check out these awesome, fun, and interactive retro websites we’ve handpicked. These websites are recognized by Awwwards associated with the best retro websites. Meanwhile, the digital marketplace has much more to offer your brand regarding [retro web design](https://colorlib.com/wp/vintage-retro-style-wordpress-themes/).
Check out these creative, fun, vintage ones with a modern flair to help unleash your creativity. Your website should best represent you worldwide. Hence, you should always strive hard to make them stand out! So, if you’re looking for graphic design ideas, you shouldn’t miss these wonderful retro websites.
## Best Retro Website Designs
### 1. [Caava Design](https://caavadesign.com/)
**A custom built WordPress website hosted with Pantheon manged hosting**
![Caava Design](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/caava-design.jpg)
There’s no doubt about the effectiveness of digital marketing in the success of most brands. Likewise, retro-styled websites are still powerful schemes to boost one’s brand. Caava Design is a creative company in San Diego specializing in Beijing brand, interactive and packaging experiences to life. Though retro or vintage, the web elements have classy and professional designs. Moreover, this website also utilizes less saturated retro colors, thus creating an old vintage feel. Fantastic and notable features can help other brands shine, like parallax effect, cool off-canvas menu, slider, and more. The services section stands out in circles and has a nice hover effect. What’s more?
**What stands out:** A warm burnt-orange and cream palette paired with serif headings gives the portfolio an unusually sophisticated, editorial feel, reinforced by a quirky interactive contact form gate that filters leads through design itself.
### 2. [Virtual Gas Museum](https://wmgaz.pl/)
**A custom built website hosted with OzServers**
![Virtual Gas Museum](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/virtual-gas-museum.jpg)
Effectively expand your market with a retro website that’s creative enough to represent your brand. Here’s a beautiful set of retro websites that’s motivating and awe-inspiring. Virtual Gas Museum is a website platform for knowledge about the history, present and future of gas and natural gas. It’s addressed to people interested in the gas industry, and enthusiasts of engineering or technology. The website manifests retro style not just on web elements but also on product images. The web design is stunning and excellent for the colors used in this website, the typography, and the simple transition effect. It also uses sliders, sticky header, off-canvas menu, and video integration.
**What stands out:** Deliberately retains an early-2000s community-site aesthetic — text-heavy, table-based layout, hyperlinked navigation lists — making the design feel like a period artifact that matches the vintage gas-station collectibles it documents.
### 3. [Basement](https://basement.studio/)
**Website is made using DatoCMS and hosted with Vercel Application hosting platform**
![Basement](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/basement.jpg)
The retro style never loses its popularity, even in modern life. Here’s a list of retro websites providing abundant styles you can explore. Basement is a digital studio that builds the future, one project at a time. This retro website features a simple but comprehensive design on the homepage. It uses black and white on all the web elements and stands out with a simple design. Specifically, the hero header exhibits the brand’s name, what it does, and geometric shapes. The clients’ logos are arrayed well to improve the brand’s credibility, so they look attractive. Other cool features include an off-canvas menu, social media icons, a nice hover effect, and more.
**What stands out:** The entire site is navigated as an interactive 3D basement environment rendered in Three.js, with bloom and vignette post-processing effects, replacing conventional menus with spatial hotspots you discover by exploring the room.
### 4. [Harvard Film Archive](https://harvardfilmarchive.org/)
**A custom built website hosted with DreamHost**
![Harvard Film Archive](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/harvard-film-archive.jpg)
If you work with retro websites, it’s best to look into other brands that have seamlessly integrated it into their website. Here’s a creative list of retro websites for inspiration. Harvard Film Archive is a cinematheque and a film archive dedicated to the preservation and public exhibition of the film. Accordingly, the retro style coupled with modern web elements will make the website shine. This website manifests a vintage style on its images, typography, and layout but makes them look modern with the integration of the GSAP animation. The images have a grunge style highlighted using a smooth slider. To offer easy-to-use navigation, it uses a sticky sidebar menu.
**What stands out:** A full-screen animated SVG curtain with continuously spinning paths and staggered reveals opens every page load, giving the experience a deliberately theatrical, cinematic quality before any content appears.
### 5. [Three59](https://three59.ae/)
**A custom made WordPress website hosted with LeaseWeb**
![Three59](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/three59.jpg)
The web has a lot to offer regarding retro website inspiration. Retro colors are a great way to give your website a vintage feel. Here’s Three59, one of the great examples of this vintage or retro style list. It’s a full-fledged advertising and media services provider based in Dubai Production City, United Arab Emirates. The design of the homepage looks simple with a fullscreen layout. The slider beautifully showcases the products individually and even manifests smooth and sleek animation through GSAP. Aside from that, the audio enhances the design, the nice logo, and the off-canvas menu. It also integrates social media icons to boost the brand’s organic visibility.
**What stands out:** The creative studio site uses dramatic visual storytelling with full-width imagery and bold type to communicate their brand-focused design approach.
### 6. [Park West Event](https://www.parkwestchicagoevents.com/)
**The website uses a custom-made WordPress theme. Hosted with Goddady.**
![Park West Event](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/park-west-event.jpg)
Pick one retro style you want to embed in your design to invoke the brand nostalgia you prefer. Then, add a modern touch to your design to look magnificent. Here’s a cool list of retro websites worth spending time with. Park West is a concert venue located in Chicago, Illinois. This theatre opened in 1916 as a vaudeville and movie theatre by the Ascher brothers. Particularly, it can house up to 1000 guests. With the desire to incorporate a vintage feel into the site, colors, typography, and grunge textures are retro. Moreover, the nice hover effect also charm the design and the simple animations.
**What stands out:** The venue site leads with immersive event photography and uses a clean layout that lets the spaces speak for themselves.
### 7. [Tens](https://tens.co/)
**Built with** : **[Shopify](https://colorlib.com/go/shopify)**
![Tens](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/tens.jpg)
Check out these wonderful retro websites that will serve as a marvelous inspiration. Tens is an independent subclass company that prides itself on seeing things differently. This eCommerce website has a simple but captivating design on the homepage. The hero header features a split-screen layout where the image, tagline, and CTAs are well organized. Aside from the vintage colors it applies, the images also appear retro-style. The featured products look outstanding while the customer reviews in a slider are visually appealing. Other wonderful features include sticky header, Instagram feed, line icons, video integration, and more.
**What stands out:** The mega-menu transforms standard navigation into an immersive browsing experience by embedding large product photography directly inside the dropdown, turning category selection into a visual preview rather than a list of links.
### 8. [Hypergiant](https://www.hypergiant.com/)
**A custom built website made with WordPress. Hosted with[WPEngine](https://colorlib.com/out/wpengine)**
![Hypergiant](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/hypergiant.jpg)
Here’s a selection of retro websites that can kindle the interests of brands that wish to integrate retro designs into their projects. Hypergiant is an AI provider for the Fortune 500. The website’s playful colors and the retrofuturistic vibe alludes to when we felt the future was just around the corner. Particularly, the vintage background image and simple typography greatly resemble retro design. Additionally, the borders of web elements also add creativity to the overall design – from buttons, services, a list of abbreviated services, clients list, and more. In addition, the social media icons on the bottom, sticky header, and off-canvas menu are impressive.
**What stands out:** A minimal dark background with vivid purple and cyan accents anchors a bold hero, leaning hard into a tech-militaristic aesthetic that feels more defense-contractor than typical SaaS.
### 9. [Martens Brewery](https://www.martens.be/)
**Website made with[Drupal](https://www.drupal.org/)**
![Martens Brewery ](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/martens-brewery.jpg)
This collection of retro websites manifests different retro styles, but they are magnificent. So, browse this list and get motivated. Martens Brewery is a proud and ambitious family business that has already been at the top of the Belgian brewing industry for eight generations. With the high-quality products they offer, its website reveals similar design quality. Embracing the GSAP animation, the web elements shows cool and subtle animations. The presentation of the products is seamless and intuitive using a slider. It also features a sticky sidebar, so the menu is always visible. Other beautiful features include Google Maps, a smooth transition effect, and more!
**What stands out:** The brewery site combines heritage branding with modern layout to communicate decades of brewing tradition through a contemporary digital experience.
### 10. [Global Welsh](https://globalwelsh.com/)
**A custom built website hosted with Krystal Hosting**
![Global Welsh](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/global-welsh.jpg)
Vintage styles are timeless and even look more gorgeous when added with modern flair. Have a glimpse of these retro websites that will inspire you in your next web project. Global Welsh is a growing global community of people with an affinity for Wales. It is committed to creating new connections, opportunities and ideas to benefit Wale’s future. The hero header features a great vintage-looking video compilation with a call-to-action button and headline. The logo represents the brand and social media icons in the header. Since it uses a sticky header, the menu is always at hand to easily navigate the site.
**What stands out:** Custom illustrated cloud SVGs and playful imagery create an approachable, storybook visual language that contrasts with the typically corporate tone of diaspora-network organizations.
### 11. [Poolsuite](https://poolsuite.net/)
**Built with** : **[Custom](https://poolsuite.net/)**
![poolsuite retro computer interface website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/poolsuite.jpg)
Poolsuite (formerly Poolside FM) is a virtual retro computer experience that mimics a classic 90s operating system interface. The site features draggable dialog boxes, pixelated icons, skeuomorphic drop shadows, and a built-in music player — all designed to look like an old Mac OS desktop.
**What stands out:** The entire site functions as a nostalgic operating system simulation, complete with retro window chrome, pixel-perfect icons, and an ambient soundtrack — one of the most committed retro UI experiences on the web.
### 12. [Cameron’s World](https://www.cameronsworld.net/)
**Built with** : **[Custom](https://www.cameronsworld.net/)**
![camerons world geocities tribute retro website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/camerons-world.jpg)
A web collage tribute to the lost GeoCities era, built from thousands of archived pages (1994-2009). The site features animated GIFs, bright clashing colors, ASCII art, and densely packed layouts that authentically recreate the wild aesthetic of 90s personal homepages.
**What stands out:** A living museum of early internet self-expression, complete with original archived graphics and under-construction banners from the GeoCities era — pure nostalgia for anyone who remembers the early web.
### 13. [Sugarfire Pie](https://sugarfirepie.com/)
**Built with** : **[WordPress](https://wordpress.org/)**
![sugarfire pie 1950s retro bakery website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/sugarfire-pie.jpg)
A bakery site with a 1950s diner motif blended with Twin Peaks-inspired eeriness. The design features neon-styled SVG elements, vintage typography, retro color schemes, and animated graphics including clock hands that rotate with scroll position.
**What stands out:** Animated neon signs, grunge textures, and mid-century illustration style create an immersive experience that goes beyond surface-level nostalgia — every element matches the handmade pie brand identity.
### 14. [Abel’s on Queen](https://www.abelsonqueen.com/)
**Built with** : **[Webflow](https://www.abelsonqueen.com/)**
![abels on queen vintage barbershop retro website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/abels-on-queen.jpg)
An upscale barbershop website honoring its 1894 heritage through elegant serif typography, ornamental curl graphics, decorative monograms, and a rich vintage color palette inspired by classic barbering tradition.
**What stands out:** The design balances genuine historical authenticity with modern Webflow interactivity — every ornamental divider and shadow effect reinforces the shop’s 130-year legacy while remaining fully responsive.
### 15. [Mochi Online Shoppu](https://shoppu.mochi.at/)
**Built with** : **[Custom](https://shoppu.mochi.at/)**
![mochi shoppu pixel art retro ecommerce website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/mochi-shoppu.jpg)
An e-commerce site built entirely around an 80s-90s video game aesthetic. Pixel art fonts, 8-bit styled borders, Game Boy-inspired footer elements, and buttons that physically depress on hover like arcade machine controls.
**What stands out:** CSS animations use the steps() timing function to create deliberately choppy retro motion effects — the custom pixel cursor and arcade-style UI create a uniquely playful shopping experience.
### 16. [Basement Chronicles](https://chronicles.basement.studio/)
**Built with** : **[Custom](https://chronicles.basement.studio/)**
![basement chronicles pixel art adventure game retro website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/basement-chronicles.jpg)
A pixelated throwback to classic point-and-click adventure games, built as an interactive browser experience. The site uses pixel art graphics, retro game mechanics, and adventure game navigation to tell the story of the Basement Studio team.
**What stands out:** Rather than just applying retro visuals to a standard layout, the entire site is structured as a playable point-and-click adventure game with original pixel art — far beyond typical retro web design.
### 17. [We Luv Video](https://www.weluvvideo.org/)
**Built with** : **[Squarespace](https://colorlib.com/go/squarespace)**
![we luv video vhs retro squarespace website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/we-luv-video.jpg)
An Austin-based nonprofit celebrating physical media culture with a design built around neon signage, VHS tape imagery, dark backgrounds, and the tagline “The revolution will not be streamed.” The aesthetic evokes classic video rental stores and arcades.
**What stands out:** Neon logo animation and VHS-heavy imagery create an authentic video store atmosphere — proof that Squarespace can deliver a convincingly retro look when styled with strong brand direction.
### 18. [Boogie Lover Band](https://boogieloverband.com/)
**Built with** : **[WordPress](https://wordpress.org/)**
![boogie lover band disco retro website example](https://colorlib.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/boogie-lover-band.jpg)
A disco and dance band site drenched in 1970s-80s aesthetics. Deep purple backgrounds, bright cyan and hot pink accents, and gold typography create an authentically groovy disco-era visual experience.
**What stands out:** The retro styling is intentional brand alignment, not ironic kitsch — the purple, cyan, hot pink, and gold palette directly references the disco era and creates thematic coherence between the music and web presence.
