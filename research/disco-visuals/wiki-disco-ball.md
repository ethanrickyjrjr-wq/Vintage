From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Sphere with mirrored facets that rotates

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Mirrored_Disco_Ball_with_Light_and_Motor.jpg/250px-Mirrored_Disco_Ball_with_Light_and_Motor.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mirrored_Disco_Ball_with_Light_and_Motor.jpg)A mirrored disco ball
A **disco ball** (also known as a **mirror ball** or **glitter ball**) is a roughly spherical object that reflects light directed at it in many directions, producing a complex display. Its surface consists of hundreds or thousands of facets, nearly all of approximately the same shape and size, and each has a mirrored surface. Usually, it is mounted well above the heads of the people present, suspended from a device that causes it to rotate steadily on a vertical [axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_rotation "Axis of rotation"), and illuminated by spotlights, so that stationary viewers experience beams of light flashing over them, and see myriad spots of light spinning around the walls of the room.
Miniature glitter balls are sold as novelties and used for several decorative purposes, including dangling from the rear-view mirror of an automobile or Christmas tree ornaments. Glitter balls may have inspired a homemade version of the [sparkleball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparkleball "Sparkleball"), the American outsider craft of building decorative light balls out of Christmas lights and plastic cups.
## History
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Disco_ball&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/LouisianaFiveBandstand_%28restored_version%29.jpg/250px-LouisianaFiveBandstand_%28restored_version%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:LouisianaFiveBandstand_\(restored_version\).jpg)A mirrored ball can be seen above the [bandstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandstand "Bandstand") in this 1919 photo of the [Louisiana Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Five "Louisiana Five") jazz band. [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Disco_Ball.JPG/250px-Disco_Ball.JPG?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Disco_Ball.JPG)A disco ball and spotlight in the Fishbowl of [Currier House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currier_House_\(Harvard_College\) "Currier House \(Harvard College\)")
"Disco balls" were first patented by US inventor Louis Bernard Woeste as "myriad reflectors".[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-NBC-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-2) The product was produced by his company, Stephens & Woeste.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-3) The concept predated the patent, with records of a "mirrored ball" dating back to 1897.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-Vice-4)
Disco balls began to be widely used in [nightclubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightclub "Nightclub") in the 1920s.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-5) An early example can be seen in the nightclub sequence of _[Berlin: Die Sinfonie der Großstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin:_Die_Sinfonie_der_Gro%C3%9Fstadt "Berlin: Die Sinfonie der Großstadt")_ , a German [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") from 1927. From the 1960s to the 1980s, these devices were a standard piece of equipment in [discothèques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discoth%C3%A8ques "Discothèques"), and the term "disco ball" became the products most popular name.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-Vice-4)
A [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky "Louisville, Kentucky") company known as Omega Mirror Products claims to have made 90% of the disco balls used in the United States during the disco craze and remains a supplier.[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-6)[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-NBC-1)
Disco balls had a resurgence in the 2020s,[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-8) particularly as [home decor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_decor "Home decor").[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-9) In 2023, disco balls were suggested as a safe method of viewing [partial solar eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse "Solar eclipse").[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-spacecom2023-10)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-physicsworld-11)
## Popular culture
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Disco_ball&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Popular culture")]
[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Disco_Santa.jpg/250px-Disco_Santa.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Disco_Santa.jpg)Disco Santa [ornament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_ornament "Christmas ornament") typical of 2020s disco [kitsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsch "Kitsch")
Early appearances of the disco ball in popular culture include the films _[Berlin: Die Sinfonie der Großstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin:_Die_Sinfonie_der_Gro%C3%9Fstadt "Berlin: Die Sinfonie der Großstadt")_ (1927), _[Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca_\(film\) "Casablanca \(film\)")_ (1942), and _[Some Like It Hot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Like_It_Hot "Some Like It Hot")_ (1959).[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-12) Disco balls were used as [iconography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconography "Iconography") of the disco-era in films such as _[Saturday Night Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Fever "Saturday Night Fever")_. The disco ball has been utilised in contemporary films such as in a rave scene in _[Groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_\(film\) "Groove \(film\)")_ (2000),[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-13) and a disco skull used in _[Scooby-Doo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooby-Doo_\(film\) "Scooby-Doo \(film\)")_ (2002).[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-14)
In music, the disco ball has been utilised on tour by artists such as [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2 "U2"),[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-15) [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_\(band\) "Yes \(band\)"), [Paramore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramore "Paramore"), and [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna "Madonna").[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-16)[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-17) Disco balls have been used on the album covers of _[Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_All_the_Time._Disco,_Occasionally "Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally")_ and _[FutureSex/LoveSounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FutureSex/LoveSounds "FutureSex/LoveSounds")_.
The [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") hero [Dazzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazzler_\(Marvel_Comics\) "Dazzler \(Marvel Comics\)") has worn clothing inspired by disco balls.[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-18)[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-19)
The UK television series _[Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing "Strictly Come Dancing")_ and its US counterpart _[Dancing with the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_\(U.S._TV_series\) "Dancing with the Stars \(U.S. TV series\)")_ award competition winners a "Glitter Ball Trophy" (UK) or "Mirror Ball Trophy" (US).
The terms [disco ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball_\(disambiguation\) "Disco ball \(disambiguation\)"), [mirror ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_ball_\(disambiguation\) "Mirror ball \(disambiguation\)"), and [glitterball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glitterball_\(disambiguation\) "Glitterball \(disambiguation\)") have been used to name songs, albums, and television episodes.
## Design and operation
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Disco_ball&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Design and operation")]
A mirror ball typically consists of a lightweight spherical core, commonly made of plastic, foam, or metal, which is uniformly covered with hundreds or thousands of small mirrored facets. These mirrored tiles are usually made of glass or reflective acrylic and are arranged in a tight mosaic to maximise light reflection. When a focused light source, such as a pinspot or stage spotlight, is directed at the ball, each mirror facet reflects a beam of light, producing a pattern of moving dots that spread across surrounding surfaces.
Most mirror balls are mounted on a rotating motor that slowly turns the sphere, enhancing the dynamic light effect. Standard rotation speeds range from 0.5 to 3 revolutions per minute, though variable-speed and remote-controlled motors are also available. The resulting visual effect depends on several factors: the intensity and angle of the light source, the distance between the light and the ball, and the size of the mirrored facets.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-20)
Mirror "balls" have also been adapted into unique designs and shapes, including sharks and skulls.[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-21)[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-22)
## Records
[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Disco_ball&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Records")]
English rock band [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd "Pink Floyd") used a glitter ball on their [A Momentary Lapse of Reason Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Momentary_Lapse_of_Reason_Tour "A Momentary Lapse of Reason Tour") of 1987 and [The Division Bell Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Division_Bell_Tour "The Division Bell Tour") of 1994. The glitter ball used on the former was somewhat larger than normal but nowhere near as large as the glitter ball used on the 1994 tour. This particular glitter ball is one of the largest in the world: 4.90 metres in diameter, it rises to a height of 21.3 metres before opening to a width of 7.3 metres, revealing a 12 kilowatt Phoebus [HMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrargyrum_medium-arc_iodide "Hydrargyrum medium-arc iodide") lamp.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-23) Both can be seen on the video of each tour, _[Delicate Sound of Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delicate_Sound_of_Thunder_\(film\) "Delicate Sound of Thunder \(film\)")_ and _[Pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_\(1995_film\) "Pulse \(1995 film\)")_ , during the song "[Comfortably Numb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfortably_Numb "Comfortably Numb")".
American singer-songwriter [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_\(entertainer\) "Madonna \(entertainer\)") has used glitter balls in several of her tours. During [The Girlie Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Girlie_Show_\(Madonna\) "The Girlie Show \(Madonna\)") in 1993, she descended while sitting on one before performing "[Express Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_Yourself_\(Madonna_song\) "Express Yourself \(Madonna song\)")", and later in 2006, she used a 2-ton glitter ball that was embellished by 2 million dollars' worth of [Swarovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarovski "Swarovski") crystals, which used a hydraulic system to open like flower petals for her entrance during her [Confessions Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_Tour "Confessions Tour").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-24)
Despite claims that the world's largest disco ball can be found on the promenade in [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool "Blackpool"), United Kingdom,[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-25) it is smaller than the [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records") holder. The ball was made for the [2014 Bestival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bestival#Bestival_2014 "Bestival") event in England and is 10.33 metres (33ft 11in) in diameter.[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_ball#cite_note-26)
