## Vintage Pyrex sold prices, July to October 2026
The median vintage Pyrex single piece in our July to October 2026 sample sold for $29.90, and the middle half of sales fell between $17.99 and $56.94. About 12% of single pieces sold for $100 or more, and 32% of listings went for under $20.
The sample is 5,269 sold listings our scans pulled in: 5,174 single pieces and 95 lots, one row per listing. It is not every Pyrex sale anywhere. The table below has the verified figures, and the rest of this post covers what drives them.
## What separates a $17.99 sale from a $115 one
The top tenth of sales started at $115, while the bottom quarter sat at $17.99 or below. That gap is rarely about the dish itself. It comes down to four checks a reseller makes before pricing.
**Identity.** Is it a named pattern, or a plain white or primary-color piece? Patterns such as Butterprint, Gooseberry and Terra Stripe have their own buyers. A generic bowl competes with everything else on the shelf.
**Variant.** Color, size and model number change the comp. A Pyrex piece in one color can sell for far more than the same shape in another, so a comp for "Pyrex mixing bowl" tells you almost nothing.
**Condition.** Printed patterns wear. Look for faded color, scratches inside the bowl, chips on the rim and dishwasher haze. In my experience, condition is the first thing that sinks an otherwise good find.
**Completeness.** Does it have its lid? Is the nesting set whole? Several of the biggest sales below were sets, and a set with a missing bowl is a different listing.

Every title names a pattern or a color. Most add a model number, a size or a set count, such as 401, 475, 4.5qt or 441-444. The 475 title also says Employee Gift, which points to a limited item rather than a retail one. Two titles are multi-bowl sets, and one is a "collection."
Several titles also say "rare" or "HTF." Those are the seller's words, not proof. The titles say nothing about condition, so I won't guess what shape these pieces were in.
These sales are also a small slice. With 12% of single pieces at $100 or more, far more than six sold in that range.
## What to do with a vintage Pyrex find
Price against sold comps for the exact variant, not the brand. A vintage Pyrex sold comp is only useful if the pattern, color, size and completeness match your piece.
  1. **Identify the pattern and model number.** Check the bottom stamp and the printed pattern before searching.
  2. **Search sold listings, not asking prices.** Match color, size and whether the lid is included.
  3. **Grade your piece honestly.** Compare wear against the comps you found, not against the best one.
  4. **Price the set as a set.** If you have a full nesting set, check whether sets of that pattern sold together before splitting them.
  5. **List the high-value pieces first.** They deserve the best photos and a title that names the pattern, color and number.

### Where the cheap end is not worth listing
With 32% of listings under $20, this category has a large low end. Glass is heavy and breakable, so packing and shipping take a big share of a sub-$20 sale. If a plain piece comps near $17.99 and has wear, it may not clear your costs once fees and shipping come out.
Bundle those pieces instead. Lots in the sample had a median of $33, which sat close to the single-piece median of $29.90. A matching lot of plain bowls can clear a better price per listing than selling each piece separately. Otherwise, sell them locally or skip them at the thrift store.

## Common Questions
### **How much do Pyrex sell for on eBay?**
In the sold listings our scans pulled in from July to October 2026, the median vintage Pyrex single piece sold for $29.90. The middle half sold between $17.99 and $56.94. The sample is not split by marketplace, so check eBay sold listings for your exact pattern, color and size before pricing.
### **Which vintage Pyrex are worth the most?**
The highest sales in this sample were named patterns and specific variants: Terra Stripe 401 Yellow on Yellow at $1,528, a 475 Christmas Casserole employee gift at $1,500, and a Sculpted Tinted Lidded 4.5qt Mixing Bowl in blue at $1,199. Amish Butterprint and Pink Gooseberry Cinderella bowl sets also sold between $842 and $1,000. Match the exact variant before assuming a piece is in that range.
### **Is it worth selling vintage Pyrex under $20?**
Often not, as a single piece. About 32% of sold listings in this sample went for under $20, and glass shipping and fees take a large share of that. Selling cheap pieces as a matching lot or locally usually works better.
