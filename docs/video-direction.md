## Read this first

The ten things that matter most.

1. **Nothing is built or signed up yet.** No accounts, no real stock listed, no checkout. The site's listings are samples with illustrative prices. So no post links to a product until real stock and a real page for each piece exist. November is a setup month.
2. **Start free and native.** A Business Instagram account, Instagram's Edits app, one 90-minute shoot. Add Buffer in week 2. The automatic version comes last (section 7).
3. **The rules never bend** (section 11): no claims by DM or comment, no discounts, codes or offers, never "visit", "in store" or "stop by", the mirror ball is "sometimes", real photos only.
4. **The only link is the listing page.** Link in bio plus a piece number on every post. A web address typed in a public comment is not tappable.
5. **Post often and stay in one lane.** 10 or more reels a month, 3 to 5 feed posts a week, one corner, the same show names. Instagram ranks watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach.
6. **There is no proven best time to post.** Fix one weekly drop slot and run the four-week test (section 10).
7. **Sound is what separates everyday from sloppy.** A clip-on mic if you can.
8. **Pack in a plain outer box.** Branding goes inside: the insurer will not pay loss or theft claims when the outside "alludes to" the contents. Insure anything over $250 (section 13).
9. **No texting customers yet.** Email goes through Kit Free. SMS waits for a Florida lawyer to read the consent wording (section 13).
10. **Four open items gate the rest** (section 15): is the @holliewoodandvine handle ours, is there a registered business, does Hollie approve every post, and does she appear on camera.

## 1. What we are going for

**A friend with great taste, showing you what she just found. One corner, one steady take.**

- **The pieces and Hollie are the show.** The piece first, then Hollie's take on it. The set is one corner people learn to recognise.
- **Talking is how taste comes across.** Voice and on-camera are the default. How much of Hollie's face shows is her call.
- **Real beats polished.** A specific real piece, a real flaw said out loud, a person with an opinion. Keep the basics clean: steady, readable, audible.
- **Same corner, same framing, same show names.** People come back to a show, not a clip.
- **Test it.** Film some pieces both ways, talking and silent, and compare after a month.

## 3. How it looks

| Thing | Do this |
|---|---|
| Camera | Phone vertical, locked off on a clamp or tripod, eye level, waist-up. Full-length for fit checks |
| Distance | Step back and crop in. Skip the 0.5x lens up close; it bends edges |
| Set | One corner, shot on the diagonal, with 2 or 3 objects that always sit there |
| Light | A window to the side or front, never behind. Keep the lamp you are showing on, and lock exposure and white balance |
| Sound | The biggest difference between everyday and sloppy. A clip-on mic if you can; a rug, curtain or clothes rack cuts echo |
| Text | Hook line on screen from the first frame. Captions on throughout |
| Wearables | Hold to the camera 6 s, wear 10 s, full-length turn 8 s, detail 5 s (label, closure, flaw) |
| Mirrors and glass | A mirror fit check is fine; keep the phone and anything private out of the reflection. Shoot glass and mirrors for sale off-axis and farther back |
| Privacy | Keep street windows, house numbers, mail, parcels and labels out of frame. Tag a city or region only |

## 4. How to talk

### The six beats (20 to 35 seconds)

1. **Hook.** Two seconds or less, also on screen.
2. **What it is.** One fact: era, maker, material.
3. **Why you.** A taste line: "I have a thing for ___."
4. **Proof.** A flick, a click, a turn, or a measurement said aloud and shown on screen.
5. **One honest flaw.** Say it and show it close.
6. **Close.** See below.

Size: 30 to 45 words is about 15 seconds; 70 to 90 is about 30. Talk to one person. Jump-cut the breaths.

### Pick the lines that sound like you

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | "I have a thing for [x], and this might be the best one I've found." | "Okay. This one." | "Would you wear this to [place]?" |
| **Why you** | "Anything [colour, material] is an immediate yes for me." | "I saw it across the room and walked straight to it." | "I'd put it next to [x]. Tell me I'm wrong." |
| **Sign-off, now and then** | "Do it for the plot." | "Hollie dresses the set." | Stop, no sign-off |

### The close

| Now, with no link | Once pieces are listed |
|---|---|
| "I'm finding more like this. Follow so you see it first." | "Piece [No.] is on the listing page. Link in bio." |
| "Save this for the drop." | "Full photos and honest condition: link in bio, piece [No.]." |
| "Tell me what you'd put next to it." | "Gone." plus the next drop day, for a sold piece |

### Leave out

- Visit, in store, stop by, our doors, a business location pin.
- DM to buy, claim, comment SOLD, first to.
- Discount, code, offer, giveaway. The mirror ball is "sometimes", never a promise.
- "Interior design" (say style).
- A maker or "signed" unless it is marked; otherwise "in the style of" or "marked [x]".
- Safety claims on wiring or on lead in glass.
- AI product images, AI footage, AI avatars.
- "Hey guys", "welcome back", channel intros.

## 5. Three scripts

Swap every bracket for a real fact. The pieces are examples.

**Just in (about 30 s): a brass and cream ceramic lamp**

On screen: "I have a thing for brass lamps."

Spoken: "I have a thing for brass lamps, and this might be the best one I've found. It's [era], [maker or unmarked], brass and cream ceramic with a pineapple finial. [One proof only you can give: the weight in your hand, a flick, the click of the switch.] Here's the flaw: [real flaw, close-up]. And when you turn it on, the whole corner changes. Follow so you see the next one first."

**Fit check (about 25 s): a feathered cocktail hat**

On screen: "Would you wear this to [place]?"

Hold it to the lens: "A [era] cocktail hat, [material], the feather is [condition]." Put it on, full turn. "It sits [tilted, flat], it's a [size], and it needs [one honest note]." Close: "I'd wear it with [one thing]. Tell me what you'd put it with."

**Click (about 12 s): any lamp or glass**

No talking, or one line over it. Dark corner. A hand reaches the switch. The light comes on at about 2 to 3 seconds. On screen: "This corner, with one switch." Show the flaw in the last 2 seconds if there is one.

## 6. One shoot, 90 minutes

| Min | Block | What to film | Clips |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 15 | **Set** | Pick the corner, clear the frame, lock exposure, record 10 s of your voice and listen on earbuds | none kept |
| 15 to 55 | **Wearables, 6 pieces** | Hold to camera 6 s, worn 10 s, full-length turn 8 s, detail 5 s | about 24 |
| 55 to 85 | **Objects, 6 pieces** | Hold and talk 20 s, one click or turn 8 s, flaw close-up 5 s, dressed corner 8 s | about 24 |
| 85 to 90 | **Talk** | Each hook twice, each close once | about 12 |

About 60 clips make 8 to 10 posts. Aim for 10 or more reels a month, so one shoot a month covers it.

## 10. The schedule

### Every week

Once the scheduler is in:

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot day, every 4 weeks | The 90-minute batch. Copy the clips off the phone | 90 min |
| Weekly | Approve the queued week: 5 feed posts, 3 Pins, 3 Shorts. Fix anything flagged | 30 to 45 min |

### Drop day

Niche vintage shops sell on one fixed weekly slot stated in the bio, with a teaser the day before. Four shops do it: Wednesday 2 pm Central, Wednesday 7 pm Eastern, Monday 6 pm Eastern, Tuesday 2 pm Mountain. Ours is a **placeholder: Wednesday 7 pm Eastern, with a teaser Tuesday at noon.** It is a guess, not evidence. Pick the real slot and never move it. Until real stock exists, nothing is for sale and drop day is a brand post.

### How often

- **10 or more reels a month.** Instagram's FAQ says the most follower growth goes to accounts that post this much.
- **3 to 5 feed posts a week.** A study of 2.1 million posts. Mix in carousels, which led views at every follower size in a 21.6-million-post set. Add audio to every carousel.
- **Reply to comments within 7 days.**
- One 90-minute shoot makes about 60 clips, which is 8 to 10 posts. One shoot a month covers the reel target.

### How far ahead each tool queues

| Tool | Limit |
|---|---|
| Instagram, in the app | 25 posts a day, up to 30 days ahead. One older source says 75 days for professional accounts, so check in the app |
| Meta Business Suite | Free scheduling for Facebook and Instagram |
| Pinterest | Up to 30 days ahead, 10 Pins queued |
| TikTok Studio | 10 days ahead. One snippet says 30; not verified |
| Buffer free | 10 scheduled posts per channel |
| Cannot be scheduled | Collab posts and product tags: post them live |

Pinterest wants seasonal Pins live 4 to 6 weeks early, but its queue holds only 30 days. So make assets early and fill the queues monthly. The queue limits also set how long Hollie can be away: about 3 weeks of Pins and 10 days of TikTok.

### When to post: run the four-week test

No best time exists. Five studies from 2026 give five answers, up to 16 hours apart. Instagram itself says early reactions do not boost a post. So we test with Hollie's own audience.

| Week | Tue 8 am | Wed 12:30 pm | Thu 7 pm | Sat 11 am |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reel 1 (neon) | Reel 2 (lamp click) | Carousel (read a lamp) | Reel 3 (wrapping) |
| 2 | Reel 3 | Reel 1 | Reel 2 | Carousel |
| 3 | Carousel | Reel 3 | Reel 1 | Reel 2 |
| 4 | Reel 2 | Carousel | Reel 3 | Reel 1 |

Each format lands in every slot once. Room and brand content only, with no product links.

- **Measure** per post: views, viewers, average watch time, likes, shares (sends), saves and follows. The two that count are **average watch time** and **sends per viewer**. Read each at 24 hours and at 7 days.
- **Keep it fair:** the same caption template, hashtag count, cover style and call to action on all 16. No boosting, giveaways or collabs. No extra feed post within 3 hours of a test post. The same audio rule. Skip the Thanksgiving-to-New-Year weeks. One sheet for everything.
- **Decide:** a slot qualifies only if it ranks top two of four on both measures in at least 3 of the 4 weeks. Otherwise write "no detectable difference" and use the slot easiest for Hollie. Retest the top two against the bottom one for four more weeks.
- **Be honest about it.** At zero followers a test mostly measures noise. Sixteen posts cannot separate slot from weekday or content. Treat the result as a first screen. Insights adds "times your followers are active" at 100 followers, and Trial Reels open at 200.
- A second design exists (midweek midday against Sunday evening, 8 weeks). Run one design, not both, or the results blur.

## 11. Rules every post follows

- **No claims by DM or comment.** The only call to action is the listing page. No bidding or claim-to-bid lives. No discounts, no codes, "Absolutely no offers."
- **No showroom language:** never "visit", "in store", "our doors" or "stop by", and no business-pin location tag. A city or region tag only.
- **Hollie is she/her.** "Style", never "interior design." The mirror ball is "sometimes", never promised, never a giveaway: Instagram lists contest and giveaway promotion as not recommendable.
- **Real photos of real items only.** AI for captions, cuts and layout around real photos is fine. No AI product images, AI footage or AI avatars. Editorial cutouts contain generated pixels and must not sit on a cover that sells a specific piece.
- **Sold pieces say "Gone"** and stay visible.
- **No product-link post until real stock exists.**

### The link rule

A web address typed in a public Instagram comment is **not tappable**, so "reply LINK with the link" does not work as written. What works:

1. **Link in bio plus a piece number on every post.** The listing page answers the number. This always works.
2. **"Comment LINK" as an optional extra:** a public reply ("sent, check your DMs") plus a private reply or DM that carries only the listing address. It needs a tool or our own Live app. One private reply per comment, within 7 days, 750 an hour; it lands in Requests unless the commenter follows. Whether "comment LINK" counts as engagement bait is unstated, so check Account status weekly.
3. **A link sticker on Stories**, added by Hollie on drop days. A human tap, always.
4. **Never gate the link behind a follow or a comment.** Meta's Spam standard bans it.

## 12. How we grow

Ranked by how well the evidence holds. Each line is from Instagram's own statements or a source we checked. None is a promise.

1. **Make reels people send.** Sends per reach is one of Instagram's three top signals, slightly more important than likes for non-followers. Watch time is the most important for everyone. A reel that gets texted to a friend ("this lamp") beats one that earns a like.
2. **Clear the five gates to reach non-followers:** no watermarks, add audio, 3 minutes or less, original, good account status. Your own photos and video count as original.
3. **Post enough and keep one lane:** lamps, pink glass, the burgundy room. Instagram says a theme your audience already responds to earns similar distribution.
4. **Put keywords where Search reads them:** the Name field, bio and captions, plus 3 to 5 niche hashtags. Hashtags describe a post; they do not raise reach.
5. **Reply to comments within 7 days.**
6. **Trial Reels at 200 followers.** They go to non-followers first, so hooks can be tested before followers see them.
7. **Collab posts with adjacent accounts** (vintage dealers, stylists, designers), not competing ones. Post them live.
8. **A Broadcast channel as the drop list**, with no claims in it.
9. **Pinterest is the long-lead search channel.** Plain Pins to real pages, live 4 to 6 weeks before a season. YouTube Shorts mirror the same cuts.
10. **A signup-only hunt list:** collect emails or Broadcast followers for "find me a piece" requests before stock exists, with no listing link until it does.

What no source gives: a time to the first 1,000 followers or to steady sales. The nearest case is 7,100 followers in about 12 months (a Halifax shop, 2019 to 2020). The growth stories are mostly 2018 to 2021 winners. Use the four-week test and Instagram Insights instead.

Hooks: 11 of 15 sampled reels put a text hook on screen by about 1 second, median 7 words, and 9 of 15 had no speech. A bank of 30 hooks, 12 series formats, caption formulas and 10 call-to-action lines that obey the rules is in the social manual's content banks.

## 13. The shop side

### Shipping and the unboxing

- **Plain outer box, all branding inside.** Shipsurance will not pay loss or theft claims when the packaging "alludes to" the contents. It also excludes neon and float glass on both its Etsy and Shopify terms. Stained glass is excluded on the Shopify terms; the Etsy terms need a written answer. Ask Shipsurance in writing about framed mirrors, stained-glass shades and neon.
- **Pack to the standard.** USPS refuses damage claims where articles were "not properly wrapped" or where the box shows no damage. The tightest claim clock is Shipsurance concealed damage: 15 days from delivery.
- **Insure anything over $250.** Etsy Purchase Protection stops at "up to $250" and covers only the first damage claim each calendar year.
- **Use fixed shipping classes A to D on Etsy.** Etsy's calculated shipping undercharges double boxes: it is USPS-only, uses the item's size before boxing, picks the smallest box and has no handling-fee field. Use flat rates plus a handling fee on Shopify later.
- **Listing line:** "ships double-boxed, insured to sale price." Add a lamp parts bag, an "open it on camera" card, and a 15-second packing reel.
- **Do not turn on Etsy's free-shipping guarantee shop-wide.** It covers every item at $35 and up, which is the whole shop, heavy glass included.

### Email and text

- **Kit Free now, Shopify Email once on Shopify.** Send caps decide the tool, not contact caps: a weekly drop mails the whole list about five times a month.
- **First dibs an hour early:** the signup works as a hunt form with tags, a QR on the box card, and buyers are never added automatically.
- **Email law (CAN-SPAM)** is opt-out. It needs a postal address (a PO box counts) and a working unsubscribe.
- **No SMS yet.** Texts need prior express written consent, at $500 per violation federally and in Florida. From November 25, 2026 a STOP reply ends all marketing texts from that sender. Quiet hours are 8 am to 9 pm federally and 8 am to 8 pm in Florida; follow the stricter. A Florida lawyer reads the consent wording first.

### Reviews and proof

- **A one-of-one listing ends its own product reviews,** so proof lives at shop level: Etsy shop reviews and Judge.me store reviews.
- **Invite every buyer, in the same words, to post an honest review.** No incentives of any kind, including the mirror ball after a review, and no reviews from family. Google and Shopify ban both.
- **Most small vintage shops show no stars.** Proof comes from tenure, sold counts, curation and authentication: a "Rehomed" count, "Checked by Hollie" on lamps, and a "Where it landed" photo page with logged consent. A recognizable person in a buyer's photo needs consent under Fla. Stat. 540.08.
- Etsy Star Seller needs "90 days after your first sale".

### Search and content

- **One real page per piece.** Items open as `#item-<id>` pop-ups today, and Google indexes the page, not the fragment. A sold archive, product markup, Merchant Center and Pinterest all need one real address per piece.
- **Sold pages stay up** at HTTP 200, marked SOLD, with `OutOfStock` and the real price kept. Mark every piece `UsedCondition`, never `Refurbished`: Google means warranty-backed restoration.
- **Maker-and-marks guides** shot from our own pieces. People who search "identify" or "markings" land on guides and Facebook threads, not marketplaces.
- **Pinterest gets plain Pins and Article Pins.** Pinterest does not allow pre-owned products as Product Pins.
- **Google's free product listings need checkout on the site.** Until then the site gets product-snippet markup only, and the free listings come from Shopify.

### Pricing over time (proposed, not decided)

- **Offers anchor buyers low.** On eBay first offers average 61% of list, and bargained sales land at 73%.
- **A markdown ladder** published by other shops ends near 30% off by day 90: 10%, 20%, 30% at days 30, 60, 90. Add a "Recently Reduced" shelf.
- **"Was / now" is a legal claim** (16 CFR 233.1). Write "Listed at $X on [date]. Now $Y."
- Crossing a hundred matters. $245 versus $250 does not.

### Local, Southwest Florida

- **Vintage Market Days, January 29 to 31, 2027,** at the Lee Civic Center. The booth fee is unpublished: email sgulfcoastfl@vintagemarketdays.com. It is the one true vintage audience nearby and it falls in the January-to-March peak.
- **River District Art Walk ($50) and Pine Ridge ($160)** are cheap tests with eligibility catches.
- **Google Business Profile** is only for a business that "travels to customers where they are", up to 20 service areas and about two hours of driving. It fits only if Hollie really visits homes. Video calls alone do not qualify.
- **Nextdoor:** a free page. Its free-post limit is unsettled.
- **Local delivery tax:** Lee 6.5%, Collier 6%.

### Hire Hollie

- Every service seller we could read routes inquiries to its own site, not to Instagram DMs. Referrals lead; Instagram is portfolio and warm-up. Two of three small designers have more Pinterest followers than Instagram.
- The written prices sit inside public bands, with two flags: Casting Call at $95 is under the $150 sourcing floor at two competitors, and nobody publishes a rush fee.
- Show proof without clients: Hollie's own corner, mood boards, and the free Screen Test as content. Use a staged call until a lawyer reads the recording and likeness rules.
