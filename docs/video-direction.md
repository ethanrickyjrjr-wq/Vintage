# Holliewood & Vine on camera: what the shots and videos look and feel like

**Status (2026-10-30).** What Hollie films and how she talks, shot in one small corner. Written from 23 transcripts (15 decor reels, 8 vintage-clothing videos), one frame per clothing video, and a handful of practitioner pages. Raw layer and method: `research/video-direction-2026-10-30/README.md`. Nothing here is built or posted.

Tags: `observed` (read or watched in this research), `verified` (primary page read), `vendor claim`, `inferred` (my reading, no measurement), `need review`.

---

## 0. What we are going for

**A friend with great taste, showing you what she just found, in one real corner, in one steady take.** Not a store tour. Not a lookbook. Not a haul video with a ten-minute intro.

Real thoughts, no sugar:

1. **The pieces and Hollie are the show.** The show is **the piece, then Hollie's take on it**. The set is one corner you can recognise, not a room you have to tour.
2. **Talking is how taste gets across; a silent loop cannot say "she has an eye."** Voice and on-camera are the default; Hollie's comfort is the open question (section 8). Be straight about the evidence: no source I found measures talking against silent for vintage. The case is Instagram's own signals (watch time and sends per reach, `verified` in the manual, section 3A), the 8 videos below, and judgment. **Test it as the format factor inside the four-week test in Appendix E** (do not run a second test on the same early posts), and treat the result as a first screen, not a verdict: at zero followers that test mostly measures noise.
3. **"Looks raw" is not the strategy.** Instagram's head predicts a more raw aesthetic and in the same post says authenticity is "becoming infinitely reproducible" (`verified`, one sentence read on his Threads post; the rest is press paraphrase, section 7). Rough edges will be faked too. What cannot be faked is a **specific real piece, a real flaw said out loud, and a person with an opinion.** Be raw in performance, clean in basics: steady, readable, audible.
4. **Everyday means a repeatable place and a repeatable show.** Same corner, same framing, same show names. People return to a show, not to a clip.

## 2. How it looks

What the 8 new videos show (one frame each at 40 s, so counts only, `observed`, n = 8): **ordinary rooms** in 7 of 8 (bedroom, living room, hallway; the eighth is an edited title card), eye-level and waist-up in most, a window visible in at least two, a clothing rack in two and plants in four, a garment held to the camera in one, a full-length outfit from a hallway in one, and **a clip-on mic visible in at least three** (one holds hers to the lens). No lighting gear visible. That the cameras were **locked off**, and that a piece is **held up before it is worn**, cannot be seen in one frame: both are `inferred` from the transcripts and the framing.

| Thing | Do this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Phone vertical, locked off on a clamp or tripod, at eye level for talking. Waist-up for Just in, full-length for fit checks | The 8 frames; the manual's gear kits already cover the stand |
| Distance | Step back and crop in. Avoid the 0.5x lens close up | Ultra-wide distorts edges and warps the room (Ulanzi, `vendor claim`); Appendix D says the same for mirrors |
| Set | One corner, shot diagonally into it, with 2 or 3 objects that always sit there. That corner is the recognisable "set" | Diagonals add depth in a tight room (Ulanzi, `vendor claim`) |
| Light | A window to the side or front, never behind her. Turn on the lamp being shown as a warm accent; lock exposure and white balance as in Appendix D, section D.1 | Appendix D settings, `verified`; 3200K is `inferred` |
| **Sound** | The thing that separates everyday from sloppy. A clip-on mic if she will buy one (price not read); otherwise phone close and quiet. Soft things (rug, curtain, a clothing rack) cut echo in a small hard room | At least 3 of 8 wore visible lav mics; echo advice is Ulanzi, `vendor claim`. Whether the first month needs a mic is `inferred` |
| Text | The hook line on screen from the first frame, and captions on throughout | Manual: 11 of 15 sampled reels had an on-screen hook by about 1 s. Sound-off share is `need review` (no source I trust) |
| Wearables | Hold it to the camera (6 s), wear it (10 s), turn full-length (8 s), detail: label, closure, flaw (5 s) | `inferred` from the transcripts and the format lists |
| Mirrors and glass | A mirror fit check is now fine. Keep the **phone** and anything private out of the reflection. Glass and mirrors that are *for sale* are still shot off-axis, farther back | Appendix D.1 |
| **Privacy, if this is a home** | Keep out of frame: windows that show the street, house numbers, mail, parcels, labels, a car. City or region tag only. Never "visit", "in store", "stop by" | Manual section 2; the smaller space makes it matter more. Whether this is a home is Ricky's to say |

## 3. How to talk

### 3.1 What the transcripts show

| Pattern | Evidence | Take it or cut it |
|---|---|---|
| The best short decor speaker runs a five-beat line: taste, item, reaction, where she found it, an open question. 25 s: *"I have a thing for lamps and this one might be the coolest vintage 1970s chrome cylinder lamp... anything chrome is an immediate yes for me... I bought it from [place]... now I just have to figure out where I want to put it."* | `observed`, `DfDYPbQS2Sz` | **Take it.** This is the spine below |
| A proof you can hear or see: *"Hear that? That ring when you gently flick it"* (the crystal test) | `observed`, `DU4T4UejheP` | Take it: every show gets one proof |
| Opinion in the first person, with a rule: *"in my opinion"*, *"I have a couple of rules"* | `observed`, `DU4T4UejheP`, `liun5XcbFfc` | Take it: she decides, she does not hedge |
| A personal reason for the video: *"I actually wasn't going to film this... people asked for a try-on haul"* | `observed`, `y-Ors35p5ic` | Take it, in one line |
| **Channel intros of roughly 30 to 70 s** ("welcome back", "my name is", "subscribe", "grab a snack") before the first piece | `observed` in 8 of 8 | **Cut all of it.** A Reel has seconds |
| Openings that read like hooks: a thesis (*"A little bit of vintage goes a long way..."*), an opinion (*"vintage is still not appreciated..."*), a problem (*"If you love a piece but doubt you'll wear it..."*) | `observed`, three of 8, each followed by more intro; nothing measures whether they hold viewers | Take the idea, cut the sentence length |
| A drop: *"these pieces can be yours on [date] on my website"* | `observed`, `rd-iMslxU2g` | Fits the plan, but only once real stock exists |
| Honest flaws: sellers who say the flaw early are trusted more | practitioner pages, `vendor claim`; not measured | Take it: it is also your rule |

### 3.2 The six beats (20 to 35 seconds)

1. **Hook, 2 s or less, also on screen.** One line.
2. **What it is.** One specific: [era], [maker or "marked [x]"], [material].
3. **Why her.** A taste line. "I have a thing for [x]."
4. **Proof.** Flick it, click it, turn it, say the measurement and show it on screen.
5. **One honest flaw.** Said out loud, shown close.
6. **Close.** Section 3.4, depending on whether stock exists.

Rough size: **30 to 45 words is about 15 s and 70 to 90 words about 30 s** (`vendor claim`; the "130 to 160 words a minute" figure is `unverified`). Talk to one person. Jump-cut the breaths.

### 3.3 Hollie picks the voice

I am not writing Hollie's voice. Here are three versions of each line. She reads them out loud and keeps the ones that sound like her; she is the decider.

| | A. Taste | B. Reveal | C. Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | "I have a thing for [x], and this might be the best one I've found." | "Okay. This one." | "Would you wear this to [place]?" |
| **Why her** | "Anything [colour, material] is an immediate yes for me." | "I saw it across the room and walked straight to it." | "I'd put it next to [x]. Tell me I'm wrong." |
| **Sign-off** (sparingly) | "Do it for the plot." | "Hollie dresses the set." | none; just stop |

The brand lines ("do it for the plot", "You bring the plot. Hollie dresses the set.") are already in the site. Use one at the end, not every time.

### 3.4 The close: two modes

| Before real stock exists (now): **only these three, no link** | After real stock exists |
|---|---|
| "I'm finding more like this. Follow so you see it first." | "Piece [No.] is on the listing page. Link in bio." (Appendix A.4, line 1) |
| "Save this for the drop." | "Full photos and honest condition: link in bio, piece [No.]." |
| "Tell me what you'd put next to it." (a question, not a link) | "Gone." plus next drop day, for a sold piece |

Later, **once a hunt list page exists** (signup only, no listing; manual 3A item 10; nobody has built it): "Looking for [x]? The hunt list is in the link in bio." Until then the bio points at the sample storefront, whose prices are illustrative, so do not send anyone there from a piece.

### 3.5 Words that are out

- **Never:** visit, in store, stop by, our doors, a business-pin location; "DM to buy", "claim", "comment SOLD", "first to"; discount, code, offer, giveaway; "interior design" (say style).
- **Never promise the mirror ball.** It is "sometimes", not a giveaway (manual section 2).
- **Do not state what you cannot back:** "Stiffel" or "signed" only if marked; otherwise "in the style of" or "marked [x]". No safety claims on wiring or on lead in glass.
- **Cut:** "hey guys", "welcome back", "smash that", "today we're going to".
- **No AI** product imagery, B-roll or avatars (manual section 2).

### 3.6 If she does not want to be on camera

Film the pieces silent, then record her voice while watching the clip (a phone's Voice Memos works; whether Instagram Edits has its own voiceover tool is **not checked**). That works for Click and Just in. It does not work for Fit check, which needs a body in frame, neck-down at minimum.

## 4. Three scripts (illustrative; the brackets are facts only Hollie has)

The catalog items below are illustrative (`research/inventory/2026-10-26-catalog.md`). Swap in what is on the table.

**Just in (about 30 s). Illustrative: brass and cream ceramic lamp.**
Hook on screen: "I have a thing for brass lamps." Spoken: "I have a thing for brass lamps, and this might be the best one I've found. It's [era], [maker or 'unmarked'], brass and cream ceramic with a pineapple finial. *[One proof only she can give: the weight in her hand, a flick, the click of the switch.]* Here's the flaw: [real flaw, close-up]. And when you turn it on, the whole corner changes. [Before stock: Follow so you see the next one first.] [After stock: Piece [No.], link in bio.]"

**Fit check (about 25 s). Illustrative: feathered cocktail hat.**
Hook: "Would you wear this to [place]?" Hold it to the lens: "A [era] cocktail hat, [material], feather is [condition]." Put it on, full turn. "It sits [tilted, flat], it's a [size] and it needs [one honest note]." Last line: "I'd wear it with [one thing]. Tell me what you'd put it with."

**Click (about 12 s). Illustrative: any lamp or glass.**
No talking, or one line over it. Dark corner. Her hand reaches the switch. Light comes on at about 2 to 3 s. Text on screen: "This corner, with one switch." Hold the flaw in the last 2 s if there is one.

## 5. One session

About 90 minutes. **Nobody has timed this.**

| Min | Block | Clips |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 15 | **Set**: pick the corner, clear the frame (street, mail, labels), lock exposure and white balance, record 10 s of her voice and listen on earbuds | none kept |
| 15 to 55 | **Wearables, 6 pieces**: hold to camera 6 s, worn 10 s, full-length turn 8 s, detail 5 s | about 24 |
| 55 to 85 | **Objects, 6 pieces**: hold and talk 20 s, one click or turn 8 s, flaw close-up 5 s, dressed corner 8 s | about 24 |
| 85 to 90 | **Talk**: each hook twice, the three closes from 3.4 | about 12 |

About 60 clips cover eight to ten posts (`inferred`). Film some pieces both ways, talking and silent, so Appendix E's format factor compares like with like (section 0, point 2).
