# eBay photo studio: what we are doing and why

**Status (2026-11-07):** decisions from the two Sonnet sweeps in `research/ebay-photo-studio-2026-11-07/` (21 lanes, 30 claim checks; the verdicts are in that folder's README) plus tests on Ricky's four example photos in this cloud session. The script is built and tested on CPU here; the Windows install and the first GPU run happen when Ricky is at the PC (`docs/ebay-photo-studio-handoff.md`). Prices for gear are asking prices seen in search results that day, not opened pages, and are marked so. Every eBay rule quoted below comes from eBay pages fetched 2026-10-30 (`research/ebay-clothing-photos-2026-10-30/`).

## 1. The short answer

- **Photo 1 is the real garment on a real, visible form, on pure white.** The white comes from our own script, which only removes the room behind the item and never redraws a pixel. Then the back, one in-room burgundy shot (the Etsy and Shopify cover), label, fabric, closures, every flaw, and a measurements card. Eight to twelve photos per listing, square, 1600 px.
- **Close-ups stay exactly as shot.** Labels, flaws and fabric are the trust shots on vintage; the script leaves them alone and only resizes.
- **No ghost mannequin by default, no AI try-on, no AI-generated scenes.** Nothing we found shows pre-owned vintage sellers using invisible-mannequin shots, the visible vintage form is on-brand, and every AI route either carries a non-commercial licence or can quietly change the garment, which on used goods is a returns and policy risk.
- **The phone is the cutter when it matters.** Ricky's call after the frame fight: the iPhone's press-and-hold cut is clean and takes a second, so the script now accepts those transparent PNGs directly, loads no model for them, and only adds the white, the square, the size and the manifest. The model route stays for stacks of photos.
- **The program is right.** rembg with the BiRefNet model (both MIT, free, local, runs on the RTX 4060 Ti) cut the dress off a grey Jeep door cleanly. The one failure, a notch of black frame against a dark pegboard, is fixed by a rectangle repair we added and tested. Six models were compared on that print; the other five were worse.
- **The light is the real limit now, not the software.** The cut-outs are clean; the washed-out navy in Ricky's dress photo came from hard sun. A form, even light and a backdrop a step behind are the next purchase.

The model comparison on the print: `birefnet-general` was the only one of six that kept the whole frame; `birefnet-dis` and `birefnet-massive` dropped the frame, `isnet-general-use` and `u2net` kept only the artwork, and the lite model smeared the right edge. The bench (lane 19, three models on nine photos, every sheet judged by eye): `birefnet-general` never failed. The lite model is 35 to 40 percent faster and matches general on clean garment shots but loses thin frame bars and shelf contents. `isnet-general-use` failed on seven of nine, including washing the Neiman Marcus label to white. So the default stays general; lite is only for a laptop and clean garments.

Two things to change on the shoot side, seen in the photos: take the price tag off before shooting (it stayed in the cut, as the chandelier's tag did in October), and shoot labels in shade so the fabric is not blown out.

## 3. The rules we are inside

eBay, quoted from eBay's own pages (fetched 2026-10-30):

- "You can add up to 24 pictures." The first is the search thumbnail: "Make it your best shot, with your item centered and fully visible."
- "Images should have a minimum resolution of 500 x 500 pixels but we recommend images that are about 1600 x 1600." The picture policy words the floor differently: "at least one photo that is 500 pixels on the longest side". "photos in a 1:1 or 16:9 aspect ratio look best". "maximum 12MB each" (per file) on the US help page; eBay's export hub says "up to 7MB each", so 12 MB is verified, 7 MB needs review, and a 1600 px JPEG is far under both. Accepted: "JPEG, PNG, GIF, TIFF, BMP, WEBP, HEIC, or AVIF".
- "White backgrounds are generally best, but for shiny or reflective items like a piece of jewelry, a darker background may work better." eBay's clothing page: "Use a white or light-colored background for darker clothes, and a black or dark background for lighter clothes."
- Not allowed: "Photos that don't accurately represent the item", "Stock photos for used, damaged, or defective items", "Photos with added borders", "Photos with added text, artwork or marketing material", "Watermarks of any type, including those used for ownership attributions". "You can't use a catalog or stock photo if you're listing an item as used."
- eBay's own listing tool has a Background button that will "automatically replace the background with a solid white color"; the app adds AI scenes and says "Try to only use this option for a few photos". A white cut-out of our own photo is therefore the same thing eBay offers, not a stock photo.
- Flaws: "show clear, closeup images of them". Pre-owned clothing grades are Excellent, Good and Fair, and "every imperfection must be shown and described".

Etsy and Shopify (from `research/cutouts/01-channel-image-rules.md`, fetched 2026-10-27): Etsy's help page says "If a file contains transparency, the transparent parts of the image will appear black on Etsy" and wants "at least one unedited photo of the actual item against a real background"; 2000 px recommended, up to 20 photos. Shopify: "For square product images, a size of 2048 x 2048 px usually displays best". eBay's handling of transparency is unverified, so the safe rule is flat JPEG everywhere: the script writes a 1600 square for eBay, a 2048 square master for Shopify, and keeps the transparent PNG for our own site only. On AI-edited photos (eBay pages re-fetched 2026-11-07): the picture policy has no AI wording; the User Agreement, section 7, says "You assume full responsibility for the item offered and the accuracy and content of the listing, including listing content created using tools offered by eBay or third parties such as translation, image editing, and generative artificial intelligence tools"; eBay's launch story for its AI scenes only says it will "remind them to be transparent about the role of AI where appropriate", a reminder, not a rule. Two more facts from the same fetch: a seller thread measured that eBay search dims pure white to about RGB 242, and Value Added Resource reported on 2026-04-14 that eBay is "testing AI generated fashion models, adding altered images to seller listings without warning or explicit consent", with no opt-out, so a no-AI rule of our own cannot keep eBay's AI off a listing. Check a listing live after publishing.

## 4. The tools

**Use:**

- **rembg 2.0.87** (MIT, PyPI 2026-11-06; its README says Python 3.11 to under 3.14 while PyPI allows under 4.0, so Python 3.12 is the safe pick) running the **BiRefNet general** model. The BiRefNet repo and main Hugging Face card are MIT (pages read 2026-11-07); the other variant cards are MIT per the Hub API only, one untagged research checkpoint aside; MIT permits selling and sublicensing. rembg downloads its own ONNX conversion of the weights from its GitHub release and runs it at 1024 by 1024, so fine lace and fringe are decided at that size. This is `tools/cutout/cutout.py`. Always with the model named: rembg's default model is Bria's RMBG-2.0, and its README says "RMBG-2.0 is released under a BRIA license that requires a paid agreement for commercial use" and the model card says "License: Creative Commons Attribution–Non-Commercial (CC BY-NC 4.0)"; the script refuses it.
- **The rectangle repair** for frames, mirrors, boxes and books (automatic from the file name, or `--rect`).
- **Hugging Face Spaces as the no-install backup:** `not-lain/background-removal` (BiRefNet, MIT, 2,984 likes, running on ZeroGPU, where anonymous visitors get 2 GPU minutes a day and a free account 5) gives a transparent PNG to flatten onto white at home; `ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet_demo` is the author's official demo with adjustable resolution (its page walled the crawler, so the variant list is unchecked). Avoid `Xenova/remove-background-web`: it runs RMBG-1.4, which its card calls "source-available ... for non-commercial use".
- **Later, for lace, fringe, feathers and fur:** BiRefNet HR-matting at 2048 px through the `transformers` route on the GPU (lane 21 has the snippet). rembg cannot load that variant by name.

**Do not use:**

- Bria RMBG-1.4 and RMBG-2.0 (non-commercial), and any tool built on them.
- remove.bg: "The standalone website will no longer be available from 1 December 2026 at 9:00am CET" (re-fetched 2026-11-07; API users are sent to Leonardo.ai).
- AI virtual try-on (IDM-VTON, CatVTON, OOTDiffusion, FitDiT): CC BY-NC or gated non-commercial licences, and the papers themselves admit texture distortion. Kolors' demo is a thin client to a private server. Anything built on FLUX.2-klein-9B (the fal try-on LoRA, the ghost-mannequin LoRA) sits under "the FLUX Non-Commercial License" (card read 2026-11-07); the Apache-2.0 base is the 4B model, and Leffa's MIT covers its code only. If a styling image is ever wanted, it goes last in the gallery, captioned as AI and not the item, or off eBay entirely.
- Upscalers on 12 MP phone photos headed for 1600 px: nothing to gain, and Upscayl describes itself as "guessing what the details could be". Downscale well, sharpen once lightly, done.
- eBay's in-app remover and AI scenes for clothing: seller threads (fetched 2026-11-07) report the eraser and paintbrush removed, a forced black background on dark jeans, "Puckered seams on the pants are from the BG remover", and an uncroppable extra white field; the editor-tint thread is a dead page. Use its White button only as a benchmark.

**Paid fallback, only if Ricky agrees:** Photoroom's Remove Background API at "$0.02 per image on the Basic API plan" (re-fetched 2026-11-07; input PNG, JPEG, WEBP or HEIC; 10 free production calls). Plans are prepaid monthly image buckets that do not roll over, the page's example being $20 for 1,000 images, so the real floor is about $20 a month. Photoroom's app plans (pricing page fetched 2026-11-08 UTC, `research/www.photoroom.com_pricing-20261108T000254Z.md`): Pro is the lowest, 1,000 exports a month, and the page says Pro, Max and Ultra "don't include any API allowance"; the dollar figures did not render. Its bulk background-remover page (`research/www.photoroom.com_batch_background_remover-20261108T000348Z.md`) names no Google Drive import and no batch limit, and offers only "Try for free", which under the terms is non-commercial. Paid, because Photoroom's terms (effective 2026-07-29) license Free Accounts "for your own personal, non commercial purposes". Its ghost mannequin mode is a separate Plus-plan call at $0.10 per image, outputs 1024 by 1024 by default (2K and 4K are Enterprise-only, so below our 1600 px listing size) and regenerates pixels, so it would need a side-by-side check against the real garment before any listing uses it. Also priced the same day: Pixelcut's API at "$0.05 per image" (PNG and JPEG only), Pixian's credit packs from $5.00 for 250 (about one cent per 12 MP image), and Canva's remover "included with paid plans" with no price shown and a 50-image batch cap.

**Phone-only stopgap (lift steps from Apple's iOS 27 page, read 2026-11-07; the Files batch route third-party; unverified on device):** Photos lift-subject is documented by Apple: touch and hold the subject, and when an outline appears tap "Copy or Copy Subject", Add Sticker, or Share. Apple names no file format and no model limit. The Files Quick Action "Remove Background" for a stack of photos is described only by a March 2026 third-party article. Apple's Shortcuts action-list article names no background-removal action, so "nothing built in fills white" is still an absence claim. Clean Up removes objects, not backgrounds, and needs an iPhone 15 Pro or later on iOS 18.1. The PC pipeline stays the real route. Skip third-party shortcuts that upload photos to someone's server.

## 5. The shoot

**The form.** One adjustable, pinnable dress form with an adjustable pole and removable or collapsible arms and shoulders (a Vintage Fashion Guild thread: "you will be better off with a mannequin with removable arms"; Mannequin Mall sells collapsible shoulders "for sellers who need to clothe their mannequins with finished clothing articles") for the daily work, plus the cream linen vintage-look form as the room piece that also appears in shots. For sizes 6 to 10, buy the smaller form and pad up with batting rather than a larger one and pin down. The form should be darker or cream, not bright white, so it separates from the backdrop. Pinning kit: pins at the back and tissue in shoulders and sleeves (a seller guide read 2026-11-07: "Pin at the back and pad the shoulders and sleeves with tissue"), binder clips, painter's tape, and a black cloth under sheers (the cloth is our own idea, unsourced). Clips rather than pins on sheers ("pins simply won't hold the material firmly enough", Photorobot), and test any tape on a hidden seam first, since gaffer tape can leave residue on old fabric. The paint-splattered form in the catalogue is a decor piece, not the working form. Which forms are still on hand after the move is an open question for Ricky.

Prices from pages opened 2026-11-07: Dritz My Double Medium "$155.00" (bust 39½ to 45½ inches, too big for a 6 to 8; stock unclear); on Amazon, Dritz Sew You Small/Medium $129.99, SINGER Small/Medium $174.99 and PDM 6 to 14 $139.99; Bonnlo pinnable foam torsos on a tripod $29.99 (size 2 to 4), $36.00 (6) and $49.99 (10 to 12), each plus $19.99 delivery, so a full-size pinnable form under $60 does exist; Mannequin Mall French forms "from $182.99" (regular $305.00) and a professional form with collapsible shoulders from $427.99; the antique linen spindle-base form $375.00 but sold out; Fabulous Fit's padding system, Medium for women's 6 to 10, from $85.00 (that figure is the Small half-body variant). Still unverified: the Etsy $125 form, Roxy linen forms, and used Acme prices (the eBay sold search walled).

**The light and backdrop.** The sources agree on one setup: two soft lights at 45 degrees, no flash, the form at least five feet in front of the backdrop so its shadow does not climb the paper (pixc's figure; fashn's "five or six feet" is the softbox-to-form distance), the paper curved down to the floor, and a third light on the backdrop only if the white reads grey. Same bulbs in every light, 5500 to 5700 K, CRI 90 or better (the Neewer kit's bulbs state 5700 K and no CRI; its E26 sockets take other bulbs). Pale garments go on a dark backdrop per eBay's own clothing page ("a black or dark background for lighter clothes"), and the script whitens it. Cheapest tier (vendor pages opened 2026-11-07): one Neewer NK200 kit, which holds two 24-inch softboxes, two 35 W 5700 K bulbs, two 83-inch stands and a bag, "From $139.99" at neewer.com before its sale banner; a 10 by 7 foot backdrop stand, Neewer's own $59.99 (the $30 to $46 Walmart and EMART stands are unchecked); Savage #01 Super White, 53 in by 36 ft, $44.99 at B&H, which calls 53 in the one-person width and lists 86 and 107 in rolls unpriced; a phone tripod with remote $7 to $20 (unchecked); about $222 to $265 all in. Whether a 53-inch roll is wide enough for coats and the feather hat, and whether the smaller room has five feet of depth, are open.

**The shot list, in order.** Front on the form, white. In the burgundy room (unedited, the Etsy and Shopify cover). Back. A flat or hanging view where the drape on the form misleads. Label and maker tag. Fabric close-up. Closures and hardware. Every flaw, same light. Measurements card with the tape visible (pit to pit, shoulder, length, waist, sleeve), numbers also in the description. Eight to twelve photos. Flat lay only for denim, tees, scarves and accessories.

**Naming on the phone or PC before the script runs:** `B12-front.heic`, `B12-back.heic`, `B12-label.heic`, `B12-flaw1.heic`. The second word decides the treatment (section 7).

## 6. Ghost mannequin: when, if ever

Ghost (invisible) mannequin means two shots per garment, front on the form plus the inside of the neck shot separately, then a composite in Photoshop or GIMP, 10 to 30 minutes a piece by a skilled hand, up to an hour for structured outerwear, and two to three times that for a beginner (fashn's and retouch4me's own pages, read 2026-11-07). The automation field is thin: one GitHub project with no licence and no stars that needs SAM 3.1 weights and expects hanger shots, two zero-like Spaces, and a FLUX LoRA that regenerates the garment on a non-commercial 9B base. The hosted ghost services sell interior invention: WearView says its "AI reconstructs hidden garment parts (inner necklines, linings, seams)", which for a one-off used garment means a made-up neck or label. The seller threads, now read in full, are split and carry no data. One eBay seller found "the flat lay items are outselling the ones displayed on a mannequin" (one shop, an old locked thread); a high-end vintage seller "graduated to a dress form... these extras have long since paid for themselves in higher sales and price-points"; eBay's own 2014 research paper found that between mannequin and flat lay "such difference is hardly observable from the real online market data", and only a person on model wins. No eBay community page mentions ghost mannequin at all. Buyers of vintage want to see fit and flaws.

Decision: visible form by default. Ghost only clean, structured, flawless pieces (blazers, coats, tailored shirts) and always alongside a visible-form or flat shot. Never on fragile, stretched or stained pieces, and never with a generative method unless the result is compared against the real garment. If Ricky wants to try it, the cheapest test is Photoroom's single-photo mode on one blazer against a manual two-shot composite of the same blazer.

## 7. Automation: what the script now does

`tools/cutout/cutout.py` (tested here on CPU; the handoff has the Windows install):

- Reads JPEG, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC, rotates by EXIF, converts Display P3 to sRGB.
- A transparent PNG (a cut-out from the phone) is recognised on sight: no model, just trim, pure white, 1600 and 2048, manifest row "cut on the phone, framed here". The model loads only when a photo actually needs cutting.
- `--check` writes `check/NAME.jpg` for frames: the cut-out on green with the filled gaps tinted magenta, so what was patched is visible at a glance.
- `--reframe` is for framed art with an undamaged frame: the frame is rebuilt from its own bar (the widest bar's median colour profile, so glare and the model's nicks drop out) around the mat and picture exactly as photographed, with mitred corners. A chipped or worn frame must be shown, not rebuilt, so this stays an option.
- `--straighten` squares up a frame, mirror or box shot at an angle: the four corners of the outline are found and the item is warped to a true rectangle. Real pixels resampled, nothing invented, but the proportions are estimated from the photo, so it is an option to compare, not the default.
- Decides the treatment from the file name. `front`, `back`, `side` or anything else: cut out onto white. `label`, `tag`, `flaw`, `detail`, `close`, `inside`, `measure`, `fabric`, `hem`, `button`, `zipper`: kept exactly as shot, resized only. `frame`, `framed`, `art`, `mirror`, `painting`, `poster`, `box`, `book`, `sign`, `tray`, `record`, `album`: cut, then the rectangle repair. Not `print` or `canvas`: a floral print dress and a canvas tote are clothes.
- Writes `ebay/NAME.jpg` (1600 square, item at 85%), `listing/NAME.jpg` (2048 square master), `site/NAME.png` (transparent, our site only), and a `manifest.csv` row with the model, the processor, seconds, the share of pixels untouched, and warnings.
- `--shadow` adds a soft contact shadow under objects standing on a surface (lamps, shoes, hats, bags); `--shadow drop` gives a faint drop shadow behind a hanging or framed item (whitebg's recipe, MIT). Off for clothes on a form; the PNG never has one.
- `--watch` keeps running and processes each photo two seconds after it stops growing in the folder, so it can run while Ricky shoots and the phone syncs. `stop.txt` in the folder or Ctrl+C ends it. Photos already done are skipped unless `--force`.
- Warns when fewer than 99% of the item's pixels match the photo, when the item is mostly see-through (glass: list the original), and when the item is small in the frame.
- Refuses the Bria model.
- Holds memory flat across a batch: onnxruntime's CPU memory arena is off, because with it on the second photo of every run here was killed at the 16 GB limit (9.7 GB peak), and with it off three photos ran at a flat 8.1 GB, about two seconds slower each. On the GPU route this matters less.

Not built, on purpose: ghost mannequin, any generative fill, upscaling. Glass is still "list the original sweep shot".
